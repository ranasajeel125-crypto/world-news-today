Mexico Captures FBI Most-Wanted Drug Lord 'El Chapo Isidro' in Sinaloa Raid That Killed 11

world · 22 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Mexican soldiers escort alleged drug lord Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as "El Chapo Isidro", after his capture in Guasave, Sinaloa, on Friday, 20 November 2026.

Mexican military forces captured one of the FBI's ten most wanted fugitives, the alleged drug lord Fausto Isidro Meza Flores, known as "El Chapo Isidro", in a major operation in the state of Sinaloa on Friday night, Mexico's security chief said on Saturday morning. Two other leaders of his drug trafficking organisation were captured with him, and eleven people were killed in the gun battle that followed the raid.

Meza Flores, 44, is the alleged leader of the Meza-Flores transnational criminal organisation, also known as Los Mazatlecos, based in Sinaloa, the north-western state that is the cradle of Mexico's drug-trafficking industry. For more than twenty years, according to the US State Department, he and his organisation smuggled vast amounts of heroin, methamphetamine, cocaine, fentanyl and marijuana into the United States. The FBI had offered a $5 million reward for information leading to his arrest, and he had been indicted on drug-smuggling charges in the United States since 2012.

He was considered one of the leaders of the Beltran Leyva cartel, which had reportedly allied with the Los Mayos faction of the powerful Sinaloa Cartel, according to Reuters. His organisation controlled key smuggling routes along the US-Mexico border, including much of the border state of Sonora, across from Arizona, where fentanyl and other narcotics move north. The State Department said last year that his organisation "engaged in gunbattles, kidnapping, murder, torture and extortion to maintain control of its territory".

The operation was carried out by elite military forces using "stealth and surprise" to reach their target, Mexico's defence ministry said in a post on X. Special forces descended on the city of Guasave, in northern Sinaloa, on Friday night, and captured Meza Flores inside a house before transporting him to Mexico City. The defence ministry said the operation was carried out with US intelligence, and that Mexican Army special forces and National Guard special forces took part.

Seven other alleged members of the criminal organisation were arrested in the same operation, the Defence Secretariat said. They include the group's second-in-command, Germán "N", alias "Serafín", and José "N", its alleged financial operator. Armed forces seized drugs, cash, ten firearms, a grenade launcher, ammunition and other weapons, the ministry said. Video shared by the ministry showed armed soldiers escorting the three handcuffed men into a building after their capture.

The raid set off a wave of violence across Sinaloa. Four members of the Mexican Army were killed during the operation, along with seven people who allegedly attempted to stop the arrest, Mexican Security Secretary Omar García Harfuch said. The defence ministry said that, upon initiating the operation, authorities "came under attack with gunfire and explosives", and that militants set up roadblocks in several municipalities near the arrest site, torching trucks and heavy vehicles to block roads.

The fighting reached into everyday life in Guasave, an area known as the agricultural heart of Mexico. Dramatic footage, verified by CNN, showed Mexican security force helicopters firing at the ground and circling over the city. At Kuroda Park stadium, baseball fans scrambled out of the stands as a barrage of gunfire rang out on Friday night, and players from the Venados de Mazatlán and Algodoneros de Guasave teams fled the field mid-game. "Get down, duck down, down, down!" one person shouted in the panic as people clambered over barriers. The game, part of Mexico's premier professional baseball league, was suspended, and the state's public security secretariat urged residents to stay indoors and avoid travelling on the roads.

The capture is the latest in a string of high-profile suspected drug traffickers Mexico has captured or killed, under pressure from Washington. US President Donald Trump has repeatedly threatened to take unilateral military action inside Mexico if President Claudia Sheinbaum's administration does not do more to crack down on the cartels. Mexico's Security Minister Omar García Harfuch said the capture of Meza Flores and two of his accomplices represents "an important step toward the pacification of Sinaloa", a state ravaged by turf wars that have killed thousands.

Meza Flores now awaits extradition to the United States to face alleged drug-trafficking charges, Mexico's defence ministry said. Neither the US State Department nor the FBI immediately replied to requests for comment on the capture. His arrest follows the capture of another cartel cell leader earlier this month with the help of US Drug Enforcement Administration intelligence, in a campaign that has steadily removed senior figures from Mexico's trafficking organisations.

For the communities of Sinaloa, the operation was a reminder of the price of those captures. Guasave's residents spent Friday night sheltering from gunfire, its highways blocked by burning trucks, and its baseball stadium emptied in minutes. The interim governor of Sinaloa, Graciela Domínguez Nava, said residents became stranded along parts of highways as the violence escalated. Eleven people are dead, including four soldiers, and the state is counting the cost of a single night's operation against one of the most wanted men in the hemisphere.

Why it matters: the capture of "El Chapo Isidro" removes a trafficker who had run one of Mexico's most violent organisations for more than two decades, controlling the border routes through Sonora that carry fentanyl and other drugs into the United States, and it is the latest test of whether Mexico's campaign of high-profile captures — carried out under direct pressure from Washington — can weaken the cartels faster than they can replace their leaders. The gun battle that followed the raid, the torched roadblocks, the suspended baseball game and the eleven dead show the other side of that strategy: every capture is also a night of war in the streets of Sinaloa, and the cartels that remain are still capable of turning a single arrest into a city-wide siege.
