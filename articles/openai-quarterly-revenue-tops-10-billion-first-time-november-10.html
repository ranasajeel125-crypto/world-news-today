OpenAI’s Quarterly Revenue Tops $10 Billion for the First Time — But Losses Widen to $10.9 Billion

technology · 10 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (Technology Desk)

OpenAI's quarterly revenue rose about 50% to $10 billion in the third quarter, the Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday, 10 November, citing documents the company shared with investors — the first time the ChatGPT maker has crossed the $10 billion mark in a single quarter. Reuters reported the figures the same day. The growth was an acceleration after a slower second quarter, when revenue grew by only 18 per cent compared with the first quarter, and it came as the artificial intelligence industry's enormous spending faces growing scrutiny from investors over whether it can ever translate into profits.

The growth was driven by the enterprise business, which doubled quarter over quarter, Chief Financial Officer Sarah Friar said, with revenue now split roughly evenly between enterprise and consumer customers. Friar put OpenAI's annual revenue run rate at roughly $50 billion. OpenAI recently told investors that the number of paying users on ChatGPT rose to 100 million, or 8.5 per cent of its weekly active user base, with about 1.2 billion weekly users in total. OpenAI President Greg Brockman said at the Wall Street Journal's Tech conference earlier this month that the first two quarters of the year were tough but that the third quarter saw an "incredible revenue inflection".

The company is still far from profitable. Its operating loss widened to $10.9 billion in the quarter, from $10.1 billion in the second quarter, according to the documents reviewed by the Journal. Its adjusted operating margin improved to minus 109% from minus 150% — in plain terms, it lost about $1.09 for every $1 of revenue, against $1.50 a quarter earlier. The loss figures exclude stock-based compensation, which rose to $2.6 billion in the quarter. The calculations show that sales grew faster than losses, so the margin improved, but the loss still exceeded the entire quarter's revenue, and adjusted operating loss is not the same as cash burned.

A year earlier, in the third quarter of 2025, OpenAI reported $3.9 billion in sales, the documents showed — a measure of how fast its revenue has grown, and of how heavy the spending behind that growth remains. Revenue in the same quarter is now about two and a half times that figure, and the operating margin a year ago was minus 97 per cent. The spending reflects the cost of the computing power needed to train and run large AI models, as well as a broad research programme into areas such as robotics and image generation that the company says will pay off in the future. OpenAI has also cut prices for its Codex coding assistant to keep up with Anthropic, which has eaten into profit margins, and has pulled back from products such as the social-video app Sora. It is expecting that advertising, which made its debut on ChatGPT earlier this year, will improve margins.

The numbers arrive as investors scrutinise whether the artificial intelligence industry's enormous spending can translate into profits. Rival Anthropic reported $17 billion in third-quarter revenue with a much smaller operating loss of $567 million, in an amended filing made on Tuesday ahead of its much-anticipated stock market listing, which could value it at about $1.4 trillion. Anthropic's revenue was up 1,127 per cent from a year earlier, when it was $1.4 billion, and the company brought in $33.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, after $4.6 billion in all of 2025 and $386 million in 2024. Its growth has slowed from about 144 per cent quarter to quarter to about 47 per cent, and its spending commitments sit at roughly $900 billion, including $895.4 billion in purchase commitments and lease agreements as of 30 September. Anthropic reported an operating profit of $974 million in the second quarter, when revenue was nearly $11.6 billion, though its third-quarter net loss was $23.3 billion, compared with a loss of $12 billion a year earlier.

Anthropic said in its filing that it and some of its investors would sell 825 million shares of Class A stock at an estimated IPO price of between $70 and $78 a share, raising about $64.4 billion at the top of the range, and it is bidding to raise another $26 billion by selling shares to large technology companies including Google, Microsoft and Nvidia. At the top of the range, its market value would be about $1.02 trillion, with a fully diluted valuation of about $1.4 trillion when accounting for options and unvested stock awards, down from the roughly $2 trillion figure floated months ago. The company is set to begin trading on the Nasdaq on 19 November under the ticker ANT, with the final price expected on 18 November. Analysts at William Blair estimate the company will bring in $135 billion of revenue in 2027. Anthropic ended October with annualised revenue of about $81 billion, roughly $10 billion more than just a month earlier, and each employee supports $6.6 million in trailing 12-month revenue as of 30 September.

OpenAI, which is privately held and does not publish financial statements, did not immediately respond to a Reuters request for comment. The company also told investors it has raised $11 billion so far in a new funding round, and it raised money at a $1.25 trillion valuation, less than the $1.4 trillion valuation it had earlier hoped to achieve. The contrast between the two companies frames the central question facing the AI industry: demand for AI is enormous, with ChatGPT alone reaching more than a billion weekly users, but whether the companies building the models can turn that demand into a profitable business remains open. For OpenAI, the third quarter showed it can grow revenue fast; it also showed that, for now, every dollar of revenue still costs more than a dollar to produce.
