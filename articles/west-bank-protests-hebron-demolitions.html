Palestinians Protest West Bank Demolitions as Israelis Vote, UN Says Over 3,000 Displaced

world · 27 Oct 2026 · 7 min read · By Layla Haddad (Middle East Correspondent)

The United Nations said on Monday that more than 3,000 Palestinians had been forced from their homes by the Israeli military operation near Hebron.

Israeli troops fired a stun grenade on Tuesday to disperse Palestinians who gathered to protest against an Israeli military operation that has displaced thousands of residents from a neighbourhood in Hebron in the Israeli-occupied West Bank.

The unrest, which follows the deaths of two Israeli soldiers shot by Palestinian gunmen at a checkpoint last week, unfolded as Israelis were voting in an election in which the country's posture in the West Bank has been a major political issue.

Some Palestinian officials have accused Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu of launching a crackdown in the West Bank to coincide with the vote, as a way to energise his right-wing supporters. The government says its operation is a response to the attack on its soldiers. After the 23 October attack, in which both gunmen were killed, Netanyahu ordered the Israeli military to launch a large-scale operation in areas near the checkpoint, triggering protests by Palestinian residents.

A group of Palestinian men, women, and children gathered at Tuesday's protest. One elderly man held a placard that said: "No to collective punishment, no to closures, and no to displacement." The military said it had fired a stun grenade to disperse them so that its operation could continue.

On Monday, the United Nations said that over 3,000 Palestinians had been forced from their homes by the Israeli military operation, which targeted neighbourhoods near the checkpoint where Palestinian gunmen killed the two Israeli soldiers on Friday. "Collective punishment is a grave violation of international law," said UN human rights chief Volker Turk in a statement. "Threats to empty and erase entire neighbourhoods also raise serious concerns of ethnic cleansing." Turk later said the displacement of residents raised "grave concerns of unnecessary and disproportionate use of force".

The Israeli military said in a statement on Sunday that troops would carry out demolitions in the area. It said soldiers had not located any weapons in searches of the houses. Since Friday's attack on the checkpoint, Israel has blocked the entrances to most Palestinian cities and towns across the West Bank, severely curtailing Palestinians' freedom of movement. It has closed most entrances to the city of Hebron, severely curtailing freedom of movement for Palestinians, including people in need of medical attention.

Noura Nassar, a displaced resident who attended the protests, said she had struggled to get medical treatment because of the blockages. "Where is the democracy in all of this? Their elections are today, sure, but what about us? Aren't we human beings? We want to go out, we want to visit the doctor," said Nassar.

On Friday 30 October, Israel demolished the homes of the assailants who killed the two Israeli soldiers, part of the ongoing operation that has forcibly displaced over 3,000 Palestinians. The homes demolished had been occupied by the wives and children of the gunmen before the military forced Palestinians to leave the area over the past week, according to local residents. The military has also demolished other homes as well as shops, roads, and factories in Hebron since the attack, according to local officials, though the exact scale of the demolitions is unclear. In a statement announcing the demolitions, Israel's military said it would "continue to operate proactively and offensively" to thwart attacks in the West Bank and "protect residents in the area".

The operation in Hebron was different from other recent incidents. The military occupied the area in response to the deadly attack on 23 October, and it destroyed structures that it said posed a threat to future security operations, although it was extremely vague about what those operations were and why entire buildings needed to be destroyed. The operation seemed to be motivated by public statements made by Netanyahu and Defence Minister Israel Katz about clearing or destroying the neighbourhood of the two men involved in the 23 October incident, apparently a collective punishment measure. While Israel has placed closures on problematic areas in the past, it has not carried out mass evacuations and building demolitions to such an extent without a specific reason or terror connection.

Already by 25 October, the military was recommending that the evacuated Palestinians should be allowed to return, saying it had inspected all of the relevant areas and that there was no clear reason to remain. Until the elections, Netanyahu rejected the chief of staff's recommendation to leave the area and to allow the 2,000 evacuated Palestinians to return to their homes. On Monday 2 November, the military announced that it was withdrawing its forces from the Hebron neighbourhood it had occupied since the previous weekend. In its response filed with the court on Monday, the state said there was no longer a security or humanitarian need that justified preventing the return of the residents to their homes, and residents began returning shortly after 6am. Adalah, the legal centre that brought the petition, said that for 10 days, the Israeli military had forced some 5,000 Palestinians from their homes and demolished buildings, and more than 4,000 people were displaced from their homes during the 11-day military siege that followed the attack.

The pressure on Hebron has not ended. Israeli authorities later issued demolition orders for two residential buildings and a villa in the city, in the Bir Al-Mahjar neighbourhood, northwest of Hebron. Hebron Mayor Yousef Al-Jabari said the residences were built with permits from the Hebron Municipality, as they are located in Area A, where the Palestinian Authority controls security and administrative matters. The demolition orders will affect dozens of families living in 25 apartments in one building and 40 apartments in another, along with a villa. Al-Jabari said the Israeli authorities claim the structures are too close to the settler Route 35, describing the demolition notices as a policy of collective punishment targeting Hebron, and the municipality is taking legal action to stop the orders.

Netanyahu and his main rival Gadi Eisenkot both oppose the creation of an independent Palestinian state alongside Israel. Residents expelled from the Hebron neighbourhood said Tuesday's election in Israel would do little to change the reality on the ground in territories Israel has occupied since 1967. "Among themselves they will elect who is the oppressor and who will destroy the Palestinian people. That is their election," said Sana' Salman Abu Sneineh, another expelled resident.

Netanyahu's government has presided over a rapid expansion of Israeli settlements, which are deemed illegal by UN bodies and most countries, though Israel disputes this. Under Netanyahu's right-wing coalition there has also been a surge of militant settler violence against Palestinians. Attacks have surged during the Palestinian olive harvest, which starts at the end of September and ends in November, and the Israeli rights group Yesh Din reported at least 135 physical attacks on the olive harvest as of 23 October.

Hebron is one of the most sensitive cities in the West Bank. It is the largest Palestinian city in the territory, and it is also home to a small community of Israeli settlers who live in the heart of the old city, protected by a heavy military presence, near the Cave of the Patriarchs, a site holy to both Jews and Muslims. The city has been divided since 1997 into areas under Palestinian and Israeli control, and movement between them is restricted by checkpoints, closed streets and military orders. For the Palestinians who live there, the operation in October was the latest in a long series of closures, demolitions and displacements, and for the Israelis who voted on Tuesday, it was a reminder that the West Bank, and what to do with it, remains at the centre of their politics.
