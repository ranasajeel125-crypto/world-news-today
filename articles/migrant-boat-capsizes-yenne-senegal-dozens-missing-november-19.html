Dozens Missing After Migrant Boat Carrying About 100 Capsizes Off Senegal

world · 19 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Dozens of people are missing after a boat carrying about 100 migrants capsized off the Senegalese coast in the early hours of Wednesday, in the second deadly shipwreck off the country in less than two weeks.

Senegalese authorities are continuing search operations after a boat reportedly carrying about 100 migrants capsized off the coast near the town of Yenne, south of Dakar, the BBC reported. Initial reports said that one person had died, but some local media have reported that at least four bodies have been recovered. Dozens of people are still missing.

The wooden pirogue struck a rocky area and washed ashore in a badly damaged state, local officials told the AFP news agency. The accident happened overnight from Tuesday to Wednesday. Oumar Sow, a representative of the village chief, said the tragedy happened around 02:00 local time (02:00 GMT). "Cries could be heard coming from the pirogue that were audible even from the beach," AFP quoted him as saying.

A survivor questioned by the police said there were about 100 people on the boat, including five women, AFP reported. No total of survivors or rescued people has been reported, and the full toll was not immediately clear. Yenne Mayor Massamba Mbengue and local firefighters said one body had been recovered, while two other local sources put the number at two, according to the BBC.

Police recovered identity documents, several mobile phones and personal belongings from the wreckage, including a school item believed to be associated with a high school student. Gendarmes sorting through the items found eight identity documents and a school enrolment document belonging to a high school student from Kabrousse, a town in southern Senegal, along with water sachets and mobile phones.

Search operations continued throughout Wednesday and were due to resume on Thursday, as families waited for news of relatives who had been on board. The badly damaged boat washed ashore near Yenne, and children were photographed standing next to the wreckage on the beach.

The incident comes less than two weeks after a shipwreck off the coast of the north-western town of Thieppe killed at least 39 people, in one of Senegal's deadliest migration disasters. That boat capsized between Dakar and the northern city of Saint-Louis as it headed towards Spain's Canary Islands, and only one person was rescued.

At a cabinet meeting on Wednesday, President Bassirou Diomaye Faye expressed concern about a renewed increase in irregular departures by migrants hoping to reach Europe. He instructed the interior ministry to prepare a report examining the factors driving the dangerous journeys and proposing measures to tackle the problem. Following last month's tragedy, Faye had urged Senegalese people not to risk their lives attempting the dangerous sea crossings.

Local officials say Yenne has turned into a departure point for irregular migration, with smugglers coming into the village to recruit fishermen to pilot boats along the dangerous Atlantic route toward Spain's Canary Islands. Thousands of people from West Africa have died or gone missing trying to flee conflict, authoritarian rule or poverty.

The UN's migration agency, the IOM, said last month that it had recorded at least 454 deaths and disappearances this year along the Atlantic route off the West African coast alone. It warned the figures were likely to be an underestimate, as many boats disappear without leaving survivors or a trace.

The departures are part of a wider surge on the Atlantic route. On 10 November, Spain declared a migration emergency for the Canary Islands, where more than 9,000 people had landed since the beginning of November. Its Maritime Rescue Service said it had rescued 1,488 migrants on 14 dinghies, most of whom arrived on El Hierro in the Canary Islands from West Africa on 14 November.

What happens next: the search off Yenne resumes on Thursday, and the death toll may rise as more of the coast is searched. Senegalese human rights group Action for Human Rights and Friendship (ADHA) has called on the government to conduct a parliamentary inquiry into the tragedy. The interior ministry's report ordered by President Faye will be the first test of whether the government can do more than mourn the dead — by disrupting the smuggling networks that recruit boat pilots in villages like Yenne, and by giving young Senegalese a reason not to board.

Why it matters: two deadly wrecks in ten days off the same coastline show that the Atlantic route to the Canary Islands is becoming more dangerous, not less, even as arrivals surge. The Yenne boat carried about 100 people, including a school student whose enrolment document washed up with the wreckage — a reminder that the passengers are not statistics but neighbours, children and classmates. Until the search ends, dozens of families in Senegal and across West Africa are waiting to learn whether their loved ones are among the missing.
