2026 Set to Be One of the Four Hottest Years on Record, UN Report Says

climate · 9 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Every one of the past 12 years now ranks among the 12 warmest ever recorded, the WMO said; 2026 is all but certain to be the second year after 2024 to pass 1.5 degrees above pre-industrial levels.

2026 is on track to be one of the four hottest years on record, driven by a powerful El Niño and extending a warming streak of more than a decade, the United Nations' weather agency said on Monday.

The World Meteorological Organization's annual State of the Climate report showed that between January and September this year, the global average temperature was 1.47 degrees Celsius above the pre-industrial average, and that it could rise further because of a major El Niño, Reuters reported.

The report, which draws on nine data sets, showed that each of the past 12 years, including 2026, was ranked among the 12 warmest ever recorded since records began in around 1850.

"Every indicator here is telling the same story. We're seeing the heat trapped by greenhouse gasses is showing up in the ocean, in the atmosphere, in the melting of ice and the rising of sea levels," John Kennedy, the WMO's head of climate information, told reporters in Geneva ahead of the climate conference.

The planet is increasingly accumulating heat, the report said, with ocean temperatures continuing to rise this year from all-time highs reached in 2025. Global mean sea levels have also reached a record this year as glaciers and ice sheets melt and seawater expands as it heats, it said.

The European Union's Copernicus climate service has already said 2026 was all but certain to become the second calendar year after 2024 to exceed the threshold of 1.5 degrees Celsius above pre-industrial levels set in the 2015 Paris Agreement.

The findings land as countries gather in Turkey from 9 to 20 November for the next round of climate talks, COP31, in Antalya, where the WMO report provides the latest available data to inform their discussions.
