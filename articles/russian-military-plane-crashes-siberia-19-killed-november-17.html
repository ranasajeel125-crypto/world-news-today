Russian Military Plane Crashes in Siberia, Killing All 19 on Board

world · 17 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Footage purportedly showing the crash near the village of Yagunovo circulated on social media; the defence ministry said the aircraft came down in an uninhabited area.

A Russian military transport plane has crashed in Siberia, killing all 19 people on board, the country’s defence ministry has said. The Antonov An-12 went down during a scheduled flight in the Kemerovo region on Tuesday, and the ministry said it preliminarily believed a technical malfunction was the cause.

“There were 19 people on board, including the crew. All of them lost their lives,” the ministry said in a statement carried by Russian state media. It said the aircraft had been on a scheduled flight when it crashed in an uninhabited area, and that an investigation by Russia’s aviation authorities was under way.

The confirmed death toll was higher than first reported. Russian media initially reported that 17 people had been killed, citing emergency services, before the defence ministry revised the figure to 19. The BBC reported that the ministry’s statement put the number of people on board at 19, including the crew, and that all had died.

The aircraft was flying from Tolmachevo Airport in Novosibirsk to Kansk Air Base in the Krasnoyarsk region, a routine military transport route of roughly 800 kilometres across Siberia, according to reports. The plane was carrying crew members and soldiers as well as spare parts, and it came down near the village of Yagunovo, south of the regional capital Kemerovo, after a fire was reported on board.

Footage purportedly showing the moment of the crash circulated on social media on Tuesday, appearing to show the aircraft burning as it fell from the sky and then hitting the ground in a fireball. The BBC published a still from the footage, describing it as purported CCTV of the crash near Yagunovo. Russian media reported that the aircraft had suffered an engine fire and had been attempting an emergency landing at Kemerovo airport when it went down.

Kemerovo Governor Ilya Seredyuk confirmed on Telegram that an “aviation incident” had occurred in the Yagunovo district, the BBC reported. He said an emergency response team had “quickly extinguished a blaze” after the crash and that the area had been sealed off by police. A military commission from the Russian Air Force is taking part in the investigation, according to reports.

The An-12 is a Soviet-era four-engined turboprop transport aircraft, designed in the 1950s and long used as a workhorse of Russian military transport aviation. The type has been in service for decades and remains in use with the Russian Air Force for moving troops and cargo, despite its age. The aircraft involved was an An-12BK, a variant of the long-serving transport.

The crash site is far from the war in Ukraine — roughly 3,400 kilometres east of the front lines — and there is no suggestion that the aircraft was shot down or that the crash was connected to the fighting. The defence ministry’s account is that the aircraft suffered a technical failure on a routine domestic flight, and no hostile action has been reported.

Russia has suffered serious An-12 accidents before. In August 2011, an An-12 operated by Avis-Amur Airlines crashed in the Magadan region after an engine caught fire, killing all 11 people on board. In November 2019, an An-12 crashed near Lviv airport in western Ukraine, killing five people and injuring three more. And in November 2021, a Belarusian An-12 cargo plane crashed near Irkutsk in Siberia, with no survivors among those on board.

The Kemerovo crash is the latest in a series of aviation accidents in Siberia and the Russian Far East, a vast region where military and civilian aircraft fly long routes in harsh conditions. The authorities have not yet said how long the investigation will take or when the victims will be formally identified.

What happens next: investigators will examine the wreckage and the flight recorders to establish the sequence of events, including whether the fire reported on board began in an engine and whether the crew attempted an emergency landing. The defence ministry is expected to issue a fuller account once the military commission has completed its initial findings, and the names of those killed are likely to be released after their families have been informed.

Why it matters: nineteen deaths on a single routine flight make this one of the worst Russian military aviation accidents in recent years, and it raises fresh questions about the safety of the ageing Soviet-era aircraft that still form the backbone of Russia’s military transport fleet. The An-12 has been flying for more than half a century, and each new accident sharpens the debate over how long such aircraft can safely be kept in service. For now, the ministry’s explanation is a technical malfunction — but the investigation’s findings will determine whether this was an isolated failure or a symptom of a wider problem.
