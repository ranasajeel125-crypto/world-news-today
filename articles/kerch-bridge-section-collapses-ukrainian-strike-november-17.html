Section of Crimea Bridge Collapses After Ukrainian Strike

world · 17 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Images geolocated by CNN showed a section of the road bridge, including its distinctive white arch, lying in the water; the parallel rail bridge appeared still to be standing.

A section of the bridge linking Russia to occupied Crimea has collapsed into the Kerch Strait after a Ukrainian strike, cutting Moscow’s only direct road and rail link to the peninsula. It was the second attack on the crossing in two days, and the damage this time appears far more serious than in any previous strike on the bridge.

Videos and images geolocated by CNN appear to show a portion of the road bridge collapsed into the water, with the parallel rail bridge still standing. A fire could be seen burning on another section of the crossing. The BBC reported that images shared by Ukrainian officials showed a section of the Crimean Bridge — also known as the Kerch Bridge — had collapsed, and a former adviser to Ukraine’s president shared footage showing the remnants of a support arch and a section of road protruding from the water.

Russia’s transport ministry said traffic on both the road and rail sections had been suspended and that experts were assessing the damage, adding that there were no casualties, according to preliminary information. Russia’s Investigative Committee confirmed the attack on the bridge, calling it a “terrorist act by Ukrainian armed groups”, and opened a terrorism investigation. The Moscow-installed governor of Crimea, Sergei Aksyonov, confirmed the second attack and said two people had been killed in the wider attack on Crimea.

The Guardian reported that a 227-metre road arch span had fallen into the Kerch Strait, and that the adjacent railway arch had also sustained damage. The debris appears to have blocked the main navigable route beneath the bridge, which connects the Sea of Azov with the Black Sea and is used by shipping from the ports of Berdiansk and Mariupol.

The 19-kilometre bridge, opened by President Vladimir Putin in 2018 after Russia’s annexation of Crimea in 2014, is one of his signature infrastructure projects and the longest crossing in Europe. The $3.7 billion crossing was built to handle 40,000 cars a day and move 14 million passengers and 13 million tons of cargo a year, according to Russian state media, and it has been a vital supply line for Moscow’s troops on the peninsula. Ukraine has declared the bridge illegal.

The bridge had been struck the day before, on Monday, when a Ukrainian drone crashed into one of its arches. The Kremlin said on Monday that the first attack had caused only minor damage, and limited traffic resumed. The second strike, in the early hours of Tuesday, did far more damage: the road arch over the navigable span and the roadway beside it are now in the water, according to photos and video from several angles.

The bridge strike was part of what Russia described as the biggest Ukrainian drone attack of the war. Russia’s Defence Ministry said its air defences had shot down 1,441 Ukrainian drones overnight over 22 regions, surpassing Monday’s figure of 1,336, which had itself set a record, according to the ministry’s data. Air defences also repelled attacks on industrial areas in Ufa and Salavat, in the Bashkortostan region, Reuters reported.

Ukraine has not formally claimed responsibility for either strike on the bridge. Ukrainian media reported that the overnight strike used FP-5 Flamingo long-range cruise missiles, and footage released by Ukraine’s military appeared to show one of the missiles moments before impact. Ukraine has repeatedly targeted the Kerch Bridge since Russia’s full-scale invasion began in 2022, including a truck bomb in October 2022 that killed four people and limited the bridge’s capacity for months.

Moscow has moved quickly to respond. The Kremlin has convened a government commission on the consequences of the attack, led by Deputy Prime Minister Marat Khusnullin, and Russian officials said funds, designers and builders had already been gathered for repairs. Open-source analysts estimated that the damaged section could take years to rebuild, and the closure means traffic from Crimea can now leave the peninsula only via a land corridor through occupied southern Ukraine, which is vulnerable to Ukrainian drones.

The exchange of strikes ran in both directions overnight. Russian missiles hit two major bridges over the Dnipro River in Kyiv late on Monday, the city’s mayor said, damaging critical infrastructure and cutting heating and water supplies in parts of the capital. Two people were killed in Kyiv in the overnight assault, officials said.

The timing adds to the significance. The attack came two days before planned trilateral talks between US President Donald Trump, Chinese leader Xi Jinping and Mr Putin in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation leaders’ summit, where Mr Trump has said he expects to discuss the war in Ukraine. CNN noted that the strike makes Yalta, a city in annexed Crimea, effectively inaccessible from mainland Russia, at least until the bridge is repaired.

What happens next: engineers will assess the full extent of the damage and whether the rail section can carry traffic, while the government commission in Moscow weighs the repair. The Kerch Strait shipping channel remains blocked by debris, according to open-source reports, and the 37th meeting of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group takes place in Brussels on Tuesday, where the bridge strikes will be fresh evidence of how the war is being fought from the air.

Why it matters: the Kerch Bridge is more than a crossing — it is the physical link between Russia and the territory it seized in 2014, a supply artery for its forces and the most visible symbol of the annexation. Knocking a section of it into the sea, after years in which Ukraine has repeatedly tried and failed to destroy it, is the most serious blow the bridge has suffered since it opened, and it leaves Crimea dependent on a land route that runs through occupied territory within range of Ukrainian drones. With trilateral talks days away, it is also a message: neither side is waiting for diplomacy to change the facts on the ground.
