South Africa Votes in Local Polls Expected to Further Weaken ANC Grip

world · 4 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Control of Johannesburg, South Africa's commercial hub, will be the largest prize — and results are expected to be announced on Sunday.

South Africans vote in municipal elections on Wednesday, with anger over corruption, crime and failing public services threatening to deal another blow to the African National Congress.

The party of Nelson Mandela dropped below 50% support for the first time in the 2021 municipal elections, and could plunge below 40% this time, according to polls. In the event, the ANC's decline was even sharper than the polls suggested. Announcing the final results on Sunday, the electoral commission chairperson Mosotho Moepya confirmed that the ANC had won 30.76% of the vote, its worst result in history, with the Democratic Alliance second on 24.67%, former President Jacob Zuma's uMkhonto weSizwe (MK) party third on 9.76%, the Economic Freedom Fighters fourth on 6.22% and ActionSA fifth on 5.67%.

With no other party large enough to take its place, many cities in South Africa are heading for unsteady coalition governments that have been partly blamed for failing to deliver basic services including water and electricity. An unprecedented 70 of the country's 257 councils were hung following the previous local elections in 2021, and all indications are that this number will rise in 2026. The ANC achieved outright control in only 53 municipalities, compared with 122 in 2021, while the DA achieved majorities in 12 municipalities, including its stronghold of Cape Town, the Inkatha Freedom Party achieved outright control in five, down from nine, and the newcomer MK party secured three councils and leads in 19 municipalities.

The outcome of the election will set the stage for the next national election in 2029, and could influence the succession race within the ANC as President Cyril Ramaphosa approaches the end of his term as party leader in 2027. The results will offer a key measure of voters' sentiment ahead of 2029, and the scale of the ANC's decline has already set off a debate inside the party about its future direction. "We do not see ourselves as a party that would disappear, this gives us an opportunity to repackage," ANC first deputy secretary-general Nomvula Mokonyane said at the electoral commission's national results centre, where counting of votes was taking place.

"Municipal failures have contributed to declining public trust and confidence in democratic governance," said Ramaphosa, who has acknowledged failures within his party but expressed optimism that it will regain support. "This election is an opportunity to turn this around." The ANC, which has been South Africa's largest party since it led the country out of apartheid in 1994, has tended to do worse in municipal elections than nationally and has acknowledged its failures in local governance. The party, now led by Ramaphosa, has borne the brunt of criticism about years of municipal mismanagement that have left South Africans grappling with failing water supplies, persistent power cuts and crumbling public services.

Control of Johannesburg, South Africa's commercial hub, will be the largest prize and is still up for grabs after an energetic campaign for mayor by Democratic Alliance candidate Helen Zille, who is projected to win the most votes but fall short of a majority. The DA was leading with 34 percent in Johannesburg, the country's financial capital, after running a spirited campaign spearheaded by its former leader, Helen Zille, 75, according to projections by the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research. In Ekurhuleni, the DA was leading with about 41% of counted votes, ahead of ActionSA on 18.1% and the ANC on 16.8%, while the ANC took 38.2% there in 2021. In Tshwane, ActionSA was second with almost 25%.

Other metro areas to watch include Tshwane, home to the administrative capital Pretoria, and eThekwini, which includes the port city of Durban and where former president Jacob Zuma's uMkhonto weSizwe party is strong. The MK party had been tipped as one of the few parties capable of securing an outright majority in Durban, but projections by the Council for Scientific and Industrial Research put its support there at 32 percent. The DA is heavily favoured to retain control of Cape Town, and was forecast to retain control of the tourist magnet with 56 percent of the vote, with its leader, Geordin Hill-Lewis, already declaring victory.

Local governance has been a weak point for the ANC, which was forced into a coalition with the DA and smaller parties after it lost its national majority in 2024. Although the party still enjoys loyalty from some voters due to its role in ending apartheid, growing numbers of South Africans are fed up with problems such as power cuts and water shortages, even in Johannesburg. High unemployment and poor public safety are also major concerns. The ANC's national tally is projected to register as a 10% loss of electoral support, from 40% to 30% since the 2024 general elections, due to the party's last remaining strongholds such as Limpopo and the Eastern Cape also weakening.

About 15.9 million of 29.2 million registered voters cast ballots, the electoral commission said, putting turnout at an estimated 54.5 percent, up from 46 percent in the pandemic-hit 2021 local elections. The higher turnout suggests that ANC voters did not stay away, as many expected, but rather showed up and voted for alternative parties, according to Louw Nel, a senior political analyst at Oxford Economics Africa, who said an outcome of about 32 percent would be the party's worst nightmare coming true. "It's the ANC's worst result in history," the political analyst Daniel Silke told AFP, adding that opposition parties would be smelling blood ahead of the next national election in 2029.

The DA, which governs nationally with the ANC in the Government of National Unity, was the clearest winner in the cities. On the latest counts it led in five of the country's eight metropolitan municipalities, Cape Town, Johannesburg, Tshwane, Ekurhuleni and Nelson Mandela Bay, and briefly led in a sixth, Mangaung, before late results put the ANC narrowly back in front. The DA is projected to finish first in at least five metros, and the party is not expected to win a majority in any of the eight metros, which means coalition negotiations will decide who governs them.

Polls were open from 7 a.m. (0500 GMT) to 9 p.m. (1900 GMT) and results were announced on Sunday, with the electoral commission declaring the elections free and fair. The fragmented political landscape will soon result in coalition talks to decide who runs the cities, and the fate of Johannesburg, Tshwane and other hung councils now rests on weeks of coalition bargaining. For the ANC, the result is a warning that its dominance, which has lasted since 1994, is no longer guaranteed, and that the voters who once supported it out of loyalty are now judging it on whether the taps run, the lights stay on and the streets are safe.
