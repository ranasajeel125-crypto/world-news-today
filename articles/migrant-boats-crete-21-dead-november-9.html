At Least 21 Migrants Dead in Boat Emergencies Off Crete

world · 9 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Rescue crews brought survivors ashore in southern Crete after two boats got into difficulty in rough seas; the cause of the deaths has not yet been established.

At least 21 people have died in two separate emergencies involving boats carrying migrants off the southern coast of Crete, Greek authorities said on Monday, as coast guard and EU border agency crews rescued more than 100 others in gale-force winds.

A Morocco-flagged container ship spotted a migrant boat about 15 nautical miles (28 km) south of the port of Kaloi Limenes early on Monday. A coast guard vessel rescued 18 people and recovered 12 bodies; one of the survivors taken to hospital later died, bringing the toll from that boat to 13.

In a separate operation that began late on Saturday, a boat sent an alarm signal south of the island. The tug Pyrros rescued 28 people, and the coast guard later found seven more bodies on board the drifting vessel, bringing the toll from that boat to eight. Survivors said two passengers had fallen into the sea on Friday and were still missing.

Survivors told the coast guard they had left Libya on 3 or 4 November, each paying 12,000 to 16,000 Libyan dinars for the crossing. Two Sudanese nationals, aged 28 and 16, were arrested on suspicion of facilitating the crossings after other survivors identified them as smugglers.

Two more boats carrying around 40 and 100 people were located by a Frontex drone south of Crete and brought to safety. The coast guard said severe weather, with gale-force northerly winds, made the rescue operations extremely dangerous, and the cause of the deaths had not been established. Autopsies were expected to be carried out.

Crete has become one of the main entry points into the European Union for people departing from North Africa. The UN refugee agency said more than 37,000 migrants had arrived in Greece during 2026 as of 9 November, with more than 22,000 reaching Crete.
