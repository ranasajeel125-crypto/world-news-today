Paraguay's Pedro Alliana Sworn In as President After Senate Removes Santiago Pena

world · 6 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Pena was removed from office a day after offering his resignation over the ueno bank collapse; prosecutors issued an arrest warrant for him shortly after the Senate vote.

Paraguayan lawmakers swore in former Vice President Pedro Alliana to lead the country on Friday, quickly replacing President Santiago Pena after his impeachment for alleged involvement in a bank scandal, Reuters reported.

The Senate voted to remove Pena from office on Friday, with 44 of its 45 members voting in favour, CNN reported. Alliana was sworn in as president soon after the vote. Pena, 47, an economist who was elected in 2023, had offered his resignation on Thursday, a day after the collapse of digital lender ueno left millions of customers unable to access their accounts.

Paraguay's prosecutor's office sent an arrest warrant for Pena shortly after he was impeached, Reuters reported. Local outlet ABC reported that police arrested him, and he was later placed under house arrest while the public prosecutor's office determines the final charges against him.

The Attorney General has opened three criminal cases against Pena and other former officials related to the bank crisis, and the impeachment vote strips Pena of his parliamentary immunity, CNN reported. Pena has denied any wrongdoing.

Ueno, which grew rapidly from a niche fintech player into one of Paraguay's largest banks, had about 3 million customers in a country of about 6.5 million people and administered close to $850 million in public funds, including pension money. The central bank intervened earlier this week after finding the bank had breached minimum solvency requirements, and a judge has ordered six ueno executives jailed pending trial.

Pena has faced accusations of conflict of interest, misuse of pension funds and institutional cover-up over his past business ties to Grupo Vazquez, the conglomerate that controls ueno. He has said he was a shareholder until March 2025, when he sold his stake; ueno says he never had a direct relationship with the bank as a shareholder or investor.

Alliana now serves out the remainder of Pena's five-year term, which runs to 2028. He takes over a country in the grip of a financial and political crisis that has already forced out the central bank chief and left millions of Paraguayans waiting to recover their savings.
