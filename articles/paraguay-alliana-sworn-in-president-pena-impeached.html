Paraguay's Pedro Alliana Sworn In as President After Senate Removes Santiago Pena

world · 6 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Pena was removed from office a day after offering his resignation over the ueno bank collapse; prosecutors issued an arrest warrant for him shortly after the Senate vote.

Paraguayan lawmakers swore in former Vice President Pedro Alliana to lead the country on Friday, quickly replacing President Santiago Pena after his impeachment for alleged involvement in a bank scandal, Reuters reported.

The Senate voted to remove Pena from office on Friday, with 44 of its 45 members voting in favour, CNN reported. Alliana was sworn in as president soon after the vote. Pena, 47, an economist who was elected in 2023, had offered his resignation on Thursday, a day after the collapse of digital lender ueno left millions of customers unable to access their accounts.

Paraguay's prosecutor's office sent an arrest warrant for Pena shortly after he was impeached, Reuters reported. Local outlet ABC reported that police arrested him, and he was later placed under house arrest while the public prosecutor's office determines the final charges against him.

The Attorney General has opened three criminal cases against Pena and other former officials related to the bank crisis, and the impeachment vote strips Pena of his parliamentary immunity, CNN reported. Pena has denied any wrongdoing.

Ueno, which grew rapidly from a niche fintech player into one of Paraguay's largest banks, had about 3 million customers in a country of about 6.5 million people and administered close to $850 million in public funds, including pension money. The central bank intervened earlier this week after finding the bank had breached minimum solvency requirements, and a judge has ordered six ueno executives jailed pending trial.

Pena has faced accusations of conflict of interest, misuse of pension funds and institutional cover-up over his past business ties to Grupo Vazquez, the conglomerate that controls ueno. He has said he was a shareholder until March 2025, when he sold his stake; ueno says he never had a direct relationship with the bank as a shareholder or investor.

Alliana now serves out the remainder of Pena's five-year term, which runs to 2028. He takes over a country in the grip of a financial and political crisis that has already forced out the central bank chief and left millions of Paraguayans waiting to recover their savings.

The fall was remarkably fast. The scandal erupted early on Tuesday, when the central bank announced that Ueno had fallen below the legal standards for solvency, meaning it did not have enough collateral to cover its loans and other ventures. The central bank voted on Monday to put Ueno Bank into resolution after finding that its solvency ratio had fallen below the required minimum, and the following day it seized the lender, freezing the accounts of nearly 2.9 million customers in a country of 6.1 million people and transferring its remaining assets to other banking institutions as a safeguard. Millions of Ueno clients abruptly lost access to their accounts and pension payments as a result.

Ueno had rocketed to prominence in recent years, consolidating in 2023 under the brand Ueno — sometimes stylised with lowercase letters as "ueno". The digital lender was launched in 2021 and rapidly expanded by offering banking services to Paraguayans who had previously been underserved by traditional financial institutions, growing from a niche fintech player into one of Paraguay's largest banks. Some 355 million dollars in state pension funds were invested in the bank, and it administered close to 850 million dollars in public funds, including pension money. Prosecutors have questioned whether Ueno was shielded from accountability due to its government connections, and the bank had made 146 million dollars in questionable loans to companies linked to its owner, while its corrected financial accounts showed its capital position was far weaker than reported.

After days of protests, anxiety and long lines at the bank, Pena announced on Thursday that he would resign, saying he wanted to give Paraguayans peace of mind and restore stability, and citing "the need to preserve our democratic institutions". Congress rejected his resignation and instead proceeded with his impeachment. In Thursday's hearing, the Chamber of Deputies voted 78 to zero to impeach Pena, allowing the Senate to remove him from office the following day. It declined, however, to open impeachment hearings against Alliana. The Senate then voted on Friday to remove Pena from office — 44 of its 45 members voting in favour — and Alliana, 52, was sworn in as president soon after, becoming the South American country's 53rd president.

The impeachment charges alleged that Pena had conflicts of interest, manipulated regulatory oversight, diverted pension funds and participated in an institutional cover-up involving the central bank. Pena, an economist and supporter of US President Donald Trump who came to power in 2023 and is a member of the long-governing Colorado Party, has denied wrongdoing. He held shares in Ueno's parent company until March 2025, and prosecutors allege that while serving as president he influenced government agencies and state-owned entities to deposit large sums of public money into the bank, despite concerns over its financial health.

The legal consequences followed immediately. Pena was arrested after the Senate voted to remove him, and he was later placed under house arrest on suspicion of breach of trust. He has been charged by prosecutors with breach of trust and administration for personal gain, offences that could each carry a prison sentence of up to 10 years, according to prosecutor Nancy Salomón. In total he is facing up to 15 years in prison. The impeachment also strips Pena of his lifetime Senate seat and his parliamentary immunity. On November 7, Pena arrived at the Attorney General's Office in Asuncion, and on November 10 police raided his summer residence in San Bernardino as part of the investigation. A judge has ordered six ueno executives jailed pending trial, and the scandal has also implicated former Central Bank of Paraguay chief Carlos Carvallo, who previously headed Ueno until 2023.

Alliana, who survived an impeachment vote in the lower chamber on Thursday, had previously served as a congressman, leader of the Chamber of Deputies and governor of Ñeembucú. Upon being sworn in on Friday, he vowed to assume the position with "humility, responsibility and a deep commitment to all Paraguayans." He will now be the country's president for the next two years, serving out the remainder of Pena's term until August 2028. He has overhauled several government ministries by appointing new leaders.

The crisis has spilled onto the streets, with demonstrators flocking to downtown Asuncion demanding accountability for Ueno's collapse and faster compensation for depositors whose savings remain frozen. Newly sworn-in President Alliana said on Sunday that the government had refunded 1.2 million Ueno Bank customers whose funds were frozen following the bank's collapse. For the millions of Paraguayans still waiting to recover their savings, wages and pension payments, the political change at the top is only the beginning of a long process of reckoning with one of the most severe political and economic scandals in the country's recent history.
