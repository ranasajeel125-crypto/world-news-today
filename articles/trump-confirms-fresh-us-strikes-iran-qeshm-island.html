Trump Confirms Fresh US Strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, Calling Them 'Minor'

world · 7 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Trump said US forces knocked out a newly built Iranian anti-aircraft system in about 15 minutes; Iranian media reported two explosions on Qeshm Island, and Qatar said mediation to prevent escalation continues.

world · 7 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

President Donald Trump said on Friday that the US military had carried out new limited strikes on Iran, targeting an Iranian air defence system on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, describing the action as "minor".

"They built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device and we knocked it out in about 15 minutes, maybe five," Trump told reporters shortly after arriving in West Palm Beach, Florida, on Friday evening. He offered no further details about the target. He later said the operation was a minor attack and insisted it was not a war, arguing that for months nobody had been hurt or killed and the United States had not lost anybody, in contrast to the war in Ukraine.

The strikes were the second round in a week. On Monday, Iranian media reported several explosions on Qeshm Island, as American media outlets reported that the US military carried out what officials called defensive strikes in the Strait of Hormuz. Explosions were heard on the islands of Qeshm and Hengam and in the nearby mainland area of Bandar Abbas. US officials said those strikes were defensive and followed Iranian fire at an American aircraft, with Iranian forces using surface-to-air missiles against American military assets. Trump had already confirmed a strike earlier in the week, telling Fox News that the United States had struck an anti-aircraft facility on Qeshm Island early on Tuesday. "They built an anti-aircraft facility. It took them about two months. We watched it get built. As soon as it was completed, we hit it last night," he said.

US officials have said most of Iran's air defence systems were destroyed or severely degraded during a June 2025 bombardment by Israel and in the early stages of the current conflict, and have assessed that Iran would try to rebuild them over time. The facility on Qeshm Island was, by Trump's account, one such attempt at rebuilding: constructed over about two months, watched by American forces as it went up, and destroyed as soon as it was completed. Qeshm Island sits in the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway through which a large share of the world's seaborne oil passes, and an air defence system there would have threatened American aircraft operating in the strait.

Iranian state media reported the sound of two explosions in the Suza area of Qeshm Island, and the semi-official Tasnim news agency said early on Saturday that two missiles had struck the island, adding there was no official information on possible damage. No casualties have been confirmed. Iran's state broadcaster reported an army statement saying its air defences had intercepted and destroyed a US MQ-9 drone over Qeshm Island. That claim has not been independently verified, and US Central Command said the situation was "relatively calm" and had no further comment. Iranian state news agency IRNA reported five explosions in rapid succession on Qeshm Island during the earlier round of strikes, and said some residents reported seeing two aircraft flying in its skies.

Friday's strikes are the first since Tuesday's midterm elections in the United States, in which the Republican Party lost control of both chambers of Congress. They follow a separate round of what the US military described as defensive strikes on Monday, when explosions were heard on Qeshm and Hengam islands and near the mainland port of Bandar Abbas. The timing mattered: US media, citing US officials, reported that Central Command had prepared plans for strikes that could begin as early as Wednesday, after the midterm elections, when three American aircraft carriers would be in the region.

Diplomacy continued alongside the strikes. Trump said his administration was talking to Tehran, adding: "We're talking to Iran. We have been. We've made a lot of progress." He also signalled that a deal with Iran could happen, and repeated warnings of further military action, saying he was not looking to kill lots of people but that if the United States had to act, it would. Qatar, the chief mediator, said its efforts to prevent escalation were continuing, and Qatar said it remained hopeful of a US-Iran deal. Iran's President Masoud Pezeshkian warned that no one would benefit from a return to war, and Iran called the US response to its seven conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz unconvincing.

The Strait of Hormuz is at the centre of the standoff. Iran has used the threat of closing the strait, through which oil flows in record numbers of barrels, as leverage in its confrontation with the United States, and the White House said the blockade of Iran would remain in full force and effect, with oil flowing through the strait. US Central Command also announced a reward of up to $20 million, with possible relocation, for information about Iran's nuclear programme. For now, the pattern is one of limited strikes and continuing talks, with both sides insisting they do not want a wider war while preparing for one if the talks fail.
