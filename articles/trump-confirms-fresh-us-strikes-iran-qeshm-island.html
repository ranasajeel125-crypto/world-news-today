Trump Confirms Fresh US Strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, Calling Them 'Minor'

world · 7 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Trump said US forces knocked out a newly built Iranian anti-aircraft system in about 15 minutes; Iranian media reported two explosions on Qeshm Island, and Qatar said mediation to prevent escalation continues.

President Donald Trump said on Friday that the US military had carried out new limited strikes on Iran, targeting an Iranian air defence system on Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz, describing the action as "minor".

"They built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device and we knocked it out in about 15 minutes, maybe five," Trump told reporters shortly after arriving in West Palm Beach, Florida, on Friday evening. He offered no further details about the target.

US officials have said most of Iran's air defence systems were destroyed or severely degraded during a June 2025 bombardment by Israel and in the early stages of the current conflict, and have assessed that Iran would try to rebuild them over time.

Iranian state media reported the sound of two explosions in the Suza area of Qeshm Island, and the semi-official Tasnim news agency said early on Saturday that two missiles had struck the island, adding there was no official information on possible damage. No casualties have been confirmed.

Iran's state broadcaster reported an army statement saying its air defences had intercepted and destroyed a US MQ-9 drone over Qeshm Island. That claim has not been independently verified, and US Central Command said the situation was "relatively calm" and had no further comment.

Friday's strikes are the first since Tuesday's midterm elections in the United States, in which the Republican Party lost control of both chambers of Congress. They follow a separate round of what the US military described as defensive strikes on Monday, when explosions were heard on Qeshm and Hengam islands and near the mainland port of Bandar Abbas.

Diplomacy continued alongside the strikes. Trump said his administration was talking to Tehran, adding: "We're talking to Iran. We have been. We've made a lot of progress." Qatar, the chief mediator, said its efforts to prevent escalation were continuing, and US Central Command announced a reward of up to $20 million, with possible relocation, for information about Iran's nuclear programme.
