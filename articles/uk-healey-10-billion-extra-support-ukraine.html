Britain to Give Extra £10 Billion of Support to Ukraine, Healey Says

world · 28 Oct 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (Europe Desk)

Chancellor John Healey announced the package in Wednesday's Budget, saying it would be based on loans, export finance guarantees and urgent military funding for the winter ahead.

British finance minister John Healey said on Wednesday that Britain would provide £10 billion ($13.29 billion) of additional economic and military support to Ukraine to help in its war with Russia.

Healey told lawmakers the £10 billion would be "based on loans, export finance guarantees and (£500 million) in urgent military funding for this winter."

A Downing Street spokesperson said in a statement that the £10 billion would be made up of £7 billion of economic support over three years and £3 billion in export finance capacity for Ukraine.

The spokesperson added that the £500 million of military funding would "plug urgent gaps in Ukraine's air defences" as winter approaches.

Healey also said that he and Prime Minister Andy Burnham had spoken to Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy before the budget and that Zelenskiy "told us 'you've made my day'".

The announcement comes as Ukraine heads into another winter of war, with air defences under strain from Russian strikes, and as European allies weigh how to sustain support for Kyiv alongside the diplomatic push for talks.

The package was announced in Healey's first Budget as Chancellor of the Exchequer, delivered on Wednesday, October 28, the first Budget since Andy Burnham became prime minister. Healey had previously served as defence secretary under Sir Keir Starmer, and his move to the Treasury put a minister closely associated with Ukraine policy in charge of the government's finances.

According to the Treasury, the package is the largest UK support package for Ukraine ever announced. It is made up of £7 billion of economic support over three years, including up to £5.5 billion of World Bank guarantees and a £500 million grant to the World Bank's IDA crisis facility, which mobilises £1.5 billion. Alongside that, there is a £3 billion rise in UK Export Finance capacity for Ukraine, prioritised for military support, taking total UK Export Finance support for Ukraine to £6.5 billion.

The Treasury also announced £500 million of extra military support for Ukraine's air defences this winter. It was not immediately clear whether that £500 million sits inside the £10 billion total or in addition to it, and the Treasury's own figures left the question open. With the new package, the Treasury put the total UK commitment to Ukraine at £35 billion.

Healey set out the announcement in his Budget speech in the House of Commons, telling MPs: "This morning, the prime minister and I called President Zelensky to tell him about our commitments in the budget today. He told us, 'You've made my day' — £10bn of additional economic and military support, based on loans, export finance guarantees, and half a billion in urgent military funding for this winter. The whole House, the whole country, is united for Ukraine. Today we send a clear message to Moscow: Britain will stand with Ukraine for as long as it takes."

The timing matters because Ukraine is heading into another winter of war. Russia has repeatedly targeted Ukraine's energy infrastructure and cities with missile and drone strikes, and Ukrainian air defences are under strain. The £500 million in urgent military funding is intended to plug gaps in those defences before the coldest months, when attacks on power and heating systems can leave civilians without electricity and warmth.

The economic support is designed to keep the Ukrainian state functioning. Loans and World Bank guarantees help Kyiv pay for public services, keep the economy running and rebuild damaged infrastructure, while export finance guarantees allow British companies to supply Ukraine with goods and services — including military equipment — with the government underwriting the risk. The £3 billion increase in UK Export Finance capacity is prioritised for military support, reflecting the urgency of Ukraine's defence needs.

The announcement came as part of a wider Budget in which defence featured prominently. Healey said he would fund the government's additional Defence Investment Plan, worth £15 billion, and pledged to plug a gap of £4.7 billion by "reprioritising" budgets across other departments. He said he was "getting work under way" to reach spending of 3% of GDP on defence by 2030, and would set out how he would hit that target at next year's spending review. The government has also committed to spending 3.5% of GDP on defence by 2035.

The Budget also set out how the government would meet its defence commitment through new financing arrangements, including the establishment of an innovative financing taskforce, working with international partners on a multilateral defence bank, and additional investment from the National Wealth Fund and the British Business Bank, which were to benefit from a combined increase of £20.4 billion in their funding.

Healey returned to the Ukraine package in Parliament days later. In oral answers in the House of Commons on Tuesday, November 3, he said: "We know that Putin's aggression is imposing real costs both on Ukraine and here at home. That is why we provided an extra £10 billion of new economic and military support for Ukraine in the Budget, alongside new investment to bolster Britain's cyber-defences."

The package underlines how central Ukraine has become to British foreign and defence policy. Britain has been one of Ukraine's largest supporters since Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022, providing military equipment, training for Ukrainian soldiers and economic assistance. The new commitment extends that support for a further three years, signalling to Kyiv — and to Moscow — that British backing is intended to continue for as long as the war lasts. For Ukraine, the practical effect is more money for air defence this winter and a longer-term guarantee of economic support; for Britain, it is a significant new financial commitment made at a time when the Chancellor was also announcing tax rises and spending restraint at home.
