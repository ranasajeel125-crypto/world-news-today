Abu Dhabi Talks End With Prisoner Swap Agreed, No Breakthrough on Ending the War

world · 11 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The prisoner exchange was the one concrete outcome of the first trilateral meeting in nine months; the exact numbers are to be finalised on Thursday, and the envoys will then travel to Dublin to meet President Volodymyr Zelensky.

Russia and Ukraine ended two days of US-mediated talks in Abu Dhabi on Wednesday with agreement on a large-scale prisoner exchange, the one concrete outcome of the first trilateral meeting in nine months — but with no breakthrough on a ceasefire or on territory.

The second day of talks lasted less than two hours, after a four-hour opening session on Tuesday. Russia’s chief negotiator, Vladimir Medinsky, said the sides had agreed a large-scale prisoner swap, describing the agreement as “in principle”, while Ukraine’s President Volodymyr Zelensky called it an “important humanitarian agreement”. The exact number of prisoners and the timing of the swap have not been disclosed, and the final figures are to be settled on Thursday.

US envoy Jared Kushner said he and fellow envoy Steve Witkoff would travel to Moscow on Thursday to meet President Vladimir Putin, and then to Dublin to meet Mr Zelensky, to go over what was discussed and try to make more progress. “Steve and I are going to travel to Moscow tomorrow to meet with President Putin to go over what was discussed today and to try to make more progress on some of the issues that we discussed because we see some openings and some opportunities there,” Mr Kushner said at a news conference in the UAE capital.

“In some areas, we reached a mutual understanding. In others, we did not. This conversation will, without a doubt, continue,” Mr Medinsky said, giving little indication that Moscow’s demands had changed.

Ukraine’s lead negotiator, Rustem Umerov, said there had been progress on certain issues, including security matters, but that “significant differences” remain. Another Ukrainian negotiator, Davyd Arakhamia, said Russia had come to the talks with “very strict directives”, adding: “I’m in this process since 2022 and I can fairly say that this was one of the toughest ones.”

Mr Zelensky said the delegations had discussed views on security guarantees, adding: “The parties discussed views on security guarantees, and the first step must be ending this war and ensuring Ukraine’s security.” He also said broader formats for further work involving European partners had been discussed.

Europe’s role remains a sticking point. European officials were in Abu Dhabi this week for separate talks with Ukrainian and US negotiators, but Russia has so far resisted giving them a seat at the main negotiating table. Russian Foreign Minister Sergey Lavrov criticised the presence of European officials in the UAE city, and Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said no contacts between the Russians and any EU representatives had taken place. US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, speaking in Paris at a Group of Seven meeting on Tuesday, said the US administration’s goal was to expand future talks so that Europeans are “represented at that table directly”. Mr Zelensky said: “We believe that Europe must be represented at all stages of substantive diplomacy.”
