Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Resigns in Wake of Spy-Scandal Affairs; Shares Fall More Than 10%

technology · 12 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Huang, 63, co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and will also leave the board; finance chief Colette Kress takes over as interim chief executive.

Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang stepped down on Thursday, leaving the artificial intelligence industry without its biggest champion and clouding the future of the chipmaker he built into an AI powerhouse, the company said.

Chief financial officer Colette Kress will take over as interim chief executive while Nvidia searches for a permanent successor, the company said. Former Synopsys chief executive Harvey Jones, a board member, was named its first-ever chairman. Mr Huang, who co-founded Nvidia and is its fourth-largest investor with a stake of about 3.61%, will also leave the board.

Nvidia shares fell more than 10% in premarket trading, weighing on the broader market, Reuters reported.

Pressure had been mounting on Mr Huang in recent days after reports said the 63-year-old, who is married, had an affair with a Chinese businesswoman whom US authorities suspect of spying for Beijing. The board met on Wednesday to consider his removal after the New York Times reported that he had also had an affair with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia's vice president of healthcare, who reported to him.

The woman at the centre of the first reports, Alice Huang — no relation — is a former executive at Singapore-based cloud company Megaspeed whom US authorities have concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset. Beijing has denied she is a spy. Washington has also investigated Megaspeed, a coveted official Nvidia cloud partner, over suspicions it helped China obtain advanced AI chips restricted under US export controls, according to media reports.

Earlier this month Nvidia's board launched a probe, led by independent directors with outside legal counsel, into whether Mr Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. It expressed confidence in him at the time. On Sunday Mr Huang told staff he had been unfaithful to his wife of 41 years, Lori Huang, and apologised.

His departure plunges the $4 trillion company into uncertainty and threatens to disrupt an AI ecosystem he helped bankroll, while it could also have far-reaching consequences for US chip export policy, given his push to preserve access to the Chinese market and his close ties with US President Donald Trump. Mr Trump said on Sunday that Mr Huang had "done an amazing job for our country" and had told him he had no idea the Chinese woman he had an affair with was a spy.

Nvidia confirmed it would publish its third-quarter results on Tuesday, 17 November, as scheduled.
