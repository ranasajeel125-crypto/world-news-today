Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang Resigns in Wake of Spy-Scandal Affairs; Shares Fall More Than 10%

technology · 12 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Huang, 63, co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and will also leave the board; the company named finance chief Colette Kress as interim chief executive.

Nvidia chief executive Jensen Huang stepped down on Thursday, leaving the artificial intelligence industry without its biggest champion and clouding the future of the chipmaker he built into an AI powerhouse, the company said.

Chief financial officer Colette Kress will take over as interim chief executive while Nvidia searches for a permanent successor, the company said. Former Synopsys chief executive Harvey Jones, a board member, was named its first-ever chairman. Mr Huang, who co-founded Nvidia in 1993 and led it for 33 years, will also leave the board.

Nvidia shares fell more than 12 per cent in premarket trading before recovering some ground, closing 4.3 per cent lower at $166.19, their lowest close since 30 March 2026, according to Dow Jones Market Data. The stock had closed at $173.73 on Wednesday, and the roughly $4 trillion chipmaker has shed about $600 billion in stock market value this week since announcing a board investigation into Mr Huang’s relationship with the suspected Chinese spy. The fall wiped roughly $182 billion off the company’s value on Thursday alone, and the shares are now down about 30 per cent from their record high on 6 October.

Pressure had been mounting on Mr Huang in recent days after reports said the 63-year-old, who is married, had an affair with a Chinese businesswoman whom US authorities suspect of spying for Beijing. The woman at the centre of the first reports, Alice Huang — no relation — is a former executive at Singapore-based cloud company Megaspeed whom US authorities have concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset. Beijing has denied she is a spy. Washington has also investigated Megaspeed, a coveted official Nvidia cloud partner, over suspicions it helped China obtain advanced AI chips restricted under US export controls, according to media reports.

Earlier this month Nvidia’s board launched a probe, led by independent directors with outside legal counsel, into whether Mr Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence operation. It expressed confidence in him at the time. On Sunday Mr Huang told staff he had been unfaithful to his wife of more than 40 years, Lori Huang, and apologised. “I was unfaithful to my wife. My actions hurt her and our children,” he wrote in a company-wide email, asking people to give his family privacy. Both of the couple’s children work at Nvidia. On Tuesday, the independent directors brought in a former federal prosecutor and the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to run the investigation.

The board met on Wednesday to consider his removal after the New York Times reported that he had also had an affair with Kimberly Powell, Nvidia’s vice president of healthcare, who reported to him. The newspaper cited five people who had worked at the company and said the two were filmed showing affection at a hotel in San Jose in March during Nvidia’s annual developer conference. Mr Huang’s security guard stopped the person who filmed them and offered them a confidentiality agreement. The Wall Street Journal reported on Wednesday that directors were preparing to remove the longtime chief executive, and the company’s shares fell about 8 per cent in after-hours trading. Then, on Thursday afternoon, Reuters reported that Mr Huang had yet another extramarital affair with another unnamed Nvidia employee. Nvidia’s statement announcing the resignation gave no reason for his departure.

Washington came knocking as well. Three Republican House committee chairs asked Mr Huang to attend a closed-door briefing on 18 November about his ties to Alice Huang, and Senator Kevin Cramer urged him to resign, pointing to the risk of extortion. The Securities and Exchange Commission is also investigating Nvidia following the resignation, sources told the New York Post, with the probe focusing on whether the company’s disclosures misled investors, including its denial earlier this year that Mr Huang was having an affair with Alice Huang. An SEC spokesperson declined to confirm the probe, saying the agency does not comment on the existence or nonexistence of a possible investigation.

His departure plunges the $4 trillion company into uncertainty and threatens to disrupt an AI ecosystem he helped bankroll, while it could also have far-reaching consequences for US chip export policy, given his push to preserve access to the Chinese market and his close ties with US President Donald Trump. Mr Trump said on Sunday that Mr Huang had “done an amazing job for our country” and had told him he had no idea the Chinese woman he had an affair with was a spy. Mr Trump had also reportedly tried to persuade him to stay. Mr Huang was a close informal adviser to the president on AI, and his successor will still have to deal with the fallout of his downfall and what it means for future AI policy.

Mr Huang, known for his trademark leather jacket, co-founded Nvidia in a Silicon Valley diner in 1993 and built it from a maker of graphics chips for video games into the most valuable company in the world on the back of the artificial intelligence boom. The company’s organisation is heavily dependent on one person, which poses a key-person risk without him,

Nvidia confirmed it would publish its third-quarter results on Tuesday, 17 November, as scheduled. When it did, the company beat Wall Street expectations with $115.8 billion in revenue, up 103 per cent year on year, and interim chief executive Kress said full-year revenue was on track to exceed expectations. The company also said it would no longer sell its H200 chips in China, ending its participation in a controversial loophole in the US ban on the sale of advanced chips to the country, and chairman Harvey Jones affirmed that the company was not considering reinstating Mr Huang. Mr Huang has signalled he would welcome a return if the board asked him, and has said he was overwhelmed by support from employees, investors and business partners urging him to come back, but the board says a comeback is not on the table despite pressure from prominent investors and tech heavyweights including Elon Musk.
