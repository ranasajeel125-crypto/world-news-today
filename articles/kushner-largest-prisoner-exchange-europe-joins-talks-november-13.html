Kushner Says US Working Toward ‘Largest Prisoner Exchange’ of Ukraine War as Europe Joins Next Talks

world · 13 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Mr Kushner and Mr Witkoff travelled to Moscow to meet Mr Putin and then to Dublin to meet Mr Zelenskiy, after this week’s Abu Dhabi talks produced agreement on a prisoner exchange.

US President Donald Trump’s envoy and son-in-law Jared Kushner said on Thursday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to expand the next round of talks to include European nations, as Washington works toward the war’s “largest prisoner exchange.”

“We are also working toward what would be the largest prisoner exchange of the war, potentially reuniting thousands of people with their families,” Mr Kushner wrote on X, without giving details of the deal.

Expansion of the next round of talks with European partners would allow the tackling of “broader postwar European security arrangements alongside the territorial issues between Ukraine and Russia,” Mr Kushner said. Washington was encouraged by the progress made, he said, but added that significant differences remained.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy welcomed news of European presence at the next round of the US-brokered peace talks, saying he hoped the agenda would include efforts towards ceasefires and defusing the conflict. Speaking after talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Mr Kushner, he said it was important the process had resumed, following this week’s round of talks in Abu Dhabi that was the first in nine months.

Earlier Mr Witkoff said Mr Putin agreed to let European representatives join the next round of talks, though the Russian president also urged Washington to work with European allies to understand if they wanted peace or war in Ukraine. Mr Kushner said a lasting end to the fighting required credible security guarantees and “reciprocal” de-escalation commitments to reduce the risk of future conflict.

Kyiv and Moscow said major differences remained despite some progress, particularly on territorial issues. Russia holds a fifth of Ukraine and Kyiv refuses to give up the rest of its eastern region of Donbas. Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, and Mr Trump, who vowed to end the war when he returned to the White House, is yet to reach a deal on that front.
