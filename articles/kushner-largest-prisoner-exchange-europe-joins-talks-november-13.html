Kushner Says US Working Toward ‘Largest Prisoner Exchange’ of Ukraine War as Europe Joins Next Talks

world · 13 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Mr Kushner and Mr Witkoff travelled to Moscow to meet Mr Putin and then to Dublin to meet Mr Zelenskiy, after this week’s Abu Dhabi talks produced agreement on a prisoner exchange.

world · 13 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

US President Donald Trump's envoy and son-in-law Jared Kushner said on Thursday that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to expand the next round of talks to include European nations, as Washington works toward the war's "largest prisoner exchange."

"We are also working toward what would be the largest prisoner exchange of the war, potentially reuniting thousands of people with their families," Mr Kushner wrote on X, without giving details of the deal.

Expansion of the next round of talks with European partners would allow the tackling of "broader postwar European security arrangements alongside the territorial issues between Ukraine and Russia," Mr Kushner said. Washington was encouraged by the progress made, he said, but added that significant differences remained. A lasting end to the fighting, he said, required credible security guarantees and reciprocal de-escalation commitments to reduce the risk of future conflict.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy welcomed news of European presence at the next round of the US-brokered peace talks, saying he hoped the agenda would include efforts towards ceasefires and defusing the conflict. Speaking after talks with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Mr Kushner, he said it was important the process had resumed, following this week's round of talks in Abu Dhabi that was the first in nine months. He said the trilateral meeting had been unblocked, which gave hope that the war would end, that there would be another meeting after this one, and that each meeting must deliver results and bring the sides closer to peace.

The remarks came after a whirlwind of diplomacy. Mr Kushner and Mr Witkoff travelled to Moscow to meet Mr Putin and then to Dublin to meet Mr Zelenskiy, after this week's Abu Dhabi talks produced agreement on a prisoner exchange. They held lengthy overnight and morning talks in Abu Dhabi before travelling via Latvia to Moscow for detailed discussions with Putin and his negotiating team, which included Yuri Ushakov, Kirill Dmitriev and Vladimir Medinsky. They then flew to Dublin, where they met Zelenskiy on the day of the European Political Community summit, and held a joint press conference with him overnight into Friday. The representatives of five European nations and the European Commission also met the US envoys, after which it was confirmed that a new multilateral meeting would be fixed soon.

Earlier Mr Witkoff said Mr Putin agreed to let European representatives join the next round of talks, though the Russian president also urged Washington to work with European allies to understand if they wanted peace or war in Ukraine. Mr Kushner said Putin was willing to give European participation a try. The Kremlin did not confirm whether Putin had agreed to include Europe in future talks, which Kyiv and its allies have long been seeking. Zelenskiy stressed that Europe would be part of the negotiating team next time, not in some indirect way and not separately, but with a direct voice in the discussions. "They will have a voice. We cannot conclude this deal without Europeans being at the table," Kushner said.

The prisoner exchange is the most concrete result of the week so far. At the joint press conference in Dublin, Kushner said the envoys had been able to finalise the prisoner-of-war exchange, and Zelenskiy called it the largest prisoner-of-war exchange of the war, saying that Ukrainian soldiers would come home. Prisoner exchanges have been one of the few areas where Russia and Ukraine have cooperated throughout the war, with hundreds of prisoners swapped in deals brokered by intermediaries. But an exchange involving thousands of people would be on a different scale, and for the families of prisoners held for years, it would be the most important news of the war.

Kyiv and Moscow said major differences remained despite some progress, particularly on territorial issues. Russia holds a fifth of Ukraine and Kyiv refuses to give up the rest of its eastern region of Donbas. That dispute, over land, is the hardest issue in the negotiations, and neither side has shown any sign of moving. Russia launched a full-scale invasion of Ukraine in February 2022, and Mr Trump, who vowed to end the war when he returned to the White House, is yet to reach a deal on that front.

The talks in Abu Dhabi on November 10 and 11 were the first three-way meeting between Russian, Ukrainian and US negotiators since February, and the first round of talks between the sides in nine months. They opened under the shadow of Russian strikes on Ukraine's power grid, and Russian Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov said Moscow would not agree to an immediate ceasefire without knowing what would happen next. US envoy Jared Kushner said recent contacts had narrowed the gaps, but the gaps remain wide, and the territorial question, the question of security guarantees for Ukraine, and the question of what a ceasefire would look like are all unresolved.

Kushner also said his team was working closely with Zelenskiy's team on a postwar plan for Ukraine's economy and society, praising the country's potential, innovation, ingenuity and resilience demonstrated during the war. That work, on what Ukraine looks like after the fighting stops, is part of the reason European participation matters: any settlement will need European money, European security guarantees and European markets to be real, and the Europeans have insisted that they cannot be asked to pay for a peace they had no part in negotiating. The next round of talks, with Europe at the table, will test whether the progress of this week can be turned into something lasting, or whether the differences over territory and security will once again bring the process to a halt.
