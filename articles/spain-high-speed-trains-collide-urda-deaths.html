Dozens Killed as Two High-Speed Trains Collide in Central Spain

world · 28 Oct 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (Europe Desk)

A Renfe AVE service travelling from Madrid to Seville ran into the rear of an Iryo train near Urda, knocking two carriages off the track and down an embankment; Madrid–Andalusia high-speed services were suspended.

Two high-speed trains collided on Wednesday morning near the town of Urda in Toledo province, central Spain, killing at least 68 people, with officials warning the death toll would almost certainly rise as rescue teams worked through the wreckage. The toll later rose to 69, making it one of Europe's deadliest rail disasters this century and Spain's second deadly high-speed rail disaster in less than a year. About 80 people were injured, 50 of them seriously and 15 critically, and later reports put the number of injured at more than 100, with 58 people receiving medical treatment at hospitals.

Rail infrastructure operator Adif said traffic on the Madrid–Andalusia high-speed line was suspended after the collision, which happened shortly before 9 a.m. local time between Los Yébenes and Urda, around 100 km south of Madrid. A Renfe AVE service travelling from Madrid to Seville ran into the rear of an Iryo train travelling in the same direction, knocking two carriages off the track and down an embankment, according to reports from El País and state broadcaster RTVE. The regional 112 emergency service said the alert came at about 8:54 a.m. The trains collided on a raised section of track between Urda and the neighbouring town of Los Yébenes, near the ancient city of Toledo, in a flat stretch surrounded by olive fields. Reporters on the scene saw one train embedded into another and some carriages lying on their side at the bottom of an embankment strewn with debris.

Both trains were travelling from Madrid's Atocha station to Seville's Santa Justa station. The Iryo train, service 6076, using a Frecciarossa 1000 trainset, departed Atocha at 7:54 a.m. with 147 people on board. Six minutes later, at 8:00 a.m., the AVE train, service 2080, using a Class 103 trainset, departed from the same station, also bound for Seville, with 397 people on board. The moving train was run by Renfe, Spain's state-owned rail company, and it hit the rear of the stalled train operated by Iryo, a private company which entered the country's high-speed railway service market in 2022. The AVE train was travelling at 160 to 200 kilometres per hour when it collided with the rear of the stationary Iryo train at the 118-kilometre marker near Urda.

The confirmed toll rose sharply through the morning. A spokeswoman for the Castilla-La Mancha regional government initially put the confirmed count at 10 and said it was likely to keep rising. Later reports put the toll far higher: The Times reported that emergency services had cited 39 dead, while the El Español website, citing regional civil protection sources, reported at least 46 killed. Those higher figures had not been confirmed by the central government at the time of publication, and the toll remained developing as firefighters, civil protection teams, medical helicopters and ambulances worked at the scene. Emiliano García-Page, president of the Castilla-La Mancha regional government, later told reporters at the scene that 68 people had died, raising an earlier toll of 64, and warned that some of the most seriously injured might not survive. Emergency crews continued to clear the wreckage and search for victims trapped under the rubble, and 52 bodies had been recovered by the time the toll stood at 68. Of the 69 people who ultimately died, 51 were Spanish nationals.

The cause began to emerge within hours. At about 8:30 a.m., a computer failure knocked out LZB, the main signalling system that automatically keeps trains on the line a safe distance apart, Transport Minister Óscar Puente said. All trains on a stretch of around 63 to 69 kilometres stopped. But at 8:36 a.m., controllers cleared the Renfe train to resume running at its normal speed, according to a preliminary investigation by El País that cites train data and recordings of calls with controllers. The Renfe train restarted and travelled 25 kilometres until it collided with the stationary Iryo at 8:53 a.m. Puente said the crash was caused by what he called an "inexplicable" order from the control centre after the safety system failed, and government sources gave the likely cause as human error in the manual operation of signalling after the automated system had failed.

Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez said he was "shocked" by the accident, writing on X that he remained in constant contact with the transport minister and that emergency services were working on the ground to assist those affected. Speaking from Urda, he said the government would declare three days of official mourning from 29 to 31 October. He added: "What is unacceptable for a country like Spain is to suffer two train accidents in the same year," but said the causes of the two crashes appeared unrelated. The regional government activated its emergency plan, and Renfe said all services between Madrid, Ciudad Real and Andalusia were suspended. People waited at Seville's Santa Justa station amid train delays and cancellations after the collision.

The crash is the second high-speed rail disaster on the same corridor in ten months. In January, two high-speed trains collided near Adamuz in Córdoba, killing 46 people in what was then Spain's worst rail disaster in more than a decade. On 18 January 2026, at 7:43 p.m., an Iryo train travelling from Málaga to Madrid derailed on a straight section of the Madrid–Seville high-speed line, crossed onto an adjacent track and crashed into an oncoming Renfe train heading from Madrid to Huelva. The Iryo train was travelling at 110 kilometres per hour and the Renfe train at 200 kilometres per hour. The disaster killed 46 people and injured 292 others, including 15 in critical condition, and was Spain's worst railway disaster since the Santiago de Compostela derailment in 2013, in which 80 people died, and the fourth deadliest railway accident ever recorded in the country. The Urda crash comes just weeks before national elections, with Sánchez, who faced heavy criticism for the first disaster, seeking to overturn unfavourable polls and extend his eight-year premiership in an early general election on 29 November. The cause of Wednesday's collision is now the subject of a formal investigation, and the focus is on why a train was allowed to resume full speed on a stretch of track where the safety system had failed and another train was standing still ahead of it.
