FAA Grounds All US Flights Nationwide After Air Traffic Control Communications Failure

world · 13 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Nearly 7,000 flights were delayed or cancelled, according to flight tracking service FlightAware.

The US Federal Aviation Administration grounded all departing flights nationwide on Friday after a communications failure hit air traffic control centres across the country, disrupting thousands of flights.

The FAA issued the order “due to extraordinary circumstances and for reasons of safety,” according to an advisory from its Air Traffic Control System Command Center. Planes already in the air were allowed to continue to their destinations and land, but aircraft on the ground could not take off. International flights heading to the United States were to be managed on a case-by-case basis.

“The FAA issued a nationwide ground stop for all U.S. airports following a communication issue impacting all Air Route Traffic Control Centers. FAA technicians are investigating the issue. We will provide immediate updates as we have them,” Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy said.

Flight tracking service FlightAware said nearly 7,000 flights were delayed or cancelled because of the nationwide ground stop. About 3,100 aircraft were airborne and bound for US airports when the order took effect, and the FAA manages an average of more than 45,000 flights a day across US airspace, about 28,000 of them commercial.

The FAA advisory was issued at about 3:54 p.m. Eastern time and listed an effective period running until 11:30 p.m. Eastern, but the directive instructed departing aircraft to remain grounded “until further notice” — and the listed end time does not guarantee that flights will resume by then. The FAA has given no estimate for when normal operations will return.

Shortly before the national order, the FAA halted traffic at Chicago O’Hare and Chicago Midway because of air traffic control communications issues. There were reports of radio frequency and radar outages affecting several air traffic control facilities at O’Hare.

The cause of the failure has not been confirmed. The FAA has said only that it is a communications problem affecting its Air Route Traffic Control Centers, the regional facilities that guide aircraft at cruising altitude between airports. Law enforcement officials said preliminary reports indicated the ground stop was not because of a cyberattack. One source told CNN that a communications issue started in the northeast of the country and then spread nationwide, and experts speculated about a rolling frequency outage — a cascading failure in the radio communication systems that pilots and air traffic controllers use to talk to each other.

Friday’s halt was the second nationwide ground stop of all departures since the September 11, 2001, attacks. The last one came on January 11, 2023, when a failure in the FAA’s pilot notification system — the system that sends safety notices to pilots — halted departures for about 90 minutes.

The disruption is the latest sign of strain on the US air traffic control system, which has been beset by equipment failures, communications problems and shortages of controllers, even as it handles tens of thousands of flights every day.

The background: air traffic control in the United States is run through a network of regional Air Route Traffic Control Centers, each responsible for aircraft at cruising altitude in its area, working alongside airport towers and approach control. A failure that takes those centres offline at once leaves controllers unable to issue the instructions that keep aircraft safely separated in the cruise phase — which is why the FAA’s response was to hold departures on the ground rather than risk adding aircraft to a system it could not fully manage.

What happens next: the FAA said technicians are investigating the communications issue and that it will provide updates as it has them. Until the agency says otherwise, departing traffic is to stay on the ground, and airlines and airports will be working through the backlog of delayed and cancelled flights. Passengers have been advised to check the status of their flight with their airline before travelling to the airport.

Why it matters: a nationwide ground stop is one of the rarest measures in American aviation, used only when the system itself cannot be trusted to run safely. Even a halt of a few hours cascades far beyond the flights held on the tarmac — crews and aircraft end up in the wrong place, connections are missed, and the disruption ripples through airports in the United States and overseas. How quickly the FAA can restore communications, and whether it can explain what failed, will shape confidence in a system that millions of travellers depend on every day — and the answers will matter well beyond this one Friday afternoon.
