Russian Drone Strike Kills at Least 14 on Crowded Dnipro Rail Platform

world · 29 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (Europe Desk)

A jet-powered drone struck a platform at Dnipro's main station where a commuter train was preparing to depart for Zaporizhzhia, state railway Ukrzaliznytsia said.

A Russian drone strike killed at least 14 people on a crowded rail platform in the Ukrainian city of Dnipro on Thursday and five in daytime strikes on other cities, officials said, even as a local truce in Kherson raised cautious hopes for a resumption of diplomacy, Reuters reported.

The state rail company Ukrzaliznytsia said a jet-powered drone had struck a platform at Dnipro's main station where a commuter train was preparing to depart for the nearby city of Zaporizhzhia. Regional governor Oleksandr Hanzha said at least five more people were wounded, and Dnipro Mayor Borys Filatov wrote on Telegram: "Many people were killed. Lots were injured."

Images posted by President Volodymyr Zelenskiy showed bodies strewn across the ground as rescuers rushed the wounded away on stretchers. "Russia is deliberately hunting civilians: on trains, at stations, in residential buildings," Ukrainian Prime Minister Sergiy Koretskyi said on Telegram. "There is no justification for this terror, and there never can be."

Two teenagers were killed on a central street in Chernihiv in northern Ukraine, and three people were killed in a strike on an apartment building in the central city of Kremenchuk. In Chernihiv, 23 people were also wounded in a drone attack on a busy downtown street.

Moscow had made clear that a six-day humanitarian truce brokered with US help applied only to south Ukraine's Kherson region, to enable humanitarian relief to reach the Russian-held town of Oleshky, marooned in a war zone since the start of Russia's full-scale invasion in early 2022.

Elsewhere, Russian drone and missile attacks on critical infrastructure including the rail network have been making civilian life increasingly fraught as well as burdening the economy, exploiting an acute shortage of air defence missiles. Like Kyiv, Moscow denies deliberately targeting civilians.
