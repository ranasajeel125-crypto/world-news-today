Russian Missiles Land in Poland as NATO Allies Hold Drills Nearby; Warsaw Calls It a Deliberate Provocation

world · 20 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Polish police officers and firefighters near the site where an object fell near Nowy Lipowiec, Poland, on 20 November 2026, after Russian missiles and a suspected drone came down on Polish territory.

Russia violated Poland's airspace overnight with three objects that were probably two missiles and a drone, the Polish defence minister said on Friday, denouncing what he called a provocation by Moscow. The objects came down in south-eastern Poland during one of the largest Russian attacks on Ukraine of the war, and at the very moment European allies were gathering in Poland for military exercises.

Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz told a press conference: "There has been a violation of Polish airspace and an incursion by objects from the Russian Federation." He identified the objects as a Kh-101 cruise missile, a second missile, and what was most likely a jet-powered drone.

"There was a Kh-101 missile... In the second case, one can also speak of a missile; in the third, it was most likely a jet-powered drone," Kosiniak-Kamysz said, according to Reuters.

Nobody was hurt in the incidents, and no damage to civilian property was reported. But the fact that Russian weapons landed on the soil of a NATO member state, during a major Russian bombardment of neighbouring Ukraine, made it one of the most serious spillover incidents of the war.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk said there was no doubt the objects that landed in Poland were Russian. "It seems we are dealing with an intentional provocation," he said, adding that NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte would visit Warsaw on Saturday.

Tusk's assessment was shared by his defence minister, who pointed to the timing. The incursions were reported as Poland prepared to hold military exercises on Friday involving the "Coalition of the Willing", the group of Ukraine's allies planning a possible postwar security force. Defence ministers from France, the United Kingdom and the Baltic states were present, along with eight chiefs of staff.

"These exercises, combined with the presence of defence ministers from France, the United Kingdom, and the Baltic states - along with eight chiefs of staff, including those from our key allies - suggest that the convergence of these events is no accident," Kosiniak-Kamysz said.

The exercises went ahead as planned, and Poland said it would ask NATO for more support in securing the alliance's eastern flank. The British- and French-led exercise, known as Koalicja 26.2, began on 9 November and runs for two weeks, bringing together forces from 11 countries in south-eastern Poland.

Interior Minister Marcin Kierwinski said investigations were under way at three locations: Nowy Lipowiec, Molodycz, about 50 kilometres to the south, and Rudnik nad Sanem, to the west. The Kh-101 cruise missile went down roughly 50 kilometres from the Ukrainian border, according to the Associated Press, while what officials believe was likely a jet-powered drone hit about 30 kilometres from the border. One missile is believed to have crashed around 35 kilometres from where the military exercises were taking place.

The clearest account of the morning came from the village of Chmielek, near Nowy Lipowiec, where footage shared on social media from what appeared to be a security camera showed an explosion in the distance. Local resident Natalia Szponar described hearing the impact as she got her children ready for school.

"I had just gotten up. I was making coffee, getting the kids ready for school, and then I heard a whistling sound. And then a thud - a dull, muffled kind of impact," Szponar said. "I was a little panicked. I felt a bit scared."

Andrzej Borowiec, mayor of the neighbouring Aleksandrow county, said an object around seven metres long had fallen at 0540 GMT. "Smoke was rising - black smoke... something that looked like one large, distinct mushroom cloud," he said.

Infrastructure Minister Dariusz Klimczak said a traffic controller had heard a loud bang near railway premises in Rudnik nad Sanem, but no visible damage to the railway had been found. Air traffic controllers were evacuated at the airport in Rzeszow, the largest city in south-eastern Poland, and police said debris was found scattered across a large expanse of uncultivated agricultural land in Molodycz.

NATO's secretary general, Mark Rutte, said he had spoken with Tusk "about the grave violations of Polish airspace overnight" and stressed that the alliance stands "in solidarity with Poland and will continue to strengthen our ability to deter and defend." NATO's Supreme Allied Commander for Europe, US Air Force General Alexus Grynkewich, was in close contact with Poland's chief of defence and minister of defence as the investigation continued, according to CBS News.

The Russian embassy in Warsaw did not immediately respond to an emailed request for comment from Reuters.

The reaction from Britain was swift. Prime Minister Andy Burnham, speaking at an EU-UK summit press conference in Brussels, said: "The incursion into Polish airspace is utterly unacceptable. I condemn it unreservedly." Defence minister Wes Streeting, speaking at a joint press conference in Nowa Deba, Poland, described the incident as another example of a Russian provocation aimed at dividing Europe.

"Europe will not be deterred from our support for Ukraine," Streeting said. "We will not allow (Russian President Vladimir) Putin to divide our continent, and we recognise that the frontline in Ukraine is the frontline for freedom and democracy everywhere."

The incursion came during a huge Russian attack on Ukraine. President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said Russia fired more than 400 drones and 110 missiles at Ukraine overnight and early on Friday, including cluster munitions, in what he called one of the largest attacks since the war began. The strikes killed at least eight people in Ukraine, and Russian ballistic missiles hit three bridges over the Dnipro River in Kyiv.

That attack came just a day after Trump, Putin and Xi Jinping held their first-ever trilateral meeting in Shenzhen, China, and issued a peace declaration, and a day after European leaders of the Coalition of the Willing met in Berlin with Zelenskiy to discuss security guarantees for Ukraine. France has called for a meeting of the UN Security Council after the strikes in Kyiv.

The incident in Poland is not the first time the war has crossed NATO's borders. Drones have repeatedly strayed into the airspace of countries on NATO's eastern flank during Russia's war in Ukraine, fuelling tension with the Western military alliance and stoking fears that the conflict could spread, as Reuters noted in its report. Each time, both sides have sought to avoid open conflict between NATO countries and Russia, as the BBC pointed out in its coverage.

What makes this case different is the scale and the timing. Three objects, including a long-range cruise missile launched by Russian strategic bombers, landed inside Poland on the same morning that allied defence ministers and chiefs of staff were gathered for exercises preparing a possible postwar mission in Ukraine. Warsaw reads that as a message, not an accident, and Tusk said the lesson was clear: "Today, it is clearer than ever before that Poland must participate in decisions and discussions regarding the future of Ukraine and security in Europe."

What happens next will turn on the investigation and on Rutte's visit to Warsaw on Saturday. Poland has asked NATO for more support securing the eastern flank, and the alliance's response will be watched closely in Kyiv and in Moscow. For now, the exercises in south-eastern Poland continue, the debris fields are being examined, and Europe's allies are weighing how to answer a provocation that landed, quite literally, on NATO soil.
