Pope Leo Begins 12-Day Latin America Tour Weeks After Lung Cancer Surgery

world · 6 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Leo, 71, had the lower lobe of his right lung removed last month; the Vatican says he has completed his recovery and needs no further treatment. The tour runs from 6 to 17 November.

Pope Leo XIV arrived in Montevideo, Uruguay, on Friday for a 12-day, three-country journey across Latin America — his longest and most far-reaching trip yet, and his first major overseas visit since undergoing lung cancer surgery.

Along the way, Leo will take 12 flights, including two helicopter rides, give 36 speeches and visit 10 cities, including Cusco, Peru, which sits more than 11,000 feet above sea level. The tour takes in Uruguay, Argentina and Peru and runs from 6 to 17 November.

Last month, Leo underwent surgery to remove the lower lobe of his right lung after doctors found a tumour during a routine scan. Vatican officials say he has completed his two-week recovery period and requires no further treatment. His doctors are "very confident", the Vatican's new communications chief, Maria Montserrat Alvarado, said, adding that he will have "plenty of medical care on board should something happen".

Uruguay's left-wing President Yamandu Orsi presided over the welcome ceremony outside Montevideo's main cathedral, after a proposed papal address to parliament was scrapped following public criticism. Crowds of up to 30,000 people, according to the Vatican, lined the streets as Leo drove past in his Popemobile.

Leo wasted little time addressing Uruguay's secular traditions and its liberal laws on abortion and euthanasia. Speaking in Spanish, he emphasised the importance of protecting family life and criticised both laws, saying: "It is everyone's responsibility to ensure that each person can live with dignity, receive the care they need, and be accompanied until the natural end of his or her life."

By Sunday evening, Leo will be in neighbouring Argentina, arriving in Buenos Aires on a visit that fulfils a wish of his predecessor, Pope Francis, who had hoped to return to his homeland but never made the journey. It will be the first papal visit to Argentina since St John Paul II in 1987.

The final stop is Peru, where the visit is being billed as a homecoming. As a missionary priest and later Bishop of Chiclayo, Leo spent two decades in the country, became a naturalised Peruvian in 2015, and has said the country shaped his life more than anything else. He will meet President Keiko Fujimori in Lima and visit several cities where he lived, including Cusco and towns in the Amazon.
