Brazil Chooses Between Lula and Bolsonaro's Son in Knife-Edge Contest

politics · 25 Oct 2026 · 4 min read · By Rafael Costa (Latin America Correspondent)

Voters queue at a polling station in Brazil. The contest between the Lula and Bolsonaro camps has split the country.

Brazil went to the polls in a knife-edge election that pits President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva against the son of former president Jair Bolsonaro — a dynastic face-off that has divided Latin America's largest democracy down the middle.

For Lula's supporters, the vote is about protecting social programmes, the Amazon and Brazil's democratic institutions. For the Bolsonaro camp, it is about economic freedom, law and order, and ending what they portray as a lurch to the left. Polls going into the weekend suggested a contest too close to call.

The campaign has been fought as much on social media as on the stump, with both sides accusing the other of spreading disinformation. Brazil's electoral court has been under intense pressure to police online content while keeping the vote free and fair.

The result will shape far more than Brazil. The winner inherits an economy watched nervously by markets, a Congress that will constrain any radical programme, and a foreign policy that balances Washington, Beijing and the BRICS bloc. Counting is expected to be fast, but with margins this thin, both camps are preparing for a long night.
