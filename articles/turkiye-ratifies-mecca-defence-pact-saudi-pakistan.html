Türkiye's Parliament Ratifies Mecca Defence Pact With Saudi Arabia and Pakistan

world · 6 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Lawmakers approved the pact by 279 votes to 119 on Thursday; ratification alone does not deploy Turkish troops abroad — any deployment would need a separate parliamentary mandate.

Türkiye's parliament has ratified the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, a trilateral pact that treats an armed attack on any one of the three countries as an attack on all three. Lawmakers approved the measure on Thursday by 279 votes to 119, with 398 of the chamber's 592 members taking part in the vote.

The bill approving the pact was adopted article by article, with the first article passed on Wednesday and the remaining articles debated and voted on Thursday afternoon. The law will now go for presidential approval and publication in the Official Gazette, after which the agreement takes effect once the final ratification document is deposited with the pact's secretariat in Saudi Arabia.

The 16-article agreement was signed in Mecca on 7 August 2026 by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif. Its first article commits the signatories to use "all necessary means" to eliminate threats to one another's territorial integrity, while a second article requires immediate assistance if a member faces an armed attack or military aggression. The pact also covers counterterrorism, intelligence sharing, joint exercises and defence-industry cooperation, and runs for an initial 10 years, during which none of the three countries may withdraw.

The ratification comes as Riyadh faces renewed attacks by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis, and builds on the bilateral strategic mutual defence agreement that Saudi Arabia and Pakistan signed in September 2025. Saudi Arabia and Pakistan have already completed their own ratification procedures, Pakistan's Foreign Office announced on 23 October.

Ratification does not by itself mean Turkish troops will be deployed to Saudi Arabia. Any deployment of military personnel would require a separate mandate from parliament, and the precise nature of Ankara's military assistance has yet to be determined. Opposition lawmakers argued during the debate that the pact could draw Türkiye into regional wars, while the government said the agreement was aimed at expanding regional security cooperation and was not directed against any third country.
