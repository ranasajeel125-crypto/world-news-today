Türkiye's Parliament Ratifies Mecca Defence Pact With Saudi Arabia and Pakistan

world · 6 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Lawmakers approved the pact by 279 votes to 119 on Thursday; ratification alone does not deploy Turkish troops abroad — any deployment would need a separate parliamentary mandate.

Türkiye's parliament has ratified the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement with Saudi Arabia and Pakistan, a trilateral pact that treats an armed attack on any one of the three countries as an attack on all three. Lawmakers approved the measure on Thursday by 279 votes to 119, with 398 of the chamber's 592 members taking part in the vote.

The bill approving the pact was adopted article by article, with the first article passed on Wednesday and the remaining articles debated and voted on Thursday afternoon. The law will now go for presidential approval and publication in the Official Gazette, after which the agreement takes effect once the final ratification document is deposited with the pact's secretariat in Saudi Arabia.

The 16-article agreement was signed in Mecca on 7 August 2026 by Turkish President Recep Tayyip Erdoğan, Saudi Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman and Pakistani Prime Minister Shehbaz Sharif. Its first article commits the signatories to use "all necessary means" to eliminate threats to one another's territorial integrity, while a second article requires immediate assistance if a member faces an armed attack or military aggression. The pact also covers counterterrorism, intelligence sharing, joint exercises and defence-industry cooperation, and runs for an initial 10 years, during which none of the three countries may withdraw.

The ratification comes as Riyadh faces renewed attacks by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis, and builds on the bilateral strategic mutual defence agreement that Saudi Arabia and Pakistan signed in September 2025. Saudi Arabia and Pakistan have already completed their own ratification procedures, Pakistan's Foreign Office announced on 23 October.

Ratification does not by itself mean Turkish troops will be deployed to Saudi Arabia. Any deployment of military personnel would require a separate mandate from parliament, and the precise nature of Ankara's military assistance has yet to be determined. Opposition lawmakers argued during the debate that the pact could draw Türkiye into regional wars, while the government said the agreement was aimed at expanding regional security cooperation and was not directed against any third country.

The vote followed a lengthy debate. Türkiye first signed the Mecca Joint Defence Agreement in August and sent it to parliament for ratification last month; on October 20, Türkiye submitted the pact to its parliament. Article 1 of the bill approving the pact was adopted on Wednesday, but afterwards Deputy Parliament Speaker Celal Adan adjourned the session until Thursday afternoon, the Anadolu news agency reported. The agreement ultimately passed with 279 votes in favour and 119 against, with 398 of the chamber's 592 lawmakers taking part in the vote. Some reports described the parliament as a 600-seat body, but the vote count of 398 participants from a 592-member chamber was the figure reported by Anadolu and Reuters.

The pact's text is sweeping. In Article 1, the signatories shall use "all necessary means" to eliminate threats to one another's territorial integrity. Article 2 states: "In the event one of the parties is subjected to an armed attack or military aggression by another country or group of countries, the parties shall immediately provide assistance to the party under attack, utilising the full range of mutual assistance measures available to them." The 16-article agreement also commits the three countries to work together to deter threats and deepen military cooperation, share intelligence and strengthen defence industry ties. The draft approved by Turkish lawmakers commits member countries to the pact for 10 years, after which they may withdraw by giving three months' notice.

The ratification took effect quickly. A law approving Türkiye's ratification of the joint defence agreement was published on Saturday in the country's Official Gazette, and the law took effect upon publication. The agreement itself stipulates that it enters into force when the last instrument of ratification is deposited with its secretariat. Deputy Foreign Minister Levent Gümrükçü told a parliamentary committee on October 27 that Saudi Arabia and Pakistan had completed their domestic approval procedures and formally notified the other parties.

The pact links three significant military powers. It commits NATO's second-largest military — Türkiye — to the group's aid in case of attack, alongside Pakistan, the Islamic world's only nuclear-armed state, and Saudi Arabia, the Gulf's leading power. Turkish Parliament's Foreign Affairs Committee Chairman Fuat Oktay said the alliance is entirely about defence, and not for attacking other countries, TRT World reported. The government said the agreement was aimed at expanding regional security cooperation and was not directed against any third country.

The immediate context is the conflict in Yemen. The ratification comes as Riyadh continues to face attacks by Yemen's Iran-backed Houthis, and the pact clears the way for Ankara to send military support to Riyadh amid those attacks. It builds on the bilateral strategic mutual defence agreement that Saudi Arabia and Pakistan signed in September 2025, extending that two-way commitment into a trilateral one.

Opposition parties objected during the debate, arguing that the agreement could embroil Türkiye in regional wars and put Turkish soldiers in harm's way. The government rejected that argument, but the question of deployment remains open: ratification alone does not deploy Turkish troops abroad, and any deployment of military personnel would require a separate mandate from parliament. The precise nature of Ankara's military assistance has yet to be determined, and the form it takes — whether advisers, air defence, intelligence or equipment — will be decided in the months ahead.
