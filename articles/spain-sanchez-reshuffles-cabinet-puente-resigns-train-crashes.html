Spain's Sanchez Reshuffles Cabinet as Transport Minister Puente Quits Over Deadly Train Crashes

world · 4 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Puente's resignation, six days after the Urda crash that killed 69 people, leaves the ruling Socialists without one of their most high-profile ministers weeks before the November 29 election.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said on Wednesday his deputy and Economy Minister Carlos Cuerpo would provisionally assume the Transport Ministry's portfolio following Oscar Puente's resignation late on Tuesday after two deadly high-speed train crashes this year.

Puente surprised lawmakers by quietly announcing he was stepping down on Tuesday night following a tense four-hour grilling by lawmakers from a caretaker parliament body, in which most parties demanded he face responsibility for the two rail disasters that have killed 115 people combined.

The 57-year-old minister revealed he had in fact already made his decision in the immediate aftermath of the train crash in central Spain's Urda, only telling his family while keeping even Sanchez in the dark. Puente had previously vowed to stay on to tackle the investigation into last week's crash and fix the system's flaws, despite widespread calls for his exit from both the opposition and coalition partners.

Puente's resignation will only become effective once it is published in the official state gazette, but the exact timeline for this remains unclear. He also said he would not stand as a candidate in the upcoming election on November 29.

The surprise announcement comes after polls run following last Wednesday's deadly crash showed a slight drop in support for the ruling Socialists, who had hoped to fight the election campaign on their ability to uphold social services and a housing crisis.

Sanchez praised Puente after the announcement, saying he had "stood up, offered solutions and then stepped aside". A lawyer and former mayor, Puente took on the post of transport minister in November 2023, overseeing the expansion of Spain's rail network and rollout of investments supported in part by EU recovery funds.
