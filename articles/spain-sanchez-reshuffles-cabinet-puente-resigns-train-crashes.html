Spain's Sanchez Reshuffles Cabinet as Transport Minister Puente Quits Over Deadly Train Crashes

world · 4 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Puente's resignation, six days after the Urda crash that killed 69 people, leaves the ruling Socialists without one of their most high-profile ministers weeks before the November 29 election.

Spanish Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez said on Wednesday his deputy and Economy Minister Carlos Cuerpo would provisionally assume the Transport Ministry's portfolio following Oscar Puente's resignation late on Tuesday after two deadly high-speed train crashes this year.

Puente surprised lawmakers by quietly announcing he was stepping down on Tuesday night following a tense four-hour grilling by lawmakers from a caretaker parliament body, in which most parties demanded he face responsibility for the two rail disasters that have killed 115 people combined. "I wish to inform you that I am not only stepping down from my position as Minister of Transport, but that I am also withdrawing my name from the lists for the early legislative elections on November 29," he told the committee at the end of the hearing.

The 57-year-old minister revealed he had in fact already made his decision in the immediate aftermath of the train crash in central Spain's Urda, only telling his family while keeping even Sanchez in the dark. "I made my decision about my future in the first minutes after the tragedy, as soon as I learned of it," he said. He had ruled out resigning on several occasions since the accident, and had earlier pledged to work tirelessly to restore confidence in the world's second-largest high-speed rail network, but stayed on only while the government coordinated its initial response, preliminary investigations began and the first measures to compensate victims and improve rail safety were put in place.

The crash that ended his tenure happened on Wednesday 28 October, when a train operated by the state-owned Renfe ploughed into the back of a stationary train belonging to the private Spanish company Iryo outside the village of Urda, around 130 kilometres south of Madrid, on the Madrid-Seville high-speed line. At around 8:30 a.m., a central computer fault knocked out the LZB signalling system, which keeps trains safely spaced, across roughly 63 kilometres, and trains on the section stopped automatically. Control passed to a backup system, and central controllers in Madrid, who had assumed control after the failure, authorised the Renfe train to continue its journey, resulting in a collision with the Iryo train. Early findings by the independent investigator AITAT put the impact speed at 160 to 200 kilometres per hour; two carriages derailed and slid down an embankment.

The Urda crash killed 69 people, 40 men and 29 women, all adults, and injured more than 200, one of the worst railway disasters in Europe this century. Among the dead were the driver of the Renfe train and former employment minister Fatima Banez. The death toll rose through the day from 10 to 64 and 68 before settling at 69, confirmed on 3 November, and all 69 victims have since been identified. Puente also announced an aid package of 30 million euros for the families of the dead and the scores of people who were injured. He called the control-centre decision inexplicable.

"A transport minister in a country like Spain cannot continue in office with two accidents of this severity in less than a year," Puente said, referring also to a high-speed train derailment in January in Andalusia that killed 46 people. On 18 January, a train derailed near Adamuz in Cordoba province and struck an oncoming train, the first of the two disasters on the same Madrid-Andalusia high-speed corridor in a single year. The Urda crash became Spain's deadliest rail accident since the derailment at the Angrois curve near Santiago de Compostela in July 2013, which killed close to 80 people.

The fallout spread quickly beyond the ministry. Renfe introduced a new safety directive for high-speed operations, requiring trains to halt and proceed at a maximum speed of 40 km/h under visual supervision after cab signalling failures, and on 7 November AITAT issued interim safety recommendations along the same lines. The European Union Agency for Railways is providing technical assistance to Spanish investigators, while the European Public Prosecutor's Office has opened investigations into the possible misuse of EU funds earmarked for renovation of the line, conducted with Spain's UCO police unit. On 19 November, a court in Orgaz allowed Adif, the state rail infrastructure manager, to begin rebuilding the damaged section of track under two conditions, including a complete record of every element repaired or replaced. The government also carried out a clear-out of railway leadership, appointing new presidents for Renfe and Adif.

Puente's resignation will only become effective once it is published in the official state gazette, but the exact timeline for this remains unclear. He also said he would not stand as a candidate in the upcoming election on November 29. The surprise announcement comes after polls run following last Wednesday's deadly crash showed a slight drop in support for the ruling Socialists, who had hoped to fight the election campaign on their ability to uphold social services and a housing crisis. Sanchez called the early parliamentary election for 29 November, and polls suggest his ruling Socialist party could lose.

Sanchez praised Puente after the announcement, saying he had "stood up, offered solutions and then stepped aside". A lawyer and former mayor, Puente took on the post of transport minister in November 2023, overseeing the expansion of Spain's rail network and rollout of investments supported in part by EU recovery funds. His interim successor, Cuerpo, has promised major reforms within three weeks, said lower train speeds remain an option, and has proposed a support office for victims, while insisting there are no plans to close the Madrid-Barcelona high-speed line. Rail unions, which called the ministry's proposals insufficient, planned strikes for 10 and 12 November, and the victims' association from Adamuz is pressing for a cross-party state pact on rail safety.
