Rescuers Search for 36 Missing After Fire at Candy Factory in Indonesia Kills Three

world · 22 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Rescuers in Indonesia were searching on Sunday for 36 workers after a fire at a candy factory in western Java killed three people and injured four.

Rescuers in Indonesia were searching on Sunday for 36 workers after a fire at a factory in western Java killed three people and injured four, Reuters reported. Firefighters were still tackling smouldering hotspots a day after the blaze broke out, and search teams were combing the damaged building for people believed to be trapped inside.

Fifteen people survived the fire, four of whom were taken to medical facilities, Reuters reported. The fire broke out on Saturday evening at a candy factory in Cikande, in Serang regency in Banten province, and the authorities have not announced the cause.

Search teams were combing sections of the damaged building where victims were believed to be trapped, while firefighters carried out cooling operations as flames and hotspots persisted in parts of the factory. The work was still a rescue operation on Sunday, not a recovery one — with 36 people unaccounted for, the teams were searching for the living.

The numbers explain why the operation is so large. Three people are confirmed dead and four injured, but 36 are missing — more than twice the number known to have survived. Until the search teams clear the building, the final toll cannot be known, and it could rise sharply if the missing were inside when the fire took hold.

What is known so far is limited to the bare outline: a Saturday-evening fire at a candy factory in Cikande, in the industrial belt of Serang regency in Banten province, western Java. The authorities have said nothing about what started the blaze, how many people were inside when it began, or the condition of the four injured workers taken to medical facilities.

The fact that flames and hotspots were still burning a day after the fire broke out shows how difficult the search is. Firefighters cannot simply walk into a building where smouldering sections remain, and cooling operations have to come first in the areas where the fire is still active. That slows the search of exactly the sections where victims are believed to be trapped.

For the families of the 36 missing workers, Sunday has been a day of waiting for news from the search teams. The confirmed dead number three; the injured number four; the survivors number fifteen. The gap between those figures and the 36 unaccounted for is the measure of the uncertainty hanging over Cikande.

The factory is a candy plant, and the fire broke out on a Saturday evening — a time when the number of people on site would depend on the shift pattern, which the authorities have not described. That detail matters, because it determines how many workers could plausibly have been inside, and whether the 36 missing were all in the building or include people who simply cannot be contacted.

Attention will now turn to the search teams' progress and to the question of what caused the fire. No cause has been announced, and officials have not said when the search might be completed or when the damaged building will be safe enough for a full inspection. Until the hotspots are out and the structure is searched, both the toll and the trigger remain unknown.

Why it matters: thirty-six people missing after a factory fire is a major industrial disaster in the making. Three are confirmed dead, four are injured, fifteen survived — and the fate of more than twice that number is still unknown, a day after the blaze. The search in Cikande is now a race against the smouldering building itself, and the final toll will not be known until the teams can reach every section where victims are believed to be trapped.
