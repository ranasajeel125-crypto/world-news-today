Airstrikes Blamed on Saudi Arabia Kill at Least 27 Near Yemen's Capital, Houthis Say

world · 7 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

At least 27 people were killed and 36 wounded in what the Houthi-run Saba news agency said were Saudi airstrikes on a village near Yemen's capital Sanaa on Saturday, in one of the deadliest reported attacks since fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Iran-aligned group resumed in July, Reuters reported.

The Houthi-run health ministry said the strikes hit homes in Bani Hushaysh, a Houthi stronghold east of Sanaa, and that most of those killed were women and children. A spokesman for the ministry, Anees al-Asbahi, told the Associated Press he expected the toll to rise as search and rescue work continued.

Reuters said it could not independently verify the account. Saudi Arabia's Center for International Communication did not immediately comment, saying it would look into Reuters' inquiries. The ministry blames Saudi Arabia for the strikes; Riyadh has not confirmed carrying them out.

Abdul-Aziz al-Dailami, director of Sehatuna Hospital in Sanaa, said many victims remained trapped under the rubble, including first responders. “The strikes keep coming. Just as rescuers arrive, another strike hits. We have even lost some of our own medical responders, who have been martyred,” he said, adding that many of the wounded arrived in very critical condition with head, chest and leg injuries and fractures.

Later on Saturday, Houthi military spokesman Brig. Gen. Yahya Saree said the group had carried out missile and drone operations against targets in Saudi Arabia in response. Houthi officials also said at least 30 people were killed on Friday in a strike on a market in Taiz province.

The renewed fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis has shattered a 2022 UN-brokered truce that largely halted attacks between the Saudi-led coalition and the group, which controls Sanaa. The Houthis have since launched missile and drone attacks on Saudi airports and oil facilities, while Riyadh has carried out airstrikes in Yemen in support of its Yemeni allies.

The fighting, a spillover from the wider US-Iran conflict, has revived a complex 12-year war in Yemen that has caused one of the worst humanitarian crises globally and fragmented the Arab world's most impoverished country. The number of people displaced since the latest round of fighting began has climbed to more than 200,000, the International Organization for Migration said in October.
