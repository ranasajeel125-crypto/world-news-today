Poland's Top Court in Crisis as President Cuts Short Trip and Returns to Warsaw

world · 31 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Police detained three people after some protesters breached the tribunal's fence and others threw objects at officers.

Police secured Poland's Constitutional Tribunal on Friday as protesters gathered to demonstrate against what they perceive as the wrongful suspension of the court's president, Bogdan Święczkowski, by the current pro-European government. Police said they detained three people after some protesters breached the tribunal's fence and others threw objects at officers.

The information about Święczkowski's suspension was published on the court's website at around 6 p.m. local time on Friday. The website went down soon after, and The Associated Press could not independently verify the official reasons given for the suspension. Reports said a judge, Anna Korwin-Piotrowska, announced one-month suspensions for Święczkowski and his deputy, Bartłomiej Sochański, and claimed temporary charge of the court — a move the tribunal's existing leadership rejected, insisting Święczkowski remains president and describing the events on its X account as an attempted coup. That “coup” characterisation is a political claim by the tribunal's leadership, not an established legal finding.

President Karol Nawrocki, who was flying to Senegal for a state visit, turned his flight back to Warsaw. “At a moment like this, the President must be in Poland. I am cutting short my flight to Senegal. I am returning,” he wrote on X. Polish media reported that after landing late on Friday he headed for urgent talks.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk defended the move. “Restoring the rule of law is our obligation,” he wrote on X on Friday evening, adding: “After years of lawlessness under PiS this is a difficult process, sometimes painful and it requires decisive action. There is no other way.” Justice Minister Waldemar Żurek said he had sought the lifting of immunity for Święczkowski and his deputy so criminal proceedings could be brought.

Politicians from the national-conservative Law and Justice party (PiS), currently the largest opposition force, condemned the suspension. “This is a blatant, criminal attack on the constitutional order,” former Justice Minister Zbigniew Ziobro wrote on X on Friday evening, calling for resistance that would be “loud, firm, solidary and fearless.”

Poland's top judicial bodies, including the Constitutional Tribunal, have been a major bone of contention between the current pro-European government and PiS. Critics in Poland and the European Union accuse PiS, which led the country between 2015 and 2023, of imposing political control over key judicial institutions, including the tribunal, and the PiS reforms led the EU to impose fines on Poland. Tusk's government came to power in 2023 with a promise to restore the independence of the judiciary, but its efforts have since been blocked by veto by two successive PiS-aligned presidents, including Nawrocki. Święczkowski became head of the tribunal under the PiS government, and Tusk has accused him of blocking judges elected by the parliamentary majority from taking up their posts.

The standoff is expected to continue, with legal challenges to the suspensions and further protests in Warsaw anticipated in the coming days.
