Supreme Court Rejects Trump Bid to Set Aside California Ballots

politics · 3 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The one-sentence order gave no explanation and recorded no noted dissents, leaving California's existing ballot procedures in place for Election Day.

The Supreme Court on Monday quickly rejected a bid by President Donald Trump's administration to temporarily block California from counting mail-in ballots from more than 20,000 registered voters who federal officials allege are likely non-US citizens, Reuters reported, acting on the eve of the 3 November midterm elections.

The justices denied the Justice Department's emergency request just hours after it was filed, in a brief, one-sentence order that offered no explanation of the court's action, as is common when it handles emergency matters. No justice publicly dissented, and the court did not ask California for a response to the administration's request, a step that usually signals serious consideration of an emergency case.

The administration had demanded that California separate any ballots cast by people on a list of 20,000 registered voters amassed by the US Department of Homeland Security from other ballots, and not count them until those voters are determined to be US citizens. Lower courts had already refused the same request: US District Judge David Carter denied it on 28 October, and the San Francisco-based 9th US Circuit Court of Appeals refused it on Friday in a 2-1 decision.

California Governor Gavin Newsom, a Democrat, called the decision a welcome sight, adding that Californians can feel secure in knowing the state will carry out a free and fair election. The decision represented another defeat for Trump as his fellow Republicans fight to retain control of Congress in Tuesday's midterms, Reuters reported.

The emergency ruling does not end the underlying lawsuit, which continues in the lower courts. The Justice Department sued California in federal court on 15 October, claiming the state is unlawfully preventing enforcement of a 1996 US law that makes it a criminal offense for noncitizens to vote in federal elections, while California has said its safeguards against noncitizen voting are robust.
