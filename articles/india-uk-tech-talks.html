India and UK Explore Deeper Ties in Space, Nuclear Energy and AI

technology · 24 Oct 2026 · 5 min read · By Tom Becker (Technology Editor)

India and the United Kingdom have held bilateral talks to strengthen cooperation in space, civil nuclear energy and emerging technologies, India's Ministry of Science and Technology announced. The discussions, held on 12 October 2026 in New Delhi, took place alongside the eighth meeting of the India-UK Science and Innovation Council, and brought together India's Minister of State for Science and Technology, Dr Jitendra Singh, and the UK Secretary of State for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade, Jonathan Reynolds.

The two ministers reviewed research partnerships and industry engagement, and discussed how scientific discoveries in both countries could be turned into commercially viable technologies. The Indian delegation included Professor Umesh Waghmare, Secretary of the Department of Science and Technology, and Dr Praveenkumar Somasundaram, the department's adviser and head of international bilateral cooperation. The British side included Reynolds, the British High Commissioner to India, Lindy Cameron, and Amanda Brooks, interim permanent secretary at the UK Department for Business, Innovation, Science and Trade.

Space was one of the main items on the agenda. Dr Singh said India's space-sector reforms had created opportunities for private companies, startups and technology developers, while allowing established research institutions to concentrate on advanced research. The two sides discussed collaboration in space science, satellite development and research, along with stronger engagement between scientific institutions and technology enterprises. With around 800 space-technology startups now operating in India, Dr Singh proposed greater connectivity between Indian and British startups and smaller companies, to support innovation, strengthen supply chains and expand commercial opportunities.

India's private space sector has grown rapidly since the government opened the industry to private participation in 2020 and created IN-SPACe, the Indian National Space Promotion and Authorisation Centre, to regulate and promote private activity. Where only a handful of private space companies existed a few years ago, hundreds now work on launch vehicles, satellites, ground stations and data services. The UK, for its part, has a long-established satellite manufacturing and services industry, and British companies have looked to India both as a market and as a partner for low-cost launch and manufacturing capacity.

Civil nuclear energy was the second major strand of the talks. Dr Singh outlined India's nuclear-sector reforms, including the Sustainable Harnessing and Advancement of Nuclear Energy for Transforming India, or SHANTI, Act of 2025. Drawing on the experience of opening up the space sector, he said India was creating avenues for wider participation in nuclear energy and technology development. The two sides explored cooperation in small modular reactors, advanced nuclear technologies and associated industrial capabilities, particularly against the backdrop of rising energy needs, industrial development, electrification and data-centre demand.

Small modular reactors, known as SMRs, are compact nuclear reactors designed to be built in factories and assembled on site, rather than constructed as single large plants. Supporters argue that they can be deployed more quickly and at lower upfront cost than conventional reactors, and that they suit locations where a full-scale plant would be impractical. They are also seen as a way to supply reliable low-carbon electricity to energy-hungry facilities such as data centres, which are expanding rapidly in both India and the UK. No SMR design has yet been deployed commercially at scale in either country, so cooperation at this stage is about research, regulation and industrial supply chains rather than immediate construction.

Dr Singh also highlighted applications of nuclear technology beyond electricity generation, including radioisotopes, nuclear medicine and cancer-related healthcare. These offered opportunities for collaboration with British expertise in life sciences and medical research. Nuclear medicine uses small amounts of radioactive material to diagnose and treat disease, and radioisotopes produced in reactors and accelerators are used in imaging scans and in targeted cancer therapies.

Artificial intelligence, quantum technologies, cyber-physical systems and cybersecurity also featured prominently in the talks. Dr Singh said India was exploring smaller-scale AI models suited to local needs and available resources, and the discussions addressed practical applications, the risks that come with new technologies and the importance of cybersecurity as emerging technologies become integrated into critical systems. Reynolds identified AI as a priority for the UK's forthcoming presidency of the G20 and welcomed engagement with India on technological innovation and productivity.

The talks also produced a concrete scientific milestone. The two ministers jointly inaugurated the BRIC-CDFD-ONT India Genomics Centre of Excellence, a new centre for genomics research that brings together Indian biotechnology research institutes and Oxford Nanopore Technologies, the British DNA sequencing company. Genomics centres use sequencing technology to read genetic code, supporting research into disease, agriculture and biodiversity.

The Science and Innovation Council reviewed cooperation under the India-UK Research and Innovation Memorandum of Understanding, the Technology Security Initiative and India-UK Vision 2035, the long-term framework for the bilateral relationship. The Department of Science and Technology's collaboration with Innovate UK, the British innovation agency, supports joint industrial research and development aimed at creating intellectual property, technologies, processes and prototypes. Eight projects have been agreed for support in advanced manufacturing, power electronics and net-zero technologies, and are at different stages of implementation.

The meeting fits into a wider deepening of the India-UK relationship. The two countries agreed a comprehensive trade deal in 2025 and adopted Vision 2035 as a ten-year plan for cooperation across trade, technology, defence, climate and education. The technology talks in New Delhi showed that effort moving from broad declarations into specific sectors: satellites and startups, reactors and radioisotopes, artificial intelligence and quantum computing. Officials said the start dates for the new initiatives would be announced separately, but the breadth of the agenda, spanning satellites to small reactors, makes this one of the most substantive technology engagements between the two countries since the 2030 Roadmap that guided the partnership earlier in the decade.
