Cyclone Murasu Makes Landfall on India's Eastern Coast

world · 3 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The storm's centre was expected to cross the coast near Ongole by about 4 a.m. local time, with winds of 100–110 km/h gusting to 120 km/h.

Severe Cyclonic Storm Murasu began making landfall along India's eastern coast early on Tuesday, the country's weather department said, the second storm to hit the region in a week as authorities evacuated thousands of people to safety.

The India Meteorological Department said the severe cyclonic storm started making landfall at 1:00 a.m. local time over the coast of the south-eastern state of Andhra Pradesh, with the inner spiral band in the northern sector entering south coastal Andhra Pradesh. The landfall process takes a few hours, and the storm's centre crossed the coast between Nellore and Narsapur, close to Ongole, between 3:30 a.m. and 5:30 a.m., with winds of 100–110 km/h gusting to 120 km/h. At 2:30 p.m. on the same day the system had moved north-northwestwards and weakened into a cyclonic storm, with winds of 80–90 km/h gusting to 100 km/h, and by the following day it had weakened further into a depression over east Telangana and adjoining coastal Andhra Pradesh.

The weather department had warned of a storm surge of about 1.5 to 2 metres above the astronomical tide, very likely to inundate low-lying areas of south coastal Andhra Pradesh during landfall, with the maximum peak surge expected over Bapatla and over Krishna and Prakasam districts. Ongole city recorded light rain of 18 mm as of 1 a.m., while Kothapatnam in Prakasam district recorded 74.7 mm, and rainfall picked up in the nearby coastal district of Krishna overnight. Residents of Ongole Mandal reported inland sea ingression as the surge pushed water ashore.

Authorities began evacuating thousands of people on Monday and planned to move some 30,000 to safety in Andhra Pradesh, which is in the path of the cyclone, Reuters reported. By Tuesday evening, 12,230 people were in rehabilitation centres in Guntur, Bapatla and Palnadu districts. The state government activated a dedicated WhatsApp-based grievance facility through its Mana Mitra platform, allowing residents in cyclone-hit regions to report emergencies and civic disruptions such as power outages, uprooted trees, collapsed walls and waterlogging by messaging a dedicated number.

The damage was extensive. At least two people were killed on Tuesday after the storm hit south coastal Andhra Pradesh, destroying standing crops spread over 52,459 hectares and damaging 312 houses and power transmission lines. The storm uprooted thousands of trees, damaged hundreds of electric poles and wrecked fishing boats, and brought heavy rain to the districts of Prakasam and Palnadu, leaving a trail of destruction in Guntur, Bapatla and Palnadu districts. Preliminary reports estimated losses of 344 crore rupees.

Cyclone Murasu formed over the Bay of Bengal, a major region for tropical cyclones that has spawned deadly storms on India's east coast. IMD forecasts said the states of Andhra Pradesh and Tamil Nadu to its south would be worst hit, and the system was tracked continuously through Doppler weather radars at Machilipatnam and Visakhapatnam as it moved northwards after landfall, with heavy to very heavy rainfall, squally winds and localised flooding remaining a concern even as it weakened.

At least six people were killed in Cyclone Arnab, which was classified by IMD as a very severe cyclonic storm and made landfall in Andhra Pradesh on 28 October, Reuters reported, making Murasu the second cyclone to strike the state's coast within a single week. Arnab, the third-highest category on the IMD scale, crossed the coast between 2 a.m. and 8 a.m., about 20 km south of Yanam, with wind speeds at landfall reaching 120 km/h and gusts of up to 150 km/h, and in some reports 125–135 km/h. The official death toll was six, an Andhra Pradesh state government official said, although the Times of India newspaper, citing local officials, put the figure at nine. The deaths were caused by electrocution as well as tree and house collapses, including a tree falling near a church in Bhimavaram, an electric pole snapping in Bondadavari Street, and a wall collapse in Tenali mandal that killed an 11-year-old boy.

Arnab flooded large areas, uprooted trees and electric poles and disrupted transport across several districts, with Konaseema and Kakinada the worst affected, and power supply in several areas along India's eastern coast remained disrupted a day after the storm. In neighbouring Odisha, officials raced to airlift people from about 300 villages marooned by flooding in Ganjam district. Andhra Pradesh Chief Minister N Chandrababu Naidu wrote to federal home minister Amit Shah to seek an assessment of the damage to crops and infrastructure.

Two severe storms in a single week put India's east-coast disaster machinery under unusual strain, testing evacuation plans, shelter capacity and power restoration in districts that had barely begun clearing the debris of the first storm when the second arrived. The Bay of Bengal's cyclone season typically peaks around October and November, when warm sea-surface temperatures and favourable atmospheric conditions allow low-pressure systems to intensify rapidly, and the back-to-back landfalls underlined why early warning, pre-positioned relief and rapid evacuation have become the centrepiece of India's cyclone response.
