ANC Takes a Beating in South Africa Municipal Vote, Setting Up Tough Coalition Talks

world · 8 Nov 2026 · 7 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The ANC's share fell from 45.59% in 2021; in Johannesburg the Democratic Alliance led with about 35% to the ANC's 20%.

South Africa's African National Congress (ANC) was dealt a stinging blow in last week's municipal elections, results showed on Sunday, weakening its hand in the coalition talks that will follow to form city governments.

The ANC ended up with 30.76% of the vote against 24.64% for its main rival, the Democratic Alliance (DA). Former President Jacob Zuma's party uMkhonto weSizwe had 9.76% of votes, putting it in third place, while the rest was split between hundreds of smaller parties. It was the ANC's worst electoral performance since the end of apartheid. The Electoral Commission of South Africa (IEC) confirmed the final results on Sunday evening, following three days of counting after the 4 November vote, and declared the elections free and fair. Turnout was 52%, up from 45.9% in 2021.

The outcome marks a further painful setback for the ANC, which has seen its vote share fall steadily in local elections over the last 15 years, largely due to failures in basic services such as water and electricity. The former liberation movement got 45.59% of votes in the last municipal elections in 2021, and about 54% in 2016 — the first time it had failed to clear half the vote in a countrywide election. It then won 40.2% in the 2024 national poll, losing its parliamentary majority for the first time. The latest result is a fall of nearly 15 percentage points on 2021. In seats, the ANC took 3,246 council seats, the DA 1,695, uMkhonto weSizwe 785, the Economic Freedom Fighters (EFF) 588 on 6.22% of the vote, and ActionSA 313 on 5.67%. Despite remaining the largest party nationally, the ANC secured outright control in only 53 municipalities and emerged as the leading party in 128. The DA secured outright control in 12 municipalities and led in 32, while the Inkatha Freedom Party controlled five and led in 19, and uMkhonto weSizwe controlled three and led in 19.

Since no party won more than 50% of votes in many cities, including South Africa's commercial hub Johannesburg, the ANC and DA will have to team up, or hash it out with smaller parties to form majority municipal governments. The number of municipalities where coalitions will be needed more than doubled from 2021, reflecting a fragmentation of South Africa's electorate. Parties will have 14 days to negotiate before city councils are formed, and the new councils must meet by 27 November.

In brief remarks after the results announcement, President Cyril Ramaphosa said he was proud of the multi-party nature of South Africa's democracy. "We celebrate the enduring strength of our democracy and the sovereign right of the people of South Africa to choose their representatives," he said.

ANC Secretary-General Fikile Mbalula called the result "catastrophic" for the once-dominant party of Nelson Mandela but said the party would not go begging for positions when coalition talks begin soon. "When the people tell us we have fallen short... the honest answer is not to argue with them but to go home and put our house in order," Mbalula said on Saturday.

The metropolitan results tell the story of the ANC's decline most sharply. The DA led in five of the eight metros: Johannesburg, Tshwane, Ekurhuleni, Nelson Mandela Bay and Cape Town, where it secured an outright majority with 56.42% of the vote. The ANC's one metro lead was Buffalo City, where it took 41.93%, and it also led narrowly in Mangaung with 33.91% against the DA's 33.49%. In eThekwini, which includes Durban, the MK Party knocked the ANC off the top spot with 38.29% of the vote and 86 of the metro's 223 council seats — short of the 112 needed for a majority.

In Johannesburg the pro-business DA got about 35% of votes, compared to 20% for the ANC. That could give DA veteran Helen Zille, who had campaigned on fixing potholes and leaking water pipes, a good chance of becoming mayor, depending on the coalition agreements reached. Zille is a former mayor of Cape Town and former premier of the Western Cape, and her party won 91 of the council's 270 seats, leaving a majority of 136 out of reach on its own. ActionSA, which came third in Johannesburg with 16% of the vote, will not support the DA with Zille as its mayoral candidate, the party's leader Herman Mashaba told Reuters on Sunday. Mashaba, a former mayor of Johannesburg who quit the DA in 2019 after a fallout with Zille, has emerged as the kingmaker in the city. On 7 November he wrote to DA leader Geordin Hill-Lewis offering to leave the mayoral race if Zille also stepped aside — under his offer the DA would keep the mayoralty with another candidate and ActionSA would take the deputy mayor's post. In Tshwane, where the DA led with 34.37% and ActionSA came second on 22.07%, Mashaba proposed that the DA's mayoral candidate Cilliers Brink step aside for ActionSA's Dr Nasiphi Moya. The ANC has also reportedly proposed a deal under which Mashaba would become Johannesburg mayor, ActionSA would keep the Tshwane mayoralty and the ANC would keep Ekurhuleni, shutting the DA out of all three Gauteng metros — though no deal was final, and mediation by the Freedom Front Plus between the DA and ActionSA failed in the days that followed.

Analysts have said the best option for stability in cities like Johannesburg would be for the ANC to partner with the DA, mirroring the arrangement they have had in national government for the past two years. The two parties sit together in a 10-party government of national unity formed after the ANC lost its parliamentary majority in 2024. But the two are ideologically opposed on key issues such as racial redress, and a local fight to exclude the DA tests that partnership. A pact between the DA and the ANC in Johannesburg would hold 154 seats, while a four-party bloc of the ANC, ActionSA, the EFF and uMkhonto weSizwe would hold 138, according to people familiar with the talks — enough to elect a mayor of their own choosing.

The ANC's first formal coalition talks were held on Sunday 8 November, between Mbalula and ActionSA national chairperson Michael Beaumont, a meeting both parties described as productive. On 13 November the ANC's National Executive Committee authorised its negotiation teams to engage with all political parties, including smaller organisations, civic movements and independent candidates — a significant departure from the days when the ANC could comfortably form governments on its own. "This situation has imposed on all political parties a duty to reflect and determine the best and most stable coalitions to serve the people of South Africa," the party said.

The election leaves South Africa's largest party facing a new reality: it is still the biggest party in the country, but it no longer commands a majority in any of its eight metropolitan councils, and in the economic hubs of Gauteng and KwaZulu-Natal it trails rivals it once dwarfed. Whether the next city governments are stable will depend on negotiations conducted in the two weeks after the vote — and on whether parties that spent the campaign attacking each other can agree on who fixes the water, the power and the potholes.
