Nvidia Shares Slide as Board Probes CEO Huang Over Spy Claims

technology · 9 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Huang told staff he had been unfaithful to his wife; the board says it has no reason to believe trade secrets were compromised.

Nvidia shares tumbled on Monday after the chipmaker said its board had opened an independent investigation into whether chief executive Jensen Huang had been targeted by a foreign intelligence effort, putting the future of the world's most valuable chipmaker under scrutiny.

The shares fell around 8% in early trading, extending last week's sell-off and pushing the stock to fresh multi-month lows, before paring losses to about 6%. At the early lows, the company stood to lose around $350 billion in market value from Friday's closing price of $186.64, Reuters reported.

The probe, announced on Sunday, followed a New York Times report last month that Huang had a close relationship with Alice Huang, an unrelated former executive of Singapore-based company Megaspeed, who US authorities investigated and concluded was a Chinese intelligence asset. Huang told Nvidia employees he had been unfaithful to his wife, without disclosing details of the relationship.

Nvidia said a committee of independent directors would oversee the investigation with the assistance of independent outside legal counsel. The board said it had no reason to believe any trade secrets had been compromised.

China's foreign ministry rejected the espionage allegations, calling them a groundless accusation and a smear, Reuters reported.

Huang, 63, has become the most prominent salesman of the artificial intelligence boom, with Nvidia's value rising from about $420 billion in late 2022 to $4.5 trillion as of 6 November. Its next milestone is its fiscal third-quarter results call on 17 November, while shares in key Asian suppliers including TSMC rebounded on Monday after last week's sell-off.
