Anthropic's Record $81 Billion IPO Debuts on Wall Street as Shares Jump 17%

business · 20 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (Business Desk)

Anthropic shares closed at $97.17 on their first day of trading, up 17% from the $83 IPO price, after briefly valuing the Claude maker at more than $2 trillion.

Anthropic, the artificial intelligence lab behind the Claude chatbot, made the largest stock market debut in history on Thursday, raising $81.3 billion in an initial public offering that valued the company at about $1.5 trillion and turned its first day of trading into a referendum on the AI boom.

The company sold 980 million shares at $83 each, pricing the offering above its initial range of $70 to $78 set when it launched the roadshow. An additional $26 billion raised through a private placement takes the total haul to more than $107 billion. The amount raised could increase to as much as $93.5 billion if underwriters exercise their option to buy additional shares.

Trading on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, the shares opened at $115, briefly pushing Anthropic's valuation above $2 trillion in early trading, before settling back. The stock closed its first day at $97.17, up 17% from the IPO price. Anthropic's stock market value ended the day at about $1.3 trillion based on its outstanding shares, or $1.8 trillion based on its fully diluted share count.

The listing makes Anthropic the first major frontier AI model developer to go public, and it joins the world's trillion-dollar club alongside technology giants such as Microsoft, Apple and Nvidia. Its arch-rival OpenAI is reportedly eyeing a listing of its own in 2027, which would make Anthropic the benchmark against which the next wave of AI companies is judged.

The debut came at the end of a week that Wall Street treated as a two-part test of the AI trade. On Tuesday, Nvidia, the chipmaker at the centre of the AI buildout, reported quarterly results that looked strong at first glance but received a rocky reception from investors, a sign that the euphoria around the sector has cooled. Anthropic's listing was the second test. "I've looked at this whole week as a referendum on AI. Nvidia gave us a read on the buildout, and Anthropic is about to give us a read on investor appetite for the next generation of AI companies," Jake Behan, head of capital markets at Direxion, said before the debut.

The strong demand for Anthropic shares offered hope to a sector that has turned sceptical in recent weeks, even as the broader market refused to join the celebration. US stocks slumped 2% on Thursday, with the S&P 500 and Nasdaq making fresh seven-month lows, as investors shrugged off the euphoria of the record debut and focused instead on growing concerns about the longer-term outlook for the AI trade.

That tension sits at the heart of the story. Anthropic's record $81 billion IPO brings to Wall Street an avatar of a sector that proponents herald as an economic panacea, while detractors warn it has stoked a market bubble and could ultimately threaten humanity itself. Debate has intensified across global markets in recent months over whether the AI boom is veering into bubble territory, with economists, some of the world's biggest chief executives and political leaders split over its sustainability.

Investors who once embraced the boom have turned sceptical over concerns that the enormous spending needed to build out the sector, and the incipient crushing debt loads that come with it, may not be justified by the eventual returns. An extramarital-affair scandal involving Nvidia's chief executive and a spike in Treasury yields have further unnerved investors in recent weeks.

Much of the scrutiny has focused on circular financing arrangements. A complicated web of deals between AI companies, chipmakers, cloud providers and investors has raised questions over how much of the industry's growth is being fuelled by money circulating within the same ecosystem rather than by outside demand.

Anthropic's breakneck growth has so far defied those concerns, but the numbers behind the valuation are stark. Its annualised revenue grew from about $1 billion at the start of 2025 to about $81 billion last month, as its valuation climbed from $61.5 billion in March 2025 to $965 billion in the latest private round this May. Analysis by the Financial Times suggests Anthropic would need to be generating annual revenue of $600 billion, far more than Alphabet and Apple currently turn over, to justify its price tag over time.

The company has nearly $900 billion in computing commitments with partners including Amazon and Alphabet's Google, underscoring how its rapid growth hinges on securing vast amounts of future computing capacity, some of which relies on financing beyond the company's control. Some investors have said they may wait for the initial euphoria to subside before buying. "It doesn't have to be a bad company for it to be a bad investment at the IPO price," said Nic Puckrin, cross-asset analyst and founder of Coin Bureau. Others see the demand differently: "Some of the demand may simply reflect investors not wanting to miss out on the rare opportunity to gain exposure to a leading AI company, even if its long-term profitability remains uncertain," said Lukas Muehlbauer, an associate at IPOX.

The debut delivered multibillion-dollar windfalls to the venture capital firms that backed Anthropic early, at a moment when the industry needs fresh liquidity to fund more bets on AI companies. For decades, venture capital thrived on small bets that became billion-dollar companies; Anthropic rewrote that playbook, forcing investors to write fatter checks to buy stakes at unheard-of valuations.

Menlo Ventures and Spark Capital emerged with some of the biggest returns by multiple. Spark Capital was the first venture capital firm to lead an Anthropic investment, in a $450 million Series C round in 2023 at a valuation of $4.5 billion. Spark general partner Yasmin Razavi, who spearheaded the investment, remains the only investor who sits on Anthropic's board. Menlo wrote the largest check in the firm's history, investing more than $1 billion for a stake of nearly 1.5%, which at a $1.5 trillion market capitalisation is worth more than $20 billion. To invest beyond the capacity of its existing fund, Menlo raised a $750 million special-purpose vehicle from its limited partners, a tactic that has become increasingly common as funding rounds grew.

The late-stage bets paid off too. Sequoia Capital, long known for its early bets, passed on Anthropic at lower prices, then wrote its first check at the start of this year, joining a round led by Singapore's GIC and Coatue at a $380 billion post-money valuation in February. It later co-led, alongside Altimeter, Dragoneer and Greenoaks, a round at $965 billion. Sequoia has invested $3 billion in Anthropic and continued to invest more through the IPO. Lightspeed Venture Partners, which invested more than $3 billion, now holds over 2% of the company, making it the largest financial investor in Anthropic. Iconiq, which invested $6.1 billion, is the second-largest financial investor by ownership. Strategic backers supply much of the capital and computing power as well: the combined stake of Amazon and Alphabet's Google in Anthropic is about 28.4%.

Demand for the shares stretched far beyond Silicon Valley. Japanese investors sought to buy more than 1.5 trillion yen, about $9.4 billion, worth of Anthropic shares at the listing, and ultimately secured $2.98 billion worth, according to a filing. London-based index rebalance analytics firm Intropic estimates that passive demand from index-tracking funds will account for about 28% of Anthropic's shares offered, or roughly $19 billion. The listing also gives retail investors a chance to pile into one of the AI industry's hottest names, potentially amplifying swings in the stock as trading begins, and the buzz has already spilled onto prediction markets, where traders are placing bets on everything from its opening pop to where the stock will close on its first day.

The listing caps a year in which the US IPO market has proved a study in contrasts, with fundraising shattering records while the number of companies going public stays well below historical highs. Anthropic surpassed SpaceX, which raised $75 billion at its listing in June, to set the record for the world's largest IPO. SpaceX and Anthropic alone will account for about two-thirds of this year's IPO proceeds, so the headline fundraising figure overstates how broad the recovery really is. Some deals are being pushed into 2027 as Anthropic's listing crowds out smaller issuers, Bank of America chief executive Brian Moynihan said this week.

Anthropic's own prospectus added a note of caution that no other newly listed company has carried. The company warned that AI could behave unpredictably and advance faster than oversight mechanisms, posing potentially "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity". It will also need to balance growth with calls to slow the development of advanced AI models over concerns that they pose cybersecurity threats and could be exploited by bad actors to target critical government and private infrastructure.

What happens next will be watched as closely as the debut itself. Investors will be watching Anthropic's trading closely, with analysts expecting the stock to emerge as a proxy for the broader AI trade as questions over valuations, spending and returns weigh on sentiment. The company must now prove, quarter after quarter, that revenue growing from $1 billion to $81 billion in under two years can keep climbing towards the hundreds of billions its valuation implies, while funding nearly $900 billion in computing commitments in a market that has just spent a week questioning whether the AI buildout is worth its cost.

Why it matters: Anthropic's debut is the biggest market event of the year and the clearest test yet of whether investors still believe the AI story at any price. A 17% first-day gain on the largest IPO in history says appetite for the leading AI names is still there. A 2% fall in the broader market on the same day says the doubts about the boom are not going away. Both can be true at once, and which one wins will decide not just Anthropic's share price, but the funding of the entire AI industry.
