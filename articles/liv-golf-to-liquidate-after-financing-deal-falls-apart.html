LIV Golf to Liquidate After Last-Ditch Financing Deal Falls Apart

sport · 6 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Jon Rahm walked away from an estimated $100 million in future guaranteed money; Bryson DeChambeau is owed about $5.7 million.

LIV Golf, which shook up world golf in 2022, will liquidate after losing big-name players and the last-ditch financing it needed to stay alive, the breakaway circuit said on Thursday.

"In light of the current circumstances, we believe shifting toward an orderly wind-down is the most responsible path forward for the League at this time," Gene Davis and Jon Zinman of LIV Golf Board’s Special Committee said in a press release.

LIV had secured $300 million of potential financing from London-based BC Partners to allow the staging of tournaments in 2027 but scrapped the plan after a number of notable players opted not to commit to the league going forward. The future of the circuit had been in question after it lost financial support from the deep-pocketed Saudi Arabia Public Investment Fund beyond the now-completed 2026 season.

Throughout the wind-down process, LIV said it will remain open to, and continue to consider, any value-maximizing alternatives with third parties that may arise.

The collapse is likely to leave players as free agents and creditors with little to recover, restructuring expert John Sparacino of law firm McKool Smith told Reuters. Jon Rahm reached a separation agreement with LIV and walked away from an estimated $100 million in future guaranteed money in exchange for cutting ties early, while Bryson DeChambeau is owed approximately $5.7 million.

Sparacino said the case could move quickly into a Chapter 7 liquidation. Aside from intellectual property, branded merchandise and possible contractual rights, the league’s remaining assets may also be limited, and he said he had seen no indication the company possessed significant litigation claims that could generate recoveries for creditors.

Incoming PGA Tour commissioner Brian Rolapp has said the tour’s position is that until LIV players are out of contract, the tour will not engage in conversations. Rolapp has also said he is not considering another returning member program like the one Brooks Koepka took advantage of to rejoin the tour.
