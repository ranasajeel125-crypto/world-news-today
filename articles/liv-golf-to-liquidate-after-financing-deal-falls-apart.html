LIV Golf to Liquidate After Last-Ditch Financing Deal Falls Apart

sport · 6 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Jon Rahm walked away from an estimated $100 million in future guaranteed money; Bryson DeChambeau is owed about $5.7 million.

LIV Golf, which shook up world golf in 2022, will liquidate after losing big-name players and the last-ditch financing it needed to stay alive, the breakaway circuit said on Thursday.

"In light of the current circumstances, we believe shifting toward an orderly wind-down is the most responsible path forward for the League at this time," Gene Davis and Jon Zinman of LIV Golf Board's Special Committee said in a press release.

LIV had secured $300 million of potential financing from London-based BC Partners to allow the staging of tournaments in 2027 but scrapped the plan after a number of notable players opted not to commit to the league going forward. The future of the circuit had been in question after it lost financial support from the deep-pocketed Saudi Arabia Public Investment Fund beyond the now-completed 2026 season.

Throughout the wind-down process, LIV said it will remain open to, and continue to consider, any value-maximizing alternatives with third parties that may arise. The league's search for new capital followed a familiar restructuring playbook, but BC Partners required LIV to secure commitments from a critical mass of elite players, a condition that appeared increasingly uncertain in recent weeks, and BC Partners was believed to be LIV's only serious funding option rather than the winner of a competitive process.

The collapse is likely to leave players as free agents and creditors with little to recover, restructuring expert John Sparacino of law firm McKool Smith told Reuters. Jon Rahm reached a separation agreement with LIV and walked away from an estimated $100 million in future guaranteed money in exchange for cutting ties early, while Bryson DeChambeau is owed approximately $5.7 million.

Sparacino said the case could move quickly into a Chapter 7 liquidation. Aside from intellectual property, branded merchandise and possible contractual rights, the league's remaining assets may also be limited, and he said he had seen no indication the company possessed significant litigation claims that could generate recoveries for creditors. Sports leagues such as LIV are generally asset-light businesses, and he questioned how much value LIV's trademarks and other branding would fetch in a liquidation.

LIV Golf was launched in 2022 with the backing of Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, and it set out to challenge the PGA Tour, the established home of professional golf in the United States. It offered players enormous guaranteed contracts, far larger than anything the PGA Tour could match, and it staged tournaments in a new format: 54 holes instead of 72, shotgun starts, no cut, and team competition alongside the individual event. The name LIV is a reference to the Roman numeral for 54.

The circuit lured some of the biggest names in golf, including former world number one Jon Rahm, Brooks Koepka, Bryson DeChambeau, Patrick Reed, Sergio Garcia and Tyrrell Hatton, and it provoked a bitter legal battle with the PGA Tour, which suspended players who joined LIV and was in turn accused of anti-competitive behaviour. The dispute divided the sport, with fans, sponsors and broadcasters forced to choose sides, and it raised uncomfortable questions about Saudi Arabia's human rights record and the use of sport to improve the kingdom's image, a practice critics call sportswashing.

In late April, after pouring more than $5 billion into LIV Golf, the Saudi fund said the circuit no longer fitted its investment strategy, and it would pull funding after the 2026 season. LIV filed for bankruptcy protection in September, pinning its survival on a $300 million investment from BC Partners Credit to relaunch as a leaner operation. Under the proposed deal, the firm's model swapped guaranteed contracts for equity in the league, a trade several stars, including Jon Rahm, Tyrrell Hatton and Sergio Garcia, declined. Between 15 and 40 players had expressed varying levels of interest, but some sought payment of financial guarantees that had been cancelled during the bankruptcy process and wanted the option to withdraw from the agreement even after signing commitments.

Leading golfers, such as former world number one Jon Rahm, announced their departures from LIV Golf after it filed for bankruptcy protection in September. Among the high-profile golfers to leave LIV since its bankruptcy filing were Jon Rahm, Brooks Koepka and Patrick Reed.

The PGA Tour ultimately rejected the Saudi fund's $1.5 billion investment proposal, which was contingent on LIV Golf remaining intact and PIF governor Yasir Al-Rumayyan serving as co-chair of the PGA Tour Enterprises board. That rejection closed off one of the last routes by which LIV might have been absorbed into the mainstream of the sport, and it left the breakaway circuit with no obvious future.

The shutdown comes despite rising viewership under a multiyear deal signed with Fox Sports in January 2025. LIV averaged 386,000 viewers across 14 Fox telecasts in 2026, up 12% year over year, with its Indianapolis finale drawing a league-record 702,000. Those gains still trailed the PGA Tour, and the Fox rights agreement expired at the close of the 2026 season.

Incoming PGA Tour commissioner Brian Rolapp has said the tour's position is that until LIV players are out of contract, the tour will not engage in conversations. Rolapp has also said he is not considering another returning member program like the one Brooks Koepka took advantage of to rejoin the tour. That leaves the players who stayed with LIV until the end in an uncertain position, as free agents with no tour to play on and no clear route back to the PGA Tour.

BC Partners has not given up entirely. The firm has revived efforts to create a successor to LIV Golf, the Financial Times reported on Sunday, after the breakaway circuit said it would wind down operations. BC Partners is seeking to acquire a $50 million bankruptcy loan from Saudi Arabia's Public Investment Fund, the debtor-in-possession financing that would allow it to exert control over LIV Golf's Chapter 11 bankruptcy process. BC Partners has told counterparties it still believes a global team-based golf group can succeed and remains willing to commit additional money and time to launching a LIV successor, according to the report.

For the sport, the end of LIV Golf closes a chapter that began with enormous ambition and enormous spending. The circuit changed professional golf, forcing the PGA Tour to increase prize money and to rethink its schedule, and it showed that even $5 billion could not buy a lasting rival to an established tour if the players, the sponsors and the broadcasters did not follow. The players who took the money will keep it, the creditors will recover little, and the fans will be left with a sport that, after four years of division, is once again whole, but poorer for the experience.
