Blast Beside School in Afghanistan's Kunar Kills at Least Six Students, Injures 97

world · 28 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

An explosion in a house beside a middle school in the Andar Ser area of Kunar province, eastern Afghanistan, killed at least six students and injured 97 others on Saturday morning, a hospital official said.

At least six students were killed and 97 others injured on Saturday when an explosion tore through a house beside a middle school in Kunar province in eastern Afghanistan, a hospital official said, in one of the deadliest incidents at a school in the country in months.

Mawla Dad, the head of Kunar Provincial Hospital, told Al Jazeera that the figures were initial and the casualty numbers could change as more information becomes available. The injured were taken to the hospital, where staff were treating them on Saturday.

The explosion happened at about 10am local time (05:30 GMT) on Saturday in the Andar Ser area of Kunar province, Mawlawi Sami-ul-Haq, the province’s director of information and culture, told Al Jazeera. The blast was in a house beside a middle school, not inside the school building itself, according to the account given by officials.

Sami-ul-Haq said the house belonged to people involved in mining operations who had stored dynamite there. He did not say what set the explosives off, and he gave no further details about the incident, including the number of casualties or the extent of the damage. He added that no further information was available at the time.

Reuters separately reported the explosion at a school in Afghanistan’s northeastern province of Kunar on Saturday, quoting Sami-ul-Haq as saying he could not provide details on the nature of the explosion or whether there were casualties. The two reports agree on the place and the timing; the casualty figures come from the hospital.

Investigative teams from Kunar province were at the scene on Saturday, Al Jazeera reported. Kunar is a mountainous province on Afghanistan’s border with Pakistan, and Andar Ser is one of its rural areas.

What officials have not yet said is whether any of the injured were adults, how badly the school building itself was damaged, or how the stored dynamite came to detonate. Those are the questions the investigation now has to answer, and the answers will decide whether this was a preventable accident involving explosives kept beside a school.

The storage of dynamite in a house next to a school will be the central question. Mining is a common livelihood in parts of Kunar, and explosives used in mining are sometimes kept in homes rather than in purpose-built stores. If that is what happened here, the blast points to a failure of basic safety rules around explosives, not to an attack — no group has claimed responsibility, and officials have described stored dynamite, not a bomb.

Schools in Afghanistan have been hit before. In August, a hand grenade attack at a school complex in the Afghan capital of Kabul wounded more than 40 children aged between nine and 14. Saturday’s blast in Kunar is different in kind — an explosion in a neighbouring house rather than an attack on the school — but the victims are the same: children who were at school on a Saturday morning.

What happens next depends on the investigation and on the hospital. The casualty figures are preliminary, and the hospital head has warned they could change. Investigators will need to establish how much dynamite was stored in the house, how it was kept, and whether anyone will be held responsible for keeping explosives beside a school.

Why it matters: six children are dead and nearly a hundred people are injured because explosives were stored in a house beside a school. The figures are still preliminary, but the pattern is already clear — a blast that should never have been able to happen next to a classroom. Until the investigation explains how dynamite came to be kept there, every other school in a mining area in Kunar carries the same risk.
