Apartment Building Fire in Taiyuan Kills 14

world · 9 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Smoke billowed over the 40-storey building in downtown Taiyuan as firefighters on cranes battled the flames, in video verified by Reuters.

world · 9 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

A fire in a high-rise apartment building in Taiyuan, the capital of China's northern Shanxi province, killed 14 people, the state-run Xinhua news agency said on Monday.

The blaze broke out at about 4:59 p.m. local time (0859 GMT) on Sunday in a building in the Liuxiang subdistrict of Yingze district, in the commercial heart of downtown Taiyuan. The building was the Haosheng International Apartment on Liuxiang Street, a 40-storey mixed-use tower with flats, a hotel and children's education centres. State media said the fire appeared to have started around the fourth floor of an annex before spreading to the eighth and ninth floors, which house a hotel.

Five people had been confirmed dead by late on Sunday night, and the toll rose to 14 on Monday morning as people taken to hospital died of their injuries. Thirty-eight people were taken to hospital; 25 remained in hospital on Monday, nine of them seriously injured, state media said. The Yingze district government said the flames were extinguished by about 7 p.m. and search and rescue operations ended at 8:30 p.m. on Sunday, roughly two hours after firefighters brought the blaze under control.

Video verified by Reuters showed smoke billowing over the 40-storey structure as firefighters poised on cranes outside battled flames spreading from the building's centre. The footage captured the scale of the emergency in one of Taiyuan's busiest commercial areas, a district close to hospitals, schools, commercial centres and tourist sites. The building held flats, at least one hotel, restaurants, short-term rental apartments and academic coaching centres for children, according to property listings and photographs published by local media, which meant that on a Sunday afternoon it was full of residents, guests and families.

Authorities are investigating the cause of the fire, state media said. No cause has yet been given. The district government said all-out efforts were being made to treat the injured, and that follow-up work was under way. The investigation will need to answer several questions: where exactly the fire began, how it spread so quickly from a lower annex to the hotel floors above, whether alarms and evacuation procedures worked as intended, and whether the building's fire safety systems functioned properly.

The fire is a reminder of the particular dangers of high-rise fires in China, where mixed-use towers combining homes, hotels, shops and tutoring centres have become common in city centres. In such buildings, a fire that starts in one part of the structure can spread through stairwells, lift shafts and ventilation systems, and smoke can travel faster than flames, filling upper floors before residents know they need to leave. Firefighters, meanwhile, must work from outside with cranes and ladders, and from inside through smoke-filled corridors, while evacuating large numbers of people from dozens of floors at once. In Taiyuan, the rescue operation was completed within about three and a half hours, but for the 38 people taken to hospital, and for the families of the 14 who died, the consequences will last far longer.

China has seen a series of deadly fires in residential and commercial buildings in recent years, and each one has prompted calls for stricter enforcement of fire safety rules, better inspection of older buildings, and clearer responsibility for safety in mixed-use properties where flats, hotels and businesses share the same structure. In buildings like the one in Taiyuan, responsibility can be divided between the property management company, individual businesses and residents, and gaps in that chain are often exposed only after a fire. Investigators in Taiyuan will be looking closely at the building's safety record, its evacuation routes, and whether any recent renovation work or electrical fault played a role.

Taiyuan itself is a city of several million people and the capital of Shanxi, a province built on coal mining and heavy industry that has been working to diversify its economy. Its downtown, around Liuxiang, is a dense commercial district of shops, restaurants, hotels and apartments, and the tower that burned sits at its heart. The fire brought that district to a standstill on Sunday evening, as emergency vehicles crowded the streets and crowds gathered to watch the smoke rise over the 40-storey building.

For now, the focus is on the injured. Twenty-five people remained in hospital on Monday, nine of them seriously injured, and doctors were working to stabilise them. The authorities have said follow-up work is under way, which in China typically means support for the victims' families, compensation arrangements, and the formal investigation that will determine what went wrong. Until that investigation reports, the cause of the fire that killed 14 people in the heart of Taiyuan remains unknown, and the building stands as a reminder of how quickly an ordinary Sunday afternoon can turn into a disaster in a crowded high-rise.
