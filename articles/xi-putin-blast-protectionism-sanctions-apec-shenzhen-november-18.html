Xi and Putin Blast Protectionism and Sanctions at APEC Summit Ahead of Trump Trilateral

world · 18 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Xi spoke at the opening of the APEC CEO summit in Shenzhen, with Putin following him on stage, a day before the three leaders are due to meet on the sidelines of the summit.

Chinese President Xi Jinping on Wednesday urged support for what he called “free and open trade” without investment barriers, in an opening speech at an Asia-Pacific summit where he was due to meet his US and Russian counterparts, Reuters reported. Xi was speaking at the opening of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) CEO summit in the southern Chinese tech hub of Shenzhen, ahead of a gathering of the member states’ leaders in the evening.

“We must firmly uphold the rules-based multilateral trading system with the World Trade Organization at its core, support free and open trade and investment, maintain the stability and smooth operation of global industrial and supply chains,” Xi told an audience of business executives, after posing a series of rhetorical questions on how the Asia Pacific should respond to a changing world, Reuters reported. France 24 reported that Xi also called for stronger international cooperation on artificial intelligence.

Xi’s remarks came after Washington announced further restrictions on China’s access to advanced AI chips from companies such as Nvidia, which could further complicate a fragile trade truce with Beijing, Reuters reported. China has pledged to retaliate. The truce was agreed at last year’s APEC summit in South Korea and will expire in January, France 24 reported.

In his opening address, Xi urged support for free and open trade while trying to portray his country as a stable partner on the global stage, in contrast with its main economic rival the United States, which has raised tariffs and adopted a more muscular foreign policy under President Donald Trump, Reuters reported. Xi said China will continue to invest in its industrial complex and develop new technologies that would also benefit other countries, and that the world’s second-largest economy will lower tariffs and further open up its markets, while standing up against protectionism and unilateralism.

Western trade partners have criticised China for running annual trade surpluses in excess of $1 trillion through policies that support its manufacturing sector at the expense of its consumers, Reuters reported. Beijing says its growth model is appropriate for an economy still catching up with the world’s richest, but Washington, Brussels and others are concerned about China’s supply chain dominance providing the country with geopolitical leverage, and say its policies threaten their industries and jobs.

Xi’s message is likely to land well beyond China. “His message is likely to resonate with many Global South countries and reinforces Beijing’s effort to contrast its technology strategy with that of Washington,” George Chen, partner and chair of digital practice at The Asia Group, told Reuters. For governments across Asia, Africa and Latin America that trade heavily with both Washington and Beijing, a Chinese leader presenting his country as the defender of open markets is a pitch aimed squarely at them.

Russian President Vladimir Putin landed overnight and followed Xi on stage with a speech that described Western powers as “former hegemons”, Reuters reported. He said Europe was in a “humiliating decline” and that anti-Russian sanctions imposed after his invasion of Ukraine had backfired, instead making his country stronger.

“Experience has shown that the traditional tools of economic dominance and de facto coercion have largely run their course, having lost much of their intimidating power,” Putin said, according to Reuters. “While sanctions, tariffs, and export restrictions may still exert a certain deterrent effect, they are incapable of halting growth — let alone crushing the economies of competitors.” He described the world as “turbulent” and “marked by a fragmenting global economy and rising military-political risks”, but said disagreements between countries can be addressed by “sitting at the table”.

Putin was speaking after Ukraine destroyed the vital Kerch road bridge linking Crimea to Russia, in a major blow to his four-year-old war, Reuters reported. On Tuesday, Taiwan and Japan flagged incursions by Russian aircraft into their airspace. “I therefore expect big potential benefit from the upcoming trilateral meeting between the leaders of Russia, China and the United States,” Putin said.

The three leaders are set for high-stakes talks on Thursday on the Iran and Ukraine wars, with flashpoints such as trade, AI and nuclear weapons also on the agenda, Reuters reported. Trump was due to arrive in Shenzhen later on Wednesday after concluding meetings with Southeast Asian and other regional leaders in Manila, where he attended the first Quad leaders’ meeting in two years. Trump also floated the possibility that he might meet North Korean leader Kim Jong Un, but that has not been confirmed, Reuters reported.

The meeting has drawn comparisons with the 1945 Yalta Conference between the US, Britain and the Soviet Union, which formalised spheres of influence, set the stage for the post-Second World War order and became shorthand for great-power deals imposed on smaller countries, Reuters reported. It has also unnerved leaders of smaller economies, particularly in Europe and Asia, who fear the world’s greatest military powers will disregard their interests.

The Kremlin this week dismissed comparisons with the Yalta Conference as “games of political scientists”, but said the meeting will have a major impact on global affairs, Reuters reported. Beijing said the trilateral is of “groundbreaking significance” and will promote world peace. APEC leaders will meet behind closed doors on Thursday, The Hindu reported, and APEC does not take binding decisions — so whatever the three leaders agree in Shenzhen will have to be carried into other forums to take effect.

The Shenzhen encounter sits at the intersection of the week’s two biggest stories. The Gulf conflict has widened this week, with Iran firing drones at US radar sites in Bahrain and Kuwait and American jets striking an Iranian missile site on Qeshm Island, while in Ukraine the destruction of the Kerch bridge has raised the stakes of any negotiation. Thursday’s talks put the leaders of the three countries most directly involved in those conflicts in the same room, at a summit whose formal business is trade.

Trump’s trip to Asia is his first since his Republican Party’s defeat in midterm elections, Reuters reported, while allegations involving Nvidia founder Jensen Huang and a suspected Chinese spy add to the tension around the US-China relationship. Still, most analysts expect Washington and Beijing to keep ties relatively stable, Reuters reported, as Trump and Xi are set to follow their Thursday meeting in Shenzhen with their fourth of the year at the Group of 20 gathering in Miami in December.

Why it matters: Shenzhen is where the world’s three most powerful leaders meet this week, and the two speeches on Wednesday set out the terms of the argument before they sit down together. Xi presented China as the guardian of open trade and multilateral rules, Putin dismissed Western sanctions as spent tools of a fading order, and both spoke directly to an audience of countries caught between them and Washington. What happens on Thursday — on two wars, on the chips that power artificial intelligence, and on the trade truce that expires in January — will shape not only US, Chinese and Russian relations, but the smaller economies watching from the sidelines and wondering whether their interests will survive a deal made over their heads.
