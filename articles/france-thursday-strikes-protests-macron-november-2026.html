Hundreds of Thousands March Across France as Thursday Strikes Test Macron

world · 5 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The mobilisation, called by trade unions including the left-wing CGT and backed by student groups, follows demonstrations that triggered the worst civil unrest France has seen in years.

Hundreds of thousands of protesters marched through French cities and public workers went on strike on Thursday, in a fresh test for President Emmanuel Macron after weeks of unrest that have rattled his government and dominated the agenda five months ahead of a presidential election, Reuters reported.

The mobilisation was called by trade unions including the left-wing CGT and backed by student groups, after demonstrations over living costs, public services and education triggered the worst civil unrest France has seen in years. Authorities deployed around 70,000 police nationwide, BFM TV reported, and the domestic intelligence service expected between 500,000 and 700,000 people to join demonstrations outside Paris. A total of 220 demonstrations were planned across the country on Thursday.

"We are extremely vigilant because it is out of the question to relive October 17," government spokeswoman Maud Bregeon told a press conference after Wednesday's cabinet meeting, referring to the day last month when riots and looting caught security forces off guard, leaving damaged shops and public buildings across the country.

The Education Ministry said 17.5% of public primary school teachers were expected to strike, while unions warned of likely disruption to rail and public transport services. French power unions also joined Thursday's strike, state-owned utility EDF said; a previous power sector strike in mid-September reduced electricity supply in the country and cut delivery capacity at France's biggest liquefied natural gas terminal, Dunkirk LNG.

Macron convened a defence and security meeting on Wednesday ahead of the protests, underlining how a movement that began with student anger over deteriorating school conditions has evolved into a broader challenge to his government. "The goal in the coming weeks is to get the river that overflowed back into its bed and to launch the presidential election in the calmest possible conditions," an official close to Macron said.

Among those marching on Thursday were students who say conditions in schools and growing uncertainty over their future remain major concerns. "We're trying to keep mobilising as much as possible. Some people aren't doing so because they're afraid. A lot of people are afraid of police violence," said Irma de Banville, a 19-year-old student in Paris. The French government has defended police tactics while saying any abuses would be investigated.

Nationwide protests last Saturday were generally peaceful, with some clashes on the fringes. A grassroots protest movement backed by some unions is set to hold a new round of demonstrations on Saturday, as it has done weekly since the October 17 protest, and some unions and housing rights associations have called for pan-European protests from November 20 to November 22.
