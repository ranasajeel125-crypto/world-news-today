Russian 'Triple-Tap' Strike on Zelenskyy's Hometown Kryvyi Rih Kills Two and Wounds 60

world · 21 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

A 'triple-tap' Russian strike on Kryvyi Rih killed a seven-year-old boy and a 20-year-old paramedic and wounded 60, including six children.

A Russian strike on the centre of Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy’s hometown, Kryvyi Rih, killed two people and wounded 60 others, local officials said on Saturday. A seven-year-old boy and a 20-year-old paramedic were killed in the attack on Friday night on the city in central Ukraine, the head of the city administration, Oleksandr Vilkul, wrote on Telegram. The head of the regional military administration, Oleksandr Hanzha, said 60 people were wounded, including six children.

The attack was what officials and CNN described as a “triple-tap” strike. Ballistic missiles first hit a restaurant parking lot, and then jet-powered drones struck the rescue workers who had rushed to respond, Vilkul said. A further drone strike hit the same location a short time later. In total, 60 people were wounded, and 28 remain in hospital, including children — among them a three-year-old girl — with five people in intensive care.

The strike caused four fires and damaged about 25 apartment buildings, a hospital, schools and an industrial facility, as well as businesses and vehicles, Vilkul said. The attack hit the city centre, near a popular restaurant, on Children’s Day — November 20 in Ukraine, a day meant to draw attention to children’s rights and their safety. A local broadcaster named the seven-year-old boy who was killed as Tikhon, describing him as kind, quick-witted and talented. Vilkul described the events as a “tragic evening and a tragic night for our city.”

President Zelenskyy said the attack on Friday night hit an ordinary civilian area of his home city. The strike came in a wider overnight wave across Ukraine: 11 people, including two children, were wounded when a Russian drone attacked a five-storey apartment building in the Kharkiv region, Ukraine’s State Emergency Service said on Saturday, and one person was wounded in an attack on the capital, Kyiv. Zelenskyy said cities and villages in the Kharkiv, Donetsk, Dnipro, Rivne, Chernihiv, Mykolaiv and Zaporizhzhia regions were also hit, and a railway station in the Sumy region came under attack.

The attack fits the pattern of Moscow’s heavy responses to eye-catching Ukrainian attacks, such as the ones this week on the Kerch Bridge linking Russia to illegally annexed Crimea. At a meeting with the UK defence secretary, Wes Streeting, in Kyiv on Saturday, Zelenskyy said Ukraine’s repeated attacks on the bridge were a “fair response” to scores of Russian attacks on Ukrainian bridges. He said Putin “doesn’t want to stop the war” and is looking to escalate.

The missile attack came hours after two Russian missiles and a suspected drone came down in Poland, in what its prime minister said was likely a provocation timed to coincide with European military exercises in his country for a possible postwar mission in Ukraine. European troops were taking part on Friday in an unprecedented two-week military exercise that began on November 9 in south-eastern Poland, preparing for a possible postwar mission aimed at helping to stabilise Ukraine and rebuild its military. NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte, speaking to reporters in Warsaw on Saturday, called the airspace violation “dangerous” and said all allies stood “in full solidarity with Poland.”

Meanwhile, a Russian cargo ship bound for Kaliningrad caught fire in the Gulf of Finland on Saturday after it was hit by drones, Finnish and Russian officials said. The Finnish prime minister warned that the war was spilling into the region. In Russia, local officials said on Saturday that Ukrainian drone attacks wounded three people in the Bryansk and Belgorod border regions.

The attacks came after a busy week of diplomacy, including a meeting between US President Donald Trump, China’s Xi Jinping and Russia’s President Vladimir Putin at a summit in Shenzhen, China, on Thursday. Trump described Putin as a “friend” when they met, but in a CNN interview afterwards, Zelenskyy said: “For us, he is an enemy. And of course, for us, he is a killer.” He warned that describing the Russian leader in friendly terms could mean “he doesn’t feel enough pressure.”

The three-way meeting was followed by a large “Coalition of the Willing” gathering in Berlin, attended by European leaders as well as US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner. In a joint statement afterwards, the group of key Ukrainian allies declared they were ready to join direct negotiations with Moscow, while German Chancellor Friedrich Merz stressed that Russia’s “maximalist demands are not realistic.” Zelenskyy on Saturday described the meeting between Putin, Trump and Xi as “very unsuccessful” for the Russian leader, who he said had failed in his goal of trying to recreate a Yalta-style conference at which Allied powers divided up the postwar world in 1945. “So, the plan to redraw the world’s borders didn’t work out… I think the summit was a major failure for Russia,” he said.

Why it matters: a strike that kills a seven-year-old boy on Children’s Day, wounds 60 people and deliberately hits the rescuers who come to help is a measure of how the war is being fought in its fifth year — against cities, hospitals and the people who answer the first call. It landed in the president’s own hometown on the same night Russian weapons fell in Poland and drones hit a Russian ship in the Gulf of Finland, a reminder that the war keeps spilling beyond Ukraine’s borders even as Washington tries to add momentum to talks to end it. The diplomacy continues — in Berlin, in Shenzhen and in the negotiations the allies say they are ready to join — but the strikes on Kryvyi Rih show what the fighting looks like while the talking goes on.
