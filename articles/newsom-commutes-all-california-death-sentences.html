Newsom Commutes All 548 California Death Sentences in Largest Mass Clemency in US History

politics · 5 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

About a third of the commutations take effect immediately; the rest need approval from the California Supreme Court.

California Governor Gavin Newsom announced on Thursday he is commuting the death sentences of all 548 people on the state's death row to life in prison without the possibility of parole, in the largest mass commutation in American history, the Death Penalty Information Center said.

California has the largest death row in the Western Hemisphere, with 529 men and 19 women currently sentenced to death, according to the governor's office. The move cuts the total number of people sentenced to death across the United States by nearly a third.

The commutations do not repeal the death penalty, which California voters chose to keep in 2012 and 2016, and prosecutors can still seek death sentences in new cases. Newsom imposed a moratorium on executions shortly after taking office in 2019, and California has not carried out an execution since 2006.

About a third of the commutations take effect immediately. The remaining two-thirds — cases involving people convicted of more than one felony, about 382 people — require approval from the California Supreme Court under the state constitution, and Newsom submitted those cases to the court on Thursday.

In an open letter to Californians, Newsom wrote that the death penalty was unjust and error-prone. "But as Governor, I have concluded the state cannot morally kill them," he wrote, adding that offenders could now die in obscurity while their victims are remembered and honoured.

Among those on death row are serial killers Randy Kraft and Charles Ng, and Richard Allen Davis, who kidnapped and murdered 12-year-old Polly Klaas in 1993, CNN reported.

Newsom, a Democrat who is expected to seek his party's presidential nomination in 2028, is nearing the end of his second and final term as governor, which ends in January, Reuters reported.
