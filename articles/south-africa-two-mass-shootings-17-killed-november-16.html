Two Mass Shootings in South Africa Leave 17 Dead

world · 16 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Seventeen people were killed in two separate shootings in South Africa on Sunday night, police said on Monday — eight in a bullet-riddled minibus outside Pietermaritzburg and nine in a shack west of Pretoria.

Seventeen people were killed in two separate shootings in South Africa on Sunday night, police said on Monday, in the latest outburst of gun violence in a country with one of the world's highest murder rates. Eight men were found dead in a bullet-riddled minibus outside Pietermaritzburg in KwaZulu-Natal province, and hours later nine people were found shot dead inside a shack in an informal settlement west of Pretoria. Police say the two incidents are unrelated, and no motive has yet been established for either attack.

Police in KwaZulu-Natal said they responded to reports of a shooting late on Sunday and found a white seven-seater vehicle riddled with bullet holes in Sweetwaters, outside Pietermaritzburg, with eight men inside. Officers declared them dead when they arrived at the scene. Provincial police spokesperson Colonel Robert Netshiunda said some families had come forward to help identify the victims, but that had yet to be confirmed. “It’s still too early to tell what the motive could have been and who could be behind the shooting because we are yet to identify the victims,” he said.

In the second attack, police found the bodies of six men and three women inside a four-room shack in the Jeffsville informal settlement in Atteridgeville, west of Pretoria. Police said the shooting took place between 10pm and 11pm on Sunday, and officers were alerted at about 07:30 on Monday morning. “On arrival, members found the bodies of nine people, six males and three females, inside the four-roomed shack with gunshot wounds,” said police spokesperson Brenda Muridili. The case is being investigated by Gauteng province’s serious and violent crimes unit, and no arrests have been made.

The two crime scenes are about 530 kilometres (330 miles) apart, and police have said the attacks are not connected to each other. Neither set of victims has been publicly identified, and no suspects have been named in either case.

Gauteng Police Commissioner Tommy Mthombeni said the Jeffsville killings could be related to a previous shooting at a nearby informal settlement in Saulsville last year, in which 12 people were killed and 13 injured. He said one of the people killed in Jeffsville was a “person of interest” in that earlier shooting. “We cannot rule out that they may be linked, and may possibly have been a retaliation attack,” Mthombeni said. He added that police had made a breakthrough in the Saulsville case and arrested one suspect believed to be one of the mass shooters.

Gauteng Premier Panyaza Lesufi visited the Jeffsville crime scene with police on Monday. He said the provincial government was coordinating with other provincial departments to support the police in their investigations of similar crimes happening in the province, which includes the city of Johannesburg.

Several mass shootings linked to criminal gangs have occurred in South Africa in the past year. In September, three gunmen barged into a small house near the coastal city of Durban and opened fire on people attending a party, killing at least 11, including a pregnant woman. In the same month, 17 people were killed and several others wounded when gunmen armed with AK-47 rifles stormed a tavern and opened fire at patrons outside the bar before moving inside and continuing to shoot.

In June, multiple shooters killed 12 people in a poor neighbourhood in Johannesburg in an attack police linked to criminal gangs, and in the same month an armed gang opened fire in another poor Johannesburg neighbourhood, killing 12 people and wounding at least nine before fleeing in a minibus. Police said they had arrested three people in that shooting and believed it was linked to territorial battles between rival illegal mining gangs. Two mass shootings two weeks apart in December last year killed 21 people, also carried out by multiple gunmen.

The shootings underline the scale of violent crime in South Africa. According to the latest police statistics cited by the BBC, 5,427 people were murdered across the country between April and June 2026, an average of about 60 killings a day. In September alone, 28 people were killed in two separate mass shootings.

Why it matters: two mass shootings in a single night, more than 500 kilometres apart, show that the mass-casualty gun attacks that have recurred through 2026 are not confined to one city, one gang or one dispute. The possible link between the Jeffsville killings and last year’s Saulsville attack points to cycles of retaliation that police have so far been unable to break, even as they report arrests in earlier cases. For a country already recording about 60 murders a day, each new mass shooting deepens the sense that the violence is outpacing the response.

What to watch next: whether police identify the victims and name suspects in either case, and whether the investigation confirms a link between the Jeffsville attack and the Saulsville shooting. Any arrests, and the formal charges that follow, will show whether the police assessment of a retaliation motive holds — and whether the two investigations, now running in two provinces, converge or stay separate.
