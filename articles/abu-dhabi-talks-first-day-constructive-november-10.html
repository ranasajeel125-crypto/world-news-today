First Day of Abu Dhabi Peace Talks ‘Constructive’, Zelensky Says

world · 10 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The delegations discussed humanitarian steps, including prisoner exchanges and the return of Ukrainian civilians and children, as well as security guarantees for Ukraine; the talks resume for a second day on Wednesday.

Russia and Ukraine held their first US-mediated talks in nine months on ending their war on Tuesday, with US Secretary of State Marco Rubio calling the meeting “very productive” and Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky saying the session was “constructive and quite substantive”.

The session in Abu Dhabi, the capital of the United Arab Emirates, lasted four hours, and another is scheduled for a second day on Wednesday. It is the first trilateral meeting since February, with the United States represented by President Donald Trump’s son-in-law Jared Kushner and envoy Steve Witkoff. Neither Mr Rubio nor Mr Zelensky attended the talks.

Mr Zelensky said the talks were largely about humanitarian issues, including prisoner exchanges and returning Ukrainian civilians in captivity, as well as children taken from the country. Security guarantees for Ukraine were also discussed, he added during an appearance at the University of Copenhagen in Denmark.

A senior Ukrainian official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said Ukraine and Russia had talked about a large-scale prisoner swap, although the details will be discussed in the coming days.

Mr Kushner said he believed there were “some good ideas how to bridge” the differences between the two sides. Mr Rubio, speaking in Paris at a meeting of the Group of Seven, urged patience: “There’s a lot of work to be done in that regard. It’s not like something that’s on the verge of being solved in the next 24 hours or anything like that.”

The two sides remain far apart on the key elements of any deal. Russian President Vladimir Putin wants Ukraine to pull its troops back from four regions of Ukraine that Moscow annexed in September 2022 but has never fully captured, and demands that Kyiv abandon its bid to join Nato and accept strict limits on its military. Mr Zelensky rejects ceding territory and any limits on Ukraine’s military or its arrangements with Western partners, which Kyiv sees as essential security guarantees.

There is no sign the fighting is abating. Russia launched 162 drones and a ballistic missile at Ukraine overnight, Ukraine’s air force said, while Russia’s Defence Ministry said its forces had downed 155 Ukrainian drones overnight in eight Russian regions, as well as over Crimea.

The talks took place inside the five-star Emirates Palace Mandarin Oriental in Abu Dhabi, and the negotiations stretched over two days. On the Ukrainian side, the delegation was led by Kyrylo Budanov, who faced Dmitry Minaev, the head of counterintelligence for the Russian security service, the FSB, and a member of the Russian delegation. After four hours of discussion on the first day with no tangible results, Zelensky still welcomed what he called a "constructive" debate, while the Russian side, resistant to a ceasefire demanded by Ukraine and its allies, remained silent.

The meeting was the first trilateral session since February, following security consultations that took place in January and February. The two-day session was the first such meeting in nine months on Russia's full-scale invasion, which analysts and humanitarian organisations say has killed hundreds of thousands of soldiers and thousands of civilians in four and a half years of war. The US administration was pleased simply that the meeting took place after nine months of deadlock.

Zelensky described the day in a post on X after being briefed by his negotiating team: "The trilateral format was constructive and quite substantive. They had time to discuss the need and possible parameters for humanitarian steps." He said the day had begun with a meeting with European and American partners, followed by the trilateral format between the Ukrainian delegation and the American and Russian delegations.

Kushner was upbeat in his opening remarks, saying the goal was to "create a new framework and a new understanding building upon the different institutions and understandings of the past". He added: "We take what's worked, we try to identify what's failed, why we're in this position and hopefully create a new framework that leads us for decades, if not centuries, of peace." After the first day he wrote on X: "We are encouraged by the constructive discussions today." He said Donald Trump was "committed to seeing peace" because the war "has gone on for too long", adding that the president was concerned about the conflict's humanitarian toll. Senior US officials told reporters that Witkoff and Kushner might travel to Moscow and Kyiv to speak directly with Putin and Zelensky.

The two American envoys brought considerable inventiveness to the negotiations. In recent months, the president's son-in-law and his golfing partner had tried to persuade Europe and Ukraine that doing business with Russia — even before the war ended — would be a way to move toward peace. Kushner said on X on Monday that a "comprehensive and lasting settlement will also require direct engagement between Russia and our European partners on the broader security arrangements that affect them."

Europe's role in Abu Dhabi was secondary. European governments had to settle for a secondary role but managed to remind Witkoff and Kushner of their priorities before the trilateral meeting took place: first there must be a ceasefire, then security guarantees for Ukraine to prevent any new Russian aggression, and only after that could there be talk of a broader plan. In Abu Dhabi, Kushner stated that Europe's security was a US responsibility, and Rubio assured that his country's goal was to eventually broaden these discussions.

The second round of talks was scheduled for November 11, with the sides due to discuss security guarantees, Moscow's demands and Ukraine's postwar future. But the gap between the two sides remained wide: Moscow wants Ukrainian troops withdrawn from four regions it annexed in 2022 but never fully captured, an end to Kyiv's NATO bid and strict limits on Ukraine's military, while Zelensky rejects ceding territory and any limits on Ukraine's military or its arrangements with Western partners. With no sign the fighting was abating — 162 drones and a ballistic missile launched at Ukraine overnight, and 155 Ukrainian drones downed over Russia — the talks in Abu Dhabi were a first step after nine months of silence, not a settlement.
