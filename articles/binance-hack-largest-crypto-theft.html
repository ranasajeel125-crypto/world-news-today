Binance Hit in Largest Crypto Hack on Record as $3 Billion Frozen

business · 30 Oct 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (Business Desk)

Blockchain analysts estimate about $5.9 billion was taken — nearly four times the previous record — while Binance says customer losses will be covered.

Binance, the world's largest cryptocurrency exchange by trading volume, was hit on Thursday in what analysts say is the largest crypto theft on record, with an estimated $5.9 billion drained from its hot wallets, according to blockchain analytics firm Chainalysis. Binance itself later put the total impact at around $6.3 billion, and its November incident report put the figure at about $6.34 billion.

The attacker targeted two Binance hot wallets, known as Binance 14 and Binance 16, and moved bitcoin, ether and stablecoins across several blockchains, on-chain data showed. The theft began at 20:05 UTC on 29 October on the XRP Ledger and ended on Ethereum at 23:56 UTC, three hours and 51 minutes later, with most of the value moved out in roughly 20 minutes. In total, the attacker moved assets across ten blockchains. The hacker's wallets at one point held more than $5.7 billion in various cryptocurrencies stolen from Binance, according to Arkham, including nearly $1.5 billion in bitcoin and $900 million in ether. The wallets also held roughly $1.5 billion in USDT and over $800 million in USDC stablecoins. Binance held roughly $144 billion in assets on its platform before the hack, according to DefiLlama data.

Security researchers said the attacker exploited an Ethereum feature that let a wallet hand control to a piece of software, turning it into a sweeper that forwarded incoming funds to the attacker's own addresses. The feature is EIP-7702, introduced in Ethereum's Pectra upgrade in May 2025, which lets an ordinary wallet address delegate its behaviour to a smart contract. A delegation transaction on the hot wallet labelled Binance 14 carried 50 authorisations covering different nonces, one matched, and funds were swept minutes later. Binance's initial investigation suggested the security incident may have involved a compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, which allowed an attacker to gain access to a service container and submit unauthorised withdrawal transactions. Binance said it had found no evidence of any wallet private key leak, and its later incident report said the attack began with a fake job interview that compromised an employee's computer and gave the attackers access to an internal service.

Binance said its security teams were investigating the breach and that user funds are safe. Co-chief executive Yi He said on X that the platform would cover all hacking losses, and that withdrawals were suspended until its environment is confirmed safe. Binance confirmed the security incident and paused withdrawals on several networks, then suspended all deposits and withdrawals while it investigated. The exchange began restoring withdrawals at 06:00 UTC on 1 November, in phases, and said it had covered about 98 per cent of losses by 31 October, paying for the replacement from part of its SAFU emergency fund, part of its futures insurance fund and its own treasury. It took a new proof-of-reserves snapshot at midnight UTC on 31 October and said bitcoin, ether, USDT, BNB, Solana, USDC, XRP, USD1, RLUSD and Zcash were fully backed.

Stablecoin issuers moved quickly to freeze part of the haul: Tether said it froze more than $1.567 billion in USDT, Circle froze around $823 million in USDC, and Ripple froze about $200 million. Binance said on Friday that the verified total frozen had reached $3 billion and could rise further as investigators trace additional assets. The final loss remains undetermined. Tether's chief executive said the company froze about $1.57 billion in USDT connected to the incident, and Circle's chief executive said Circle had frozen over $800 million in USDC in cooperation with law enforcement. Binance's November incident report put the amount frozen or intercepted at about $2.85 billion, a figure subject to reconciliation and price swings. The wallet linked to the exploit also moved assets through the decentralised exchange Uniswap, and the exploiter borrowed at least 20,000 wrapped ether, worth around $44 million, through the decentralised lender Aave using stolen USDT as collateral.

At an estimated $5.9 billion, the theft would be nearly four times the $1.5 billion Bybit exploit of February 2025, the previous record. "This is the biggest crypto hack in history," Ido Ben-Natan, co-founder and chief executive of crypto security firm Blockaid, told CoinDesk. The hack rattled crypto markets, driving down bitcoin and other digital assets, Reuters reported on Friday, and also weighed on shares of rival exchange Coinbase. The hack was the second on Thursday, after prediction market provider Polymarket announced one of its third-party providers had been breached.

Investigators including ZachXBT and security firm Blockaid have suggested North Korea was likely behind the attack, though that attribution has not been officially confirmed. On 31 October, the FBI said the attack was the work of TraderTraitor, the North Korean group it blamed for the Bybit hack, and asked exchanges and analytics firms to block the addresses involved. TRM Labs attributed the attack to TraderTraitor with high confidence, and Chainalysis linked the theft to North Korea. The FBI, the Secret Service and IRS Criminal Investigation said the thieves were converting stolen assets mainly into ether, and expected the funds to be laundered further through decentralised finance and instant swap services. Their alert listed 608 Ethereum addresses, 19 Bitcoin addresses and one Solana address. North Korea denied involvement: on 3 November, Kim Yo Jong, the sister of Kim Jong Un, issued a statement through the Korean Central News Agency denying North Korean involvement and accusing the United States of using the incident against the country's reputation.

The recovery effort continued for weeks. On 13 November, the US Justice Department said it had obtained court authorisation to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion of stolen assets, part of more than $2.7 billion frozen through government and private-sector efforts. On 18 November, Binance said the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts had granted it a worldwide freezing injunction worth up to $6.5 billion against the unidentified people behind the theft, with a separate order directing 18 named companies, including Circle, BitGo and World Liberty Financial, to keep existing freezes in place. The injunction can be served directly through the blockchain, which is a legal first, and is backed by a continuously updated list of about 26,000 wallet addresses linked to the stolen funds. A further hearing was scheduled for 1 December to decide whether the injunction should continue. Three weeks after the breach, investigators reported that about $2.82 billion had been frozen, but only $32 million had actually been recovered — a reminder that freezing stolen cryptocurrency and getting it back are very different things. Binance said it would provide updates through its official channels as the investigation continues.
