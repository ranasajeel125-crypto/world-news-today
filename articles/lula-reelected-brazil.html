Lula Wins Fourth Term as Brazil's President; Bolsonaro Refuses to Concede

world · 26 Oct 2026 · 3 min read · By Rafael Costa (Latin America Correspondent)

Lula, 80, overturned the first-round outcome to win a fourth term as Brazil's president.

Leftist President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva won Brazil's presidential election, the country's electoral authority said on Sunday, defeating right-wing Senator Flavio Bolsonaro in a runoff to secure a fourth non-consecutive term.

Lula, 80, overturned the outcome from the first round of voting, in which Bolsonaro had picked up 47% of ballots cast to Lula's 45%. Lula's fourth term begins in January.

Bolsonaro, the son of former President Jair Bolsonaro, refused to concede defeat. He called into question a late-Sunday decision by a Lula-appointed Supreme Court justice that extended voting by two hours over alleged voter transport irregularities, and said he would ask for the result to be annulled.

“It is impossible to recognize the election results, given the circumstances under which things unfolded,” Bolsonaro told journalists.

Yet the scale of the loss, with Bolsonaro trailing Lula by over 4 million votes, makes it unlikely his efforts to annul the vote will succeed, analysts said. His petition is also undermined by the fact that his campaign also sought a voting extension due to heavy rains in the conservative-leaning south.

Bolsonaro's refusal to concede could spark a wave of protests in a country where faith in the electoral system, and in judicial independence, has been strained in recent years, observers said. Jair Bolsonaro plunged Brazil into a deep democratic crisis after the election in 2022, when his supporters stormed government buildings in protest against the result. He later received a 27-year sentence for plotting a coup.

Leaders across Latin America congratulated Lula on his victory, with Mexico's President Claudia Sheinbaum saying “the people of Brazil won” and Paraguay's President Santiago Pena saluting “this great democratic journey.”
