EU and China Urged to Join Forces on a Carbon Border Levy

climate · 24 Oct 2026 · 3 min read · By Elena Petrova (Climate Correspondent)

China and Europe should jointly introduce a levy on carbon-intensive imports like the EU's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM), Adair Turner, co-chair of the Energy Transitions Commission, told the Bund Summit in Shanghai on Thursday.

Turner said the two should commit to carbon prices high enough to drive decarbonisation of heavy industries such as cement and steel, and argued that China would eventually need to raise its carbon price to around $100 per tonne to fully decarbonise iron production by 2050 or 2060. China's national carbon market closed at RMB 96.66 per tonne (about $15) on Thursday, according to the Shanghai Environment and Energy Exchange.

Jeromin Zettelmeyer, director of the Bruegel think tank, said a joint EU–China pricing commitment would create a market too large for other developing countries to ignore — effectively setting a global floor price for industrial carbon whether Washington joins or not.

The proposal faces obvious obstacles: trade tensions between Brussels and Beijing, disputes over subsidies for Chinese electric vehicles and solar panels, and resistance from developing economies that see carbon border taxes as green protectionism. But with the EU's CBAM entering its definitive phase, the idea of linked carbon markets is moving from conference talk to policy agenda.
