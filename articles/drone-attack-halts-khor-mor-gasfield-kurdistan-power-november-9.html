Drone Attack Halts Khor Mor Gasfield and Cuts Power to Most of Iraqi Kurdistan

world · 9 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Footage verified by Al Jazeera's Sanad agency showed smoke and flames rising from the gasfield in Sulaimaniyah governorate; no casualties were reported.

A drone attack on Iraq’s Khor Mor gasfield late on Sunday started a large fire and halted production at the Kurdistan Region’s biggest source of power-plant fuel, local officials and security sources said.

Two security sources told Reuters that a drone was used in the attack on the field in Chamchamal district, in Sulaimani province. An engineer at the field told the agency that a gas storage facility was hit and that all crew were ordered into shelters in case of further attacks. Kurdistan24 reported that the strike hit the field’s Old Field section, which produces liquefied petroleum gas and had not been targeted before. The attack struck at about 11 p.m. local time on 8 November, hitting a liquefied petroleum gas storage tank, and the regional natural resources and electricity authorities described it as a terrorist attack.

The Kurdistan Regional Government’s electricity ministry said the strike knocked between 2,500 and 2,800 megawatts of generation off the grid, cutting the region’s power capacity by 70 to 80 percent. More than 70 percent of the Kurdish region was left without electricity. Ministry spokesman Omed Ahmad said gas supplies to power plants in Erbil, Sulaimaniyah, Bazian, Chamchamal and Duhok were suspended, and that hospitals and water stations would be given priority as the ministry ran hydroelectric stations and plants burning alternative fuel. Ahmad said the full effect would be felt from midnight, and that normal supply depended on gas production restarting. Gas deliveries to power stations elsewhere in Iraq were also disrupted, and witnesses said the shutdown caused a severe shortage of power as well as LPG cooking gas in key cities in Iraqi Kurdistan.

No casualties were reported among employees or security staff, Kurdistan24 said, and an alarm sounded before the impact, allowing workers to reach shelters. Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi said an initial assessment indicated there had been no casualties, and he ordered a joint investigation with the Kurdish authorities. Firefighters were working to contain the blaze. Dana Gas, the UAE-based company that operates the field, said on Monday that the facility had been targeted overnight, causing a fire and forcing the suspension of gas production, and that the fire had since been extinguished with no injuries to personnel.

It was not clear who carried out the attack or whether it was linked to the United States and Israel’s war on Iran. No group has claimed responsibility. A security source told Rudaw the attack was launched from the disputed town of Tuz Khurmatu, a claim that has not been independently confirmed. Two Iraqi security sources told Reuters that preliminary information indicated the drone was launched from areas east of Tuz Khurmatu, around 80 kilometres southwest of Khor Mor, and that an ongoing investigation had so far ruled out the possibility that it originated in Iran. Al Jazeera’s fact-checking agency Sanad verified footage of smoke and flames rising from the gasfield in Sulaimaniyah governorate.

By Thursday, the Kurdistan Region had restored enough electricity generation to supply residents with 1,500 megawatts, raising daily public electricity supply to six to eight hours, the electricity ministry spokesperson said. Authorities had increased generation from approximately 1,000 megawatts immediately after Sunday’s attack to 1,550 megawatts, of which 50 megawatts are reserved for essential facilities and factories,. Even so, the electricity available covers only 40 per cent of the region’s demand, leaving a 60 per cent shortfall, and no date had been established for production to resume. The attack is a major setback for the KRG’s Runaki initiative, which had brought round-the-clock electricity to 85 per cent of the Kurdistan Region before the strike,.

Khor Mor is operated by Dana Gas and Crescent Petroleum through the Pearl Petroleum consortium. The field has been hit repeatedly: a drone strike on 26 November 2025 set a storage tank on fire and halted supplies to power plants, and an April 2024 attack killed four foreign workers. Sunday’s attack came after a lull of nearly four months, and a Kurdish official said in July that the field had been hit 12 times during the war that began in February. At least eleven attacks on the field were documented between 2022 and 2025, making Sunday’s strike the twelfth or more in that series. A drone attack in January 2024 temporarily halted production, an attack on 2 February 2025 caused no casualties or damage, and in July 2026 Dana Gas suspended operations at the field for about 10 days after receiving security threats, saying it would resume production once the risks had subsided. In April 2024, a drone strike using a Shahed-101-type drone killed four Yemeni workers and wounded eight more.

The field is one of the largest in the Kurdistan Region and the main domestic source of gas for the region’s power stations, supplying more than 80 per cent of Kurdistan’s electricity generation needs. It has produced gas since October 2008 and is recognised as Iraq’s largest non-associated gas field, having produced over 500 million barrels of oil equivalent cumulatively as of April 2025. Pearl Petroleum has invested more than $2.1 billion at the site, and in October 2025 the consortium completed its KM250 expansion project, a reported $1.1 billion upgrade that raised processing capacity by 50 per cent to 750 million standard cubic feet per day. Dana Gas and Crescent Petroleum each hold a 35 per cent stake in Pearl Petroleum, while Austria’s OMV, Hungary’s MOL and Germany’s RWE each hold 10 per cent.

The strike comes as the United States enforces a blockade on Iran under Task Force Iron Gate, and days after US strikes on Iran’s Qeshm Island, in a war that has repeatedly spilled over onto energy infrastructure across the region. Kurdish officials have blamed Iran-linked militias for earlier attacks on the region’s energy sites, and the pattern of strikes on Khor Mor has long been read in Erbil as a message that the region’s energy infrastructure, and with it its economic lifeline, is exposed. The attack has also prompted disagreement between Iraqi and Kurdish officials over responsibility for the strike, even as both sides pursue the joint investigation ordered by the prime minister.
