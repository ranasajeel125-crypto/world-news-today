Drone Attack Halts Khor Mor Gasfield and Cuts Power to Most of Iraqi Kurdistan

world · 9 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Footage verified by Al Jazeera's Sanad agency showed smoke and flames rising from the gasfield in Sulaimaniyah governorate; no casualties were reported.

A drone attack on Iraq's Khor Mor gasfield late on Sunday started a large fire and halted production at the Kurdistan Region's biggest source of power-plant fuel, local officials and security sources said.

Two security sources told Reuters that a drone was used in the attack on the field in Chamchamal district, in Sulaimani province. An engineer at the field told the agency that a gas storage facility was hit and that all crew were ordered into shelters in case of further attacks. Kurdistan24 reported that the strike hit the field's Old Field section, which produces liquefied petroleum gas and had not been targeted before.

The Kurdistan Regional Government's electricity ministry said the strike knocked between 2,500 and 2,800 megawatts of generation off the grid, cutting the region's power capacity by 70 to 80 percent. More than 70 percent of the Kurdish region was left without electricity. Ministry spokesman Omed Ahmad said gas supplies to power plants in Erbil, Sulaimaniyah, Bazian, Chamchamal and Duhok were suspended, and that hospitals and water stations would be given priority as the ministry ran hydroelectric stations and plants burning alternative fuel. Ahmad said the full effect would be felt from midnight, and that normal supply depended on gas production restarting. Gas deliveries to power stations elsewhere in Iraq were also disrupted.

No casualties were reported among employees or security staff, Kurdistan24 said, and an alarm sounded before the impact, allowing workers to reach shelters. Iraqi Prime Minister Ali al-Zaidi said an initial assessment indicated there had been no casualties, and he ordered a joint investigation with the Kurdish authorities. Firefighters were working to contain the blaze.

It was not clear who carried out the attack or whether it was linked to the United States and Israel's war on Iran. No group has claimed responsibility. A security source told Rudaw the attack was launched from the disputed town of Tuz Khurmatu, a claim that has not been independently confirmed. Al Jazeera's fact-checking agency Sanad verified footage of smoke and flames rising from the gasfield in Sulaimaniyah governorate.

Khor Mor is operated by Dana Gas and Crescent Petroleum through the Pearl Petroleum consortium. The field has been hit repeatedly: a drone strike on 26 November 2025 set a storage tank on fire and halted supplies to power plants, and an April 2024 attack killed four foreign workers. Sunday's attack came after a lull of nearly four months, and a Kurdish official said in July that the field had been hit 12 times during the war that began in February.

The strike comes as the United States enforces a blockade on Iran under Task Force Iron Gate, and days after US strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island, in a war that has repeatedly spilled over onto energy infrastructure across the region.
