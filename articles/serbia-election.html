Serbia Votes in Tight Race Between Vučić and Student-Led Movement

politics · 25 Oct 2026 · 3 min read · By Elena Petrova (Balkans Correspondent)

The National Assembly in Belgrade. Serbia's vote is being watched closely across Europe.

Serbia voted on Sunday in an election that has turned into a genuine contest, with longtime leader Aleksandar Vučić facing a serious challenge from the student-led anti-corruption movement that has shaken the country's politics.

The student movement grew out of months of protests demanding accountability and an end to corruption, and it has channelled the frustration of younger voters and urban professionals who feel shut out of a system dominated by Vučić's party for more than a decade.

Vučić's campaign leaned on his record of stability, infrastructure building and Serbia's balancing act between the European Union, Russia and China. The opposition argues that stability has come at the price of captured institutions and a pliant media landscape.

Turnout and the count in Belgrade and other big cities are expected to decide the result. Whatever the outcome, the vote marks the moment Serbia's protest generation moved from the streets into the ballot box — and European capitals will be reading the result closely.
