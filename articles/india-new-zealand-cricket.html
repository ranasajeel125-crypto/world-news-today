Varma's Blistering Fifty Powers India to Series Lead Over New Zealand

sport · 24 Oct 2026 · 5 min read · By Ravi Menon (Sport Desk)

India chased 181 with a ball to spare in Christchurch to take a 2–0 series lead.

India chased 181 with a ball to spare in Christchurch to take a 2–0 series lead.

India's middle-order batter Tilak Varma hammered a half-century off 25 balls as India chased down a target of 181 with a ball to spare, taking a 2–0 lead in the five-match Twenty20 series with New Zealand on Saturday, 24 October, at Hagley Oval in Christchurch. The win was India's 200th victory in Twenty20 international cricket, and it came from a position that had looked close to hopeless midway through the chase.

New Zealand captain Mitchell Santner's destructive spell of 4–21 had left India shaken, with 68 runs needed from the last five overs. India had slipped to 70 for five at one stage and then to 112 for six in the 15th over, still 69 runs short. But Varma, unbeaten on 51 from 26 balls with six fours and a six, and Nitish Kumar Reddy, unbeaten on 26 from 13, targeted the pacers in the final three overs, adding 69 runs off 30 balls for the seventh wicket to secure a four-wicket win. It was India's highest seventh-wicket partnership in Twenty20 internationals, breaking the previous record of 63 between Dinesh Karthik and Krunal Pandya.

The finish was a thriller. Nitish lifted India immediately with a six off Jacob Duffy, and Varma followed with two crucial boundaries to bring the equation down to 29 off 12 balls. The penultimate over, bowled by Nathan Smith, effectively sealed the game, as Varma found the boundary three times and cleared the ropes once in an 18-run over. Needing 11 from the final over, Varma slammed one past extra cover and reached his fifty off the next ball, before Nitish ran the double that finished the match with a ball to spare. Varma was named player of the match, having also taken two catches in the field.

'The Hyderabadi boys were in play today… what everyone believed, I have done it, so I'm really proud of myself,' said player of the match Varma. He and Nitish, both from Hyderabad and both Telugu speakers who often use the language to chide each other over running between the wickets, shared a long embrace at the end, with the Indian dugout on its feet.

Earlier, the Christchurch pitch looked slower than in Thursday's high-scoring contest as India's pacers restricted New Zealand to 43–3 during the powerplay. Tim Seifert fell for seven, caught by Varma off Arshdeep Singh, and the hosts were in trouble before Glenn Phillips and Bevon Jacobs turned the innings around, adding 116 for the fourth wicket. Phillips hit 70 from 43 balls, with four boundaries and four sixes, before Arshdeep Singh (2–33) had him caught near the fence, and Jacobs made 58 from 46. Michael Bracewell's 10 off two balls helped the hosts reach 180–5, a total that proved just short.

The result continued a remarkable start to the series for India. In the first Twenty20 international on Thursday, also at Hagley Oval, New Zealand posted 225 for four, their highest Twenty20 total against India on home soil, thanks to an unbeaten 82 from 40 balls by Glenn Phillips and 45 from 24 by Finn Allen. India chased it down in a record run chase, reaching 230 for six in 19.4 overs, with Sanju Samson making 67 from 33 balls, captain Shreyas Iyer 42 and Naman Dhir an unbeaten 42 from 15, to win by four wickets with two balls to spare. That match was played just two days after India had landed in New Zealand, and less than five days after the fifth Twenty20 international of their home series against West Indies, a schedule Iyer said he was not worried about.

The series is part of a 12-match tour of New Zealand by India, and the two sides have built one of the most competitive rivalries in the short format. New Zealand, known as the Black Caps, have long been among the most consistent teams in world cricket despite their small player pool, while India, the game's financial powerhouse, have dominated Twenty20 cricket in recent years with a deep squad of young batters and versatile bowlers. Matches between the two are often decided in the final over, and this series has followed that pattern, with both games in Christchurch going to the last few balls.

For New Zealand, the defeat was a bitter one. Santner's spell of four for 21 had put them in command, and Phillips' form with the bat has been the story of the series so far, with the all-rounder scoring 82 not out and 70 in the first two matches. But their bowlers could not defend 180 on a ground where the boundaries are short and the bounce is true, and the death bowling, in particular, came under pressure as Varma and Nitish found the fence with regularity. The hosts will also rue the powerplay, when they lost three early wickets and were forced to rebuild rather than attack from the start.

For India, the win underlined the depth of their batting. With the top order gone and the middle order under pressure, it was two young players, Varma and Nitish, who finished the job, and Naman Dhir, who made 31 from 17 balls in this match after his unbeaten 42 in the first, has also impressed. The third match of the series is scheduled in Wellington on Tuesday, with India now needing one more win to take an unassailable lead, while New Zealand must win to keep the series alive.
