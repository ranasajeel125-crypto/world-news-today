US to Hold Trilateral Talks With Ukraine, Russia in Abu Dhabi Next Week

world · 3 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Lead US negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are expected to represent the US in Abu Dhabi, the official said.

The United States will bring together delegations from Ukraine and Russia in Abu Dhabi on November 10-11 to discuss ending Moscow's war against Ukraine, post-war security agreements and de-escalation, a senior US official told Reuters on Monday.

The senior US official, speaking on condition of anonymity, said the new Abu Dhabi discussions will cover "post-war security agreements, de-escalation, humanitarian measures, and ending the war." US envoy Steve Witkoff announced the planned meeting on Monday, writing on X that Washington would mediate working-level negotiations between Moscow and Kyiv. The Kremlin confirmed the talks on Tuesday, with spokesman Dmitry Peskov telling reporters that Russian President Vladimir Putin's aide Vladimir Medinsky would lead Moscow's delegation.

The lead US negotiators, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, are expected to represent the US in Abu Dhabi, the official said. The US delegation was also to include General Alexus Grynkewich, commander of US European Command and NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe, who was to lead a military working group on post-war ceasefire monitoring and security arrangements. The Ukrainian delegation was to be led by foreign intelligence chief Rustem Umerov, alongside presidential office head Kyrylo Budanov, first deputy head Serhii Kyslytsia, parliamentary faction leader David Arakhamia and military intelligence deputy chief Vadym Skibitsky. The Russian delegation included military intelligence chief Admiral Igor Kostyukov, and Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov was to take part for the first time.

Russian President Vladimir Putin agreed to a new round of trilateral talks after meeting Witkoff in St Petersburg two weeks ago, in the latest US push to stop Europe's bloodiest conflict since World War Two. US negotiators have been seeking a breakthrough in the long-running conflict before the winter sets in; the last US-mediated peace talks took place in February, before the United States and Israel launched a war against Iran, and the Abu Dhabi round would be the first meeting in this format in nine months.

The US official said American negotiators will meet at the working level this week in Brussels with representatives from France, Germany, Britain, the European Union and NATO to discuss "Ukraine's security guarantees, Europe's future security framework, and economic support for Ukraine's reconstruction." Ahead of the trilateral talks on 10 November, the US and Ukrainian delegations met with representatives of Germany, France, Italy and the United Kingdom, as well as officials from the European Commission, the European Council and NATO. The Kremlin said the talks themselves would not include European officials, a position that appeared to conflict with a statement by French Foreign Minister Jean-Noel Barrot that officials from France, Germany, the United Kingdom, the European Commission and NATO would be present, although an EU official said national security officials for the bloc would be on site without it being clear how they would be involved.

European governments were anxious about the format. Representatives of the E3, Germany, France and the United Kingdom, travelled to Abu Dhabi to speak to the American mediators, fearing that Washington might offer further concessions to the Kremlin in an effort to secure a hasty settlement. They told Witkoff and Kushner that there must first be a ceasefire, then security guarantees for Ukraine to prevent any new Russian aggression, before wider discussions on European security architecture. In recent months, Witkoff and Kushner had tried to persuade Europe and Ukraine that doing business with Russia, even before the war ended, would be a way to move toward peace, an approach that alarmed Kyiv's allies.

The official said the meetings build on recent discussions among the parties and "reflect President Trump's continued efforts to reach a lasting peace agreement between Russia and Ukraine." Ukraine and Russia remain far apart when it comes to the terms of any possible peace deal, though Trump has recently intensified pressure on Ukraine to strike some kind of a deal. US officials downplayed expectations for a breakthrough, saying the talks aimed only to map out positions and build a framework for future negotiations, while Moscow continues to demand the entire Donetsk region. The talks were to run on two tracks: the military working group on ceasefire monitoring and security arrangements, which had made significant progress during the last round in February, and a political working group on the most sensitive topic, territorial demands.

When the talks took place, they produced no breakthrough on ending the war, but they did yield a concrete humanitarian outcome. After a four-hour session on Tuesday and a session of just under two hours on Wednesday inside the Emirates Palace Mandarin Oriental hotel, Russia and Ukraine agreed in principle to a large exchange of prisoners of war. Kushner called it the biggest humanitarian release of prisoners in the war, saying the numbers would be finalised the following day, and both Medinsky and Umerov confirmed the planned exchange. The talks were largely about humanitarian issues, including prisoner exchanges, the return of Ukrainian civilians held in captivity and children taken from the country, and the delegations also discussed an energy truce and unblocking the Black Sea. Zelenskyy later said the ambitious goal was to exchange everyone for everyone, or at least a large number of people, and that after a meeting in Moscow, Witkoff and Kushner had agreed with Putin on an exchange of more than a thousand prisoners from both sides, with the final number to be 1,200 or 1,300.

On 12 November, Witkoff and Kushner travelled to Moscow for talks with Putin, before heading to Dublin to meet Zelenskyy and European leaders on the sidelines of the European Political Community summit. Medinsky said European security had been the central issue in the Abu Dhabi talks and that it was essential to establish a new, comprehensive and fair European security system based on Russia's proposals from December 2021. With little territory changing hands on the front line this year and trust between the sides in short supply, the prisoner exchange stood as the one tangible result of the first direct negotiations between Russian and Ukrainian delegations in nine months.
