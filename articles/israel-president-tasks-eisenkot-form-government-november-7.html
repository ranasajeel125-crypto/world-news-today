Israel's President Tasks Opposition Leader Eisenkot With Forming Government After Deadlocked Election

world · 8 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Eisenkot, leader of the centrist Yashar party, has 28 days to assemble a coalition commanding 61 of the Knesset's 120 seats; Netanyahu stays on as caretaker prime minister.

Israel's president on Saturday chose Gadi Eisenkot, Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu's strongest rival, to try to form a new government, following a deadlocked election that left neither man with a clear path to power, Reuters reported.

President Isaac Herzog will formally hand Eisenkot the mandate at a ceremony at the president's residence on Sunday. The former military chief, who leads the centrist Yashar party, will then have 28 days to assemble a coalition, with a possible 14-day extension.

Netanyahu's religious-nationalist coalition lost its majority in the 27 October election but still emerged as the largest bloc, with 53 of the Knesset's 120 seats; his Likud party finished first with 27 seats. Eisenkot's four-party alliance won 48 seats, and two Arab parties won a combined 19 seats, leaving no camp with the 61 seats needed to govern.

In consultations with Herzog, factions representing 67 of the Knesset's 120 members recommended Eisenkot for the first attempt at forming a government, against 46 for Netanyahu. The ultra-Orthodox United Torah Judaism party, which holds seven seats, declined to recommend either man.

The arithmetic behind the recommendation is fragile: 19 of the 67 recommendations came from the two Arab parties, whose support Eisenkot's bloc has refused to rely on for governing, while Eisenkot has repeatedly said he will not serve in a government with Netanyahu.

Netanyahu, Israel's longest-serving leader, remains in office as caretaker prime minister while Eisenkot tries to build a coalition. If he fails, the mandate is expected to pass to Netanyahu for a further 28 days.
