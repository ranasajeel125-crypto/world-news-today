Aramco Tower Hit in Riyadh as Houthis Launch Fresh Strikes on Saudi Capital

world · 8 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Footage authenticated by BBC Verify showed at least one floor of the tower badly hit; Saudi Arabia said the damage was minor and no one was hurt.

A missile and drone attack struck the Aramco Tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District on Sunday morning, blowing out windows across several floors of the 36-storey skyscraper in the heart of the Saudi capital's financial district.

Footage authenticated by BBC Verify showed at least one floor of the tower, also known as Villas in the Sky, appearing to have been badly hit by a missile or drone strike, with windows shattered on more than one level. A Reuters witness reported seeing blown-out windows across several floors of the building.

Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry said an administrative building belonging to state oil giant Saudi Aramco had been subjected to an attempted attack, and that falling shrapnel had caused minor damage to the building, with no injuries or fatalities reported. Officials said the building, which also contains residential units, was empty at the time.

The Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis said its air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched towards Riyadh. It said it also intercepted cruise missiles and a drone over Riyadh and the southern regions, and a ballistic missile aimed at the oil-rich Eastern Province.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group had carried out six operations targeting Aramco facilities, Riyadh airport and Saudi military positions. The Houthi-run Saba news agency confirmed the group had targeted Riyadh's business zone, and the Houthis also said the airport and Aramco oil storage facilities were targeted in separate strikes.

The attack came a day after the Houthi-run health ministry said at least 33 people were killed and 39 wounded in Saudi airstrikes on a village near the Yemeni capital Sanaa on Saturday. Fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis resumed in July after a 2022 UN-brokered truce unravelled, and the United Nations says more than 1,600 people have been killed and more than 300,000 displaced since then.

The strikes were the latest claimed by the Yemen-based armed group against targets deep inside Saudi Arabia, following months of missile and drone attacks on Saudi airports, oil facilities and tankers in the Red Sea. The Houthis say they are retaliating for what they call a Saudi blockade of their territory.
