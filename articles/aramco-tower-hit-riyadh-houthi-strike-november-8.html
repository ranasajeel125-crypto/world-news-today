Aramco Tower Hit in Riyadh as Houthis Launch Fresh Strikes on Saudi Capital

world · 8 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Footage authenticated by BBC Verify showed at least one floor of the tower badly hit; Saudi Arabia said the damage was minor and no one was hurt.

A missile and drone attack struck the Aramco Tower in Riyadh's King Abdullah Financial District on Sunday morning, blowing out windows across several floors of the 36-storey skyscraper in the heart of the Saudi capital's financial district.

Footage authenticated by BBC Verify showed at least one floor of the tower, also known as Villas in the Sky, appearing to have been badly hit by a missile or drone strike, with windows shattered on more than one level. A Reuters witness reported seeing blown-out windows across several floors of the building. An eyewitness told AFP they saw a projectile strike the building, and another witness said multiple floors appeared to have been affected, with shattered glass and damage visible on part of the tower's facade. Multiple loud booms were heard in Riyadh earlier on Sunday, shortly after Saudi Civil Defence issued an early-warning alert.

Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry said an administrative building belonging to state oil giant Saudi Aramco had been subjected to an attempted attack, and that falling shrapnel had caused minor damage to the building, with no injuries or fatalities reported. Officials said the building, which also contains residential units, was empty at the time. The ministry did not name the building or say what kind of weapon produced the debris, and Saudi authorities have not said what struck the tower. The discrepancy between the ministry's account of minor damage and the footage showing at least one floor badly hit was noted in reporting on the attack, and it was not clearly confirmed whether the damage was caused by a direct impact or by debris that fell during the interception. Saudi Arabia's Civil Defence later announced that the danger had passed in Riyadh city and Najran Province, following warnings issued through the National Early Warning Platform. Authorities also warned that filming, publishing or sharing information related to missile and drone interceptions, or their impact sites, could expose individuals to legal liability.

The Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis said its air defences intercepted and destroyed two ballistic missiles launched towards Riyadh. Coalition spokesperson Turki al-Maliki issued four separate updates during the day: a ballistic missile destroyed over the Eastern Province at about 05:26 local time, two ballistic missiles aimed at Riyadh at about 08:30 local time, two cruise missiles over the Riyadh and southern regions about half an hour later, and a drone destroyed over the southern region at about 11:30. It said it also intercepted cruise missiles and a drone over Riyadh and the southern regions, and a ballistic missile aimed at the oil-rich Eastern Province. The coalition said later on 8 November that it had destroyed two ballistic missile launchers in Saada used in the attempted attack on Riyadh and one launcher in Sanaa used against the Eastern Province. These coalition statements have not been independently verified.

Houthi military spokesman Yahya Saree said the group had carried out six operations targeting Aramco facilities, Riyadh airport and Saudi military positions. The Houthi-run Saba news agency confirmed the group had targeted Riyadh's business zone, and the Houthis also said the airport and Aramco oil storage facilities were targeted in separate strikes. In an English-language statement, the group said it had carried out four qualitative military operations using ballistic and cruise missiles and drones, listing targets including the financial centre, Riyadh airport, Aramco oil storage tanks and the eastern Saudi city of Abqaiq. Saree also said the group launched a second barrage against an Aramco facility in Abqaiq. The Houthis claimed responsibility for the attack, and said they were retaliating for Saudi airstrikes in Yemen.

The attack came a day after the Houthi-run health ministry said at least 33 people were killed and 39 wounded in Saudi airstrikes on a village near the Yemeni capital Sanaa on Saturday. The Houthi-run Health Ministry said on Sunday, 8 November, that 33 people were killed and 39 wounded in Saudi-led air attacks on al-Shariya, a village in the Bani Hushaish district east of Sanaa, including eight women and 13 children. The ministry's spokesperson, Anees al-Asbahi, described the figures as close to final, after earlier figures of 22 dead and 30 wounded on Saturday and 27 dead in the Saba news agency's first report. Al-Asbahi said the attacks targeted homes, roads, paramedics and civil defence teams in the village. There was no immediate comment from the Saudi-led coalition on the reported strikes, and the reports could not be independently verified. Saree claimed the coalition had carried out 105 air strikes and missile attacks over 24 hours targeting Sanaa and seven other governorates, taking the total number of strikes since the escalation began to 4,369.

Fighting between Saudi Arabia and the Houthis resumed in July after a 2022 UN-brokered truce unravelled, and the United Nations says more than 1,600 people have been killed and more than 300,000 displaced since then. The region witnessed a deterioration of the truce when Saudi Arabia struck Sanaa International Airport to prevent an Iranian plane from landing; in response, the Houthis targeted Abha Airport in Saudi Arabia as well as ships using the Red Sea as an alternative route for oil exports. The Houthis have escalated attacks on targets in Saudi Arabia since the truce collapsed, striking King Fahd International airport in Dammam, the Ras Tanura and Rabigh oil refineries, and other airports across the kingdom. At least 13 people were killed and more than 300 wounded in a Houthi strike on Riyadh's King Khalid International Airport on 10 October, which forced the suspension of flights for two weeks. The attacks come as fighting intensifies across Yemen between the Houthis and government forces, who launched the Dawn of Yemen operation aimed at retaking Houthi-held territory, including the capital, Sanaa, last month.

The strikes were the latest claimed by the Yemen-based armed group against targets deep inside Saudi Arabia, following months of missile and drone attacks on Saudi airports, oil facilities and tankers in the Red Sea. The Houthis say they are retaliating for what they call a Saudi blockade of their territory. The attack on the Aramco Tower also came hours after the kingdom's education ministry announced, late on Saturday, the return of in-person schooling after weeks of remote learning in Riyadh following an earlier wave of strikes — a sign of how the war, once confined to Yemen's borders, now shapes daily life in the Saudi capital. The King Abdullah Financial District is a major business and commercial hub in Riyadh, with high-rise office buildings, retail outlets, residential facilities and a metro station, and a strike on its most prominent tower was a deliberate choice of target: not an oil field or a military base, but the symbol of the financial centre at the heart of the kingdom's Vision 2030 economic plan.
