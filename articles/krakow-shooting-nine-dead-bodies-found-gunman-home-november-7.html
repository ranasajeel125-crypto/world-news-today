Krakow Shooting Toll Rises to Nine as Three Bodies Found at Gunman's Home

world · 8 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Police said the three bodies found in Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski, about 150 km from Krakow, were two women and a man; the defence minister described the attack as an act of terror.

The death toll from Saturday's mass shooting in Krakow rose to nine after police found three more bodies at the gunman's home, hours after he opened fire in the city's Main Market Square, killing six people including a British citizen, Polish officials said.

Prime Minister Donald Tusk said the bodies of two women and a man were found at the suspect's home in Ostrowiec Swietokrzyski, a city about 150 kilometres (93 miles) northeast of Krakow, and were believed to be members of the gunman's close family.

Six people — four women and two men — were killed in the square, among them three Polish citizens, one British citizen and two people who had not yet been identified, police said. At least 15 people were injured, including two police officers, one of them critically.

The 25-year-old Polish suspect, who was shot and wounded by officers, remains in hospital, police said. Interior Minister Marcin Kierwiński said the man held “very radical” and “ultranationalistic” views, and police said they believed he was inspired by far-right ideology. Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz described the attack as an “act of terror”.

Police said the gunman drove onto the square at around 3 p.m. local time on Saturday and fired at random people from his car, using an automatic weapon and military-style equipment. More weapons and ammunition were found inside his car, and a period of mourning has been declared in Krakow.

Police said they were not looking for a second person. Public shootings are rare in Poland, where gun laws are stricter than in the United States.
