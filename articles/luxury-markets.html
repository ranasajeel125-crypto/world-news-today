Luxury Giants Shed €250bn in Value as Chinese Shoppers Pull Back

business · 25 Oct 2026 · 3 min read · By Daniyal Ahmed (Business Correspondent)

Luxury shopping districts built their boom on Chinese demand. That engine is now sputtering.

The world's luxury groups have lost around €250 billion in value as Chinese consumer demand fades, according to the Financial Times — a brutal correction for an industry that spent a decade assuming China's shoppers would keep spending forever.

Flagship groups built their growth on China's expanding middle class: bigger stores in Shanghai and Beijing, marketing aimed at young Chinese consumers, and product lines designed for the market. A weaker property sector, youth unemployment and shifting tastes have now hit that model all at once.

The pain is not evenly spread. Brands at the very top — those selling scarcity and heritage — are holding up better than labels that leaned on aspirational buyers, who are the first to cut back. Watchmakers and leather-goods houses have reported the sharpest slowdowns.

Executives are responding by courting American and Middle Eastern customers and by rethinking prices after years of aggressive increases. But with China accounting for such a large share of the industry's growth, there is no quick substitute — and investors have repriced the whole sector accordingly.

Source: Financial Times reporting, via the 25 October world brief
