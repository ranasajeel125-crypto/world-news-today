Russian Strikes Cut Power to Hundreds of Thousands in Ukraine as Drones Damage Russian Facility

world · 1 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Ukraine's air force said it shot down 371 of 433 long-range drones launched by Russia since Saturday evening; Russia said it downed 235 Ukrainian drones overnight.

world · 1 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Russian strikes left hundreds of thousands of Ukrainians without power on Sunday, President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said, while Russian authorities reported major damage to an industrial site following Ukrainian drone attacks on Nizhny Novgorod region, Reuters reported.

Even as a six-day local ceasefire held on its final day around the Russian-controlled Ukrainian town of Oleshky, there was no letup in fighting elsewhere along the front line and both sides kept up long-range air attacks.

Zelenskiy said Russia had hit power infrastructure in four Ukrainian regions overnight, cutting electricity to hundreds of thousands of households. The regions were Dnipro, Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr and Khmelnytsky, and the targets were substations, transformers and power lines that provide electricity to people's homes. "The intensity of the strikes is very high, and the Russians are seeking to maximally exploit the deficit of ballistic missile interceptors," he said on Telegram. As of Sunday, hundreds of thousands of families remained without electricity, and the attacks came as cold weather set in across Eastern Europe.

The scale of the air campaign that week was enormous. Zelenskiy said that in the week leading up to Sunday, Russia had launched 1,940 strike drones, more than 1,580 glide bombs and 44 missiles of various types over Ukraine, 36 of which were ballistic. On November 1 alone, Russian forces attacked the country with 433 drones, fired from Oryol and Shatalovo in Russia, as well as from the Russian-occupied region of Donetsk. Ukraine's air force said it had shot down 371 of the 433 long-range drones launched by Russia since Saturday evening. In its morning update, the air force said dozens of Russian drones were still in the air as of Sunday morning, and that six separate drone alerts had been issued in Kyiv since midnight.

He said Ukraine, meanwhile, had hit the Kamensky plant in Russia's Rostov region, which he said was involved in the manufacture of Russian missiles. Moscow did not comment on the reported attack in Rostov. The attack came less than 24 hours after the Ukrainian military said it had targeted the Russian state-owned Kombinat Kamensky chemical and defence enterprise with drones and Neptune coastal missile systems. According to Zelenskiy, a Pantsir-S2 air defence system and a command and staff vehicle of an S-400 Triumf system were also hit, with the total value of the air defence assets struck put at around $70 million.

In Nizhny Novgorod region, Governor Gleb Nikitin said a large-scale overnight Ukrainian drone attack caused "significant damage" to an industrial facility. He did not identify the site that was hit. The region is home to the NORSI oil refinery, one of Russia's largest. Nikitin said more than 100 drones were destroyed in the attack, with no casualties reported so far, and Russia's Investigative Committee opened a criminal case on terrorism charges over the attack. Ukrainian media reports suggested the strike had hit the Lukoil refinery in Kstovo as well as the combined heat and power plant in Novogorkovskaya, although that could not be independently verified.

Overall, Russia's Defence Ministry said its forces had downed 235 Ukrainian drones over Russian regions overnight. Kyiv did not immediately claim responsibility for the Nizhny Novgorod attack. Nizhny Novgorod's regional government also did not identify the facility that was struck, but said it was taking steps to ensure gas stations were supplied with fuel, urging motorists to "buy fuel only as needed and avoid panic buying," Interfax reported. The warning suggests the damaged facility was linked to the region's oil-refining or fuel-supply infrastructure.

On Friday, Russia said Ukraine had resumed strikes on its oil refineries and accused Kyiv of ignoring a request from US President Donald Trump to stop such attacks, which Trump has blamed for raising the cost of diesel before US midterm elections next week. Ukraine's campaign against Russian refineries has become a central part of its strategy, aimed at cutting the fuel supplies and the export revenues that fund Russia's war, and at imposing costs on Moscow for its attacks on Ukrainian cities. Russia, in turn, has made Ukraine's power grid its principal target, in a campaign that has now run for several winters.

Ukraine says it supports a full ceasefire on energy targets, including its own battered power grid, but that Moscow is unwilling to agree to this. In an interview with state TV channel Vesti on Sunday, Russian Deputy Prime Minister Alexander Novak, who oversees the energy sector, did not mention the Nizhny Novgorod strike, but said Ukrainian strikes on Russian refineries were adding to global fuel market imbalances and affecting prices. The exchange of strikes on energy infrastructure, on both sides, has become a war within the war, fought in the dark over power stations, refineries and substations, and paid for by civilians on both sides who lose light, heat and fuel. With winter approaching, and with Ukraine's generation capacity already reduced by almost two-thirds since the full-scale invasion began, the stakes of that campaign are higher than ever.
