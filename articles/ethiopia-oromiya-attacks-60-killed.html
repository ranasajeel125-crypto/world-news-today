At Least 60 Killed in Two Attacks in Ethiopia's Oromiya Region, Orthodox Church Says

world · 28 Oct 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (Africa Desk)

The Ethiopian Orthodox Church said 14 people were killed in Merti Woreda on 20 October and 46 in Sherka Woreda on 24 October; more than 20 homes were burned and more than 20 people were hospitalised with severe injuries.

At least 60 people were killed in two separate attacks in Ethiopia's conflict-hit Oromiya region in the past week, the Ethiopian Orthodox Church said, in one of the deadliest episodes of violence against civilians in the region in recent years.

Church authorities said the dead were all Christians but did not go into details on how they died or who was behind the attacks. The Holy Synod of the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church said 14 believers were killed in Merti Woreda on 20 October and 46 in Sherka Woreda on 24 October, in a statement issued on Monday. The church said 14 people were killed on 20 October in Gora Silingo, Angada Kebele, in Merti district, while another 46 were killed on 24 October in the villages of Tereta, Chole and Birbo in Shirka district, both districts in the Arsi Zone in central Ethiopia's Oromia region, about 270 kilometres south of the capital, Addis Ababa.

More than 20 homes were also burned and more than 20 people were hospitalised with severe injuries, the church's statement added, underscoring the scale of the assaults on rural communities in the Arsi Zone of Oromiya, Ethiopia's largest region. The church described the killings as "extremely tragic cruelty and suffering inflicted on our citizens" and as "brutal and merciless massacres", and said armed men had gone from house to house killing residents with firearms and knives. Elderly people and children were among the victims, and the death toll remains provisional. One church was reportedly damaged, while churches in neighbouring villages provided shelter to people fleeing the attack. The area is inhabited by Muslims, Protestants and Orthodox Christians. A separate count by the conflict monitor ACLED put the number killed in Shirka at at least 43.

The Oromiya regional government and a source linked to a rebel alliance fighting government forces traded blame for the attacks. The regional government said the rebel alliance launched the attacks to try to spark religious conflict, without providing evidence. Oromia regional government spokesperson Hailu Adugna blamed the Oromo Liberation Army, or OLA, for the attacks, and the regional government pledged to pursue those responsible. The source linked to the rebel alliance said the Oromiya regional government had carried out the killings, citing conversations with two local residents. According to information available so far, a rebel coalition identified by Ethiopian authorities as "Tsimido" is believed to be operating in the area, reportedly including the Tigray People's Liberation Front, the Amhara Fano militias and the OLA, which Addis Ababa classifies as a terrorist organisation.

The Oromo Liberation Army rebel group, which the regional government has blamed for previous attacks on Orthodox Christians, did not immediately respond to a request for comment. The OLA is one of seven armed groups that announced last month that they had formed an alliance aimed at overthrowing Prime Minister Abiy Ahmed's government. On 20 September, seven Ethiopian armed groups announced the formation of the Ethiopian Peoples' Forces Alliance for Survival, a coalition comprising the Amhara Fano National Movement, the TPLF, the OLA, the Ogaden National Liberation Front, the Afar Revolutionary Democratic Unity Front, the Benishangul People's Liberation Movement and the Gumuz People's Democratic Movement. The alliance, whose founding statement was read by Fano commander Zemene Kassie on Tigrai TV, pledges to overthrow the Abiy government and establish an inclusive transitional government.

The alliance brings together movements with very different enemies, territorial claims and histories, and analysts question whether seven commands can fight as one. The TPLF dominated Ethiopian politics for nearly three decades before Abiy took office, and fought a devastating war with the federal government in 2020-2022; Fano factions stood with the federal government against the TPLF during that war and have fought federal forces in Amhara since 2023; the OLA has fought federal forces in Oromia since 2018. The Ogaden National Liberation Front has denied joining the coalition. Three days after the alliance was announced, Tigray Defence Forces launched an offensive in Tigray, Afar and Amhara, seizing the airports of Mekelle, Axum and Shire. Experts say the alliance has not created a single coherent military force capable of marching on Addis Ababa, but that its claim to speak for seven peoples can delegitimise Abiy abroad and raise the price of any settlement.

The competing claims leave the question of responsibility unresolved, and the church has called for the perpetrators to be held to account. In an unprecedented intervention, the Holy Synod demanded stronger government action to defend worshippers and provide "essential security until the problem is resolved", and on 27 October called on Christians worldwide to pray for the victims. Catholic bishops in Ethiopia warned that continuing conflict is deepening divisions and threatening religious coexistence. An Ethiopian rights group is cited as counting more than 400 people killed in the area in about four years. The attacks add to a long-running pattern of insecurity in Oromiya, where government forces have fought the OLA insurgency for years and civilians have repeatedly borne the brunt of the violence, and where the church says it has repeatedly raised the issue with authorities with no solution.
