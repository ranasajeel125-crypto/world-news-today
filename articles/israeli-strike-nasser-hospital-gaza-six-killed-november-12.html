Israeli Strike on Gaza’s Nasser Hospital Kills Six, Including Surgeon

world · 12 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis is one of the few functioning medical facilities left in Gaza; Reuters video showed extensive damage inside the surgery department.

An Israeli airstrike hit a building at Nasser Hospital in Khan Younis, in southern Gaza, shortly after midnight on Thursday, killing at least six people, including a surgeon, and wounding more than 20 others, Gaza's civil defence service and health ministry said. The strike hit the hospital's surgery department and caused extensive damage, with the hospital's director later putting the number of wounded at 28.

Mahmoud Basal, the civil defence spokesman, said the strike hit the surgery department and caused extensive damage. Medics identified the dead surgeon as Rezik Abushira, described by colleagues as a significant and prominent surgeon who had featured in a 2026 documentary. Gaza's health ministry gave the same death toll to the Associated Press and said a doctor was among the dead. A 13-year-old boy, named by the hospital as Jihad Abu Teilkh, was also among those killed. Most of those killed and injured were patients, including people who had been scheduled to undergo surgery the following day, according to Al Jazeera's correspondent in Gaza.

The hospital's director, Dr Ahmed Al-Farra, said the bombing targeted the Al-Yasin building of the medical complex, which housed operating rooms, radiology departments, laboratories and surgical inpatient units, and that several essential departments had ceased operations until further notice. He said the hospital had been struck with two missiles, but that one had failed to explode. Reuters video from inside the hospital showed rubble and furniture strewn across rooms, with wires dangling from damaged ceilings, and video verified by Storyful showed debris littering the floors and pipes hanging from ceilings. A British surgeon volunteering at the hospital, Goher Rahbour, told AFP that the strike occurred just after midnight and shook the whole building.

The Israeli military later said it had targeted a senior Hamas militant and other members of the group's armed wing who it said were operating inside the hospital. In a joint statement, the military and the Shin Bet security service said the target was Muhammad Abu Ismail, commander of Hamas's Khan Younis Brigade, along with additional militants hiding inside the hospital complex. They said Abu Ismail was responsible for managing the brigade's activities and directing attacks against Israeli troops and civilians, and that he had been responsible for holding numerous hostages captive during the war.

The military said Hamas had established a presence within the hospital complex and was using it as a command centre, storing weapons there, operating command-and-control rooms and using hospital facilities as observation posts and shelters for its operatives. It said the group's internal security and intelligence services had conducted interrogations and torture inside the complex, and that it had used precision munitions and aerial surveillance to reduce the risk of harm to civilians. The Wall Street Journal reported that the strike came without an evacuation warning. Reuters could not independently verify the Israeli military's allegations.

Nasser Hospital is the largest hospital complex in southern Gaza and one of the few functioning medical facilities left in the territory, where the health system has been badly damaged by the war. Hospitals have been a flashpoint throughout the conflict, and the health ministry warned that restrictions on the entry of medical supplies and essential equipment were endangering the hospital's continued operation.

The strike was reported amid a ceasefire that took effect on 10 October 2025, after two years of an Israeli offensive in Gaza triggered by the attack carried out by Hamas against Israel on 7 October 2023. Both sides have accused each other of violating the ceasefire. More than 1,590 Palestinians have been killed since the ceasefire began, according to Gaza's health ministry, with other tallies putting the figure above 1,600. The dawn attack followed a series of deadly strikes on Wednesday that killed five Palestinians in Gaza City and the Bureij refugee camp, while a sixth person succumbed to injuries from an earlier raid, and on Tuesday Israeli forces killed seven people, including a 13-year-old boy, while the military said it had targeted military operatives. At least ten Palestinians were killed in Israeli attacks across Gaza on Thursday in total.

Reactions were swift. Hamas called the strike a war crime and held the Board of Peace responsible for failing to prevent it, while a senior Hamas official, Basem Naim, said the board was providing cover for Israel's actions. The Board of Peace said it deplored the death of any civilians and strongly condemned what it called Hamas's continued exploitation of hospitals and other critical infrastructure for military purposes. The Gaza health ministry described the strike as a war crime and called on the international community to intervene and protect Gaza's health system.

Two days later, the foreign ministers of 18 nations, including the United Arab Emirates, Saudi Arabia, Qatar, Egypt, Jordan, Türkiye, Pakistan and Indonesia, condemned the airstrike in the strongest terms, saying attacks on medical facilities violate international humanitarian law, which affords special protection to medical facilities and healthcare workers. They also condemned continued Israeli attacks on Palestinian civilians, including strikes on tents and other sites sheltering displaced people, and said the attacks undermine the ceasefire and efforts to restore security. The ministers called on Israel to uphold the ceasefire, protect civilians and medical facilities, and ensure the sustained and unimpeded delivery of humanitarian aid, including shelter and medical supplies, as winter approaches. The United Nations had made an emergency appeal for waterproof tents to shelter hundreds of thousands of people exposed to flooding from heavy rains, and the ministers warned that inadequate shelter and deteriorating water, sanitation and healthcare services put civilian lives at grave risk, particularly those of children and infants.
