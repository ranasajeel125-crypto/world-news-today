Yankees Beat Dodgers in Mumbai as Baseball Meets Cricket

sport · 25 Oct 2026 · 5 min read · By Ravi Menon (Sport Desk)

Mumbai's cricket fans packed the stands at the Andheri Sports Complex on Saturday to watch Indian World Cup-winning captain Suryakumar Yadav swing a bat — just not the kind they are used to.

The occasion was MLBx: Mumbai, Major League Baseball's Indian debut, held on 24 October 2026 at the Mumbai Football Arena in the Andheri Sports Complex. The one-day event introduced audiences to baseball through a distinctly Indian lens, blending the sport with live music, interactive fan experiences and ballpark-inspired food, and drawing thousands of fans to a celebration that extended well beyond the boundaries of the game.

Indian cricketers Suryakumar Yadav, Shardul Thakur, Deepak Chahar and Radha Yadav lined up alongside ex-Major League Baseball players Nick Swisher, Adrian Gonzalez, Jake Arrieta and Jonny Gomes in a three-on-three tournament pitched as a bridge between the two bat-and-ball games. They were joined by fellow Indian cricketers Jitesh Sharma and Kanika Ahuja, and by professional softball athletes Jordan Woolery and Rachel Garcia. Each team mixed a baseball veteran, an Indian cricketer and an American softball player, with scoring that rewarded both muscle and precision: two points for a home run, four for hitting the target zone, one for a catch, and totals doubled by five consecutive out-of-the-park hits. Each player had one two-minute at-bat during a three-inning game, while the opposing team's three players set up in the outfield.

The four teams carried the names of some of the most historic franchises in Major League Baseball: the New York Yankees, the Los Angeles Dodgers, the Boston Red Sox and the Chicago Cubs. The semifinals offered a thriller and a stroll, as Gonzalez's Los Angeles Dodgers overturned the Shardul-led Chicago Cubs' 44 runs with a minute to spare, while Suryakumar's New York Yankees cruised past the Boston Red Sox. The Yankees' 59-40 semi-final win was powered by a standout round from Swisher, who scored 25 points during his at-bat. That set up a rematch of the 2024 World Series — this time in India — with the Yankees posting 78 and holding off the Dodgers to be crowned champions. Softball player Rachel Garcia was named the tournament's most valuable player.

Before the final, Suryakumar Yadav of the Yankees and Jitesh Sharma of the Dodgers settled who would bat first with a quick game of rock-paper-scissors. Yadav won the exchange and chose to bat first. Swisher proved instrumental once again, delivering in the closing seconds to help the Yankees claim the championship with 78 points against the Dodgers' 59. Suryakumar's celebrations spilled into the stands as he placed the gold chain he won around his wife's neck after the win.

The event was the centrepiece of MLB's push into India, a country where cricket dominates the sporting landscape but where baseball has almost no professional presence. Suryakumar, who guided India to victory at this year's T20 World Cup on home soil, kicked off his role as MLB India Brand Ambassador at the 2026 MLB All-Star Week in Philadelphia, where he first met Swisher. The former outfielder said that at the moment he knew Suryakumar had something special, and he showered praise on the former India T20 captain after the Mumbai event.

Suryakumar said he is confident baseball will grow in India: "You must be strong from head to your lower body, have a good core and strong arms. If you want to hit 450 feet, 500 feet, you got to be strong. Work hard. No substitutes." He also said cricketing skills can translate to success in the American sport, and the format was designed to prove the point. Cricket and baseball share a family resemblance — a bowler or pitcher delivering a ball, a batter trying to hit it, fielders trying to catch it — but they demand different techniques. A cricket bat is flat and broad, a baseball bat is round and narrow, and a baseball is smaller and harder than a cricket ball. The Mumbai format stripped baseball down to its most spectacular elements, power hitting and athletic fielding, so that cricketers could compete on near-equal terms with professionals.

The 2024 World Series rematch gave the event a ready-made storyline. The Dodgers beat the Yankees in the 2024 World Series to win the championship, so a Yankees victory in Mumbai offered a small measure of revenge, even in an exhibition format. The crowd responded to the names: the Yankees and Dodgers are two of the most famous teams in world sport, and their rivalry is one of the oldest in American baseball.

Beyond the action on the field, the evening unfolded as a lively cultural experience. Live hip-hop performances, including from Badshah and KR$NA, interactive batting and pitching challenges, larger-than-life baseball installations and ballpark-inspired food gave fans ways to experience baseball culture in the heart of a cricket city. From watching cricket legends play baseball to swinging for the first time, catching a ball in the crowd or joining in live shows, MLBx: Mumbai combined sport, music, cuisine and culture in a single unified experience.

For MLB, the logic of the event is straightforward. India has more than a billion people, a huge young audience and a deep love of bat-and-ball sport, but baseball has barely registered there. By borrowing cricket's biggest stars, staging the event in Mumbai's sporting heartland and packaging it as entertainment rather than a straight sporting contest, MLB hopes to plant a seed that could grow into playing participation, broadcast audiences and eventually a commercial market. Whether that seed takes root will depend on what follows: coaching programmes, school competitions and regular broadcasts. But on one Saturday night in Andheri, with a World Cup-winning cricket captain in pinstripes and a packed stand cheering a home run derby, baseball had its first real moment in India.
