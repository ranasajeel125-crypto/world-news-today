Trump, Xi and Putin Hold First-Ever Trilateral Summit in Shenzhen and Issue Joint ‘Peace Declaration’

world · 19 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The 90-minute meeting at Kylin Villa in Shenzhen ended with a joint declaration that a nuclear war ‘cannot be won and must never be fought’ — but named no nuclear cap and did not mention Ukraine.

US President Donald Trump, Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin held the first-ever trilateral meeting of the leaders of the world’s three biggest nuclear powers on Thursday, ending 90 minutes of talks in Shenzhen with a four-point joint declaration on nuclear risks, energy supplies and United Nations reform — but no agreement on the wars in Ukraine or Iran.

The three leaders met at Kylin Villa, a secluded government retreat in Shenzhen, on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit. Together the United States, China and Russia oversee the world’s largest nuclear arsenals, and the meeting was the first time the three leaders had ever sat down together.

In the joint declaration, published by the Kremlin after the talks and described by the leaders as a ‘peace declaration’, the three countries committed to promoting reform of the United Nations, to increasing the production and supply of refined petroleum products, and to upholding the principle of national consent and respect for the immunity of state officials, saying the International Criminal Court and similar bodies ‘must not abuse their jurisdiction’.

On nuclear weapons, the declaration reaffirmed that ‘a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought’, and said the three countries would engage in dialogue on reducing nuclear risks and preventing nuclear proliferation. It named no cap on arsenals and set no timetable.

What the declaration did not do was settle any of the conflicts the leaders had come to discuss. It did not explicitly mention Ukraine, and it announced no agreement on Ukraine or Iran, despite the wars dominating the agenda of the day’s separate bilateral meetings.

Trump opened the trilateral session by saying the three leaders were ‘making history’, and made an early push for denuclearisation. He added that a second meeting would follow later, saying: ‘We’ll have a second meeting later on, as you probably know, but we will have this right now, and it’ll be great.’

Before the trilateral, Trump held a bilateral meeting and working lunch with Xi. He raised the war in Ukraine almost immediately, telling Xi: ‘We’re looking to both help with respect to the war with Russia and Ukraine. I’d like to see it ended. I think I can speak for both of us very much.’ Xi’s opening remarks made no mention of Ukraine. The two leaders also discussed trade and critical minerals, and Trump suggested the United States, Russia and China could pursue nuclear arms reductions, though he offered few details on how such an initiative might work.

Trump also held a separate one-on-one meeting with Putin during the day, though US and Russian officials had said no full-scale bilateral negotiations were planned for it. Earlier, the two men had shared a brief handshake at a photo opportunity in Shenzhen, where they were placed on either side of Xi; Russia’s Interfax news agency said they did not have a substantive conversation at that point.

Xi called during the trilateral talks for a complete ban on and elimination of nuclear weapons, Russia’s RIA Novosti news agency reported.

After the talks, Trump boarded Air Force One and departed Shenzhen for Washington, bringing to an end a two-day visit to China that had also included the APEC leaders’ summit. He returns to a political landscape reshaped by sweeping Democratic victories in the midterm elections, Reuters noted.

Expectations for a major breakthrough from the trilateral had been low before it began. Analysts quoted by Reuters said the three leaders had plenty of reasons to appear together but far fewer to agree on substance, with different priorities: Trump pressing for limits on nuclear weapons, Xi preferring to avoid the subject while China expands its arsenal, and Putin looking for an off-ramp from the war in Ukraine on favourable terms.

The declaration’s language on the immunity of state officials is likely to draw attention because the International Criminal Court has issued an arrest warrant for Putin over alleged war crimes connected to the war in Ukraine. The three leaders’ statement that the court must not abuse its jurisdiction sits alongside their pledge to uphold strategic stability and international security.

What happens next: the leaders pledged to keep talking about reducing nuclear risks and preventing proliferation, and Trump said a second trilateral meeting would follow, though no date was announced. Attention will now turn to whether the dialogue produces anything concrete — on nuclear arsenals, on energy supplies disrupted by the war in Iran, or on the wars the declaration left unmentioned.

Why it matters: no three leaders have ever met in this format before, and the symbolism alone marks a shift towards great-power diplomacy conducted over the heads of allies and smaller states — Ukraine and European governments were not at the table. The declaration’s pledges are broad and non-binding, and the absence of any deal on Ukraine or Iran shows the limits of the format. But the first-ever meeting of the leaders of the world’s three largest nuclear powers, ending with a joint statement that a nuclear war must never be fought, is a moment the rest of the world will be measuring for months.
