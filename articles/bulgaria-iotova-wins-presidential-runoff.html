Iotova Wins Bulgaria's Presidential Runoff, Rival Concedes

world · 2 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Initial results put Iotova on about 58% to Gyurov's roughly 38%; turnout was estimated at 27-29%, up from the record-low 23% of the first round.

Acting President Iliana Iotova is the apparent winner of Bulgaria's presidential runoff, according to initial results of the vote count on Sunday, with former interim Prime Minister Andrey Gyurov conceding defeat, the Associated Press reported.

According to counts by several polling agencies, Iotova received about 58% of the vote, against roughly 38% for Gyurov. If confirmed by official results, she would become Bulgaria's first elected female head of state. Official results were not expected before Monday.

“This is how the Bulgarian citizens have voted,” Gyurov said as he conceded, adding that the results had to be accepted as they stood.

Flanked by Prime Minister Rumen Radev, a jubilant Iotova declared victory, saying her message had focused on “incomes, peace and the challenges that lie ahead”. European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen congratulated Iotova and said she looked forward to continuing joint work “for a strong Bulgaria in a strong Europe”.

The vote was the first major test for Radev's Moscow-friendly Progressive Bulgaria party, which endorsed Iotova. Iotova, 62, took over the presidency earlier this year from Radev, who stepped down to run in parliamentary elections, and is set to become the first elected woman president of the country of 6.4 million people.

The race for the largely ceremonial post — which still carries roles including commander-in-chief of the armed forces — pitted Iotova, an ally of Radev who favours dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Ukraine, against Gyurov, a banker and former caretaker prime minister backed by the pro-Western opposition.

Exit polls put voter participation at about 27 to 29%, slightly higher than the first-round race last week, which had a record-low turnout of 23%. In the first round, Iotova won about 35% of the vote, with Gyurov on about 32%, a much narrower gap than the 20-point lead opinion polls had predicted for Iotova.

The election was held against a backdrop of a series of drone attacks on commercial vessels in the Black Sea that have raised security concerns in the EU and NATO member state.

Source: Associated Press
