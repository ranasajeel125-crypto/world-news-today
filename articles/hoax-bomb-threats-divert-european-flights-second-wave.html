Hoax Bomb Threats Divert European Flights for Second Time in a Week

world · 6 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

An Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome was diverted to a French military air base; no explosives were found on any plane searched.

Airlines across Europe received a new wave of bomb threats on Thursday, the second this week, forcing flights to make unplanned landings and others to be escorted by fighter jets, though none of the threats has proved genuine, Reuters reported.

At least 21 aircraft were targeted on Thursday, the BBC reported, with some counts putting the figure higher. An Aer Lingus flight from Dublin to Rome, carrying 151 passengers and six crew, was diverted to the Evreux-Fauville air base in Normandy after a suspected bomb threat and landed safely.

Italian fighter jets escorted a Finnair flight from Helsinki to Rome, which landed as scheduled, while Bulgarian jets escorted three Ryanair flights bound for Sofia. A Wizz Air flight from Milan to Warsaw was rerouted to Vienna, and Czech police said four planes heading to Prague had been targeted. Flights bound for Austria, Belgium, Bulgaria, the Czech Republic, France, Italy, Poland and Romania were affected.

No explosive device has been found on any aircraft searched this week, and no one has been injured. The British government called the incidents "malicious hoax threats", and threats received by Heathrow and Birmingham airports were assessed as not credible.

Some of the threat emails were signed by a group calling itself the "Fighters of the Male State", threatening to "turn the skies red". European officials warned there is currently no evidence linking the group to the threats, and no one has claimed responsibility. Officials have not attributed blame to any state or organisation.

The disruption follows a first wave on Tuesday, when at least seven flights and a cruise ship were targeted, and a Monday incident in which a Wizz Air flight from London to Sofia was diverted to Budapest after a bomb threat. No explosives were found in any of those cases either.

Air France-KLM chief executive Ben Smith said on Thursday he had never previously seen threats reported in such a coordinated fashion. Aviation security experts say the apparent coordination and timing of the current incidents is unusual, though bomb threats against planes are not new.
