Two Major Kyiv Bridges Hit in Russian Attack, Mayor Says

world · 17 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Two major bridges over the Dnipro River in Kyiv were struck in a Russian missile attack late on Monday, the city’s mayor said, damaging critical infrastructure and cutting heating and water in parts of the capital.

Russian missiles struck two major bridges over the Dnipro River in Kyiv late on Monday, the city’s mayor said, damaging critical infrastructure and cutting heating and water supplies in parts of the Ukrainian capital.

Mayor Vitali Klitschko said on Telegram shortly before midnight local time that, according to preliminary information, the capital’s North and South bridges had been hit. The post came shortly after the Ukrainian air force warned of incoming missiles. Loud explosions were heard across the capital, and Klitschko later said critical infrastructure systems had been damaged and that heating and water supplies in some districts were affected.

Some of Kyiv’s above-ground subway services were damaged in the overnight attacks, officials said, after civilians were told to go to civil defence shelters. City officials had not given a casualty figure in the hours after the attack, and the full extent of the damage to the bridges was still being assessed. Reuters witnesses in Kyiv reported a series of loud explosions during the attack, and Reuters said it could not immediately independently confirm the reports of the bridge strikes.

The strikes were part of a broader wave of Russian drone and missile attacks across Ukraine overnight. Explosions were reported in Smila, Kharkiv, Zaporizhzhia, Dnipro, Cherkasy and Pavlohrad, and three people were injured in Smila, according to the regional authorities. DTEK, Ukraine’s largest private energy company, said separately that an attack on energy facilities had left Dnipro completely without electricity. Russia’s defence ministry said its forces had carried out a major attack using high-precision weapons launched from land and air, as well as drones, on Ukrainian military facilities and infrastructure.

Eight major bridges span the Dnipro River, connecting the western and eastern parts of Kyiv, and an estimated three million residents rely on them to travel across the city. The bridges have been targeted before: Russian forces launched a series of attacks on bridges crossing the Dnipro earlier in November and during October, and a massive Russian strike hit Kyiv’s Northern bridge on 7 November.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy had warned earlier on Monday that Ukrainian intelligence expected Russian strikes on Monday evening and through Tuesday, and urged people to heed air raid alerts. The reported attacks on Kyiv’s bridges came during that period.

The strikes came a day after Ukraine launched what Moscow described as its largest recorded drone attack on Russia since the start of the war. Kyiv also struck the Kerch Bridge, the landmark crossing linking Russia to occupied Crimea, for a second consecutive day on Tuesday, according to the region’s Moscow-installed governor, who said traffic had been suspended. On Monday, a Ukrainian drone crashed into one of the bridge’s arches.

The exchange comes as diplomacy continues in parallel. US envoy Jared Kushner said last week that Russia and Ukraine had agreed to expand the next round of peace talks to include European nations, and that Washington was working towards what he called the war’s “largest prisoner exchange”. The 37th meeting of the Ukraine Defence Contact Group, in the Ramstein format, is set for Tuesday at NATO headquarters in Brussels, convened by the UK and Germany.

The campaign against Ukraine’s infrastructure has run alongside the talks. Russia pounded Ukraine’s power grid as US-backed talks opened in Abu Dhabi on 10 November, and Ukraine fired nearly 1,200 drones at Russia on Friday in one of its biggest attacks of the war. Moscow holds a fifth of Ukraine, and Kyiv refuses to give up the rest of its eastern Donbas region — the territorial question on which the talks have repeatedly stalled.

The timing matters for the diplomacy as much as for the fighting. The Ramstein-format meeting in Brussels on Tuesday brings together the allies who supply Ukraine’s air defences, and the bridge strikes will be the freshest evidence in the room of what those defences are up against. At the same time, the prisoner-exchange track that Mr Kushner described last week is the one part of the process where both sides have kept moving, with the Abu Dhabi talks producing agreement on a swap. The question for the coming days is whether the strikes harden positions or push both sides back to the table.

What happens next: engineers will assess whether either bridge can carry traffic, and Kyiv’s heating and water networks will be tested as temperatures fall. Zelenskyy’s warning runs through Tuesday, and Russia’s defence ministry has shown no sign of pausing the campaign against Ukraine’s infrastructure.

Why it matters: the bridges are not only crossings but the seams of the capital — the links between its two halves, its power and water networks, and its evacuation routes. Striking two of them in one night, as winter approaches, is a blow aimed at the city’s ability to function rather than at a single military target. And the timing, a day after Ukraine’s biggest drone attack on Russia and hours before allies meet in Brussels, suggests both sides are still trying to shape the war from the air while the talks meant to end it wait for a date.
