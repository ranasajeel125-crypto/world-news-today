Appeals Court Again Rejects Trump Bid to Block More Than 20,000 California Ballots

politics · 30 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

In a 2-1 decision, the 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals declined to block the ballots from being counted while the Justice Department's lawsuit against California proceeds.

A federal appeals court on Friday rejected the Trump administration's latest attempt to prevent California from counting mail ballots from more than 20,000 registered voters the federal government alleges are likely noncitizens, handing the administration its second defeat this week.

In a 2-1 decision, a panel of the San Francisco-based 9th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals declined the Justice Department's request to block the ballots from being counted while its lawsuit against California proceeds, leaving California's existing ballot-counting procedures in place just days before the 3 November midterm elections.

U.S. Circuit Judges Mark Bennett, a Trump appointee, and Gabriel Sanchez, appointed by President Joe Biden, pointed to a lower court's concerns about the reliability of the federal government's voter list. 'There is a material risk that granting the United States its requested relief would result in disenfranchisement,' Bennett and Sanchez wrote.

The ruling marks the second time this week that the 9th Circuit has rejected an emergency request from the Trump administration involving the ballots. The Justice Department returned to the appeals court after U.S. District Judge David O. Carter denied its request for a temporary restraining order on 28 October, saying the proposed order would 'wreak havoc and disrupt the election'.

The dispute stems from a Justice Department lawsuit filed on 15 October seeking to require California and Los Angeles County election officials to separate ballots connected to more than 20,000 voters the federal government identified as likely noncitizens. The department wanted those ballots held apart and not counted until officials verified that the voters were U.S. citizens. California officials have disputed the federal government's evidence and maintained that the state already has safeguards against noncitizen voting.

Friday's decision does not end the underlying lawsuit, but it leaves the disputed ballots subject to California's normal election procedures as voting continues toward Election Day. Separately, Los Angeles County election employees have received federal grand jury subpoenas ordering them to appear in court on 4 November, the day after Election Day, and provide documents connected to the dispute.
