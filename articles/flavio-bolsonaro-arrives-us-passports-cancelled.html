Defeated Candidate Flávio Bolsonaro Arrives in US After Brazil Cancels His Passports

world · 28 Oct 2026 · 4 min read · By World News Today (Americas Desk)

Bolsonaro, 45, lost Sunday's presidential run-off to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and had said he would stay in Brazil to lead the opposition; his exact route out of the country remains unclear, and neither he nor his brother Eduardo could be reached directly for comment.

Defeated Brazilian presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro has arrived in the United States, sources told the BBC, hours after Brazil cancelled his passports on Tuesday when he missed a deadline to hand them over to the authorities.

The 45-year-old senator left Brazil after the Supreme Court ordered him on Monday to surrender his passports within 24 hours as part of an investigation into alleged money laundering. Bolsonaro has not been charged and denies the allegations, and at the time he said he had no plans to leave the country, insisting he would stay in Brazil to lead the opposition to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, who won Sunday's run-off.

Sources told BBC Brasil and BBC Mundo that Bolsonaro met his younger brother, Eduardo Bolsonaro, in Florida, though his exact travel route from Brazil to the United States remains unclear. CNN separately reported that a person familiar with the situation said Bolsonaro had arrived in the US on Tuesday afternoon, and that President Donald Trump had approved allowing him entry, with the US issuing visas to Bolsonaro and his family.

Trump indicated overnight that he would agree to Bolsonaro coming to the US, saying: “Well, I would approve. I think they’re fine people. The election was very strange, and I thought it was terrible.” It remains unclear whether Trump and Bolsonaro spoke directly before his departure.

The route out of Brazil is disputed. Argentina's immigration ministry said early on Wednesday, Argentine time, that Bolsonaro and his family had entered Argentina from Brazil late on Tuesday local time and shortly after boarded a flight to Guayaquil in Ecuador, according to its flight plan — after Argentina had initially said it had no record of him entering the country. Federal officers in Brazil had checked border posts to see whether he had crossed into Argentina.

When the deadline passed without him appearing, Bolsonaro's own lawyers told BBC Brasil they were “completely in the dark” about his whereabouts. Lula reacted sharply at an event in Brasília, saying the act of leaving Brazil was “shameful” and that his rival could have stayed to fight his accusers in court. Lawmakers from Lula's Workers' Party have pressed for his detention, while his party leadership said it was surprised by the departure, having expected him to lead the opposition after winning roughly 60 million votes.

Bolsonaro had initially refused to concede Sunday's result, saying his team would seek annulment, though his Liberal Party later said it recognised the result. His departure mirrors his father Jair Bolsonaro's move to Florida after losing the 2022 election, and it leaves Brazil's opposition without its defeated standard-bearer days after a run-off decided by about 4.4 million votes.
