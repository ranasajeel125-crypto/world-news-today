Defeated Candidate Flávio Bolsonaro Arrives in US After Brazil Cancels His Passports

world · 28 Oct 2026 · 7 min read · By World News Today (Americas Desk)

Bolsonaro, 45, lost Sunday's presidential run-off to Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva and had said he would stay in Brazil to lead the opposition; his exact route out of the country remains unclear, and neither he nor his brother Eduardo could be reached directly for comment.

Defeated Brazilian presidential candidate Flávio Bolsonaro has arrived in the United States, sources told the BBC, hours after Brazil cancelled his passports on Tuesday when he missed a deadline to hand them over to the authorities.

The 45-year-old senator left Brazil after the Supreme Court ordered him on Monday to surrender his passports within 24 hours as part of an investigation into alleged money laundering. Bolsonaro has not been charged and denies the allegations, and at the time he said he had no plans to leave the country, insisting he would stay in Brazil to lead the opposition to President Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva, who won Sunday's run-off.

Sources told BBC Brasil and BBC Mundo that Bolsonaro met his younger brother, Eduardo Bolsonaro, in Florida, though his exact travel route from Brazil to the United States remains unclear. CNN separately reported that a person familiar with the situation said Bolsonaro had arrived in the US on Tuesday afternoon, and that President Donald Trump had approved allowing him entry, with the US issuing visas to Bolsonaro and his family.

Trump indicated overnight that he would agree to Bolsonaro coming to the US, saying: “Well, I would approve. I think they’re fine people. The election was very strange, and I thought it was terrible.” It remains unclear whether Trump and Bolsonaro spoke directly before his departure.

The route out of Brazil is disputed. Argentina's immigration ministry said early on Wednesday, Argentine time, that Bolsonaro and his family had entered Argentina from Brazil late on Tuesday local time and shortly after boarded a flight to Guayaquil in Ecuador, according to its flight plan — after Argentina had initially said it had no record of him entering the country. Federal officers in Brazil had checked border posts to see whether he had crossed into Argentina.

When the deadline passed without him appearing, Bolsonaro's own lawyers told BBC Brasil they were “completely in the dark” about his whereabouts. Lula reacted sharply at an event in Brasília, saying the act of leaving Brazil was “shameful” and that his rival could have stayed to fight his accusers in court. Lawmakers from Lula's Workers' Party have pressed for his detention, while his party leadership said it was surprised by the departure, having expected him to lead the opposition after winning roughly 60 million votes.

Bolsonaro had initially refused to concede Sunday's result, saying his team would seek annulment, though his Liberal Party later said it recognised the result. His departure mirrors his father Jair Bolsonaro's move to Florida after losing the 2022 election, and it leaves Brazil's opposition without its defeated standard-bearer after a run-off decided by about 4.4 million votes.

The journey, pieced together from flight-tracking data, immigration records and official statements, was as dramatic as the election that preceded it. It started on Sunday with his refusal to concede in the run-off, and culminated in his arrival in Miami via Argentina and Ecuador aboard a private jet on Wednesday — less than three days after his election defeat.

On Monday, Bolsonaro posted a video on social media saying federal police had requested he turn in his passports following an order from Supreme Court Justice Alexandre de Moraes. He was given 24 hours to surrender his passports, but the documents were cancelled after the deadline lapsed, two sources said. In a court ruling seen by Reuters, the Supreme Court cited the risk of Bolsonaro fleeing Brazil and his "significant financial resources" as reasons to seize his passports. The ruling relates to a money laundering probe in which he denies wrongdoing, and the court also ordered police to investigate alleged foreign funding of his campaign and other interference from abroad. Bolsonaro, who loses his immunity from prosecution when his Senate term ends in February, said on Monday he had no plans to flee.

On Tuesday, reports of his departure began to fly. At 1:00 a.m., Argentine news site Infobae reported that Bolsonaro had left Brazil for Argentina and planned to continue to the US aboard a private jet waiting at Puerto Iguazu airport near the border. At noon, Bolsonaro missed the deadline to surrender his passports; although his whereabouts were unknown, federal police sources told Reuters the documents had been cancelled. A police source told AFP that Bolsonaro's Brazilian and diplomatic passports had been cancelled, adding that "there is no formal record of Flavio Bolsonaro leaving the country". At 2:15 p.m., CNN Brasil reported, citing Bolsonaro allies, that the senator had travelled to the US. At 2:30 p.m., a US official told Reuters that Bolsonaro was on a plane bound for the US. At 6:05 p.m., a senior US State Department official said Bolsonaro and his family would be allowed to enter the country and would receive visas. "The State Department will issue visas to Flavio Bolsonaro and his family and allow them entry into the United States," the official said.

In fact, Bolsonaro was still in Brazil while the world speculated about his whereabouts. He only crossed the border into Argentina at 01:50 GMT on Wednesday, according to the neighbouring country's immigration authorities — over 12 hours after the first reports emerged that he had left. Argentina's migration authority said Bolsonaro and his family had entered the province of Misiones late on October 27 using valid travel documents, crossing the Tancredo Neves Bridge in Puerto Iguazu, and later boarded a private Baires Fly flight bound for Guayaquil, Ecuador.

Flight-tracking data filled in the rest. At 12:22 a.m. on Wednesday, a Baires Fly Gulfstream G400, registration LV-JQW, departed Puerto Iguazu. At 5:46 a.m., the aircraft arrived in Guayaquil, where it made a brief refuelling stop at the virtually empty airport. At 6:22 a.m., the plane departed Guayaquil for Miami's Opa-locka Executive Airport, according to flight-tracking firm FlightAware, and AFP reporters watched the aircraft take off again. At 10:31 a.m., the aircraft arrived in Miami, and Reuters TV images showed Bolsonaro disembarking along with his wife and two daughters. He wore a black shirt and carried a backpack as he stepped off the plane at Miami-Opa Locka Executive Airport. He entered the United States with his cancelled diplomatic passport, according to the Department of Homeland Security website that tracks border controls. Bolsonaro also holds an Italian passport.

Trump said on Wednesday that he approved of Bolsonaro coming to the United States after losing Sunday's presidential election, describing him and his family as "fine people". Upon his arrival in Miami, the Department of Homeland Security granted him one-year humanitarian parole, authorising Bolsonaro and his family to remain in the United States through October 2027. In a letter after his arrival, he thanked Argentina's President Javier Milei for his support.

The departure threatens to deepen a rift between the two most populous nations in the Americas. Lula reacted sharply, saying the act of leaving Brazil was "shameful" and that his rival could have stayed to fight his accusers in court. Lawmakers from Lula's Workers' Party pressed for his detention, while his party leadership said it was surprised by the departure, having expected him to lead the opposition after winning roughly 60 million votes. His departure mirrors his father Jair Bolsonaro's move to Florida after losing the 2022 election — Jair is now under house arrest for attempting to overturn his own electoral loss to Lula four years ago. Flavio Bolsonaro, who had said he would stay in Brazil to lead the opposition, is now in the United States, close to the Trump administration, attacking Brazil and saying he is a persecuted person trying to portray himself as a victim of Brazilian persecution.
