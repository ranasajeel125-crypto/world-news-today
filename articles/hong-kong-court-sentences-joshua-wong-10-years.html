Hong Kong Court Sentences Joshua Wong to 10 Years on National Security Charge

world · 5 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Wong pleaded guilty in September to conspiring in 2020 to ask foreign countries to impose sanctions or blockades on Hong Kong and China.

Activist Joshua Wong, 30, who became the international face of Hong Kong's democracy movement, was sentenced on Thursday to 10 years in jail on a charge of collusion with foreign entities under the city's national security law.

Wong was accused of conspiring with self-exiled activist Nathan Law and others in 2020 to request that foreign countries, institutions, organisations or individuals outside China impose sanctions or blockades, or engage in other hostile activities directed against the governments of Hong Kong and China.

He pleaded guilty to the charge in September.

High Court Judge William Tam told a packed courtroom that the punishment must fit the gravity of the crime, Reuters reported.

The offences took place between July 1 and November 23, 2020, a time when Hong Kong was facing a high risk to its rule of law, the judge said.

Wong rose to prominence as the teenage face of Hong Kong's 2014 pro-democracy demonstrations and has been in detention since November 2020. He was already serving a prison term of four years and eight months imposed in November 2024 for conspiracy to commit subversion, and had been due for release in January 2027.

The national security law was imposed on Hong Kong by Beijing in 2020 after sometimes violent demonstrations, and has effectively outlawed the protest efforts of Wong and other young activists.

The sentence was handed down by High Court judge Tam Yiu-ho, a national security judge appointed by Hong Kong Chief Executive John Lee. The judge read out his decision for about an hour and a half — far longer than the 30 minutes estimated in the scheduling announcement — while Wong remained standing. Standing in the courtroom as his new sentence was announced, Wong shook his head slightly and made notes, but appeared calm.

Judge Tam ruled that Wong's offence was of a "grave nature" under the national security law, which carries a minimum penalty of 10 years in jail and up to life imprisonment. Colluding with foreign forces is one of the four major offences established by the law. The judge set a starting point of 13 years, then reduced the sentence to 10 years in recognition of Wong's guilty plea. He described Wong and Law as "advocates at the forefront colluding with foreign forces".

The court found that Wong and fugitive former lawmaker Nathan Law Kwun-chung had partly contributed to the imposition of sanctions and adverse measures against mainland and Hong Kong officials, which significantly undermined national security. Prosecutors said Wong, Law and others had persistently colluded with foreign countries, actively lobbied the United States to pass legislation concerning Hong Kong, and openly solicited foreign countries to impose sanctions against China and Hong Kong.

Chief Executive John Lee welcomed the sentence, saying in a statement: "Wong Chi-fung, Law Kwun-chung and others persistently colluded with foreign countries or external elements, actively lobbied the United States to pass so-called legislation concerning Hong Kong, and openly solicited foreign countries to impose sanctions against China and Hong Kong."

Wong was charged in June 2025 with conspiracy to collude with foreign forces to endanger national security, and he pleaded guilty in the High Court on September 2. The charge was his second case under the Beijing-imposed national security law. The offences were said to have taken place between July 1 and November 23, 2020, a period when Hong Kong was facing a high risk to its rule of law, according to the judge. After the national security law came into force, the judge said, Wong and his conspirators "continued to promote such calls not only through their own social media accounts but also via prominent news media or outlets in the West, reaching both local and international audience."

The new sentence means Wong, who turned 30 last month, faces the prospect of entering middle age in prison. He had been scheduled to complete his sentence of four years and eight months for conspiracy to commit subversion on January 7, 2027. The court ordered two months and two days, representing the remaining time Wong is to serve in that earlier case, to run concurrently with his latest jail term, meaning his expected release date is pushed back to November 2036, when he turns 40. He has already spent almost six years behind bars over various offences.

Wong first led student protests as a teenager and became internationally renowned during the huge protests that convulsed Hong Kong. He rose to prominence as the teenage face of the 2014 pro-democracy demonstrations, and was a visible figure during the protest movement of 2019. He was the former secretary-general of Demosisto, a political party that has since been disbanded. He has been in detention since November 2020.

His earlier conviction came in the city's biggest national security case, linked to an unofficial primary election held by the pro-democracy camp. Of the 47 activists charged in that case, most pleaded guilty while 14 were convicted and sentenced to up to 10 years in prison, after a 2024 trial that marked the single largest prosecution of pro-democracy activists under the law.

The national security law was legislated by the National People's Congress in Beijing and imposed on Hong Kong on June 30, 2020. It led to the arrests of hundreds of pro-democracy politicians, civil activists, journalists and students, the shuttering of vocal news outlets and the dissolution of civic groups. Critics say the law has been used to dismantle the city's once-thriving pro-democracy movement, while the authorities say it was necessary to restore order after the sometimes violent demonstrations of 2019.

Ahead of the sentencing on Thursday morning, police had heightened security outside the court near Hong Kong's financial district, where dozens of people had camped out — some for two nights — to catch a glimpse of one of the city's most famous prisoners. The case is the second national security conviction against Wong, and the 10-year term is among the longest handed down under the law.
