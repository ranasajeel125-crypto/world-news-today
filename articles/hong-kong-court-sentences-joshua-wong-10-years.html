Hong Kong Court Sentences Joshua Wong to 10 Years on National Security Charge

world · 5 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Wong pleaded guilty in September to conspiring in 2020 to ask foreign countries to impose sanctions or blockades on Hong Kong and China.

Activist Joshua Wong, 30, who became the international face of Hong Kong's democracy movement, was sentenced on Thursday to 10 years in jail on a charge of collusion with foreign entities under the city's national security law.

Wong was accused of conspiring with self-exiled activist Nathan Law and others in 2020 to request that foreign countries, institutions, organisations or individuals outside China impose sanctions or blockades, or engage in other hostile activities directed against the governments of Hong Kong and China.

He pleaded guilty to the charge in September.

High Court Judge William Tam told a packed courtroom that the punishment must fit the gravity of the crime, Reuters reported.

The offences took place between July 1 and November 23, 2020, a time when Hong Kong was facing a high risk to its rule of law, the judge said.

Wong rose to prominence as the teenage face of Hong Kong's 2014 pro-democracy demonstrations and has been in detention since November 2020. He was already serving a prison term of four years and eight months imposed in November 2024 for conspiracy to commit subversion, and had been due for release in January 2027.

The national security law was imposed on Hong Kong by Beijing in 2020 after sometimes violent demonstrations, and has effectively outlawed the protest efforts of Wong and other young activists.
