G20 Foreign Ministers Skip Group Photo in Atlanta Amid European Objections Over Russia

world · 30 Oct 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Ministers, including Russia's Sergei Lavrov, began the first session without having posed for photographers.

Foreign ministers from the G20 skipped a traditional group photo ahead of their meeting in Atlanta on Friday after European representatives objected to the presence of Russia.

The US State Department agenda for the meeting did not include the so-called family shot, and ministers — including Russia's Sergei Lavrov — began the first session without having posed for photographers.

"In no case will there be a family photo with European partners and Mr. Lavrov together because ... there is no normalcy with Russia," German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul told reporters earlier on Friday.

European officials had earlier suggested there might be two separate pictures — one with Lavrov and one without.

European G20 members back Ukraine, which has objected to US President Donald Trump's recent decisions on Russia, including a deal to buy diesel and waive some sanctions.

The decision to drop the photograph was taken by US Secretary of State Marco Rubio, who was chairing the two-day gathering. According to US officials, Rubio concluded that the dispute over the picture risked becoming a distraction from the substantive talks, and he decided to forgo the photo altogether rather than allow a ceremonial moment to dominate the opening of the meeting. One US official said the photograph was cancelled after it became clear that some delegations were unwilling to take part in a joint picture, adding that Washington preferred to keep the focus on the discussions themselves.

The scale of the objection was considerable. Two Western officials said more than a third of the attending officials had threatened to walk out of a picture with the Russian foreign minister. A Western diplomat said a third of the participants, including Australia and Canada, had made clear they opposed being photographed alongside Lavrov. Polish Foreign Minister Radoslaw Sikorski called the cancellation the right decision, telling reporters in Atlanta that he was not the only minister who did not want to be photographed with Lavrov.

Before Rubio intervened, officials had explored a compromise. According to three sources familiar with the discussions, the United States had considered arranging two family photographs — one including Lavrov and one without him. The idea was floated by European officials earlier in the week, but it was ultimately abandoned when Rubio decided to scrap the picture entirely.

Lavrov himself dismissed the controversy at a press conference in Atlanta later on Friday. "If what matters most to them is how they look in the family photo and who is standing next to them, well, that says it all," he told reporters. He arrived in Atlanta ahead of the talks, and Moscow said on Thursday that he would use his speech to argue against international sanctions. Lavrov remains under Western sanctions imposed after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

The row over the photograph exposed the deep divisions running through the G20. Rubio opened the talks with an appeal for unity, telling his colleagues: "We are facing a series of interconnected economic and geopolitical crises." He described the moment as critical, but diplomats who attended the opening session said there was little sign that the ministers could reach agreement on many of the most contentious issues before them.

The wars in Ukraine and in Iran overshadowed the gathering, and the talks were taking place just days before the US midterm elections on November 3. Ukraine was not invited to the Atlanta meeting, and European members have objected to recent decisions by President Trump on Russia, including sanctions waivers allowing Russia to export diesel to ease fuel prices ahead of the midterms. A diplomatic source said Russia's presence at the G20 "in no way legitimises its actions".

The divisions extended beyond the photograph. Diplomats said the foreign ministers were expected to end the two-day meeting on Saturday without reaching consensus on several critical global security issues, and the group would not release a joint communique. Instead, Rubio was expected to release a chairman's statement summarising the talks at the conclusion of the meeting. Participants described the opening session as cordial, but said Russia's position on Ukraine was unyielding, as was its opposition to Poland's membership of the G20.

Lavrov also said Russia saw no point in inviting Poland to join the G20 meeting, adding that the country "adds nothing" to the grouping. Sikorski, whose country is a strong ally of neighbouring Ukraine, had already made his own position clear. Britain's Foreign Secretary Ed Miliband also spoke out, saying that if ministers were serious about their values and interests they must work harder to secure a just and lasting peace.

The Atlanta talks were the last ministerial meeting of the US G20 presidency before leaders meet in Miami on December 14 and 15. The leaders' summit, which will bring together leaders from 55 nations and organisations, is due to be hosted by President Trump at his golf club in Doral, Florida. The foreign ministers' meeting was meant to set the stage for that summit, but the absence of consensus in Atlanta suggested the leaders' gathering would face the same fault lines.

The G20 brings together the world's largest economies, and its foreign ministers' meetings have traditionally ended with a family photograph symbolising a shared commitment to dialogue, even when members disagree. European leaders have opted not to appear in photographs with Russian delegations at earlier G20 meetings this year too, as fighting continues after Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine. The decision in Atlanta to abandon the picture altogether, rather than stage two separate versions of it, underlined how far that practice has hardened into an open refusal.

For the host country, the episode was an awkward start to a meeting intended to demonstrate American leadership of the group. Rubio's choice to cancel the photograph avoided a public walkout, but it also meant the opening images from Atlanta showed ministers arriving separately for the first plenary session, with Lavrov seated alongside the foreign ministers of Saudi Arabia and Turkey, rather than standing together as a group. The substantive agenda — the wars in Ukraine and Iran, global energy prices, and trade — remained on the table, but the first day's headlines belonged to a photograph that was never taken.
