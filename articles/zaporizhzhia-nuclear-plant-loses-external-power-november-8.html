Zaporizhzhia Nuclear Plant Loses External Power Again, Runs on Diesel Generators

world · 8 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The International Atomic Energy Agency said the plant's fuel reserves can run a limited number of emergency diesel generators for about two weeks; Ukraine's nuclear operator warned a prolonged blackout risks a radiation accident.

Ukraine's Zaporizhzhia nuclear power plant lost all external electricity early on Sunday, leaving emergency diesel generators to cool its reactors and run other essential safety systems, the plant's management and the UN's nuclear watchdog said.

The 330 kV Ferosplavna-1 transmission line, the plant's only remaining connection to the grid, was disconnected at 02:36 on Sunday after protective systems shut it down because of damage, the Russian-installed management of the plant said. An attempt to reconnect the line automatically failed.

Backup diesel generators started as designed and are supplying power to the equipment required for safe operation of the station, the management said in a statement posted on the Russian state-backed messenger app MAX, adding that radiation levels at the site and in the surveillance zone were normal.

The International Atomic Energy Agency (IAEA), which has a team stationed at the plant, said the cause and location of the latest disconnection are not yet known. The agency said information given to its team showed that existing fuel reserves can run a limited number of emergency diesel generators for about two weeks.

IAEA Director General Rafael Grossi called for the grid connection to be restored and for diesel stocks at the plant to be increased, stressing the urgency of keeping the plant's cooling systems running.

Ukraine's nuclear operator, Energoatom, warned that operating in blackout mode creates a direct risk of failure of life-support systems and nuclear fuel cooling if the diesel generators break down or exhaust their fuel, which could lead to a radiation accident.

It was the 27th complete loss of off-site power at the facility since the conflict began, the IAEA said, and the 16th since the start of 2026, according to Energoatom. The agency said on 23 October that the plant's main Dniprovska power line remained unavailable.

Zaporizhzhia, Europe's largest nuclear power plant, has been under Russian control since March 2022 and produces no electricity. All six of its reactors have been in cold shutdown since 2022, but the plant still needs external power to keep its nuclear fuel cool.

The blackout comes two days before US, Ukrainian and Russian delegations are due to meet in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November for talks on ending the war, and as Russian strikes continue to hit Ukraine's energy infrastructure.
