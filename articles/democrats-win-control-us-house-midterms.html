Democrats Win Control of US House in Midterm Elections, Projected to Retake Chamber from Republicans

politics · 4 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

CNN and Decision Desk HQ projected a Democratic House majority on Tuesday night; control of the Senate had not been projected at the time of the House call.

Democrats will win back control of the US House of Representatives in Tuesday's midterm elections, CNN and Decision Desk HQ projected, ending Republican control of the chamber and setting up divided government for the final two years of President Donald Trump's term.

CBS News projected Democrats would win at least 218 seats — the number needed for a majority in the 435-seat chamber — with Republicans on at least 160, the BBC reported. The final size of the Democratic majority will take longer to settle as counting continues in western states.

The result positions House Minority Leader Hakeem Jeffries to become speaker when the new Congress convenes in January. Democrats pitched the midterm elections as an opportunity to elect lawmakers who would serve as a check on the Trump administration, while the White House has said it expects Democrats to increase oversight of the president's Cabinet.

Among the early flips, Democrat Jamie Ager won North Carolina's 11th Congressional District, the first House seat to change hands in the cycle, and Democrat Rebecca Bennett defeated incumbent Republican Thomas Kean Jr. in New Jersey's 7th District, according to live results coverage.

Control of the Senate had not been projected at the time of the House call. Democrats had also won Senate races in Georgia, North Carolina and New Hampshire, and were running tight races in battlegrounds including Kansas, Texas, Ohio and Michigan, according to live results coverage.

Responding to the Democratic gains on Tuesday night, President Donald Trump said: "I wasn't on the ballot."
