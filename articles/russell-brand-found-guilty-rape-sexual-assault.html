Russell Brand Found Guilty of Rape and Sexual Assault

world · 5 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Brand was remanded in custody and told to expect a significant custodial term; he will be sentenced on Monday, 9 November.

British comedian and actor Russell Brand was found guilty in a London court on Thursday of rape and sexual assault and told he faces jail, Reuters reported.

Brand, 51, was convicted at Southwark Crown Court of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. He was acquitted of two other counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault.

The jury of eight men and four women deliberated for around eight and a half hours before delivering its verdicts.

Brand was found guilty of raping a 21-year-old fan in 2009, days after meeting her at one of his stand-up shows at the Hammersmith Apollo in west London. He was also found guilty of groping an MTV worker at a party in a Soho bar in 2004, sexually assaulting a Capital Radio worker in a corridor in 2005, and sexually assaulting a Channel 4 worker during a forced massage in 2009.

Judge Joel Bennathan, formally Mr Justice Bennathan, told Brand the consequence of the verdicts was a "significant custodial term" and remanded him in custody. Sentencing will take place on Monday, 9 November.

Brand had denied all seven charges, which related to six women and alleged offences between 1999 and 2009, saying the incidents had either been consensual, reinvented or re-imagined. Prosecutors had described the 51-year-old, who starred in the films "Get Him to the Greek" and "Arthur", as a ruthless, sexual predator.

The trial began at Southwark Crown Court on October 12. The case followed a joint investigation by The Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches in 2023, after which police interviewed more than 100 witnesses and reviewed thousands of documents.

London's Metropolitan Police confirmed that investigations are continuing into two further allegations of rape made against Brand, according to Britain's Press Association.

The verdicts came after a four-week trial that attracted massive media attention. The jury of eight men and four women deliberated for around eight and a half hours before delivering four unanimous guilty verdicts. Brand stood straight in the dock as the verdicts were revealed and closed his eyes as he heard he was found guilty of rape. His wife Laura watched from the public gallery, and Brand showed no emotion as the jury delivered the verdicts.

The details of the offences were set out in court. Brand was found guilty of raping a 21-year-old fan in 2009, days after meeting her at one of his stand-up shows at the Hammersmith Apollo in west London. He was also convicted of groping an MTV worker at a party in a Soho bar in 2004 — at a Nordic bar, where he groped her breasts just seconds after being introduced to her — of kissing and groping a Capital Radio worker in a corridor in 2005, and of sexually assaulting a Channel 4 worker during a forced massage in 2009, putting his head into her groin. He was cleared of two other rape charges and one count of attempted indecent assault.

Brand had denied all charges and said he never had sex without consent, telling the court the incidents had either been consensual, reinvented or re-imagined. Prosecutors had described him as a ruthless, sexual predator who "used his influence, status and fame to exploit and sexually offend against women," in the words of London's Metropolitan Police Service after the conviction. Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy, from the Metropolitan Police Service, said the guilty verdict was "first and foremost a testament to the extraordinary courage, resilience and determination of the women who came forward and reported what happened to them."

The trial, which began at Southwark Crown Court on October 12, lasted just over three weeks and featured testimony from six accusers, as well as from Brand himself. The case followed a joint investigation by The Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches in 2023, after which police interviewed more than 100 witnesses and reviewed thousands of documents. Camilla Long, a journalist for The Sunday Times, wrote that sitting in court hearing how Brand "grabbed, pushed, groped and degraded his victims" was one of the most disgusting experiences of her life.

Judge Bennathan refused Brand's request for bail so he could spend the weekend with his three children, and Brand spent his first weekend in a prison cell in London. The judge said the case was "very well prepared", according to Britain's Press Association, and told Brand he faced a lengthy prison term. The maximum sentence for rape in the UK is life, and the average sentence is 10 years; commentators suggested Brand was likely to receive between four and 13 years.

On Monday, November 9, the sentence was handed down. Mr Justice Bennathan sentenced Brand to 10 years and six months in prison, telling him he had acted with "staggering arrogance" and shown "contempt" for his victims, who had suffered significant and lasting harm. The judge said Brand had been a "sexual predator" whose fame had protected him, and told him he had acted "how you liked and when you liked, unhindered by the rules by which decent people live." Brand, who had spent the weekend in prison, was in the suit he was convicted in and sat holding a set of white rosary beads as the victim impact statements were read. The sentence was handed down live on camera. His wife Laura Gallacher, with whom he has three children, slumped in her seat in the public gallery as the total sentence of 10 years and six months was announced. Brand told his wife, who has supported him in court throughout the case, "we'll be OK" as he was led down to the cells.

Brand's career had taken him from stand-up comic and MTV host to a political analyst esteemed by Britain's left, and then to a conspiracy theorist and Christian convert beloved by the US right. He was previously married to US pop star Katy Perry. The conviction ends a long fall from the celebrity status he enjoyed in the early 2000s, when he became a celebrity darling as a raunchy, irreverent comedian.

London's Metropolitan Police confirmed that investigations are continuing into two further allegations of rape made against Brand, according to Britain's Press Association, and he reportedly also faces further accusations that could lead to additional criminal charges. The guilty verdicts, and the 10-and-a-half-year sentence that followed, close the first chapter of a case that began with the 2023 investigation — but for the women who came forward, and for Brand himself, the legal story may not yet be over.
