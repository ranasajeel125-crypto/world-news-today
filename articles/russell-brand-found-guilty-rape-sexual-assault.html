Russell Brand Found Guilty of Rape and Sexual Assault

world · 5 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Brand was remanded in custody and told to expect a significant custodial term; he will be sentenced on Monday, 9 November.

British comedian and actor Russell Brand was found guilty in a London court on Thursday of rape and sexual assault and told he faces jail, Reuters reported.

Brand, 51, was convicted at Southwark Crown Court of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault. He was acquitted of two other counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault.

The jury of eight men and four women deliberated for around eight and a half hours before delivering its verdicts.

Brand was found guilty of raping a 21-year-old fan in 2009, days after meeting her at one of his stand-up shows at the Hammersmith Apollo in west London. He was also found guilty of groping an MTV worker at a party in a Soho bar in 2004, sexually assaulting a Capital Radio worker in a corridor in 2005, and sexually assaulting a Channel 4 worker during a forced massage in 2009.

Judge Joel Bennathan, formally Mr Justice Bennathan, told Brand the consequence of the verdicts was a "significant custodial term" and remanded him in custody. Sentencing will take place on Monday, 9 November.

Brand had denied all seven charges, which related to six women and alleged offences between 1999 and 2009, saying the incidents had either been consensual, reinvented or re-imagined. Prosecutors had described the 51-year-old, who starred in the films "Get Him to the Greek" and "Arthur", as a ruthless, sexual predator.

The trial began at Southwark Crown Court on October 12. The case followed a joint investigation by The Sunday Times and Channel 4's Dispatches in 2023, after which police interviewed more than 100 witnesses and reviewed thousands of documents.

London's Metropolitan Police confirmed that investigations are continuing into two further allegations of rape made against Brand, according to Britain's Press Association.
