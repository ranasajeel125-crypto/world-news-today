Democrat Scott Colom Wins Mississippi Senate Seat in Historic Upset

world · 11 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Colom led Hyde-Smith by about 4,600 votes with 99% of ballots counted; Hyde-Smith conceded on Wednesday evening, according to Colom’s campaign.

Democrat Scott Colom has won the race for a United States Senate seat in Mississippi, defeating the sitting Republican Cindy Hyde-Smith in a result few people had predicted and one that took more than a week to settle. The Associated Press called the contest for Colom on Wednesday, after local officials finished counting the ballots that had kept the outcome uncertain since election day.

With 99% of the vote counted, Colom had 49.2% to Hyde-Smith’s 48.7%, a lead of about 4,600 votes, according to the Associated Press. Hyde-Smith conceded the race on Wednesday evening, according to Colom’s campaign.

Colom, 43, a district attorney for Mississippi’s 16th Judicial District and a seventh-generation Mississippian, is the first Democrat to win one of the state’s Senate seats since 1982, when John C. Stennis was re-elected, and the first Black politician elected statewide in Mississippi since the Reconstruction era following the American Civil War.

The result is the eighth Senate seat Democrats have flipped from Republicans in this year’s midterm elections, giving them a minimum 55-seat majority going into 2027, with the Alaska race still to be decided. Mississippi was one of two Senate seats that had remained uncalled for more than a week after Election Day.

The Cook Political Report had listed the contest as Likely Republican going into the vote, and President Donald Trump won Mississippi by more than 23 points in 2024. Colom flipped at least 18 counties that voted for Trump in 2024, according to the USA TODAY Network.

Final certified totals are not expected until Friday at 5 p.m., and Mississippi has no recount process for a federal race.
