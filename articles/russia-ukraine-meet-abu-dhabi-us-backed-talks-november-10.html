Russia and Ukraine Meet in Abu Dhabi for US-Backed Talks as Trump Says Broad Deal Possible

world · 10 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The talks in Abu Dhabi are scheduled to begin at around 0630 GMT on Tuesday, with US envoys mediating and European officials present to be briefed separately by Kyiv.

Russian and Ukrainian officials were due to meet on Tuesday for two days of US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi — their first since February — after President Donald Trump suggested last week that a broad peace deal was possible in their four-and-a-half-year-old war and could incorporate Europe's security.

The discussions were scheduled to begin at around 0630 GMT on Tuesday, officials in Kyiv said. European powers, including France, Germany and the European Commission, will send officials to the talks, but Russia has made it clear it does not want the Europeans to take part in the negotiations. Instead, Ukrainian delegates will meet separately to brief their European allies on the progress of the discussions.

“We are doing everything to ensure that Europe is a participant in the diplomatic process,” Rustem Umerov, the head of the Ukrainian delegation, said on Monday.

Russia's delegation is headed again by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky as chief negotiator and will include Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov for the first time and senior military officer Yuri Zhigarlovsky. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are to lead the US mediation effort over the two days.

US negotiators believe that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy and Russian President Vladimir Putin may be willing to strike a compromise on the vital question of territory, each ceding a little, a US official told Reuters. Under the proposed deal, Ukraine would get an end to the war, security guarantees and a roadmap to accession to the European Union. A senior Ukrainian official strongly denied that the government would consider any such concessions, saying the issue of territory could be discussed only at a leaders' level and only in the context of appropriate security guarantees.

Publicly, there has been no sign that Putin is willing to drop his insistence that Ukraine surrender the entire eastern region of Donbas before a peace deal can be struck, and Zelenskiy has staunchly refused to hand over land his forces have died defending. On the eve of the negotiations, Zelenskiy said Ukraine's leverage was increasing as a result of its recent battlefield performance, which has included retaking some territory in Donetsk, and said Russia was suffering “record losses” — a claim disputed by Moscow.

Trump said last week that envoys had been discussing a broader security deal that could involve all of Europe. “It's going to be a big agreement, and it's going to involve more than Ukraine ... I could even say it might involve all of Europe,” he said. His comments prompted a backlash from some European officials, who said any negotiations on European security needed direct involvement by the relevant governments. G7 foreign ministers are also expected to discuss Ukraine on Tuesday, the second day of their gathering in Paris, and Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha will join the talks.

A truce on striking energy targets announced by Trump last month quickly fell apart. Ukraine has escalated its strikes on Russian oil refineries in recent weeks and Moscow has pounded Ukraine's electricity infrastructure and cities. Trump has frequently said an agreement to end Europe's deadliest conflict since World War Two was close without a deal emerging, though negotiations have picked up again in recent weeks after months in which Washington's attention was focused on the war in Iran.
