Russia and Ukraine Meet in Abu Dhabi for US-Backed Talks as Trump Says Broad Deal Possible

world · 10 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The talks in Abu Dhabi are scheduled to begin at around 0630 GMT on Tuesday, with US envoys mediating and European officials present to be briefed separately by Kyiv.

Russian and Ukrainian officials were due to meet on Tuesday for two days of US-brokered talks in Abu Dhabi, their first since February, after President Donald Trump suggested last week that a broad peace deal was possible in their four-and-a-half-year-old war and could incorporate Europe's security.

The discussions were scheduled to begin at around 0630 GMT on Tuesday, officials in Kyiv said. European powers, including France, Germany and the European Commission, will send officials to the talks, but Russia has made it clear it does not want the Europeans to take part in the negotiations. Instead, Ukrainian delegates will meet separately to brief their European allies on the progress of the discussions.

"We are doing everything to ensure that Europe is a participant in the diplomatic process," Rustem Umerov, the head of the Ukrainian delegation, said on Monday.

Russia's delegation is headed again by Kremlin aide Vladimir Medinsky as chief negotiator and will include Deputy Foreign Minister Sergei Ryabkov for the first time and senior military officer Yuri Zhigarlovsky. The Russian delegation also included Admiral Igor Kostyukov, head of military intelligence, and senior officers from the General Staff and the FSB. US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner are to lead the US mediation effort over the two days, and the US delegation also included General Alexus Grynkewich, Commander of US European Command and NATO Supreme Allied Commander Europe.

US negotiators believe that Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy and Russian President Vladimir Putin may be willing to strike a compromise on the vital question of territory, each ceding a little, a US official told Reuters. Under the proposed deal, Ukraine would get an end to the war, security guarantees and a roadmap to accession to the European Union. A senior Ukrainian official strongly denied that the government would consider any such concessions, saying the issue of territory could be discussed only at a leaders' level and only in the context of appropriate security guarantees.

Publicly, there has been no sign that Putin is willing to drop his insistence that Ukraine surrender the entire eastern region of Donbas before a peace deal can be struck, and Zelenskiy has staunchly refused to hand over land his forces have died defending. On the eve of the negotiations, Zelenskiy said Ukraine's leverage was increasing as a result of its recent battlefield performance, which has included retaking some territory in Donetsk, and said Russia was suffering "record losses", a claim disputed by Moscow.

Trump said last week that envoys had been discussing a broader security deal that could involve all of Europe. "It's going to be a big agreement, and it's going to involve more than Ukraine ... I could even say it might involve all of Europe," he said. His comments prompted a backlash from some European officials, who said any negotiations on European security needed direct involvement by the relevant governments. G7 foreign ministers are also expected to discuss Ukraine on Tuesday, the second day of their gathering in Paris, and Ukraine's Foreign Minister Andrii Sybiha will join the talks.

A truce on striking energy targets announced by Trump last month quickly fell apart. Ukraine has escalated its strikes on Russian oil refineries in recent weeks and Moscow has pounded Ukraine's electricity infrastructure and cities. Trump has frequently said an agreement to end Europe's deadliest conflict since World War Two was close without a deal emerging, though negotiations have picked up again in recent weeks after months in which Washington's attention was focused on the war in Iran.

The talks in Abu Dhabi took place inside the five-star Emirates Palace Mandarin Oriental, and the first day of talks concluded after nearly four hours, with no tangible results. Zelenskyy described the first day as "constructive and quite substantive", saying humanitarian issues and security guarantees had been discussed, and Kushner said he believed there were "some good ideas how to bridge" the differences between the two sides. Kushner said in opening remarks that the goal was to "create a new framework and a new understanding building upon the different institutions and understandings of the past". Before the start of the main meeting, the Ukrainians met separately with European, NATO and European Union representatives and the two US envoys.

The only outcome of the Abu Dhabi talks was a preliminary agreement on a prisoner-of-war exchange, although its precise terms were not announced. Medinsky said after the talks that European security was the central issue on the agenda, and that it was essential to establish a "new, comprehensive and fair" European security system based on Russia's proposals from December 2021. The American message shifted in Abu Dhabi, with Kushner stating that Europe's security was a US responsibility, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio assuring that his country's goal was to eventually broaden these discussions.

The talks follow a successful humanitarian operation in Oleshky, a southern Ukrainian town that has been occupied by Russian forces since 2022, and the question was whether that mission could lay the groundwork for progress in Abu Dhabi. The negotiations also follow security consultations that took place in January and February, and three earlier rounds of negotiations involving Ukraine, Russia and the US: two in Abu Dhabi in January and February and another in Geneva in February. The announcement of the November talks followed a meeting between Putin and Witkoff in St Petersburg on 21 October, after which Kremlin foreign policy aide Yuri Ushakov said Moscow was prepared to resume trilateral negotiations around 10 November.

On 12 November, US negotiators Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner travelled to Moscow for talks with Putin, before heading to Dublin to meet Zelensky and European leaders on the sidelines of the European Political Community summit. Witkoff said on 12 November that Putin had agreed to let European representatives join the next round of talks.

The war is now in its fifth year, and it is Europe's worst conflict since the Second World War. The talks in Abu Dhabi did not end it, and they produced little beyond a preliminary agreement on a prisoner exchange, but they did something that had not happened for nine months: they brought Russian and Ukrainian officials into the same room, with American mediators, to talk about how the war might end. For a conflict that has killed hundreds of thousands of people and displaced millions, even that small step was presented by Washington as progress, and the fact that the talks continued, in Moscow and in Dublin, in the days after Abu Dhabi, suggested that the diplomatic process, however slow, was still alive.
