Fuel Tanker Crash Ignites Massive Fire on Cairo Ring Road, Killing at Least 31

world · 25 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The fire broke out above Tunnel 10 in the Warraq district of Giza, on the Ring Road that encircles the Egyptian capital. Thirty-nine ambulances were sent to the scene and the injured were taken to three hospitals.

A crash involving a fuel tanker ignited a massive fire on a major highway surrounding Cairo on Wednesday, killing at least 31 people and injuring at least 21 others, Egyptian authorities said. The blaze on the Ring Road, the key artery that encircles the Egyptian capital, also left more than two dozen cars and motorcycles charred, according to the latest figures released by the Health Ministry.

The fire broke out above Tunnel 10 in the Warraq district of Imbaba, in Egypt's Giza governorate, near Cairo's Ring Road, according to the Egyptian Ministry of Health. The Egyptian Petroleum Ministry said the privately operated tanker veered off the road and crashed before exploding, setting off a fire that spread quickly through the traffic around it.

At the scene, rescue workers were pulling people out of burning cars as firefighters worked to put out the flames spreading among the vehicles, the Associated Press reported. Hundreds of people gathered near the scene in the densely populated Warraq district, a neighbourhood in Giza that forms part of the Greater Cairo area. Thirty-nine ambulances were deployed to the scene, and the injured were taken to three hospitals, the Health Ministry said.

Ahmed Sameer, who was stuck in traffic as the road was blocked after the accident, saw the fuel truck and many cars on fire. He said he saw at least eight bodies being removed from burned cars. “It was a huge fire, the cars got stuck together because of the fire,” he told the Associated Press, describing the fear and panic that followed. “It was a shocking scene.”

Two social media videos geolocated by CNN show the scale and context of the blaze. The first, filmed from above near the scene, shows an intense fire with several vehicles engulfed in flames and thick black smoke rising into the sky, with secondary blasts audible as someone off camera exclaims. The second, filmed from a distance, shows the fire spreading across a large area, as well as a busy bridge and highway interchange with heavy traffic and numerous vehicles and people visible from afar.

The casualty figures remain preliminary. Health Minister Khaled Abdel Ghaffar said the death toll was preliminary, raising concerns it could rise further, and the Health Ministry said authorities were continuing to monitor the incident and provide medical care to those injured. Initial statements said 25 people were injured, but that figure was later revised to 21. The minister placed nearby hospitals on high alert and visited the scene and treatment centres to oversee the response, according to the ministry.

Egyptian prosecutors said they had opened an investigation into the crash and the fire, with investigators dispatched to the scene and to hospitals to interview the injured and examine the bodies of those killed. Prosecutors said preliminary estimates showed 27 vehicles, including cars and motorcycles, were destroyed by fire. They ordered DNA samples to be taken from two victims whose identities had not yet been established, and said investigators were examining the wreckage, damage and circumstances surrounding the crash to determine its cause. The Public Prosecutor's office said DNA investigations were underway to identify the victims.

Egyptian President Abdel-Fattah el-Sissi expressed his condolences to the victims in a statement. The Egyptian Red Crescent said it also deployed emergency response teams to the scene and to the hospitals to support civil defence, ambulance crews and other authorities responding to the fire.

Egypt's Financial Regulatory Authority directed the country's compulsory motor insurance fund to expedite compensation payments to the families of those killed and to people injured in the fire. In a statement on Wednesday, the authority said representatives would contact victims' families to complete the required paperwork.

The health minister's spokesman told state-linked al-Qahera News that the Ring Road had been reopened and the remnants of the accident cleared, after the stretch of highway through Warraq was closed while emergency crews worked at the scene. The Ring Road is one of the busiest main arteries in Greater Cairo, connecting key highways and linking the governorates of Cairo, Giza and Qalyubia, which is why a crash there at a busy hour trapped so many vehicles in the fire's path.

The crash is the latest reminder of Egypt's poor road safety record. Deadly traffic accidents claim thousands of lives every year in Egypt, and speeding, bad roads and poor enforcement of traffic laws are the main reasons behind most of the crashes, according to the Associated Press. In January, 21 people were killed and 16 were injured in a massive multicar pileup on a road near the southern province of Beni Suef — a toll the Cairo fire has now exceeded in a single incident.

Why it matters: thirty-one people dead on one of Greater Cairo's busiest highways, in a fire that trapped drivers in their cars, is a major disaster by any measure — and it happened in a country where such crashes are a recurring national toll rather than a rarity. The death toll is still preliminary and may rise, the cause is still under investigation, and the families of the dead are only now being contacted about compensation. Until prosecutors establish why a privately operated tanker carrying petroleum products left the road and exploded, the questions the crash raises — about the tanker, the road, and the enforcement meant to keep both safe — remain unanswered.
