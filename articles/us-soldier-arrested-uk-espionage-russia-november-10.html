Senior US Soldier Arrested in UK Over Alleged Espionage for Russia

world · 10 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The 41-year-old was arrested on Tuesday, 10 November, and remains in custody at a London police station, as searches were carried out at two locations in Gloucestershire.

A senior member of the US Army has been arrested in the UK as part of an investigation by Counter Terrorism Policing, working closely with US authorities, the US military and the FBI, British police said on Tuesday.

The 41-year-old man was arrested near Gloucester on Tuesday, 10 November, on suspicion of offences under Section 1 and Section 3 of the National Security Act 2023. Section 1 of the act concerns obtaining or disclosing protected information, and Section 3 concerns assisting a foreign intelligence service. Officers are carrying out searches at two locations in Gloucestershire, and police said the country to which the allegations relate is Russia. The man has been detained under the National Security Act 2023, which allows police to hold him without charge for 14 days, and remains in custody at a London police station.

The soldier was based at Imjin Barracks in Innsworth, Gloucestershire, the headquarters of NATO's Allied Rapid Reaction Corps, a British-led multinational force drawn from 20 nations that can deploy at short notice anywhere in Europe or globally under the direct operational command of NATO's Supreme Allied Commander Europe. A senior NATO official confirmed that the arrested soldier was assigned to the corps. He was later identified as Lieutenant Colonel Brandon Tyner, 41, a senior human resources officer with the corps, who entered active-duty service in January 2008 and deployed to Afghanistan from December 2013 to December 2014, Qatar from July 2016 to July 2017, and Iraq from December 2019 to July 2020, earning a Bronze Star for his overseas service.

FBI Director Kash Patel said: "This important arrest involves allegations of espionage, which is of the utmost concern to the United States and our allies. While this investigation is continuing, I want to send a clear message, this FBI has no tolerance for anyone who breaks their oath to the United States and whose actions endanger our national security and that of our partners." Patel later said the officer was in US custody as a result of an espionage investigation between the FBI's Philadelphia field office and its London legal attache office, alongside UK Counter Terrorism Policing, MI5, UK Defence Counter Intelligence and the US Army Counterintelligence Command.

Brig. Gen. Sean Stinchon, commanding general of the US Army Counterintelligence Command, said the alleged conduct of a senior soldier entrusted with sensitive information was "a sobering reminder that insider threats are real and persistent". He later said the case demonstrated the strength of the partnership between his command, US Army prosecutors, the FBI, the US Department of Justice and the UK. Deputy Assistant Commissioner Vicki Evans, Senior National Coordinator for Counter Terrorism Policing, said: "This significant arrest and investigation is a result of close collaboration between UK and US authorities. The allegations are serious and our investigation is very much ongoing."

The case is believed to be the first time a US soldier has been found under suspicion of spying for a foreign government while in the UK, and the first time a US soldier has been arrested on British soil on suspicion of spying offences. It is understood to have followed a long-running undercover operation by British and US authorities, including MI5 and the FBI. The Times reported that Tyner was suspected of attempting to share intelligence that would be highly damaging in the hands of a foreign power, but that officials were confident no sensitive information was passed to a foreign state, because he was allegedly snared in an undercover sting. The Associated Press, citing a US official, also reported that Tyner had not provided information to Russia.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters on Wednesday that Russia had no connection to the arrest. "This arrest has nothing to do with us, and we do not know what it relates to," he said, adding that it did not concern Moscow in any way. The arrest came amid heightened tensions between Moscow and various European capitals after Russia's 2022 full-scale invasion of Ukraine, and days after the United States removed aircraft from British military bases over a separate apparent threat from Iran.

A week after the arrest, British authorities handed the investigation to the United States. On Tuesday 17 November, the head of Counter Terrorism Policing, Assistant Commissioner Laurence Taylor, said the force had presented its case to the Crown Prosecution Service, which consulted the attorney-general's office and US partners, and a decision was made to pass the investigation to the US military to progress, in line with existing international protocols. Crown prosecutors had been ready to charge Tyner until the attorney-general's office intervened, according to the Times, a decision that came as a surprise to British officials who had expected him to be prosecuted in the UK. One official said that after he is tried in the UK, he is expected to stand trial in the US as well under American military law. The investigation is being led by officers from Counter Terrorism Policing London and enquiries continue. Police said the investigation is not linked to any other ongoing Counter Terrorism Policing investigations.
