Morocco Says It Will Uphold Western Sahara Ceasefire After UN Mission Ends

world · 4 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The 35-year-old UN mission ended after the Security Council did not renew its mandate last week, removing the international presence that monitored the ceasefire in the decades-old dispute.

Morocco will stay committed to the ceasefire in Western Sahara despite the end of the United Nations peacekeeping mission in the territory, the Royal Palace said on Wednesday.

The 35-year-old UN peacekeeping mission came to an end after the Security Council did not renew its mandate last week, the UN said on Sunday, removing the international presence that has monitored the ceasefire in the decades-old dispute.

The Western Sahara conflict pits Morocco, which considers the territory its own, against the Algeria-backed Polisario Front, a political and military organisation that has been seeking a separate state there since the departure of former colonial power Spain in 1976.

Morocco's "voluntary commitment" to the ceasefire will not change with the end of the UN mission, the palace said in a statement, following a meeting chaired by King Mohammed VI with the prime minister, royal adviser, and the army chief.

Morocco expressed satisfaction at the non-renewal of the mission, the Polisario said it was "concerned" and Algeria expressed "deep regret."

Morocco controls all of Western Sahara's urban centers and its coastline. It built a security wall in the 1980s stretching 2,700 kilometers (about 1,700 miles), leaving most of the territory under its control, while the Polisario operates east of the wall with rear bases in the area of Tindouf in southwestern Algeria, where it administers refugee camps.

In 2020, the Polisario said it resumed its "armed struggle" when Morocco took a border crossing with Mauritania. But the UN describes the conflict as "of low intensity." The palace said the UN mission had proven unable to ensure compliance with the ceasefire and that its initial functions had become outdated.

The king urged vigilance in ensuring "strict respect for the Kingdom's territorial integrity, national security and the tranquility of its citizens," the palace said. It added last week that the end of the UN mission means the withdrawal of the Western Sahara issue from consideration by the Security Council, and Foreign Minister Nasser Bourita said in a state media interview on Saturday that the next step for Rabat would be to remove Western Sahara from the UN's decolonisation committee, saying Rabat had the support of two-thirds of UN member states.
