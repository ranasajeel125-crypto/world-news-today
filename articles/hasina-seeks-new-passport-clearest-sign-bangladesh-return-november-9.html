Hasina Seeks New Passport in Clearest Sign of Bangladesh Return

politics · 9 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Hasina, who fled to India in 2024, has told Reuters she plans to return to surrender before a court; her party, the Awami League, is now banned.

Ousted Bangladeshi prime minister Sheikh Hasina requested on Monday a new passport and identity card so she can return from self-imposed exile in India, according to a letter seen by Reuters, her first such communication since fleeing in 2024.

The request marks the clearest step by Hasina to act on her plan to return to Bangladesh in December, more than two years after a deadly uprising ended her long rule, raising the prospect of fresh political and legal tensions in a country where her party is now banned.

She has told Reuters she plans to return to surrender before a court, and her aides said on Monday the date would be announced once various formalities were completed.

Bangladesh's war-crimes court sentenced her to death in November 2025 in her absence for ordering a deadly crackdown on the student-led uprising, though she has denied the charges from exile.

"In view of the reported revocation of her existing Bangladeshi passport and National Identity Card (NID) by the Interim Government and other authorities, I respectfully request that you take the necessary steps to issue a new Bangladeshi passport and National Identity Card for our client," Hasina's lawyers at London-based Matrix Chambers wrote to Preeti Rahman, acting high commissioner at Bangladesh's embassy in Delhi.

The letter asked for the applications to be processed "expeditiously" on the basis of her Bangladeshi nationality and in accordance with "obligations arising under international human rights instruments".

Rahman in Delhi and spokespeople for Prime Minister Tarique Rahman in Dhaka did not immediately respond to requests for comment, Reuters said.

Hasina's Awami League party was the country's largest before it was banned following her fall. Many of her party colleagues have either been jailed or are in self-imposed exile in India or elsewhere.

Having ruled Bangladesh for 20 years as prime minister across multiple terms, Hasina still enjoys support among sections of the population, and a return could sharpen political divisions as the government in Dhaka seeks to restore stability after more than two years of upheaval.

On the other hand, it could improve Bangladesh's strained ties with India, which deteriorated sharply after New Delhi gave her refuge.

The letter was written by barrister Steven Powles KC of Matrix Chambers, who said he represented Hasina. It was dated November 9, 2026, and addressed to Preeti Rahman, the acting high commissioner at the Bangladesh High Commission in New Delhi. Her existing diplomatic passport was revoked in August 2024 and her NID was locked in February 2025 by the interim government that took power after her departure.

Dhaka's response was swift and blunt. State Minister for Foreign Affairs Humaiun Kobir said: "Bangladesh will not issue a passport to any terrorist; there is no provision for such an action." He added that the government would seek her return "through an acceptable prisoner exchange process" and asked: "Should we treat her like an ordinary commercial traveller and tell her to come and visit her in-laws' residence?" Chief Prosecutor of the International Crimes Tribunal, Md. Aminul Islam, stated that existing passport laws do not allow a passport to be issued to a convicted fugitive and that any return must take place under the extradition treaty. Bangladesh first requested her extradition from India in December 2024, and its law minister has said all legal formalities for her extradition have been completed and the decision now rests with India.

Hasina has said she intends to return voluntarily and surrender before a court. In an interview with the Associated Press earlier this month, she said even death could not prevent her from returning, and she has acknowledged that she could be arrested or imprisoned after reaching Bangladesh. She left Bangladesh for India on August 5, 2024, after protests over government job quotas grew into a nationwide movement against her government, and she has remained in India since her departure.

Her lawyers pressed the matter again. On November 19, Steven Powles KC wrote a second letter to the Bangladesh High Commission, attaching a signed authorisation from Hasina dated November 9, in which she formally authorises him to submit the passport and NID request. The authorisation identifies her as a Bangladeshi national currently residing in India, born in Tungipara on September 28, 1947. Powles noted press reports that the High Commission had received the application but required confirmation that he was acting for Hasina, and wrote: "We look forward to your reply as a matter of urgency." The second application has not yet received a proper response.

India's position has been cautious. Ministry of External Affairs spokesperson Randhir Jaiswal has described extradition as "a legal matter and it will be dealt with accordingly," and noted: "Obviously, for the travel of people, you need a travel document." India has not indicated any obstacle to a voluntary return, and New Delhi has said the extradition request is under legal review. India has maintained that it seeks a positive and constructive relationship with Bangladesh while declining to comment on internal developments.

The announcement of her planned return has already fuelled unrest. The immediate trigger was Hasina's announcement that she intends to return to Bangladesh in December, ending her exile in India. Her banned Awami League party responded to the government's refusal to issue her a new passport and national identity card by organising protests and calling for a nationwide shutdown on Thursday, November 19, followed by a week-long campaign of demonstrations. In Dhaka, police arrested 107 people suspected of links to the Awami League and its affiliated organisations over 24 hours, including former education minister Nurul Islam Nahid.

The politics around her return are charged. Prime Minister Tarique Rahman, who came to power following elections in February 2026, ending the interim administration that had governed Bangladesh since Hasina's departure, said at a public event in the northern district of Lalmonirhat that Awami League leaders "have not looked at the interests of Bangladesh and its people". Both the Yunus-led interim administration and Rahman's government have sought her extradition. Her continued presence in India remains a major point of disagreement between Dhaka and New Delhi, and relations deteriorated sharply following her ouster, although ties improved somewhat after Rahman assumed office in February.

Hasina was sentenced to death by the International Crimes Tribunal in November 2025, in her absence, for crimes against humanity over the deadly crackdown on the student-led uprising. She has denied the charges from exile. The legal argument over her documents turns on the Bangladesh Passport Order of 1973 and on international human rights law: Article 13(2) of the Universal Declaration of Human Rights and Article 12(4) of the International Covenant on Civil and Political Rights, to which Bangladesh is a party, protect the right to enter one's own country. Dhaka argues that a convicted fugitive wanted for extradition does not need a new passport to be handed over. Whether she returns in December as a voluntary surrender or through extradition, her arrival would reopen one of Bangladesh's deepest political divisions.
