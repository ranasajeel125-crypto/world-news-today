Shops Looted in Frankfurt as Germany's Gen Z Protests Turn Violent

world · 1 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Frankfurt police reported “violent riots” in the city centre, smashed shop windows and Molotov cocktails thrown at emergency services.

Rioters looted shops in central Frankfurt late on Saturday and clashed with police there and in other German cities after largely peaceful mass protests over the cost of living, the BBC reported.

Thousands had earlier joined demonstrations organised by the campaign group Gen Z regelt — meaning “Gen Z takes charge” — in 34 cities including Berlin, Frankfurt, Munich, Cologne and Hamburg, protesting underfunded schools, the high cost of living, public spending cuts and compulsory military service.

While the daytime protests were largely peaceful, the demonstrations turned violent as night fell. In a post on X, Frankfurt police said that “violent riots” were occurring in the city centre, that the windows of several shops had been smashed and that Molotov cocktails had been thrown at emergency services.

A large section of Frankfurt's main shopping street was closed off by police, AFP reported, and several arrests were made. In later updates, officials said the windows of a gun shop had been smashed and that two weapons had been seized after a suspect was arrested, while a Reuters witness reported at least two stores looted and several more damaged.

Elsewhere, police used water cannon to disperse protesters who targeted emergency workers with fireworks, stones and bottles. In Cologne, police fired water cannon after saying officers had been targeted with fireworks; in Berlin, demonstrators threw bottles and eggs while officers used pepper spray in some places; and in Hamburg, barricades were set alight.

Authorities in Frankfurt, Munich and Berlin reported as many as 18,000 protesters, roughly half of what the organisers had estimated on their website.

Roman Poseck, interior minister of Hesse state, of which Frankfurt is the biggest city, said that around 100 clearly identified left-wing extremists had infiltrated the Frankfurt demonstration, calling its ending shameful.

The unrest in Germany follows similar youth-led mass protests in France, where more than 320,000 people also demonstrated on Saturday — the outcome covered earlier on World News Today.
