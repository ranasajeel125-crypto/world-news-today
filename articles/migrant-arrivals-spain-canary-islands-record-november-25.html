Record 2,100 Migrants Reach Spain's Canary Islands in a Single Day as El Hierro Is Overwhelmed

world · 25 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Most of the boats reached El Hierro, an island of about 11,400 residents. The island has now received more migrants in November alone than it has people.

A record 2,100-plus migrants arrived in Spain's Canary Islands over 24 hours in overcrowded boats, maritime rescue service data showed on Wednesday, in the biggest single-day arrival ever recorded in Spain — a surge that has overwhelmed the tiny island of El Hierro and turned migration into the defining issue of the country's general election campaign.

Most of the boats reached El Hierro, an island of about 11,400 residents that has become one of Europe's key entry points for migrants arriving from West Africa. The Times reported that 2,100 migrants landed on the island on Tuesday alone, in 15 wooden boats known as cayucos, taking the island's November tally to 15,025 — more than its entire resident population.

The crossing claimed at least one life and produced another at sea. One migrant was found dead in a boat, the rescue service said, and a woman gave birth during the perilous journey from West Africa. The Times reported that the baby girl was four days old by the time she reached land. Among the survivors who arrived on Tuesday were 203 women and 127 children, and several people arrived in poor health after up to seven days at sea. The boats had set off from Senegal and Gambia.

The daily arrivals surpassed the previous record of about 1,500 set just two weeks ago, on 14 November — a figure The Times put at 1,580 — which had itself beaten the previous Spanish record of 1,461 set in November 2020. Arrivals so far this year had climbed to 28,103 as of late November, more than last year's total, though still well below the all-time high of 46,843 recorded in 2024, when most crossings occurred during the first nine months of the year. Nearly 21,000 people have reached the archipelago in the last two months alone.

The scale of the arrivals has made El Hierro, whose population is smaller than that of many Spanish villages, the epicentre of the latest migration crisis. The island has received the overwhelming majority of the migrants arriving in the Canaries this month, placing enormous pressure on its reception facilities and emergency services. Fernando Clavijo, president of the Canary Islands, has warned that the archipelago's 7,000 reception places risk being overwhelmed and has demanded greater European intervention, including help from the EU border agency Frontex.

In a post on X, Clavijo said: “The Canaries can't take it anymore. We're experiencing an unprecedented migration pressure,” adding that he had requested an urgent meeting with European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen. “These people come to Europe and it's not fair that we alone respond to a drama of this magnitude,” he said.

Clavijo has also complained that the Canary Islands are being left to shoulder responsibility for thousands of unaccompanied migrant children. The regional government says it is already caring for 3,325 children, with its facilities operating at several times their intended capacity, and has called on other Spanish regions to accept more minors.

Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez, who has faced tough criticism of his open stance on migration during the campaign ahead of Sunday's parliamentary election, said on Wednesday that Europe needed a comprehensive approach to migration, including stronger border controls in cooperation with Senegalese and Gambian authorities. Still, he said Spain needed “to open legal migration pathways” for workers in sectors such as housing that are short of qualified labour. Sanchez also said on Wednesday that the migration situation in the archipelago was “under control”, prompting an angry response from the regional government. Alfonso Cabello, a spokesman for Clavijo's administration, accused Madrid of being disconnected from the reality facing the island and said the crisis required a structural European response.

The arrivals have become a central issue in the campaign for Sunday's general election. Santiago Abascal, who leads the hard-right party Vox, has described the influx as an “invasion”, while the conservative People's Party has attacked Sanchez over his handling of immigration.

The Atlantic route from West Africa to the Canary Islands is one of the world's deadliest migration crossings. Thousands of migrants attempt the journey each year in overcrowded boats, seeking to reach Spanish territory despite rough seas, strong currents and long distances. Arrivals tend to pick up in the autumn months, when sea conditions along the route are generally calmer and more favourable for crossings — which helps explain why nearly 21,000 people have arrived in the past two months, a pace that has broken the daily record twice in a fortnight.

Why it matters: El Hierro has now received more migrants in a single month than it has residents, and the archipelago's reception system is close to its limits, with 3,325 unaccompanied children already in care. The record comes four days before Spaniards vote in a general election in which migration has become the central argument, and the regional government is now asking Brussels directly for help it says Madrid cannot provide alone. Whether the European Commission answers Clavijo's call, and whether the autumn surge eases as winter seas arrive, will decide whether this record is a one-off spike or the new normal for one of Europe's busiest migration frontiers.
