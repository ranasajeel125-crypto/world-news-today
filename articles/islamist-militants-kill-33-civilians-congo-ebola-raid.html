Islamist Militants Kill at Least 33 Civilians in Congo Raid as Ebola Patients Flee

world · 6 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The attackers set fire to a health post in Cantine-Aloyath, North Kivu, an Ebola hotspot; patients from a nearby hospital fled, including one confirmed Ebola case.

Islamic State-linked militants raided a village in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo, killing at least 33 civilians and forcing patients with confirmed and suspected Ebola infections to flee, authorities said on Friday.

The attackers, from the Allied Democratic Forces, a group recognised by Islamic State as an affiliate, struck Cantine-Aloyath, about 50 km west of Beni in North Kivu province, around 11 p.m. local time on Thursday, officials said. They set fire to a health post and several houses during the assault.

A total of 33 bodies were recovered after the attack, most bearing gunshot wounds, while others had been burned or hacked with machetes, according to Tchetche Aliamini, a local civil society leader. Beni territorial administrator Colonel Charles Omeonga told Reuters a death toll could not yet be established because heavy rain hampered army operations and investigations.

Patients from a nearby hospital also fled, including one confirmed Ebola patient and five suspected cases, among them three high-risk contacts, the head doctor there, Christian Kakule Vulembera, told Reuters.

The raid began late on Thursday night, when fighters from the Allied Democratic Forces, known as the ADF, entered Cantine, a village about 50 kilometres west of Beni in North Kivu province. They set fire to a health post and torched more than 50 houses, according to local officials, and killed civilians as they moved through the village. Most of the victims had gunshot wounds, while others had been burned or attacked with machetes, civil society leader Tchetche Aliamini told the Associated Press. "So far, we have counted 33 bodies and more than 50 houses set on fire, along with various other forms of material destruction," he said.

Colonel Charles Omeonga, the Beni territorial administrator, blamed the ADF for the attack. He told Reuters that heavy rain had hindered army operations and investigations, so a full death toll could not yet be given. The army was still working to establish what had happened in the village when the first reports reached Beni on Friday morning.

The attack struck at the heart of the Ebola response in the region. Cantine lies in North Kivu, one of the areas hardest hit by the Ebola outbreak now spreading across Congo, and the assault forced patients to flee the local hospital. About 180 patients fled the Kelekele Hospital Centre in Cantine, including one patient with confirmed Ebola and five suspected cases, three of them high-risk contacts, according to its medical director, Christian Kakule Vulembera. "High-risk contacts and other individuals under monitoring have all fled," he said.

The flight of the patients is a serious setback for efforts to contain the disease. Ebola spreads through contact with the bodily fluids of infected people, and patients who leave treatment centres unmonitored can carry the virus into new communities. Health workers must now try to trace the people who fled, including the confirmed case and the high-risk contacts, before they can infect others. The violence also forced many villagers to flee to other parts of the province, where the fastest-spreading Ebola outbreak in history is raging, according to health authorities.

The outbreak is caused by the Bundibugyo strain of the Ebola virus. North Kivu currently accounts for a large share of new infections, and the province has been a hotspot throughout the outbreak. The attack on a health post in an Ebola-affected area follows a grim pattern in eastern Congo, where armed groups have repeatedly targeted health facilities and workers, undermining the response to successive epidemics.

The ADF is one of the most violent armed groups operating in eastern Congo. It originated in Uganda in the 1990s and has operated in the forests of North Kivu for decades, carrying out massacres of civilians, abductions and attacks on villages. The group pledged allegiance to Islamic State in 2019, and Islamic State recognises it as an affiliate, though the extent of operational coordination between the two remains a matter of debate among analysts. Local people blame the ADF for a long series of deadly raids on villages in the Beni area.

Eastern Congo has been wracked by armed conflict for decades, with dozens of militias competing for control of territory, minerals and trade routes. The Congolese army, supported at times by United Nations peacekeepers and by Ugandan forces operating across the border, has struggled to protect civilians in the remote villages of North Kivu, where poor roads and heavy rain make rapid response difficult.

The raid came as the country also battles its worst cholera outbreak in decades, adding to the strain on a health system already stretched by the Ebola epidemic and by the insecurity that makes it hard for medical teams to reach affected communities. For the people of Cantine, the immediate consequences are stark: dozens of neighbours killed, homes burned, and a hospital emptied of its patients at the very moment the region most needs its health facilities to function.
