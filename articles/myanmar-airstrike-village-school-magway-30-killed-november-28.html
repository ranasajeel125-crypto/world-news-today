Myanmar Military Airstrike on Village School Kills at Least 30, Opposition Says

world · 28 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

A Myanmar military airstrike on a school and monastery compound in a rebel-controlled village in central Magway Region killed at least 30 people, including an infant and several schoolchildren, a local witness and a spokesperson for the opposition National Unity Government said on Saturday.

A Myanmar military airstrike on a school in a rebel-controlled village in central Magway Region has killed at least 30 people, including an infant and several schoolchildren, a local witness and a spokesperson with the opposition National Unity Government said on Saturday.

The strike hit a school and monastery compound in Sinbaungwe Township at around 3 p.m. local time on Thursday, during a religious ceremony, according to a joint statement by the NUG and the interim Magway Federal Unit Government, a rebel local authority aligned with opposition forces. Local reports identified the village as Ingyon.

Four 500-pound aerial bombs, 12 artillery shells and seven suicide drones were deployed in the attack, the statement said.

NUG spokesperson Nay Phone Latt and a local witness said more than 20 civilians, including women and children, were killed in the airstrike and artillery attacks, alongside nine rebel fighters. The dead included an infant and several schoolchildren.

Local news outlets reported a higher toll. Narinjara, a Myanmar news outlet, reported at least 45 people killed, including more than 30 students and seven teachers, and said the strike hit while students were gathered to practise for a dance performance. Reuters said it could not independently verify the casualty figures or accounts of the strike, and the figures should be treated as preliminary.

The local witness said there were no active clashes in the area at the time of the bombing, and added that rebel fighters only occasionally passed through the village.

Pro-military channels on Telegram claimed the airstrike targeted a graduation ceremony and a gathering for rebel forces, but the military had not yet issued an official statement by Saturday afternoon. The Myanmar government also did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

The airstrike came a week after the International Court of Justice ruled that Myanmar had committed genocide against the Rohingya minority in 2017 — a judgment this site reported on 19 November.

According to records kept by the NUG, the military launched 15,327 airstrikes in 6,653 separate incidents between February 1, 2021 and late October 2026, resulting in 6,467 civilian deaths and damaging 562 schools and 848 religious buildings.

The NUG and the local interim authorities condemned what they described as an unprovoked attack, saying it was a war crime and a breach of the Geneva Convention. Their statement said they are collecting evidence for future international legal proceedings, and urged the United Nations, ASEAN and neighbouring countries to halt the flow of aviation fuel, munitions and drone technology to Myanmar's military.

Myanmar's civil war, which began after the military seized power in 2021, has killed around 100,000 people and displaced more than 3.5 million.

Why it matters: a school and monastery compound was bombed in the middle of a religious ceremony, and at least 30 people are dead — among them an infant and schoolchildren. The toll is still unverified, and the military has said nothing, but the pattern is documented: more than 15,000 airstrikes since 2021, hundreds of schools damaged, and now a strike on a school a week after the world court found Myanmar guilty of genocide. If the accounts hold, this is not a battlefield error — it is a school, hit deliberately, in an area where no fighting was taking place.
