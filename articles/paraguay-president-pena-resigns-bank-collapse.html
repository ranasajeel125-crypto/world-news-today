Paraguay President Santiago Pena Resigns as Bank Collapse Sparks Crisis

world · 5 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Vice President Pedro Alliana is set to succeed Pena if Congress accepts his resignation.

Paraguayan President Santiago Pena resigned on Thursday after the collapse of digital lender ueno left millions of customers unable to access their accounts, triggering a political crisis in one of Washington's closest allies in South America, Reuters reported.

Pena, 47, an economist who was elected in 2023, announced his resignation in a video posted on X, saying he was stepping down with the stability of the country, social peace and the need to preserve democratic institutions in mind.

Minutes after the announcement, Paraguay's Chamber of Deputies voted unanimously to open impeachment proceedings against him, while lawmakers were debating charges brought by 35 opposition lawmakers and ruling-party dissidents accusing him of serious dereliction of duty. The Senate will determine whether the proceedings move forward, and both chambers of Congress were due to meet in a joint session to consider his resignation.

Ueno, which grew rapidly from a niche fintech player into Paraguay's fifth-largest bank, had about 3 million customers in a country of about 6.5 million people and administered close to $850 million in public funds, including pension money. The central bank intervened earlier this week after finding the bank had breached minimum solvency requirements, leaving customers temporarily unable to access their accounts.

Pena has faced accusations of conflict of interest, misuse of pension funds and institutional cover-up over his past business ties to Grupo Vazquez, the conglomerate that controls ueno. He has said he was a shareholder until March 2025, when he sold his stake; ueno says he never had a direct relationship with the bank as a shareholder or investor.

Prosecutors said on Thursday they had opened a criminal investigation into Pena, and arrest warrants have been issued for eight ueno executives this week. Pena has denied any wrongdoing.

Vice President Pedro Alliana is constitutionally in line to succeed Pena for the remainder of his five-year term, which runs to 2028, if Congress accepts the resignation.
