Bank of England Raises Rates for First Time Since 2023, Says More May Come

business · 5 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (Business Desk)

The Bank of England had held off raising rates while its main peers tightened in response to inflation pressures from the war with Iran.

The Bank of England raised interest rates for the first time in over three years on Thursday and warned that at least one more increase was likely if the US-Iran conflict continues, Reuters reported.

The central bank's Monetary Policy Committee voted 8-1 to raise rates to 4% from 3.75%. The dissent came not from a policymaker seeking to keep rates on hold but from one seeking a bigger hike: Catherine Mann voted for a half-point increase to 4.25%.

"Today's increase shouldn't be seen as the first in an inevitable long sequence of hikes. But unless the conflict in the Middle East is resolved, neither does it seem likely to be the last increase," Governor Andrew Bailey said.

British inflation is likely to reach around 5% in the middle of next year, the Bank said, more than double its 2% target — a blow for new Prime Minister Andy Burnham, who has sought to ease cost of living pressures. The Bank now sees inflation peaking at an average 4.8% in the second quarter of next year and not returning to 2% until the third quarter of 2028.

Until now, the Bank of England had not followed the US Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and other central banks in raising rates in response to inflation pressures from the Iran war. Sterling strengthened against the US dollar after the decision and two-year British government bond yields rose by 4 basis points.
