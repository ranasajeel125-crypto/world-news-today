Russia and Ukraine Say Emergency Alert Apps Hit by Cyber-Attacks

world · 2 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Ukraine's air force said the false nuclear alert disappeared and there was no radiation threat; Russia's ministry said it was working to restore its app.

Russia's emergencies ministry said its mobile app was hit by a massive cyber-attack that resulted in false information being spread to the public, state news agency TASS quoted the ministry as saying early on Monday, Reuters reported.

The ministry was working to restore the app, TASS said, without providing additional details about the attack or the false information that was circulated.

Separately, Ukraine's Air Force said late on Sunday that the country's public air raid alert app had been hacked. The app for a short time showed Kyiv and other areas subject to a nuclear alert, but the abnormality later disappeared, Reuters reported.

“The air alert app appears to have been subjected to enemy influence,” the air force said on Telegram. “There is no radiation threat! Stay calm and follow our updates on our channel!”

Neither report named a specific attacker. The incidents struck the warning systems that civilians on both sides rely on for alerts during the war, which has seen repeated strikes on infrastructure and, in recent days, Russian attacks that cut power to hundreds of thousands of Ukrainian households.

Source: Reuters
