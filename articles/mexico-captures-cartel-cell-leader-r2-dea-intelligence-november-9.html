Mexico Captures Alleged Cartel Cell Leader in Operation Aided by DEA Intelligence

Mexican authorities said on Sunday, 8 November, they arrested an alleged leader of the "Los Rs" faction of the Jalisco New Generation Cartel, in an operation in Jalisco state that was aided by intelligence shared by the US Drug Enforcement Administration. The arrest, in the city of Zapopan in the Guadalajara metropolitan area, was one of the most significant blows against the cartel in months, and it came a year after the faction was linked to the assassination of a mayor who had become a national symbol of resistance to organised crime.

Mexico's Navy said Rafael "N", alias "R2", was captured in an operation in Zapopan that was carried out by its special operations unit, the Unidad Naval de Operaciones Especiales, in coordination with the security ministry and with support from Mexican Army special forces, following intelligence work by Mexico's National Intelligence Center and information shared by the DEA. Naval personnel approached a restaurant in Zapopan while supporting an order from a Michoacán state prosecutor to locate and present individuals, and the operation was based on intelligence that had helped locate organised crime figures there. Authorities also detained 19 other people. Mexican authorities customarily withhold the surnames of suspects and identify them only by their first name, followed by the letter "N", but other reports named the detainee as Rafael Álvarez Ayala.

Rafael "N" is allegedly linked to the homicide of Carlos Manzo and 19 other people, the Navy said. Manzo was a former mayor of Uruapan in the avocado-producing state of Michoacan, who was shot and killed during a public Day of the Dead celebration on 1 November last year while he was with his family. Manzo, 40, had been mayor since September 2024 and had become known for a hard-line stance against the cartels, telling his police force to shoot criminals without hesitation and openly naming the Jalisco New Generation Cartel and the Knights Templar as the groups terrorising his city. He was often seen wearing a bulletproof vest, and had said in an interview in September 2025: "I don't want to be just another mayor on the list of those executed, those whose lives have been taken from them."

Manzo was shot seven times by a 17-year-old gunman, identified as Víctor Manuel Ubaldo Vidales, who was himself shot and killed at the scene. The attack, in Uruapan's crowded main plaza, cloaked in marigold flowers for the Day of the Dead, shocked the country on a day when many criminal groups quietly set down their weapons as families honour their dead. Manzo had been under protection since December 2024, three months after taking office, and his security was reinforced the following May with municipal police and 14 National Guard officers. Hundreds of Uruapan residents, dressed in black, took to the city's streets the next day to accompany his funeral procession, chanting "Justice! Justice! Out with Morena!", a reference to the governing party of President Claudia Sheinbaum. His wife, Grecia Quiroz, succeeded him as mayor and vowed to continue his fight, and his grassroots Sombrero Movement chose her to replace him. Manzo was among at least 13 mayors killed across Mexico in the two years since Sheinbaum took office, according to an Associated Press tally.

The investigation into Manzo's killing has led to a string of arrests. Authorities have detained 34 people in connection with the assassination, including seven of the mayor's bodyguards who were with him when he was attacked, and members of his government accused of leaking his movements. In July 2026, Mexican authorities arrested another alleged Los Rs leader, Ramón Ángel Álvarez Ayala, known as R1, who officials identified as a suspected mastermind behind Manzo's assassination, saying he had given direct instructions to monitor the mayor, track his movements and carry out the attack through an encrypted messaging platform. R2's arrest is a separate development in the same investigation and the wider crackdown on the faction.

Security minister Omar Garcia Harfuch said in a social media post that the detainee was identified as a key leader of "Los Rs", a CJNG cell operating in the states of Jalisco and Michoacan. He said the suspect has an outstanding arrest warrant in Mexico for aggravated homicide, issued in Apatzingán, Michoacán, and an arrest warrant for extradition to the US. "This cell is mainly linked to extortion, drug trafficking, homicide, illegal deprivation of liberty, kidnapping and attacks against authorities," Harfuch said. "Among its victims of extortion are business owners and avocado producers." The avocado business in western Mexico has long been shadowed by threats, extortion and cartel violence, and Michoacán, which produces most of Mexico's avocados, is one of the states where cartels most often extort growers, packers and transporters.

During the operation, naval personnel came under armed attack and returned fire, the Navy said, adding the detainee and one naval officer were wounded and are receiving medical attention. The DEA said in a social media post that "R2" was wanted in the US on federal drug trafficking and firearms charges, and described him as a senior leader of the CJNG, which the US has designated a terrorist organisation, responsible for drug trafficking, extortion, kidnapping and deadly violence across Mexico. The DEA said intelligence it shared had helped Mexican authorities locate R2, and commended the Mexican security ministry, Navy, army and intelligence centre for bringing him into custody.

The Jalisco New Generation Cartel is one of Mexico's most powerful criminal organisations, based in Jalisco state and involved in the trafficking of fentanyl, methamphetamine and other drugs, as well as extortion and kidnapping. It is one of more than 20 criminal groups designated as foreign terrorist organisations by the Trump administration, a designation that has deepened US-Mexico security cooperation even as it has raised questions in Mexico about sovereignty. The arrest of R2 is the latest in a series of high-profile captures under Sheinbaum's security strategy, which has emphasised intelligence-led operations and cooperation with US agencies, and it removes a figure accused of directing violence across two of Mexico's most violent states. The detainee now faces prosecution in Mexico and a possible extradition to the United States, where he is wanted on federal drug trafficking and firearms charges.
