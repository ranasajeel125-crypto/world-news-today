Kenyan Doomsday Cult Leader Paul Mackenzie Sentenced to 200 Years Over Shakahola Deaths

world · 6 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Prosecutors had sought the death penalty; the court handed down two 100-year terms, to run consecutively, for the starvation murders and eight direct homicides.

A Kenyan court has sentenced self-proclaimed pastor Paul Mackenzie to 200 years in prison for the murder of 191 people — 190 children and one adult — in the Shakahola starvation cult tragedy, handing down two 100-year terms to run consecutively.

Justice Diana Kavedza of the High Court in Mombasa imposed 100 years for the murder of 183 people by starvation — 182 children and one adult — and a further 100 years for eight counts of murder by direct homicide involving child victims, including strangulation, suffocation and blunt-force injuries. Prosecutors had sought the death penalty.

Mackenzie, 54, was convicted on October 19 along with 28 co-accused on all 191 counts of murder. The court also sentenced the 28 co-accused, with their terms set according to their individual roles and levels of participation.

The court found that Mackenzie, leader of the Good News International Church, instructed followers to starve themselves and their children to death in the belief that they would “meet Jesus” before the end of the world. Justice Kavedza rejected the defence claim that the victims had fasted voluntarily.

The tragedy came to light in 2023, when investigators began recovering bodies from shallow graves in Shakahola Forest in Kilifi County, near the coastal town of Malindi. More than 400 bodies have since been recovered from the forest, though the murder trial covered 191 deaths.

Mackenzie faces separate proceedings on terrorism-related and manslaughter charges, which he denies. Kenya has not carried out an execution since 1987.
