Exit Polls Put Bulgaria's Iotova Ahead in Presidential Runoff

world · 1 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Two exit polls put Iotova on 51% and 52.5% respectively, against 44.7% and 43.4% for Gyurov; turnout was estimated at 26.8%.

Exit polls published after voting ended at 8 p.m. local time (1800 GMT) on Sunday put Bulgaria's incumbent president, Iliana Iotova, ahead of former caretaker prime minister Andrey Gyurov in the country's presidential runoff, Bulgarian National Radio (BNR) reported.

The Myara exit poll for BNR, taken as of 8 p.m., gave the Iotova-Kiril Valchev ticket 51% of the vote, against 44.7% for the Gyurov-Georgi Kandev ticket, with 4.3% choosing “I do not support any candidate”. A second exit poll, by Alpha Research, put Iotova ahead by 52.5% to 43.4%. Turnout was estimated at 26.8%.

An Associated Press report on the exit polls said Iotova, at around 52%, was the likely winner, with Gyurov on around 44%, and noted that if the result is confirmed she would become Bulgaria's first woman to be elected head of state.

The exit polls are not the official result. Reuters reported before polls closed that a result was likely on Monday as ballot papers are counted, after a tight first round on 25 October in which neither candidate reached the threshold for an outright win in a field of 27 candidate tickets.

The runoff was described in reporting on the vote as a choice between Iotova, who favours dialogue with Moscow and opposes military aid to Ukraine, and Gyurov, backed by pro-Western opposition parties, at a time of heightened Black Sea security tensions. Reuters describes Bulgaria's presidency as a largely ceremonial role.

Source: Bulgarian National Radio / AP
