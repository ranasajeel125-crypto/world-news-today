Denmark Foils Alleged Russian Plot to Kill Rapper Kyivstoner and Expels Moscow's Defence Attaché

world · 20 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Danish police and security officials announced on 20 November 2026 that they had foiled an alleged Russian intelligence plot to assassinate the rapper Kyivstoner ahead of a concert near Copenhagen.

Danish police foiled an attempt by Russian intelligence services to assassinate a Ukrainian-American rapper in September, officials in Copenhagen said on Friday, in a case Denmark described as an unprecedented Russian assassination attempt on Danish soil. The target was Kyivstoner, a 34-year-old rapper, comedian and streamer whose real name is Albert Vasiliev, and the plot was stopped only minutes before he was due to perform at a concert north of Copenhagen.

The announcement, made at a press conference by police and Denmark's national security and intelligence service, PET, came as two Danish men appeared in court near Copenhagen charged with attempted murder and with assisting a foreign intelligence service. Within hours, Denmark said it was expelling Russia's defence attache in protest, in one of the sharpest diplomatic responses Copenhagen has taken against Moscow since Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

How the plot was foiled. Police thwarted the attempt when they arrested three men with links to the Danish criminal underworld shortly before Kyivstoner was due to give a concert north of Copenhagen on 25 September. Prosecutors told the court the men were driving towards the venue with two loaded pistols and two knives when police stopped them at around 9.55 p.m., five minutes before the concert was due to begin. One of the suspects had bought a ticket to the concert. The concert was cancelled.

Two of the men appeared in court near Copenhagen on Friday, charged under Section 108 of Denmark's Criminal Code, which covers assisting a foreign intelligence service, as well as attempted murder, according to court documents. Both men denied wrongdoing, Danish broadcaster DR reported.

The head of PET, Rasmus Kieffer-Kristensen, left no doubt about who Danish intelligence believes was behind the plan. "It is our clear assessment that the assassination attempt was planned by the Russian intelligence services," he told the press conference. He added that the case mattered beyond the fate of one performer: "We believe that the case is important for understanding the threat that Denmark faces from Russia."

"A Russian assassination attempt is not something we've seen in Denmark before, and that gives me an impression of an increasingly aggressive and risk-taking Russia," Kieffer-Kristensen said. That assessment frames the case not as an isolated crime but as part of a pattern of Russian activity that Danish authorities say has grown bolder since the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

According to Danish security services, the foiled plot was orchestrated by Loyal to Familia, a Danish street gang that has been banned since 2018. The Wall Street Journal reported that Danish security services accused the gang and Russian intelligence of being behind the plot, making it the latest example of a government accusing Russia of engaging proxies to commit a crime on European soil. Using local criminals as cut-outs, rather than sending its own officers, is a method Western intelligence agencies have warned about repeatedly in recent years.

Kyivstoner, who holds both Ukrainian and US citizenship, is a rapper, comedian and streamer of modest fame whose most popular songs have gathered 1.2 million streams on Spotify and 31 million views on YouTube, according to the Wall Street Journal. His Danish concert was part of a European tour and was cancelled after the arrests. Local media reported that the rapper said in an Instagram post, which is now unavailable, that Danish intelligence officials had warned him of a threat to his life and prevented him from performing in Denmark.

The Times reported that the thwarted attack marks the second time Russia has allegedly targeted Kyivstoner, who was also reportedly the target of an assassination plot in Poland earlier this year. Neither report gave further details of the earlier alleged plot, and Danish officials did not discuss it at Friday's press conference, but the claim adds to the picture of a performer who has drawn the attention of Russian intelligence more than once.

Denmark's response. Foreign Minister Lars Lokke Rasmussen summoned the Russian ambassador, Vladimir Barbin, to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs and told him that Russia's defence attache must leave Denmark within seven days. "We have just summoned the Russian ambassador to the ministry of foreign affairs and notified him of the government's decision to expel the Russian defence attache from Denmark," Rasmussen told reporters.

Rasmussen called the alleged plot an unacceptable transgression and an outrageous violation of Denmark's borders. "We know the Russian intelligence services are behind this. We are absolutely certain," he said. "This is an outrageous and completely unacceptable violation of our borders, and it demands a firm response." At the same time, he said Denmark did not want to break off diplomatic relations with Russia altogether. "We don't want to break off diplomatic relations with Russia in this particular situation that Denmark and Europe are in... But on the other hand, we want to send a very, very strong signal," he said.

Rasmussen said he would not expel Russia's ambassador, in order to maintain some level of diplomatic contact, but the expulsion of the defence attache, combined with the public accusation, amounts to one of the strongest signals Copenhagen has sent Moscow. Denmark is also considering suspending Schengen visas for Russians, he added, as the government weighs additional measures in response to what officials described as an unacceptable violation of Danish sovereignty.

Russia denies the allegation. The Russian Embassy in Copenhagen called the Danish allegations baseless. The notion that Russia would assassinate "a musician with limited political influence and questionable fame" was "absurd," the embassy said. It added that Denmark had not presented any evidence of Russian involvement and had not submitted a request for legal assistance. Russian Foreign Ministry spokeswoman Maria Zakharova said Moscow would give an appropriate response to Denmark's decision to expel the military attache, without elaborating, according to the state news outlet TASS.

Moscow has regularly denied accusations of stepping up hybrid attacks, incidents of aggression that fall short of acts of war, in the region following its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022, Reuters noted in its report. That denial follows the pattern of previous cases in which European governments have accused Russia of sabotage, cyberattacks and covert operations, and Moscow has rejected the claims as unproven.

A wider pattern of hybrid threats. The Kyivstoner case did not emerge from nowhere. PET said in September that Russia was recruiting Danish citizens to help plan sabotage against Danish defence firms with ties to Ukraine, a warning that already had Danish security services on alert weeks before the alleged assassination plot was revealed. The September warning and the September arrests are now understood as part of the same picture: a Russian intelligence effort operating inside Denmark through recruited locals.

The case also comes in a week when Denmark and its neighbours have been at the centre of European security news. On the same day the plot was announced, Russian missiles landed in Poland during a massive Russian attack on Ukraine, an incident this site reported on 20 November, and European allies were gathered in Poland for military exercises. Denmark itself has been in the headlines over Greenland and Arctic security in recent days. Together, the events have sharpened a debate in Copenhagen and across Europe about how far Russia is willing to go, and how European states should answer.

What happens next will turn on the court case and on Moscow's reply. The two men charged on Friday face trial on charges of attempted murder and assisting a foreign intelligence service, and Danish prosecutors will have to present the evidence linking them to the alleged plot. Russia's defence attache has seven days to leave Denmark, and Zakharova's promise of an appropriate response leaves open the possibility of a reciprocal expulsion. Denmark's consideration of Schengen visa restrictions for Russians could also become a further point of friction.

For Denmark, the case has already changed the conversation. A country that prides itself on openness and on a quiet, consensus-driven politics now finds itself publicly accusing a foreign intelligence service of planning a killing on its soil, and expelling a diplomat in response. As Kieffer-Kristensen put it, the case is important for understanding the threat Denmark faces from Russia, and the coming weeks will show whether Copenhagen's strong signal is the end of the matter or the start of a longer confrontation.
