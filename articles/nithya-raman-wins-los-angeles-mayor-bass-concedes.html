Democratic Socialist Nithya Raman Wins Los Angeles Mayor's Race, Ousting Karen Bass

politics · 6 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Raman led Bass by 47,253 votes, 52.7% to 47.3%, in the latest count; Bass conceded on Thursday and promised a smooth transition.

Democratic socialist-backed candidate Nithya Raman has defeated incumbent Karen Bass in the race for mayor of Los Angeles, the Associated Press projected on Thursday, in an upset that puts a democratic socialist in charge of America's second-largest city, Reuters reported.

The latest figures released on Thursday by the Los Angeles County Registrar-Recorder/County Clerk's Office showed Raman, 45, a city council member, with 460,772 votes, or 52.7%, to Bass's 413,519, or 47.3%. Bass, 73, conceded in a written statement, congratulating Raman and promising a smooth transition. "I have called Councilmember Nithya Raman to congratulate her on her victory, and I have committed to ensuring a smooth and seamless transition," she said. The Associated Press called the race on Thursday, when Raman held about 52 per cent of the vote, and her lead kept growing as counting continued, reaching nearly 68,500 votes by Friday evening. County elections officials are scheduled to certify the results on 27 November, and her term begins on 14 December.

Raman is the first Los Angeles mayoral candidate to defeat an incumbent since 2005, when Antonio Villaraigosa unseated James Hahn. The India-born urban planner, an advocate for the homeless who first won a seat on the Los Angeles City Council in 2020, was once an ally of Bass and had initially endorsed her before announcing her own candidacy, saying the city had reached a "breaking point". She filed her candidacy late, on 7 February, and in the primary Bass took first place with 34 per cent of the vote while Raman advanced to the general election with 29 per cent, edging out Republican Spencer Pratt. She overtook Bass despite endorsements for the incumbent from prominent Democrats such as California Governor Gavin Newsom, former Vice President Kamala Harris and former House Speaker Nancy Pelosi. Not one fellow councilmember endorsed her mayoral campaign, and she was stripped of her powerful Housing and Homelessness Committee chairmanship after entering the race. City Controller Kenneth Mejia was the only elected official at City Hall to endorse her.

Raman's win also makes history on representation: she is set to become the first South Asian and first Indian-American mayor of Los Angeles. Born in Kerala, India, she will be the city's 44th mayor. Bass, who made history in 2022 as the city's first female and first Black mayor, leaves office after just one four-year term, becoming the first Los Angeles mayor in more than two decades to lose a re-election bid.

Homelessness played a major role in the campaign. About 44,000 people were counted as homeless in Los Angeles in 2025, including about 27,000 living unsheltered on the streets, in vehicles or in encampments, according to the Los Angeles Homeless Services Authority. Bass, the first woman elected mayor of Los Angeles, had made fighting homelessness a top priority, but drew criticism from affordable-housing advocates who said she had made too little progress. A Berkeley IGS/LA Times poll conducted between 19 and 25 October found that around four in ten likely voters considered reducing homeless encampments a major priority for the next mayor, while ensuring public safety and building more affordable housing were each identified as top priorities by around three in ten.

Bass's standing was also undermined by criticism of the city's response to the January 2025 Palisades fire, one of the deadliest and most destructive wildfires in the city's history, which killed 12 people and destroyed more than 6,800 structures. Bass was out of the country when the fire ignited. Bass further characterised Raman's positions on policing and homelessness as "extreme" during the campaign, but her popularity had tanked following the 2025 wildfires that destroyed parts of the city.

Raman has promised to triple homebuilding and to oust the city's police chief, whom she views as too accommodating with federal immigration authorities. She wants to triple construction by speeding up permits, allowing more apartments in residential neighbourhoods and cutting bureaucratic delays, including a 60-day "shot clock" for approving new apartment projects that comply with zoning laws, high-rise zones in certain areas, and duplexes, triplexes and fourplexes in single-family neighbourhoods near transit hubs. She has pledged to cut homelessness in half before the 2028 Olympics and eliminate it entirely by 2030, shifting resources away from Bass's costly Inside Safe motel programme toward rental subsidies that place homeless residents directly into apartments, with up to 24 months of rental subsidies and intensive case management. She also plans to give the city controller the ability to audit every dollar spent on homelessness, and create an Office of Tenant Protections.

On public safety, Raman has vowed to replace Police Chief Jim McDonnell before his term expires in 2029, arguing he has failed to maintain the trust of immigrant communities amid federal immigration raids. The Los Angeles Police Protective League opposed her candidacy, warning her proposals could threaten public safety. Her first 100-day agenda also includes appointing a chief operating officer and chief financial officer, a Palisades recovery czar to accelerate wildfire rebuilding, and public dashboards tracking city departments' successes and failures.

When she takes office, Los Angeles will become the nation's second-largest city led by a democratic socialist, following New York, where Zohran Mamdani was elected mayor last year, meaning America's two most populous cities will both be led by democratic socialists. Her victory comes amid a wider wave of challenges to established Democratic leaders by candidates from the party's left wing, and her campaign attracted considerably less national attention than Mamdani's. Raman inherits a city with more than 45,000 homeless residents, a depleted police force, mounting financial pressures and the 2028 Olympics less than two years away. "Here in Los Angeles, this election is about change, too," she told supporters after polls closed on 3 November.
