Indonesia’s Vice President Gibran Resigns Amid Impeachment Push and Rift in Ruling Alliance

world · 11 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Gibran, the 39-year-old son of former President Joko Widodo, tendered his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto on Wednesday, three political sources told Reuters.

Indonesia’s Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has resigned, three political sources told Reuters on Wednesday, in a move that threatens to split the ruling alliance and deepen political uncertainty in Southeast Asia’s largest economy.

Mr Gibran’s political aide, Budi Arie Setiadi, told Reuters that the 39-year-old had tendered his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto earlier on Wednesday. Two sources at the parliamentary upper house also confirmed the resignation to Reuters. The resignation letter was handed to Mr Prabowo on Wednesday afternoon at Halim Perdanakusuma Air Force Base in Jakarta, local outlet BeritaSatu reported.

Mr Gibran had told Mr Prabowo of his intention to resign during a two-and-a-half-hour meeting on Tuesday, according to the presidential palace. The palace had not formally confirmed the resignation, or said whether Mr Prabowo had accepted it, at the time of writing. A statement Mr Gibran had been expected to make at the vice-presidential palace on Wednesday evening was cancelled without explanation.

The resignation follows rulings last month by the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court that Mr Gibran’s education documents did not meet election requirements. The Supreme Court ruled on 30 October that his overseas high-school equivalency certificate was invalid, and late on Tuesday it instructed the education ministry to withdraw the certificate. Mr Gibran, who studied abroad, used the certificate to meet the education requirements to run in the 2024 election, which he won alongside Mr Prabowo. Indonesian election law requires vice-presidential candidates to have completed senior high school or an equivalent level of education.

More than 100 lawmakers from several parties signed a petition on Wednesday opening the door to impeachment proceedings against Mr Gibran, Reuters reported, with the petition due to be submitted to the House speaker. The controversy has unfolded amid a widening rift between Mr Prabowo and former President Widodo, known as Jokowi, whose support was instrumental in securing the Prabowo-Gibran ticket’s victory in the 2024 election.

Under the constitution, the parliamentary upper house must convene to declare a vacancy, after which the president puts forward two names and the People’s Consultative Assembly elects a replacement. Mr Gibran took office on 20 October 2024, the day Mr Prabowo was inaugurated.
