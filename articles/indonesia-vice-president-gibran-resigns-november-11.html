Indonesia’s Vice President Gibran Resigns Amid Impeachment Push and Rift in Ruling Alliance

Indonesia's Vice President Gibran Rakabuming Raka has resigned, three political sources told Reuters on Wednesday, 11 November, in a move that threatens to split the ruling alliance and deepen political uncertainty in Southeast Asia's largest economy. The resignation ends a 38-day crisis over his educational qualifications and caps a political career that was controversial from the moment it began.

Mr Gibran's political aide, Budi Arie Setiadi, told Reuters that the 39-year-old had tendered his resignation to President Prabowo Subianto earlier on Wednesday. Two sources at the parliamentary upper house also confirmed the resignation to Reuters. The resignation letter was handed to Mr Prabowo on Wednesday afternoon at Halim Perdanakusuma Air Force Base in Jakarta, local outlet BeritaSatu reported. The following day, Gibran formally submitted his resignation letter to the People's Consultative Assembly in Jakarta, and Assembly Chair Ahmad Muzani confirmed the submission. Bloomberg reported that Prabowo has accepted the resignation, though it was not officially announced. Gibran left the Assembly building without making a statement, and a House plenary meeting scheduled for that afternoon to discuss his impeachment was cancelled.

Mr Gibran had told Mr Prabowo of his intention to resign during a two-and-a-half-hour meeting on Tuesday, according to the presidential palace. The palace had not formally confirmed the resignation, or said whether Mr Prabowo had accepted it, at the time of writing. A statement Mr Gibran had been expected to make at the vice-presidential palace on Wednesday evening was cancelled without explanation.

The resignation follows rulings last month by the Constitutional Court and the Supreme Court that Mr Gibran's education documents did not meet election requirements. The Supreme Court ruled on 30 October that his overseas high-school equivalency certificate was invalid, and late on Tuesday it instructed the education ministry to withdraw the certificate. In a ruling published on its website late on Tuesday, the court said it was instructing the education ministry to withdraw Gibran's education equivalence certificate, which the court has annulled, and that "no evidence was found to show Gibran had a foreign diploma or certificate that could serve as the basis for equivalency with a senior high school or equivalent qualification". The court found the certificate was issued based on evidence that did not actually prove Jokowi's eldest son had graduated, and that records showed university-level courses but no proof of high school completion. Mr Gibran, who studied abroad in Singapore and Australia, used the certificate to meet the education requirements to run in the 2024 election, which he won alongside Mr Prabowo. Indonesian election law requires vice-presidential candidates to have completed senior high school or an equivalent level of education.

More than 100 lawmakers from several parties signed a petition on Wednesday opening the door to impeachment proceedings against Mr Gibran, Reuters reported, with the petition due to be submitted to the House speaker. The petition, signed by 120 lawmakers from eight parties, was submitted by Deputy Speaker Saan Mustopa, and lawmaker Ahmad Doli Kurnia said Gibran was no longer eligible to serve as vice president following the court rulings. Protesters had gathered outside the parliament building in Jakarta on 10 November calling for his impeachment. According to Indonesian law, with the submission of the petition, the House leadership must form a special committee to determine whether Gibran has violated the constitution by serving as vice president despite not meeting the election requirements, and if the allegation is upheld, the House must ask the Constitutional Court to rule on whether he has violated the constitution.

The controversy has unfolded amid a widening rift between Mr Prabowo and former President Widodo, known as Jokowi, whose support was instrumental in securing the Prabowo-Gibran ticket's victory in the 2024 election. Analysts link the resignation to the rift, which has been fuelled by wiretapping allegations, budget cuts and Gibran's limited role in government, and some observers suggest Prabowo's silence indicates he wants Gibran's removal without bearing the political cost of openly engineering it, particularly a possible backlash from Jokowi supporters. The two men faced off twice in presidential elections, both of which Jokowi won, before joining their parties in a ruling coalition seen by many as a marriage of political convenience, with Gibran and other Jokowi allies appointed to key positions in Prabowo's government.

Gibran's path to the vice-presidency was controversial from the start. Born on 1 October 1987 in Solo, in Central Java province, he built a catering and wedding planning business as a young man, then in 2021 followed in his father's footsteps to become mayor of Solo, a post he was widely accused of obtaining through nepotism, having no history of political involvement with any party. When he was picked as Prabowo's running mate for the 2024 election, he was 36, below the minimum age of 40 required by election law. His path opened after the Constitutional Court ruled on 16 October 2023, in Decision No. 90, that a person under 40 could still stand as a presidential or vice-presidential candidate if they had previously held, or were currently holding, an elected office, including regional head. The controversy intensified because the court's chief justice at the time, Anwar Usman, was Gibran's uncle and Jokowi's brother-in-law, and the court's Honour Council later found he had committed a serious ethical violation in handling the case and removed him as chief justice, although he remained a constitutional judge. The ruling nevertheless remained in force, Golkar formally proposed Gibran as Prabowo's running mate on 21 October 2023, and Prabowo announced him the following day. The election commission declared Prabowo and Gibran the winners on 20 March 2024, and the Constitutional Court rejected all petitions disputing the results on 22 April 2024.

Under the constitution, the parliamentary upper house must convene to declare a vacancy, after which the president puts forward two names and the People's Consultative Assembly elects a replacement within 60 days. Mr Gibran took office on 20 October 2024, the day Mr Prabowo was inaugurated, becoming Indonesia's youngest vice president. His resignation, after just two years in office, makes him only the second vice president in Indonesian history to resign, after Mohammad Hatta in 1956, and the first in the post-Suharto era to leave office early. Gibran dismissed the claims against him, pointing out that he went on to obtain a marketing degree, but the diploma scandal has proved to be the last nail in his political coffin, and the toppling of Jokowi's eldest son points to trouble ahead in relations between Prabowo and his predecessor.
