Three Kyiv Bridges Hit in Major Russian Missile Attack, Mayor Says

world · 20 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Kyiv's bridges over the Dnipro River, three of which were hit in Friday's Russian missile attack, in the biggest strikes on the crossings since the start of the war.

Russian ballistic missiles struck three bridges over the Dnipro River in Kyiv early on Friday, killing three people and injuring at least 15, the city's mayor said, in the biggest attack on the crossings linking the capital's two banks since the start of the war.

Mayor Vitali Klitschko said on Telegram that the missiles hit the Podilsko-Voskresenskyi Bridge crossing and the Darnytskyi and Pivdennyi bridges, forcing closures and traffic restrictions across a city of some three million people. Fires broke out and buildings were damaged in the Obolonskyi and Podilskyi districts, and infrastructure was hit in the Holosiivskyi district. A Reuters witness in Kyiv heard a series of explosions in the early hours of Friday.

At least five people were killed across Ukraine in Friday's strikes, according to reports from the day, and more than a dozen were injured outside the capital. Five people were injured in the Dnipropetrovsk region after drones hit apartment buildings, and ten more were wounded in Kharkiv, Ukraine's second-biggest city, after Russian drones hit a hospital. One person was wounded in a drone attack on a tram in Zaporizhzhia, and rescuers extinguished a fire at an industrial facility in the Poltava region after a strike.

The attack on Kyiv was part of a wider wave of Russian strikes across Ukraine overnight and on Friday morning. Explosions were reported in Lviv and Ivano-Frankivsk in the west, where Kalibr cruise missiles and drones were used, and an energy facility was hit, prompting emergency power cuts in several districts. An air raid alert with a red threat level was declared across most districts of the Lviv region at around 6:30 a.m. Russian attacks also damaged railway infrastructure at several locations, delaying trains, Ukrainian Railways said.

The wave also reached across Ukraine's western border. Poland said it was investigating a possible violation of its airspace by unidentified aerial objects after a powerful explosion was reported near the village of Chmielek in the country's east at around 6:30 a.m. Poland scrambled military aircraft and stepped up monitoring of its airspace amid missile activity near Ukraine's western borders.

The strikes came as President Vladimir Putin promised to hit back hard after Ukraine hit the bridge linking Russia and Crimea for a third time this week. That attack, Kyiv's third on the Kerch Bridge in four days, halted rail traffic across the crossing. Russia and Ukraine, in their fifth year of the war, both deny deliberately targeting civilians, and Reuters said it could not immediately independently confirm some of the reports from Friday's strikes.

It is the third time in a fortnight that Kyiv's bridges have been hit. Russian missiles struck the capital's North and South bridges late on Monday, damaging critical infrastructure and cutting heating and water in some districts, and a massive strike hit the Northern bridge on 7 November. Eight major bridges span the Dnipro in Kyiv, and the crossings are the seams of the capital — the links between its two halves, its power and water networks, and its evacuation routes.

The attack also landed in the middle of a diplomatic week. On Thursday, European allies warned against any attempt to decide a peace settlement over their heads, after President Donald Trump embraced Mr Putin as a “friend” at a summit with Chinese leader Xi Jinping, and leaders of the coalition of the willing met in Berlin and said Europe had a united position for talks with Russia.

What happens next: engineers will assess whether the three bridges can carry traffic, and the restrictions will test a city that depends on the crossings for daily movement. Power engineers will work to restore supplies cut by the strikes on energy facilities, and the air raid alerts that ran through the morning are a reminder that the campaign against Ukraine's infrastructure has not paused.

Why it matters: striking three bridges in a single morning, as winter approaches, is a blow aimed at the city's ability to function rather than at a single military target. It is also the sharpest Russian answer yet to Ukraine's campaign against the Kerch Bridge, and it sets the terms for the talks Europe says it is ready to hold: any negotiation now begins with both sides still trying to shape the war from the air.
