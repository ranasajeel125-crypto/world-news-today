At Least 11 Dead After Motorway Pile-Ups in Bulgaria Fog

world · 10 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The Trakia motorway, which runs from Sofia to the Black Sea port of Burgas, was closed in both directions after the pile-ups near the town of Zimnitsa.

At least 11 people have been killed and 46 injured after dozens of cars and lorries were involved in two pile-ups on Bulgaria's Trakia motorway in dense fog, officials said on Tuesday.

The crashes happened at about 7 a.m. local time on Tuesday, 10 November 2026, on opposite carriageways of the same stretch of road near the town of Zimnitsa, in the Yambol region of southeastern Bulgaria. The Trakia motorway, which runs from Sofia to the Black Sea port of Burgas, was closed in both directions after the pile-ups. The first reports came in at 07:10 local time (05:10 GMT), when two calls were received via the 112 emergency telephone number regarding two separate multi-vehicle collisions. A total of 86 vehicles were involved, including more than 20 trucks: 63 vehicles on the Burgas-bound carriageway and 23 on the Sofia-bound carriageway. All of the deaths were on the Burgas-bound side.

Health Minister Katya Ivkova said 10 people died at the scene, near the town of Zimnitsa, and one more died in hospital. Sliven region governor Mihail Kasherov told journalists at the scene that more than 70 vehicles were involved, a figure later revised upwards. The injured were taken by ambulance to hospitals in the Yambol, Sliven and Burgas regions — 51 people were admitted in total, 33 in Sliven, 11 in Yambol and 7 in Burgas, 13 of them seriously hurt. The injured took up to 90 minutes to reach hospital. In the first hours the reported death toll was 12, but it was corrected to 11 after the victims were identified and the number of injured was established. Two days later, on 12 November, a twelfth person died in a hospital in Sliven, and a thirteenth victim, a 42-year-old man from Elhovo, succumbed to his injuries in a hospital in Burgas on 14 November.

Officials said dense fog reduced visibility and was the likely cause of the first crash, which happened on the eastbound carriageway at kilometre 289. The second crash, at kilometre 294 on the Sofia-bound carriageway, was likely also caused by the fog, with drivers also distracted by the first crash, the state news agency BTA reported. Witnesses told police that visibility was at first about 70 to 80 metres, then suddenly dropped to about 10 metres. Police said their initial assessment pointed to reduced visibility due to fast-forming dense fog, a wet road surface and speeds unsuitable for the conditions. The first pile-up began when a lorry blocked the path of a passenger bus; a grain-laden lorry and multiple private vehicles were pulled into the collision, and a car slammed into the rear of the grain lorry. Mangled cars and trucks were strewn along about a kilometre of motorway.

Yambol regional governor Georgi Chalakov said the first reports came in at 07:10 local time (05:10 GMT), and the injured were taken by ambulance to hospitals in the Yambol, Sliven and Burgas regions. He said rescue teams had to remove sections of the crash barrier to reach people, and the fog meant helicopters could not be used in the evacuation. Police, fire and emergency medical teams arrived at the scene, at the 289th kilometre of the motorway near the Zimnitsa junction, in under 10 minutes. Fifteen fire service teams comprising 61 personnel worked on rescue operations and clearing the roadway, and more than 20 soldiers of the 42nd Mechanised Battalion from Yambol helped. Rescuers cut through sheet metal to free those trapped. At 08:10, the BG-Alert system was activated to issue a warning regarding the incident, and the motorway was closed in both directions between the Zimnitsa and Kabile junctions. A partial state of emergency was declared for the municipalities of Straldzha, Tundzha and Yambol, taking effect at 7:30 a.m. Traffic in the direction of Sofia was restored at 01:20 on 11 November, after the roadway had been cleared and washed down.

Road administration director Galin Kostov said speeds had been unsuitable for the conditions, which included wet road surfaces, and the motorway would remain closed until it had been cleared. Traffic towards Sofia was diverted via Karnobat and Sliven, and towards Burgas via Gorno Aleksandrovo, Zimnitsa and Karnobat.

One witness who helped rescue a man from a burning vehicle blamed "very thick fog" for the pile-up. "I got out of the car, I ran forward and pulled that man out," the witness told Reuters. Another witness said "many, many people died".

Prime Minister Rumen Radev, who had been hosting European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, cut short a news conference after the news. Radev declared Wednesday, 11 November, a national day of mourning, suspended the bilateral meeting with von der Leyen and described the day as a dark one for all of Bulgaria; parliament cancelled all scheduled sessions.

The investigation is only beginning. Ten investigators from the National Investigation Service, with forensic specialists and a mobile laboratory, took over the case under the supervision of the Yambol District Prosecution Office. Two pre-trial proceedings have been opened, more than 100 people will be questioned and more than 50 forensic medical examinations are expected. A Romanian truck driver was detained after the crashes. Acting service director Marian Marinov said a lorry did not cause one of the pile-ups, but it was an obstacle on the road. Investigators are checking two versions of what happened with the lorry: in the first, it stopped because of a car in front of it; in the second, it slowed down because of fog so thick that nothing could be seen, and the driver behind did not adjust. Investigators have yet to decide how much blame the driver of the bus that hit the lorry carries. "Fog is not an obstacle on the road, but a condition. One of the duties of a driver is to take it into account and choose his speed," Marinov said, adding that responsibility should be sought from everyone who drove into the fog hoping there was no crash ahead and hit a car they did not see. Marinov called the crash very complex, with no ready-made method for investigating it. A combined expert report will establish how the collisions happened, how the injuries were caused and what each driver did, and whether each driver contributed to the injury or death of their own passengers or other people's.

The disaster has renewed attention on Bulgaria's road safety record. The country's roads rank as the most dangerous in the European Union, and the Trakia pile-ups were one of the deadliest highway disasters in Bulgaria's modern history. Officials said better marking of fog-prone stretches would be considered, and the Yambol regional governor warned that fog had already returned to the same stretch of motorway the day after the crashes.
