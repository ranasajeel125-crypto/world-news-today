At Least 11 Dead After Motorway Pile-Ups in Bulgaria Fog

world · 10 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The Trakia motorway, which runs from Sofia to the Black Sea port of Burgas, was closed in both directions after the pile-ups near the town of Zimnitsa.

At least 11 people have been killed and 46 injured after dozens of cars and lorries were involved in two pile-ups on Bulgaria's Trakia motorway in dense fog, officials said on Tuesday.

Health Minister Katya Ivkova said 10 people died at the scene, near the town of Zimnitsa, and one more died in hospital. Sliven region governor Mihail Kasherov told journalists at the scene that more than 70 vehicles were involved.

Officials said dense fog reduced visibility and was the likely cause of the first crash, which happened on the eastbound carriageway. The second crash, on the Sofia-bound carriageway, was likely also caused by the fog, with drivers also distracted by the first crash, the state news agency BTA reported.

Yambol regional governor Georgi Chalakov said the first reports came in at 07:10 local time (05:10 GMT), and the injured were taken by ambulance to hospitals in the Yambol, Sliven and Burgas regions. He said rescue teams had to remove sections of the crash barrier to reach people, and the fog meant helicopters could not be used in the evacuation.

Road administration director Galin Kostov said speeds had been unsuitable for the conditions, which included wet road surfaces, and the motorway would remain closed until it had been cleared.

One witness who helped rescue a man from a burning vehicle blamed "very thick fog" for the pile-up. "I got out of the car, I ran forward and pulled that man out," the witness told Reuters. Another witness said "many, many people died".

Prime Minister Rumen Radev, who had been hosting European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, cut short a news conference after the news. The Trakia motorway runs from west to east across Bulgaria, through the capital Sofia to the Black Sea port of Burgas.
