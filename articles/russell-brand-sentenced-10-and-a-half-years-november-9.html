Russell Brand Jailed for 10-and-a-Half Years for Rape and Sexual Assault

world · 9 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The judge called Brand a sexual predator who believed fame meant he could act as he liked; he must serve two-thirds of the sentence before parole.

British comedian and actor Russell Brand was jailed for 10-and-a-half years on Monday after being convicted of raping one woman and sexually assaulting three more around two decades ago.

The 51-year-old stood in the dock at London's Southwark Crown Court as Judge Joel Bennathan sentenced him to 10 years and six months, nine years for the rape and 18 months for the three sexual assaults, and told him he must serve two-thirds of the sentence before becoming eligible for parole. That means he will have to complete at least seven years in prison before he can be considered for release.

The judge described Brand as a sexual predator who believed "fame meant you could act how you liked, when you liked", and condemned what he called his "staggering arrogance". In his sentencing remarks, the judge said Brand's "disregard of any woman's right to say no" had "profound and lasting" consequences for his victims, and he tied the crimes to Brand's rise to fame.

Brand was found guilty on Thursday, 5 November, of one count of rape and three counts of sexual assault, for offences committed between 2004 and 2009. He was acquitted of two further counts of rape and one count of attempted indecent assault. He had denied all the charges. The trial heard testimony from six women about incidents spanning 2004 to 2009.

The rape was of a 21-year-old fan in 2009 at his London home, days after meeting her at one of his stand-up shows at the Hammersmith Apollo in west London. The three sexual assaults were of women who worked in television and radio. The judge placed the rape in category 2B of the Sentencing Council's guidelines for rape, a category that carries a jail term of between seven and nine years, and he gave Brand nine years for that offence. The sexual assault sentences, of between nine and 18 months, run concurrently with one another but consecutively to the nine-year rape sentence, creating the total of 10 years and six months.

Brand, who rose to fame in the early 2000s hosting television programmes and his own BBC radio show before becoming a Hollywood film star, was remanded in custody after the verdicts and spent the weekend at HMP Wandsworth before being brought back to court on Monday to be sentenced. His lawyer told the court he is a "fundamentally different person" from the man who committed the offences, but the judge's remarks made clear that he regarded the offending as serious and sustained, and aggravated by Brand's use of his fame.

London's Metropolitan Police said it is continuing to investigate two further allegations of rape against Brand, the BBC reported. Detective Superintendent Andy Furphy of the Metropolitan Police and David Roberts of the Crown Prosecution Service spoke to the media outside Southwark Crown Court after the sentencing.

Brand was once one of Britain's most high-profile broadcasters. He began as a stand-up comedian, became a presenter on MTV and the BBC, and went on to star in Hollywood films. He was married to the American pop singer Katy Perry, and in recent years he moved away from the mainstream, building a large online following with videos about politics, spirituality and conspiracy theories. The case against him began after an investigation by journalists, and the charges were brought by the Crown Prosecution Service after police examined the allegations.

The sentencing has been widely discussed in Britain and abroad, and it has become entangled in a debate about whether the justice system treats defendants differently according to their background. Donald Trump Jr., the son of the US president, defended Brand, saying the sentence had less to do with his crimes than with his recent turn to God and to conservative politics, and claiming that Brand was the victim of a "two-tier" justice system. A Reuters fact check examined online claims comparing Brand's sentence with that of another offender, Hamoud Al Soaimi, who was given a two-year suspended sentence in 2024 after being convicted of sexual assault and assault of a child under 13. The fact check found the comparison missed important context: Al Soaimi was 15 years old at the time of his offending, which the sentencing judge treated as significant mitigation, and the two cases were not directly comparable because Brand was convicted of a category 2B rape, which carries a longer maximum sentence.

The case is also part of a wider reckoning in British entertainment and media. In recent years, a series of high-profile figures have been investigated and prosecuted for sexual offences committed years earlier, and the willingness of complainants to come forward has been shaped by the MeToo movement and by the experience of earlier cases. Prosecutions for historic sexual offences face particular challenges: there is often no forensic evidence, and cases depend on the testimony of complainants and on the credibility of the defendant, which is why they can take years to reach court and why the outcomes are closely watched.

For the victims, the sentencing brought an end to a long process, but the judge's words about the "profound and lasting" consequences of the offences were a recognition that a prison sentence does not undo what happened. Brand listened to victim impact statements during the sentencing hearing, and the court heard about the effect of the offences on the women involved.

Brand can appeal against his conviction and sentence, and it is not yet clear whether he will do so. For now, he faces at least seven years in prison, and the Metropolitan Police's continuing investigation into two further allegations of rape means the legal process around him may not be over.
