At Least 14 Missing After Russian Drone Hits Apartment Block in Kyiv Region

world · 22 Nov 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Rescue workers search the rubble of a five-storey apartment block in Dymer, in Ukraine's Kyiv region, after a Russian drone strike collapsed part of the building on Sunday morning.

At least 14 people may still be trapped under the rubble of a five-storey residential building in Dymer, in Ukraine's Kyiv region, after it was struck by a Russian drone on Sunday morning, President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said. Part of the apartment block collapsed in the attack, and rescue teams were still working through the debris hours later, with no confirmed outcome for those still missing at the time of the first reports.

“Part of a five-story apartment building has collapsed following a Russian drone attack. I thank our first responders for acting quickly: nine people have already been evacuated, and the fire has been contained,” Zelenskiy said in a post on X, according to Reuters. Local officials said two people, including a teenager, were injured in the strike.

The building stands in Dymer, a town north of the capital, Kyiv. Euronews reported that the drone destroyed a section of the block from the first floor to the fifth, leaving a large pile of rubble where flats once stood. Footage released by Ukraine's State Emergency Service showed rescuers picking through the debris, and helping residents down from the damaged upper floors by ladder and crane platform. Nine residents, including one child, had been evacuated, according to Ukrainian authorities.

The number of people feared trapped changed as the morning went on. Ukraine's State Emergency Service initially said that up to seven people might be buried beneath the rubble. Zelenskiy later put the figure higher, saying 14 people were unaccounted for. That is the figure rescuers are working to, and it may still change as the search continues and as families and neighbours report who was in the building when it was hit.

The Dymer strike was not an isolated attack. It was part of a wider wave of Russian drone strikes reported across Ukraine overnight, Euronews reported. Ukraine's Air Force said 186 attack drones were launched in the overnight wave, of which 161 were shot down. In Kharkiv, a strike drone hit the Kyivskyi district and started a fire. In Chernihiv, a Russian strike set a wood-processing plant ablaze, while in Zaporizhzhia three people, including two children, were injured in an evening attack.

The strike comes at the end of a week of heavy Russian attacks on Ukrainian cities. On the night of 19 to 20 November, Russian forces launched what the Institute for the Study of War described as the largest missile and drone strike of November 2026 against Ukraine: 27 ballistic missiles, 84 cruise missiles and 404 Shahed and Gerbera-type strike drones, according to President Zelenskiy and the Ukrainian Air Force. At least eight people were killed in that attack, and the BBC later reported at least nine dead across the country, seven in Kyiv and two in Lviv, with at least 28 injured.

That Friday attack hit infrastructure across at least 15 Ukrainian regions and the capital. Ballistic missiles struck three bridges across the Dnipro river in Kyiv — the Podilsko-Voskresensky, Darnytskyi and Southern bridges — and all three were closed to traffic. Residential buildings and energy facilities were hit in several cities, and local officials reported water, power and heating outages in Kyiv and other parts of the country. In Lviv, two people were killed when the Sknyliv railway station was badly damaged.

The same attack also reached beyond Ukraine's borders. Two missiles and a jet-powered drone crashed in eastern Poland, Polish Defence Minister Wladyslaw Kosiniak-Kamysz said, including a Russian Kh-101 cruise missile that came down at Nowy Lipowiec, about 50 kilometres from the Ukrainian border. Polish and Swedish jets were scrambled in response. The Institute for the Study of War assessed that the Kremlin conducted the airspace violations to coincide with an ongoing Coalition of the Willing military exercise at Nowa Deba in Poland, in an effort to deter the coalition's support for Ukraine and test NATO responses.

The attacks of the past few days have also spilled into the Baltic Sea. On Saturday, a Russian cargo ship bound for the exclave of Kaliningrad was hit by drones and caught fire in the Gulf of Finland, drifting into Finnish waters, in what Finland's military said was likely an attack by Ukraine. The vessel, the Sparta II, is still burning, according to Finland's coast guard. Ukraine has sent waves of military drones towards Russia's north-western Leningrad region this year, including some that strayed into the airspace of nearby Baltic countries, Reuters reported.

The scale of the week's attacks was set out by Zelenskiy on Sunday. He said Russia had launched around 2,700 drones, close to 1,460 aerial bombs and 165 missiles at 20 Ukrainian regions over the week, according to Euronews, and that a significant number of the missiles were ballistic, including North Korean ones. Those figures come from the Ukrainian president and have not been independently verified, but they match the pattern of the week's reporting: near-nightly drone waves, and a major missile attack on Friday that Ukrainian officials said was the biggest of the month.

The timing of the Friday attack was striking. It came hours after President Vladimir Putin held talks on the war with US President Donald Trump on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, Bloomberg reported. It also came days after a Ukrainian strike badly damaged the Crimean Bridge, the road and rail link Russia built between Russia and occupied Crimea, which the BBC said was the trigger for the Russian response. Zelenskiy said of the Friday strikes: “Absolutely all the targets were ordinary civilian infrastructure – things that literally sustain people's lives. Energy facilities, logistics – bridges across the Dnipro, railway infrastructure.”

Diplomacy has continued alongside the fighting. European leaders meeting in Berlin, among them the UK's Andy Burnham, France's Emmanuel Macron and Germany's Friedrich Merz, declared themselves open to peace talks that include Moscow, the BBC reported. And on Sunday the BBC reported that Britain's Health Secretary, Wes Streeting, had met Zelenskiy in Kyiv, calling Putin a “cold-blooded killer”. But the strikes of the past few days show that, for now, the war is intensifying rather than pausing for negotiation.

For the people of Dymer, the immediate question is simpler and more urgent: who is still under the rubble. Rescue workers remained at the scene through Sunday, clearing debris by hand and with machinery, and trying to reach anyone who may have survived the collapse. Until the search is complete, the toll of this strike — like the toll of so many before it in this war — will remain a number that can still change.

Why it matters: the Dymer strike is a reminder that, even as leaders talk about peace and summits, the war in Ukraine is still being fought over the homes of ordinary people. A five-storey apartment block in a small town north of Kyiv is not a military target, and the 14 people unaccounted for there are the latest civilians caught in a campaign that has now hit bridges, railway stations, power plants and homes in the same week — and spilled over into Poland and the Baltic Sea. Until a ceasefire holds, every night brings the same risk to another town, another building, and another family waiting to hear whether their loved ones are among the rescued or the missing.
