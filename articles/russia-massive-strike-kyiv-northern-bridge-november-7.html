Russia Launches Massive Overnight Strike on Kyiv, Hitting Northern Bridge and Energy Sites

world · 7 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Russia's defence ministry said the strikes hit the Northern Bridge and the Meridian plant; Ukrainian officials said one person was killed in the Kyiv region and six people were injured in the capital.

Russia said its forces carried out a massive overnight strike on what it described as targets in Ukraine's military-industrial sector and energy and transport infrastructure in Kyiv and the surrounding region, Reuters reported.

Russia's defence ministry said the strikes hit the Northern Bridge, which it said was being used to transport weapons, military equipment and vehicles for the Ukrainian armed forces, and the Meridian plant, which Russia says produces Flamingo cruise missiles. President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said six ballistic missiles were aimed at the bridge and four struck it. Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said the roadbed, trolleybus contact line and power line were damaged, and the bridge was closed to traffic in both directions while road workers and utility workers repaired it.

The ministry also said the strikes damaged several energy and industrial facilities in the Kyiv region, including a power plant, an electricity substation and a munitions manufacturing facility. It added that Russian forces struck an electricity substation in the Zhytomyr region and a cargo vessel in the Black Sea that it said was transporting military equipment and dual-use goods to Ukraine. Zelenskyy said the attack hit a substation in Kyiv Oblast that connects the western Rivne and Khmelnytskyi nuclear power plants to Kyiv and Ukraine's central regions, and that substations were also damaged in Vinnytsia, Zhytomyr, Rivne, Kharkiv and Dnipro oblasts, causing power cuts in five regions.

Kyiv Mayor Vitali Klitschko said Russian forces launched a missile attack on the capital early on Saturday. "Explosions in the Kyiv suburbs. The enemy is hitting infrastructure with ballistic missiles," he wrote on Telegram. Debris struck a five-storey apartment building in a western district of the capital, the facade of another residential building was damaged, and the city's military administration said a building housing a kindergarten was damaged. The State Emergency Service reported damage to residential buildings, a kindergarten and a non-residential building in Kyiv's Solomianskyi district, which was hit hardest. By the morning, a group of women had come to clean the rubble in the damaged kindergarten, working quickly and in an organised way.

Ukrainian officials said one person was killed in the Kyiv region and six people were injured in the capital, Kyiv Post reported. Authorities later reported that eight people, including a child, had been wounded in the overnight strikes in Kyiv, and nine people, including a child, were wounded across the country. The attack on the wider Kyiv Oblast killed one person and injured two others, while damaging energy facilities in the Bucha and Obukhiv districts, and five districts in the region came under attack. Across Ukraine, Russian attacks killed two people and injured 37 others over the past day. The strikes disrupted electricity, heating and water supplies in the capital, straining Kyiv's power supply and heating systems as temperatures headed towards zero in a city of more than three million people.

The Ukrainian air force said Russia launched 36 ballistic missiles and 172 drones in the overnight attack and that it had neutralised or suppressed four missiles and 149 drones by 09:00. All 36 ballistic missiles were aimed at Kyiv and the surrounding region, according to Zelenskyy, and only four were intercepted, which he attributed to a lack of Patriot interceptors. The drones included 137 jet-powered Shahed-type drones, and the missiles included an unspecified number of Iskander-M, Zircon, KN-23 and S-400 types. Zelenskyy said Russia had used North Korean-made ballistic missiles to strike energy facilities, citing preliminary findings from investigations at impact sites.

The attack came in waves through the night. The first barrage struck Kyiv in quick succession, with the air force reporting at least six missiles launched at the city in the span of a few minutes shortly before 11 p.m. local time on 6 November. A fresh missile wave targeted the city shortly after 1 a.m., with at least six loud blasts reported, and explosions again rocked the city at 3:04 a.m. and 3:30 a.m. Moscow has expanded its campaign in recent weeks to target Kyiv's bridges, snarling traffic in the capital, and Zelenskyy said the Russians were striking bridges across the Dnipro from Kyiv to Zaporizhzhia.

The attack came hours after President Volodymyr Zelenskiy warned in his nightly video address that Russia was preparing new strikes on energy targets, bridges across the Dnipro River and civilian infrastructure. "Russia is preparing for trilateral talks by launching ballistic missiles at Ukraine's critical infrastructure," Zelenskyy said on Saturday, as repair and rescue operations got under way wherever the security situation allowed. It also came three days before US-led talks including Ukrainian and Russian negotiators open in Abu Dhabi on 10-11 November, with the Kremlin saying its delegation will include aide Vladimir Medinsky and military intelligence chief Igor Kostyukov, Reuters reported. Russia said its air defences intercepted 411 Ukrainian drones across ten regions, as well as over the Black Sea, the Sea of Azov and Crimea, and Russian media reported that a Ukrainian attack sparked a blaze at Oryol's Proton-Electrotex plant, which produces components for military equipment.
