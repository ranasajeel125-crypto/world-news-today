Ukraine Says Starlink Disruption Lifted as Europe Weighs a Satellite Back-Up

technology · 25 Oct 2026 · 4 min read · By Tom Becker (Technology Editor)

Ukraine's battlefield communications depend heavily on commercial satellite networks such as Starlink.

Ukraine's military says restrictions on the Starlink satellite service have been lifted and the network is working normally again, after a disruption that underlined how dependent the country's defence has become on a single commercial provider.

Starlink terminals are woven through Ukraine's command structure, guiding drones, coordinating artillery and keeping units connected where terrestrial networks have been destroyed. Any interruption is felt immediately at the front — which is why reports of restrictions, and claims about how the service is used, have become politically explosive.

Elon Musk has accused Ukraine of misusing Starlink for drone attacks, sparking a major controversy and reviving a debate European governments have been having for two years: can Europe plug the gap with a sovereign satellite network of its own?

Brussels is funding the IRIS² constellation, but it will not be fully operational for years. In the meantime, Ukraine — and Europe's own militaries — remain reliant on American commercial systems, a dependency that strategists in Paris, Berlin and Warsaw increasingly describe as a vulnerability rather than a convenience.
