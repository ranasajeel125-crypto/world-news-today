Iran Lifts Self-Imposed Limit on Ballistic Missile Ranges, Revolutionary Guard Says

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said on Saturday, 7 November, it has lifted a self-imposed limit of about 2,000 kilometres (1,200 miles) on the range of its ballistic missiles, suggesting Tehran may seek weapons capable of reaching more distant targets as fears of renewed all-out war rise, the Associated Press reported. The announcement, made at a news conference in Tehran marking Aerospace Week, was the clearest sign yet that Iran is shifting towards what it calls an "offensive" military doctrine after eight months of war with the United States and Israel.

Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohebbi, the Guard's spokesman, told a news conference in Tehran marking Aerospace Week that the restriction "has been lifted for us," according to state-linked outlets including Tasnim and Mehr. Asked about the limit on missile ranges, Mohebbi said: "That restriction has been lifted for us." He did not say whether Iran already had missiles that exceed the cap, and Iran is not known to possess missiles capable of going beyond the 2,000-kilometre limit, although US officials have warned that technology developed for its space programme could be adapted to build intercontinental ballistic missiles.

Mohebbi said Iran must demonstrate any expanded capability in the field rather than merely announce it. "Our duty is to show our capabilities on the battlefield," he said, adding that Tehran was prepared to respond to threats without geographical limitations. "Iran will demonstrate these capabilities in practice," he said, rather than make statements. He went further, saying that if Iran decided to change its nuclear doctrine and build a bomb, the necessary preparations would be made. The remarks followed comments by Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei ordering an immediate and effective response to any threat without geographical limits, and a broader update of military target lists covering US and Israeli assets.

The 2,000-kilometre cap dated from 2017, when it was set under the former supreme leader as a deliberate ceiling that kept Iranian missiles focused on regional targets. The range puts parts of Greece and the Balkans, the entire Arabian Peninsula, the Levant and most of Egypt within reach of Iran's missiles. Lifting the cap raises questions over whether Tehran now considers hitting targets beyond the Middle East, including in Europe, and it alarmed governments across the region, which have spent the year trying to prevent a return to full-scale fighting. There is no evidence Iran currently possesses operational missiles capable of exceeding the former limit, and Mohebbi cautioned against treating the announcement as proof of a new operational capability.

Mohebbi also claimed advances in air defences and underground missile production after recent fighting, including the downing of enemy aircraft and drones. Those claims could not be independently verified. Days later, the Revolutionary Guard released footage of an underground missile facility, dubbed a "missile city" by Iranian media, showing ballistic and cruise missiles, mobile launchers and military vehicles, in a ceremony attended by senior military officials including IRGC Aerospace Force commander Majid Mousavi.

The announcement comes amid stalled US-Iran talks and rising fears of renewed strikes across the region. The war began on 28 February 2026, when US and Israeli forces launched Operation Epic Fury, carrying out nearly 900 airstrikes targeting Iranian military infrastructure, air defences and leadership. Iran answered with missile and drone attacks across the region and closed the Strait of Hormuz, the narrow waterway that normally carries about a fifth of the world's oil. A ceasefire brokered by Pakistan in April, followed by talks in Islamabad involving US Vice-President JD Vance, envoy Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, ended without agreement over Iran's uranium stockpile and the reopening of the strait. On 14 June, Trump announced an agreement to end hostilities, reopen the Strait of Hormuz and lift the US naval blockade on Iranian ports, and a memorandum of understanding was signed on 17 June, but on 8 July Trump declared it "over", and both sides resumed exchanging fire. At least 620 Iranian missiles and drones have been launched since 7 July, and Brent crude oil was trading at about $93.59 a barrel on 9 November.

President Donald Trump confirmed on Friday that the United States had carried out limited strikes on an air defence system on Iran's Qeshm Island, and US, Ukrainian and Russian delegations are due to meet in Abu Dhabi on 10-11 November. On 2 November, Iranian forces attempted to target an American aircraft near Qeshm Island, and American forces struck the island without announcement on the eve of the midterm elections. On 6 November, Trump said the strike took about 15 minutes, saying the Iranians had "built a little bit of an anti-aircraft device and we knocked it out". On 7 November, US Central Command announced the establishment of Task Force Iron Gate, a new team that will monitor, detect and disrupt the illicit movement of advanced weapons systems, dual-use technologies, sanctioned materials and illegal revenue streams, as the United States widened its campaign to isolate Iran by targeting military supply routes across its land borders and the Caspian Sea. Washington says its naval blockade has stopped more than $25 billion in Iranian oil exports.

Qatar and other regional mediators have intensified efforts to prevent a return to full-scale fighting, but talks have stalled over whether the United States should first ease sanctions and the blockade or Iran should first accept nuclear limits. On 8 November, Trump told CNN it was "highly unlikely it's going to be necessary" for him to order a return to major combat operations against Iran, and the following day he said the war was "effectively over" militarily, though US forces remained in the region. Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi said on 8 November that Iran's two-day proposal for a ceasefire still stood, if the requirements, including an end to the war on all fronts, were met. Whether the lifting of the missile range limit is a warning, a negotiating signal or the prelude to a new round of escalation remains unclear, and analysts say the answer will depend on whether either side demonstrates the longer-range capability Mohebbi described, on the battlefield rather than in words.
