Iran Lifts Self-Imposed Limit on Ballistic Missile Ranges, Revolutionary Guard Says

world · 7 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

IRGC spokesman Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohebbi said the restriction “has been lifted for us”; he said Iran must demonstrate any longer-range capability on the battlefield rather than merely announce it.

Iran's Revolutionary Guard said on Saturday it has lifted a self-imposed limit of about 2,000 kilometres (1,200 miles) on the range of its ballistic missiles, suggesting Tehran may seek weapons capable of reaching more distant targets as fears of renewed all-out war rise, the Associated Press reported.

Brig. Gen. Hossein Mohebbi, the Guard's spokesman, told a news conference in Tehran marking Aerospace Week that the restriction “has been lifted for us,” according to state-linked outlets including Tasnim and Mehr.

Mohebbi said Iran must demonstrate any expanded capability in the field rather than merely announce it. “Our duty is to show our capabilities on the battlefield,” he said, adding that Tehran was prepared to respond to threats without geographical limitations.

The remarks followed comments by Supreme Leader Mojtaba Khamenei ordering an immediate and effective response to any threat without geographical limits, and a broader update of military target lists covering US and Israeli assets.

The 2,000-kilometre cap dated from 2017, when it was set under the former supreme leader as a deliberate ceiling that kept Iranian missiles focused on regional targets. There is no evidence Iran currently possesses operational missiles capable of exceeding the former limit, and Mohebbi cautioned against treating the announcement as proof of a new operational capability.

Mohebbi also claimed advances in air defences and underground missile production after recent fighting, including the downing of enemy aircraft and drones. Those claims could not be independently verified.

The announcement comes amid stalled US-Iran talks and rising fears of renewed strikes across the region. President Donald Trump confirmed on Friday that the United States had carried out limited strikes on an air defence system on Iran's Qeshm Island, and US, Ukrainian and Russian delegations are due to meet in Abu Dhabi on 10-11 November.
