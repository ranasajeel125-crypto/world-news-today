US Floated Idea of Ukraine Ceding Donbas in Exchange for NATO Membership, Zelenskiy Says

world · 14 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said US negotiators raised the idea after the latest round of peace talks in Abu Dhabi.

US negotiators have floated the idea of Ukraine withdrawing from its eastern Donbas region in exchange for joining NATO, President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said on Saturday — the most consequential new idea to surface from the latest round of US-brokered peace talks with Russia in Abu Dhabi.

“The Americans raised a question: ‘What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?’” Zelenskiy said in remarks to reporters released by his office on Saturday. “That is why I believe NATO is back on the agenda. But you have also heard (Russian President Vladimir) Putin’s signals, and this is an extremely difficult conversation.” It is the first time the question of linking NATO membership to territorial concessions has been raised during the talks, Zelenskiy said.

The Donbas — the Donetsk and Luhansk regions of eastern Ukraine — has been at the centre of the war since Russian-backed separatists seized parts of it in 2014, and its full capture has been one of Moscow’s central war aims since the full-scale invasion in 2022. Any Ukrainian withdrawal from the parts of the region it still holds would be the largest territorial concession Kyiv has ever contemplated, and one no Ukrainian leader has previously put to the public.

Putin has long said Ukrainian membership of NATO is a red line for Moscow, citing the issue as one reason for launching Russia’s full-scale invasion in 2022. That makes the trade raised by the American side a paradox at the heart of the negotiation: the prize on offer — NATO membership — is the very outcome Moscow has said it went to war to prevent.

Zelenskiy reiterated that Kyiv was not ready to concede territory, but he opened a door that Ukrainian leaders have kept firmly shut until now. If Ukraine received a comprehensive package — including security guarantees, the right to a strong and well-funded army, and funds to rebuild — he said he would put the question to a national referendum.

Putting the question to a referendum would shift the decision from the negotiating room to the Ukrainian public — a recognition of how politically explosive any deal involving territory would be at home, where conceding land has been treated as a red line of its own.

Echoing comments made by European leaders at a summit in Dublin on Friday, Zelenskiy dismissed Russia’s call for a broad agreement on the European security architecture to be reached before the specifics of ending the war are agreed. “We cannot turn everything upside down and start by talking about changing the security framework in the world or across the continent overall, and only then talk about ending this war. This war must be ended first,” he said.

Zelenskiy also dismissed Putin’s suggestion for a local humanitarian ceasefire in Kostiantynivka, a hotly contested city in Ukraine’s eastern Donetsk region, saying it reflected Moscow’s inability to take the city by force. Civilians there have already had the opportunity to evacuate, he added. “Putin simply wants us to leave Kostiantynivka so that they can move in and occupy the entire city that way,” he said.

Ukraine has given a list of seven or eight other frontline settlements where civilians, including children, are trapped, for a potential humanitarian truce, but Russia prefers to use them as military targets, Zelenskiy said. Moscow denies deliberately targeting civilians in the war.

The two days of US-brokered talks between Ukraine and Russia concluded on Wednesday in the Emirati capital without a major breakthrough, though the sides agreed to a prisoner exchange that will involve more than 1,000 people, Zelenskiy said. The final numbers for the prisoner swap were still under discussion.

US peace envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner had pledged to raise an energy ceasefire with Putin at a meeting in Moscow on Thursday, Zelenskiy said, but there was no news of any progress after their talks. “Putin personally does not support a ceasefire,” he said. Zelenskiy met Witkoff and Kushner on Thursday in Ireland after their talks in Moscow.

He said the US negotiators had told him that all sides had agreed for Europe to have a seat at the negotiating table in forthcoming peace talks. In Abu Dhabi, delegates from Europe — which is now Ukraine’s largest financial and military backer — had talks with US and Ukrainian officials but did not sit at the table with Russia. At the summit in Ireland, EU leaders agreed that Europe’s team for any Ukraine peace talks would comprise five major military powers — non-EU member Britain plus France, Germany, Italy and Poland — as well as EU officials.

More talks are planned, the envoys have said, but no date has been announced for the next round. The first test will be whether the Donbas-for-NATO idea survives contact with Moscow, which has called NATO membership for Ukraine a red line while pressing its own territorial demands. The second will be whether Europe — now with a seat at the table — accepts a trade that redraws Ukraine’s map in exchange for the alliance membership Kyiv has sought since the war began.

Why it matters: until now, the peace talks have treated territory and NATO membership as separate questions — land to be negotiated on the battlefield’s terms, and alliance membership as a distant, almost theoretical prospect. Linking the two changes the shape of any deal: it offers Kyiv the security guarantee it has wanted most, at the price of the land it has fought hardest to hold, and it asks Moscow to accept the outcome it has called a red line. That is why Zelenskiy called the conversation “extremely difficult” — and why the next round of talks, whenever it comes, will be watched for whether this idea is a serious proposal, a negotiating probe, or the first outline of an end to the war.
