ECB Holds Rates Steady as Energy Shock Lifts Inflation Risks — Euro Slips, December Hike Bets Firm

business · 29 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (Business Desk)

The ECB kept its deposit rate at 2.50% on Thursday, saying energy prices have continued to rise and pose upside risks to inflation, while the euro fell to its lowest since last May.

The European Central Bank left interest rates unchanged on Thursday in a well-telegraphed move, and said inflation risks were on the rise from surging energy costs — a warning that could solidify market bets for a December rate hike, Reuters reported.

The Governing Council kept the three key ECB interest rates unchanged: the deposit facility at 2.50%, the main refinancing operations rate at 2.65% and the marginal lending facility at 2.90%, the ECB said in its monetary policy statement.

"Energy prices have continued to rise, posing upside risks to the inflation outlook and downside risks to the growth outlook," the ECB said. "While the full effects of the energy shock are still unfolding, underlying inflation has remained broadly stable so far and wages do not show a material response at this stage."

The central bank added that financing conditions had tightened since its last meeting, which is likely to weigh on both inflation and growth, and said it would follow a data-dependent, meeting-by-meeting approach, without pre-committing to a particular rate path.

The euro fell to its lowest since last May, trading around $1.11, while rate-sensitive two-year government bond yields fell sharply. Germany's 10-year bond yield, the euro zone benchmark, was down about 6 basis points at 3.32%, and European stocks edged higher, with the broad STOXX 600 index up around 1% on the day.

The decision came on the same day ECB President Christine Lagarde announced her resignation, effective 7 February 2027 — a separate development covered in World News Today's earlier report — leaving markets to weigh both the policy outlook and the race to succeed her. The ECB has already raised rates twice since June as policymakers seek to prevent an oil-price shock linked to the Iran conflict from feeding into other prices and wages, and money markets are pricing roughly three further rate increases, which would take the deposit rate to 3.25%.

Attention now turns to the Bank of Japan, which concludes its two-day meeting on Friday.
