Indian Opposition Leader Rahul Gandhi Acquitted in Defamation Case

world · 26 Oct 2026 · 6 min read · By Arjun Mehta (South Asia Correspondent)

Gandhi was in court in Sultanpur, in Uttar Pradesh state, when the verdict was pronounced on Monday.

Rahul Gandhi, the leader of India's main opposition Congress party, was acquitted in a defamation case by a local court on Monday, television channels reported.

Gandhi, 56, the scion of the Nehru-Gandhi political dynasty and the Leader of the Opposition in the Lok Sabha, was in court in the northern town of Sultanpur, in Uttar Pradesh state, when the verdict was pronounced. His supporters burst crackers, beat drums and waved party flags as he left the court after the acquittal, images on television showed. Congress workers also distributed sweets outside the court complex.

The case dated back more than eight years. Gandhi was accused of calling India's Home Minister Amit Shah, who was then head of the ruling Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP), a "murder accused" during a press conference in Bengaluru on 8 May 2018, days before the Karnataka Assembly election. A journalist had asked Gandhi about Shah's defence of BJP leader and former Karnataka chief minister B. S. Yediyurappa, noting that Yediyurappa had received a clean chit from the courts. Gandhi responded by questioning Shah's credibility, referring to the Sohrabuddin encounter case and the death of the judge hearing it, B. H. Loya. "People in India forget that the president of BJP is a murder accused. That's the truth. The party that talks about honesty, decency, has a person who has been accused of murder as president," he said.

The complaint was filed in Sultanpur town in Uttar Pradesh state on 4 August 2018 by Vijay Mishra, a local BJP leader who was the party's district vice-president and a former chairman of the District Cooperative Bank in Sultanpur. Mishra alleged that the remarks were defamatory and had harmed Shah's reputation and, in a way, his own standing as a party worker. Gandhi disputed the allegations, saying the statement had been presented out of context, and his lawyers described the case as politically motivated.

The reference at the heart of the case was to the 2005 killing of Sohrabuddin Sheikh, who was shot dead by police teams from Gujarat and Rajasthan in what investigators later alleged was a staged encounter, along with the subsequent murder of his wife Kausar Bi and the 2006 killing of Sohrabuddin's associate Tulsiram Prajapati. Shah, who was Gujarat's home minister at the time, was among those accused in the case, but a special CBI court in Mumbai discharged him in December 2014. In April 2018, the Supreme Court dismissed petitions seeking an independent inquiry into the death of Judge Loya, who had been hearing the case, holding that the material before it indicated that he died of natural causes.

The Sultanpur court summoned Gandhi to face trial in November 2023. He appeared before the court on 20 February 2024 and was granted bail after furnishing a personal bond and sureties. His statement was recorded in court in July 2024, the complainant's testimony was recorded that October, and further witness testimony and Gandhi's statement during examination of the accused followed in 2025 and 2026. Mishra also sought to have Gandhi's voice sample matched with the recording of the Bengaluru event; the trial court rejected the application in May 2026, and subsequent challenges to that decision were dismissed by higher courts. Arguments concluded in October, with the court initially scheduling its verdict for 13 October before moving it to 26 October.

Additional Chief Judicial Magistrate Shubham Verma of the special MP-MLA court in Sultanpur acquitted Gandhi in a 49-page judgment written in Hindi. The court held that Mishra had failed to establish how the statement affected or harmed his reputation, and that he was not an "aggrieved person" entitled to file the complaint in the first place. The court observed that Gandhi's remarks did not name Mishra and only referred to Shah and the BJP in the broader political context, and that the remarks made during the Karnataka election campaign did not meet the threshold for criminal defamation in the context of political discourse.

The judgment was unsparing about the complaint itself. The court observed that the manner in which the facts had been described in the complaint appeared prima facie "suspicious", "unrealistic" and "uncorroborated", and suggested two possible explanations: either the complainant was of an overly sensitive nature, or the complaint had been instituted for political or other motives. It noted that the complaint's narrative, considered as a whole, appeared to have been brought merely for the purpose of instituting proceedings, by any means possible, which was contrary to the intent of the law. The court also noted that Mishra had said he first learnt of the comment more than two months after it was uttered, when two party colleagues came to his house, played the clip on a phone and urged him to leave the party.

The verdict turned on a basic question in Indian defamation law: who is allowed to complain. Under the Indian Penal Code, since replaced by the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita of 2023, Section 499 defines criminal defamation, and Section 199 of the Code of Criminal Procedure states that a court can take cognisance of the offence only on a complaint by "some person aggrieved". The Supreme Court held in 1972 that where a statement concerns a group, that group must be definite and identifiable so that one can identify who is aggrieved. The Sultanpur court applied that test and found that the alleged imputation was primarily against Shah and at most partly concerned the BJP, so Mishra, as an ordinary party worker, could not maintain the prosecution.

An MP-MLA court is a specialised court set up to fast-track criminal trials and legal proceedings involving sitting and former MPs and MLAs. Gandhi, the Congress MP from Raebareli, had travelled from Lucknow to Sultanpur for the verdict, arriving at Lucknow's Chaudhary Charan Singh International Airport on Monday morning. On the way, at least four vehicles and an ambulance following his convoy collided on the Purvanchal Expressway; the Congress leader was safe and his vehicle was not among those involved, though several people, including Congress workers and security personnel, were injured.

The acquittal comes at a time when Gandhi is leading a united opposition campaign against Prime Minister Narendra Modi and the Chief Election Commissioner over a controversial revision of voter lists. It also follows a different outcome in another defamation case: a court in Modi's home state of Gujarat convicted Gandhi in 2023 in a separate defamation case over a "Modi surname" remark, which briefly cost him his seat in parliament before the Supreme Court stayed the conviction and his membership was restored. The contrast between the two cases has made the Sultanpur verdict a significant moment for the opposition, even as the legal questions it raised about who can bring a defamation complaint are likely to be debated well beyond this one case.
