Netanyahu Touts Trump Endorsement on Eve of Israel Vote, Hints at New War on Iran

world · 26 Oct 2026 · 3 min read · By Layla Haddad (Middle East Correspondent)

Tuesday's vote is Israel's first since the October 7, 2023, Hamas attacks, and the first chance for voters to judge the worst security failure in generations.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu boasted of his endorsement from Donald Trump on the eve of Israel's election, suggesting the two leaders could restart their war against Iran if he wins, after Trump said they should “finish the job”.

“US President Donald Trump has come with a friendship that has never existed before between an Israeli prime minister and an American president. And it is doubtful we will ever have such a friendly president again,” Netanyahu said in a televised appearance late on Sunday night. “He has another two years, and he says he is calling on you to vote for me.”

In his own social media post on Sunday, Trump, who launched the Iran war jointly with Netanyahu in February, said: “Vote for Bibi Netanyahu for Prime Minister, we have to finish the job that we started!”

Netanyahu said Trump was willing to take action “that would finally, finally, remove the Iranian threat”, but only as long as Netanyahu himself remains in power. “And I have already instructed the Mossad and the IDF to bring down this regime, to bring down this regime because that is the only way to put an end to this threat,” he said, referring to Israel's main intelligence service and military. The White House did not immediately respond to a request for comment on Netanyahu's remarks.

Netanyahu's leading opponent, centrist former military Chief of Staff Gadi Eisenkot, praised Trump as “a true friend of Israel” and said he was confident Trump would work in full cooperation with any leader chosen by the people. Eisenkot accused Netanyahu of “relying on a campaign of fear-mongering, lies, and threats”, including images of nuclear bombs in the heart of Tel Aviv.

Tuesday's vote, Israel's first since the October 7, 2023, Hamas attacks, is the first chance for voters to deliver a verdict on the worst security failure in generations and the wars on multiple fronts that followed. Polls suggest Netanyahu's Likud party will lose seats, potentially depriving his coalition of its narrow majority, though Eisenkot and the opposition also have no clear path to unseat him.
