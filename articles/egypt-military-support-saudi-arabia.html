Egypt Authorises Military Support to Defend Saudi Arabia as Houthi Attacks Escalate

world · 26 Oct 2026 · 3 min read · By Layla Haddad (Middle East Correspondent)

Egypt, the Arab world's most populous state, has not undertaken a large-scale foreign ground deployment since the 1991 Gulf War.

Parliament authorised Egypt's military on Monday to provide support to help defend Saudi Arabia, parliamentarians said and local media reported, a move that could push Cairo directly into the regional conflict that has expanded with intensified attacks by Yemen's Iran-allied Houthis on the Gulf kingdom.

President Abdel Fattah al-Sisi had asked parliament last week to approve defending its Gulf Arab ally and the world's biggest oil exporter if Riyadh requests help against the Houthis, whose advances also threaten the strategic Bab el-Mandeb Strait — a gateway to Egypt's Suez Canal.

Egypt has frequently condemned Iranian and Iranian-backed attacks on Gulf countries but has not publicly engaged in direct combat or joined the Mecca mutual defence pact between Saudi Arabia, Pakistan and Turkey that was signed in August. Foreign Minister Badr Abdelatty said this month that Egyptian membership in the pact remains under serious consideration.

Sisi has said Egypt's support would be limited to defending Saudi territory and borders rather than attacking Yemen, and Abdelatty has said Cairo advocates exploring every opportunity to resolve the Yemen conflict diplomatically.

Egypt joined the Saudi-led coalition in an earlier phase of Yemen's war in March 2015 mainly as a naval partner, sending warships to secure Red Sea shipping lanes while pledging air support. It also deployed around 800 ground troops but played only a limited combat role, and it has not undertaken a large-scale foreign ground deployment since the 1991 Gulf War.

Saudi Arabia is a major political and financial partner of Cairo, providing billions of dollars in aid, deposits at the central bank and energy supplies. Fighting between the Saudi-led coalition and the Houthi militia, which controls the capital Sanaa, has escalated in recent weeks, becoming a volatile front in the US-Israeli war on Iran, with Houthi attacks on Riyadh's international airport and the state oil giant Saudi Aramco disrupting flights and oil exports.
