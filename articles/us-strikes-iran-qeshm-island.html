US Carries Out 'Defensive' Strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island on Eve of Midterms

world · 3 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

US officials described the strikes as limited and defensive; no damage or casualty figures have been confirmed.

The United States carried out limited strikes on Iran's Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz late on Monday, US officials told US media, describing the action as defensive in nature and meant to protect US assets in the region. The Wall Street Journal first reported the strikes, citing US officials, and CNN, citing three US officials, and the Associated Press and Reuters, citing US officials, subsequently reported the same operation.

Iran's state-owned IRNA news agency reported several explosions on Qeshm Island in rapid succession late on Monday, with witnesses saying they saw two aircraft over the island. The semi-official Fars news agency said explosions were also heard in the port city of Bandar Lengeh, coming from the direction of the sea.

One US official told CNN the attack came in response to Iran firing at a US aircraft, while an official cited by the Associated Press said the targets were Iranians using surface-to-air missiles against US assets. Officials described the strikes as limited and said they did not mark the start of expanded operations or a return to major combat. The Pentagon referred questions to US Central Command, which had not publicly commented at the time of the reports.

The Wall Street Journal reported that the strikes were the latest in a series of smaller-scale strikes the United States has carried out in recent weeks without publicising them, with officials saying the operations were intended to restrict Iran's ability to attack commercial shipping in the Strait of Hormuz and were kept quiet so as not to suggest a resumption of major combat operations.

The strikes came on the eve of the US midterm elections on Tuesday. President Donald Trump said on Monday that a deal with Tehran could happen and, when asked whether he was considering striking Iran after the elections, said “no”. Vice President JD Vance said on Monday that the United States and Iran had made some progress toward a negotiated settlement.

Earlier on Monday, Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps claimed it had captured two US reconnaissance drones south of Qeshm and destroyed an MQ-9 drone east of the Strait of Hormuz. Those claims have not been independently verified, and no damage or casualty figures from the strikes themselves have been confirmed.

The Strait of Hormuz is a key route for oil shipments, and Reuters reported on Tuesday that oil prices edged up after the report of the strikes, raising concerns about an escalation of the conflict that began in late February with US and Israeli attacks on Iran.
