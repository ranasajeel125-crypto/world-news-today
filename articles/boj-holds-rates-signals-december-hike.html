Bank of Japan Holds Rates at 1.25% but Signals a December Hike May Be Next

business · 30 Oct 2026 · 6 min read · By World News Today (Business Desk)

The Bank of Japan held its policy rate at 1.25% on Friday, but Governor Kazuo Ueda stressed vigilance against inflation overshooting its target.

The Bank of Japan kept interest rates steady on Friday, maintaining its short-term policy target at 1.25% by an 8-1 vote, but revised up its inflation forecast and stressed anew its readiness to keep pushing up borrowing costs to forestall mounting inflation risks.

Board member Hajime Takata dissented from the decision, instead calling for a rate rise to 1.5%. He argued that with underlying inflation having reached the price stability target, the situation had shifted to a new phase in which the bank needs to step up the pace of bringing the policy interest rate closer to the neutral interest rate so that it could make nimble responses. His proposal was defeated by a majority vote. No board member dissented in favour of a cut, removing a risk flagged ahead of the meeting, and two members who opposed the September hike voted with the majority this time. The decision caps a week in which the US Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand all left interest rates unchanged.

In a significant shift from its previous assessments, the Bank of Japan recognised that underlying inflation, excluding temporary factors, had reached its 2% target. It was the first time since the bank adopted the 2% price stability target in 2013 that it had made such an assessment. Governor Kazuo Ueda said the central bank must now focus on anchoring inflation around that level, adding that it must be vigilant against the risk of inflation overshooting its target. "We saw the need to scrutinise a bit more economic and price risks, as well as financial conditions. But underlying inflation has reached levels consistent with our price target," Ueda told a news conference. "We will adjust monetary policy as appropriate so that underlying inflation becomes anchored around 2%." The bank said it was vital to keep inflation around the current level going forward, and warned of the risk of underlying inflation deviating above its target.

The shift suggests the next rate hike could come as soon as December, analysts said, after the bank raised rates in September to a 31-year high of 1.25%. More than 80 per cent of economists surveyed expect another hike in December, and markets are pricing in a high chance of a further increase in December or January. Oxford Economics maintained its call that the Bank of Japan will hike in December and April to a terminal rate of 1.75 per cent. UOB expects another 25-basis-point hike at the 17-18 December meeting, then a pause in the first quarter of 2027 before a further hike to 1.75 per cent in the second quarter. Ueda stopped short of signalling an imminent hike but kept the door open, reiterating that the bank will assess the need for a rate increase at every meeting, including December, and stressed that the bank's job is not finished. Kazutaka Maeda of Meiji Yasuda Research Institute told Reuters the bank may now be weighing hikes every three months rather than every six, making December credible.

The bank's quarterly Outlook for Economic Activity and Prices reinforced the normalisation narrative, with upward revisions to growth and inflation forecasts. In its Outlook Report, the board lifted its median forecast for core consumer prices, which exclude fresh food, to 2.6 per cent for fiscal 2027, from 2.4 per cent in July. It kept the fiscal 2026 forecast at 2.5 per cent and fiscal 2028 at 2 per cent. The median forecast for core-core CPI, excluding fresh food and energy, was cut by 0.1 percentage points to 2.4 per cent for fiscal 2026, but raised for fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2028 by 0.1 points to 2.7 per cent and 2.3 per cent respectively. The board now sees real GDP rising 0.7 per cent in fiscal 2026, up from 0.6 per cent, and 0.7 per cent in fiscal 2027, down from 0.8 per cent. It expects inflation to accelerate to a level clearly above 2 per cent before easing back toward 2 per cent in the second half of the projection period as the oil shock fades. The bank's assessment of risks to its fiscal 2026 forecast is balanced for growth, but tilts to the upside for inflation. The bank also said currency moves are now more likely to feed through to prices than in the past, because firms have become more willing to raise wages and prices.

Tokyo core consumer prices rose 2.6% in October from a year earlier, well above the bank's target, government data showed on Friday. The figure was in line with the median market forecast, after 2.7 per cent in September. Tokyo's inflation figures are published ahead of the nationwide data and are closely watched as a leading indicator for the country as a whole.

Alongside the rate decision, the Bank of Japan said it would sell about 860 billion yen ($5.45 billion) of exchange-traded funds a year on a book-value basis from January 2027, accelerating the pace of sales. The change was decided by a unanimous vote. The bank started selling ETFs in January 2026 at a pace of about 330 billion yen a year, and has been conducting the sales smoothly while avoiding a major impact on the market. Given that trading value in the stock market has been expanding, the bank decided to sell its ETF holdings at a scale of about 0.1 per cent of trading value in the market, up from about 0.05 per cent. At that rate it will take about 43 years to unwind its ETF holdings, compared with an estimated 112 years under the previous pace. Converted to market value, the annual sales amount would be about 2,460 billion yen. The bank became Japan's largest single equity holder after purchasing ETFs to support financial conditions during the deflationary period, buying a total of 37.19 trillion yen of ETFs in the 2010s. When financial market conditions change, sales can be temporarily adjusted or even suspended within the annual quota.

The yen firmed and bonds rallied on Friday as investors sought safety amid a sell-off in technology shares over worries about the sustainability of spending on artificial intelligence. The 10-year Japanese government bond yield rose ahead of the meeting before easing to end the week little changed at 2.78 per cent. Markets are now pricing in a high chance of a further Bank of Japan rate increase in December or January as Ueda pursues the normalisation of policy after more than a decade of massive stimulus. The normalisation began in March 2024, when the bank ended its negative interest rate policy with its first rate rise in 17 years, and has proceeded in small steps since — a sharp contrast with the aggressive tightening seen in the United States and Europe, and a reflection of how long Japan spent fighting deflation. The October decision, with its declaration that the 2 per cent target has finally been reached, marks the moment the bank's task changed: from coaxing inflation higher to making sure it does not overshoot.
