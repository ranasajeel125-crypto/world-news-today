Bank of Japan Holds Rates at 1.25% but Signals a December Hike May Be Next

business · 30 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (Business Desk)

The Bank of Japan held its policy rate at 1.25% on Friday, but Governor Kazuo Ueda stressed vigilance against inflation overshooting its target.

The Bank of Japan kept interest rates steady on Friday, maintaining its short-term policy target at 1.25% by an 8-1 vote, but revised up its inflation forecast and stressed anew its readiness to keep pushing up borrowing costs to forestall mounting inflation risks.

Board member Hajime Takata dissented from the decision, instead calling for a rate rise to 1.5%. The decision caps a week in which the US Federal Reserve, the European Central Bank and the Reserve Bank of New Zealand all left interest rates unchanged.

In a significant shift from its previous assessments, the Bank of Japan recognised that underlying inflation, excluding temporary factors, had reached its 2% target. Governor Kazuo Ueda said the central bank must now focus on anchoring inflation around that level, adding that it must be vigilant against the risk of inflation overshooting its target.

"We saw the need to scrutinise a bit more economic and price risks, as well as financial conditions. But underlying inflation has reached levels consistent with our price target," Ueda told a news conference. "We will adjust monetary policy as appropriate so that underlying inflation becomes anchored around 2%."

The shift suggests the next rate hike could come as soon as December, analysts said, after the bank raised rates in September to a 31-year high of 1.25%. Tokyo core consumer prices rose 2.6% in October from a year earlier, well above the bank's target, government data showed on Friday.

Alongside the rate decision, the Bank of Japan said it would sell about 860 billion yen ($5.45 billion) of exchange-traded funds a year on a book-value basis from January 2027, accelerating the pace of sales. At that rate it will take about 43 years to unwind its ETF holdings, compared with an estimated 112 years under the previous pace.

The yen firmed and bonds rallied on Friday as investors sought safety amid a sell-off in technology shares over worries about the sustainability of spending on artificial intelligence. Markets are now pricing in a high chance of a further Bank of Japan rate increase in December or January as Ueda pursues the normalisation of policy after more than a decade of massive stimulus.
