IMF Warns Developing Economies Face Mounting Financial Pressure

business · 25 Oct 2026 · 3 min read · By Daniyal Ahmed (Business Correspondent)

Emerging-market investors face higher borrowing costs and a strong dollar, the IMF warns.

The International Monetary Fund has highlighted the economic challenges facing developing countries amid global uncertainty, warning that high borrowing costs, a strong dollar and slowing global growth are squeezing the economies least able to absorb the shock.

Many developing nations are spending a growing share of government revenue just servicing debt, leaving less for health, education and infrastructure. When loans are denominated in dollars, every rise in the US currency makes repayment heavier in local terms.

The Fund's message to richer nations is familiar but urgent: restructure unsustainable debts faster, keep trade open, and direct more concessional finance to countries investing in climate resilience and energy transitions.

For ordinary people in the affected economies, the pressure shows up as expensive food and fuel imports and tight government budgets. How the IMF's warnings translate into actual relief will be tested at the Fund's next round of programme reviews.
