Virgin Media O2 to Cut Thousands of Jobs in Sweeping Shakeup, Sky News Reports

business · 31 Oct 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (Business Desk)

Virgin Media O2 is jointly owned by Liberty Global and Spain's Telefonica.

British telecoms operator Virgin Media O2, which is jointly owned by Liberty Global and Spain's Telefonica, will cut thousands of jobs in a sweeping shakeup, Sky News reported on Saturday, citing people close to the company.

Up to 4,000 of Virgin Media O2's 16,000 employees could be affected, although the precise number of job cuts has yet to be finalised, the report said.

Most of the job cuts are expected to affect non-customer-facing roles, while a number of senior leadership changes are already under way, the report said.

Virgin Media O2 did not immediately respond to a request for comment, Reuters reported.

The company is also expected to review its use of contractors and suppliers in an effort to reduce costs, the report said.

The Financial Times reported in July that Virgin Media O2's owners were weighing options to reduce the company's £22 billion ($29.46 billion) debt pile, Reuters noted. In September, the Financial Times reported that the company's shareholders were seeking to reduce costs by about £600 million.

The scale of the proposed cuts is striking: 4,000 roles would represent about a quarter of the company's 16,000-strong workforce. Sky News reported that the numbers were still being finalised ahead of the company's third-quarter results, which were due to be announced on Tuesday, November 3, when a cost-reduction target was expected to be confirmed. Sources suggested as many as 4,000 jobs were likely to be affected, though the final figure had not been settled when the report was published.

The shake-up was confirmed days later. On Tuesday, November 3, the company confirmed it would axe thousands of jobs as it looked to slash its £22 billion debt mountain and reduce costs. The announcement did not state how many jobs would be cut, but it confirmed the weekend reports, initially from Sky News, that thousands of positions would likely be affected. Sky News, citing an unnamed insider, had said as many as 4,000 roles were facing the axe.

Virgin Media O2 was formed in 2021 through the merger of Virgin Media, the cable broadband and television business, and O2, the UK mobile network. The combined company is owned in equal shares by Liberty Global, the international cable group, and Telefonica, the Spanish telecoms group that owns O2. The merger created one of Britain's largest telecoms operators, selling broadband, mobile, television and fixed-line services to millions of households and businesses across the UK.

The company's debt has been a persistent concern for its owners. The £22 billion debt pile was built up in part through the financing of the merger itself and through heavy investment in network infrastructure, including the rollout of gigabit-capable broadband and the expansion of 5G mobile coverage. Servicing that debt has become more expensive as interest rates have risen from the very low levels that prevailed when much of the borrowing was arranged, putting pressure on the company's finances.

In July, the Financial Times reported that the owners were weighing options to reduce the debt, and in September it reported that shareholders were seeking to cut costs by about £600 million. The job cuts now under discussion form part of that cost-reduction effort. Alongside the redundancies, the company is expected to review its spending on contractors and suppliers, and a number of senior leadership changes are already under way as the business reshapes its management team.

The cuts are expected to fall mainly on non-customer-facing roles, which suggests that frontline staff in shops and customer service are less likely to be affected than back-office, support and management functions. Even so, a reduction of up to a quarter of the workforce would be one of the largest redundancy programmes in the UK telecoms sector in recent years, and it is likely to draw attention from unions, politicians and regulators.

The announcement comes at a difficult time for the UK telecoms industry. Operators face the cost of continuing to upgrade their networks, including the rollout of full-fibre broadband and 5G, at a time when competition is intense and customers are sensitive to price rises. Virgin Media O2 competes with BT, Sky, Vodafone and Three, among others, in markets where rivals have also been cutting costs. BT has been reducing its own workforce as it completes its fibre build, and Vodafone has announced job cuts across its European operations in recent years.

For the company's employees, the uncertainty is likely to persist until the final numbers are confirmed. Sky News reported that the precise number of cuts had yet to be finalised, and the company's confirmation on November 3 did not give a figure. Workers and their representatives will be watching for details of any consultation process, the timetable for the changes, and whether voluntary redundancy or redeployment will be offered before compulsory cuts are made.

The restructuring also raises questions about the company's longer-term strategy. Its owners have been examining ways to reduce the debt burden, which could include asset sales, refinancing or a change in the ownership structure. Any of those options would take time, and the cost-cutting programme appears to be the most immediate step. Whether the savings are enough to satisfy the company's lenders and shareholders will become clearer when the third-quarter results and the cost-reduction target are published.
