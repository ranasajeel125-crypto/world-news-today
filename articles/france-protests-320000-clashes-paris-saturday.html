320,000 Protest Across France as Clashes Erupt in Paris on Third Weekend of Unrest

world · 31 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Paris marches were rerouted to the capital's edge and 70,000 police and gendarmes were deployed nationwide.

Hundreds of thousands of mostly young protesters took to the streets across France on Saturday in a third consecutive weekend of demonstrations over living costs and education, the Associated Press reported.

About 70,000 police and gendarmes were deployed across the country, and Paris marches were rerouted to the capital's edge. Youth-led demonstrations were also reported in Germany and Italy.

France's government estimated that 320,000 people protested nationwide on Saturday, fewer than in previous protests but still a significant turnout, the Associated Press reported. Mass protests on Oct. 17 that deteriorated into some of France's worst rioting in years drew more than 500,000 people around France, and demonstrations on Oct. 24 drew 410,000, according to government estimates. Organisers' turnout estimates are much higher.

The Interior Ministry told reporters that of the 320,000 protesting on Saturday, 70,000 people are estimated to have turned out for the main protest in Paris, which police moved outside the city centre to reduce the risk of unrest around government buildings and tourist sites after a last-minute court battle.

French Interior Minister Laurent Nuñez warned of a very high risk of disorder, especially after dark on Halloween. Police had arrested thousands of people following riots and looting that marred the largely peaceful protest marches the previous two Saturdays.

At Porte de Bagnolet, on the eastern edge of Paris, thousands gathered behind a line of students who led the march hand in hand. The crowd streamed along the Boulevards des Maréchaux, the ring of avenues that skirts the edge of Paris, in a march that was joyful for hours, the Associated Press reported.

Later, clashes broke out near Porte de Charenton, where the march ended. Protesters hurled glass bottles and fireworks at police, and officers responded with volleys of stun grenades and tear gas. Many peaceful marchers turned back, coughing, their eyes streaming with tears, and left the march, some scattering into the nearby Bois de Vincennes.

The rallies started last month as a movement of high school students protesting neglected buildings, teacher shortages and a university admissions system seen as unfair to disadvantaged students. Labour unions and others angry at President Emmanuel Macron joined in with a variety of demands.

Macron said that Saturday's marches would stay out of city centres to protect shops. Police also barred people from entering Place de la Republique and Les Halles from 11 p.m. to 6 a.m. on Saturday and Sunday, and unaccompanied minors faced a separate 9 p.m. curfew around the Champs-Elysees.

Source: Associated Press
