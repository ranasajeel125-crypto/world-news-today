Trump to Meet Putin and Xi in First-Ever Trilateral Summit in China, White House Says

world · 14 Nov 2026 · 4 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The three leaders will meet on Thursday on the sidelines of the APEC summit in Shenzhen. Beijing and Moscow have yet to officially confirm they will attend.

President Donald Trump will meet jointly next week with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin, the White House said on Friday, in the first-ever trilateral meeting between the leaders of the United States, China and Russia.

The three leaders will meet on Thursday on the sidelines of the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (APEC) summit in Shenzhen, China, with the reduction of nuclear weapons at the top of the agenda. The meeting brings together the heads of the world's three biggest nuclear powers for the first time in one room.

“He will discuss the reduction of nuclear weapons in the world, whose proliferation President Trump believes is very dangerous,” White House spokeswoman Olivia Wales told reporters on a call on Friday, adding that the meeting would take place on Thursday in China.

Officials were careful to lower expectations about what the meeting can deliver. “We do not expect to get to any kind of agreement,” a senior US official said, describing the talks instead as a starting point for nuclear discussions between the three countries. The meeting comes after the last nuclear arms treaty between the United States and Russia expired in February, leaving the world's two largest nuclear arsenals without a binding limits agreement for the first time in decades.

Trump has repeatedly insisted that the first-of-its-kind meeting of the three leaders focus on nuclear arms reductions, and officials said he is prepared to address what one official described as the “difficult” issue of China's nuclear arsenal. China has been expanding its nuclear forces, and Washington has long argued that any future arms-control framework must include Beijing as well as Moscow — a position that has complicated efforts to replace the expired treaty.

The agenda will reach beyond nuclear weapons. Trump plans to discuss the war against Iran with Xi and Putin, according to US officials, while Russia's war in Ukraine is not expected to be raised at the trilateral meeting. Washington intends to handle Ukraine through its separate diplomatic track with Moscow, which this week saw US envoys hold talks with Putin in the Kremlin and with Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy in Dublin after two days of negotiations in Abu Dhabi.

The trilateral meeting will close a four-day Asia trip for Trump. He leaves Washington on Monday and arrives in the Philippines on Tuesday for a summit of the Association of Southeast Asian Nations and a meeting of Quad leaders, before travelling to Shenzhen on Wednesday for the APEC meetings. On Thursday he will hold a bilateral meeting and working lunch with Xi, and then conclude his visit to China with the trilateral session. He is not expected to hold a separate one-on-one meeting with Putin.

Billionaire Elon Musk is expected to accompany Trump on the trip to Shenzhen, officials said, although he will not take part in the three-way meeting with Xi and Putin. Beijing and Moscow have yet to officially confirm their participation in the trilateral summit, and the White House announcement on Friday was the first public confirmation that the meeting is planned.

The background: the APEC summit in Shenzhen runs on November 18 and 19, with Xi hosting this year's gathering in China's southern tech hub. Trump has made the trilateral meeting a personal priority, raising it repeatedly in public remarks as a way to bring the three nuclear powers into the same conversation — something no previous US president has attempted in this format. His administration has framed nuclear proliferation as one of the most dangerous threats facing the world, and the meeting is being presented as the opening move in a longer effort rather than a one-off event.

What happens next: the three leaders meet on Thursday in Shenzhen, and the first test will be whether Beijing and Moscow formally confirm their attendance before then. Even if no agreement is announced, the meeting itself will be closely watched for any sign of a shared framework on nuclear arms — and for what Trump says afterwards about Iran, where the United States is still carrying out strikes and where Washington is seeking to keep pressure on Tehran while its allies urge restraint.

Why it matters: a joint meeting of the American, Chinese and Russian presidents has never happened before, and it comes at a moment when all three relationships are under strain — the United States at war with Iran, Russia at war with Ukraine, and the US and China locked in rivalry over trade, technology and Taiwan. That the three leaders are willing to sit down together at all, even to talk about nuclear weapons with no deal expected, is itself a signal. Whether it becomes the start of a new arms-control process or a one-day photo opportunity will be decided in Shenzhen on Thursday — and by what follows it.
