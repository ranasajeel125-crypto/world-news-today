UK to Require Economic Benefits from Major Foreign Defence Contracts

business · 30 Oct 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (Business Desk)

The new policy, applying case by case from April 2027, will be overseen by a new Offset Authority, the government said.

Britain will require overseas defence suppliers winning selected military contracts worth more than £50 million ($67 million) to deliver jobs, investment, skills or other economic benefits in the UK, under a new policy announced on Friday, Reuters reported.

The government said the policy would apply on a case-by-case basis from April 2027, with a new Offset Authority overseeing agreements and delivery. Contractors could be required to generate economic benefits in Britain worth up to the value of a contract — meaning a £100 million contract could result in up to £100 million of investment, jobs or skills programmes.

More than £35 billion has been spent on defence contracts with overseas suppliers since 2016, representing about 11% of direct Ministry of Defence spending with industry, the government said.

Britain sees higher defence spending as a way to strengthen both its military and its industrial base, and ministers argue the new rules will make sure more of that spending supports British jobs and skills rather than flowing entirely overseas.

The announcement comes days after the government unveiled an extra £10 billion of economic and military support for Ukraine in the Budget — a separate package covered in World News Today's earlier report — as defence spending climbs the political agenda across Europe amid the wars in Ukraine, Iran and Yemen.

Source: Reuters
