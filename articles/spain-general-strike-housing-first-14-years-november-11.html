Workers Across Spain Stage First General Strike in 14 Years Over Housing Crisis

world · 11 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Average rent of €1,176 now eats up almost an entire young person’s monthly salary, and Spain faces a housing deficit of about 750,000 homes, according to the central bank.

Spanish workers went on a general strike on Wednesday, setting up picket lines despite stormy weather in a mass protest against the country’s housing affordability crisis, a few weeks before a snap general election.

It is the first general strike in 14 years to be jointly backed by Spain’s two largest unions, UGT and CCOO, as well as other labour organisations and tenants’ associations angry about rising rents and evictions. The last joint strike took place on 14 November 2012, during the government of Mariano Rajoy.

Spain has a deficit of about 750,000 homes, which is set to grow to more than a million by 2028, according to the central bank. The average rental price of €1,176 ($1,315) is equivalent to about 98.7% of a young person’s average monthly salary, according to the Youth Council of Spain.

The strike follows weeks of demonstrations and encampments provoked by the eviction and subsequent death of an 87-year-old woman, Maricarmen Abascal, in Madrid. Those protests, along with parliament’s rejection last month of government decrees aimed at curbing evictions and limiting rent pressures, prompted Prime Minister Pedro Sanchez to call an election for 29 November.

More than 150 pickets, rallies and demonstrations were planned across the country, with a march in Madrid setting off from Atocha in the evening. Two storms, dubbed Charles and Bruna, led weather agency AEMET to issue alerts for 14 regions over strong winds, rough seas and heavy rains. Mr Sanchez has defended the right to strike, and on Thursday the Permanent Deputation of Congress is due to vote on two housing decrees.
