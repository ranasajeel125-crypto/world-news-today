Taiwan Accuses China of Harassing Shipping With East Coast Patrol

world · 1 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Taiwan said two Chinese ships were involved in the latest east coast patrol, and that China also sent a ship close to the Taiwan-controlled Pratas Islands.

China's Coast Guard is harassing shipping with its latest patrol off Taiwan's east coast and trying to exert a fictional jurisdiction, Taiwan's Coast Guard said, adding that it had sent its own ships to keep watch, Reuters reported.

China, which views Taiwan as its own territory, has stepped up activity off the island's east coast since June with regular coast guard patrols. Officials in Taiwan, which rejects Beijing's sovereignty claims, say they believe China is practising for a potential maritime blockade using overlapping military, paramilitary and civilian assets.

Taipei regards the area off its east coast as critical to wartime supply routes from allies in the event of a blockade or conflict. China recognises no claims of sovereignty by Taiwan.

Responding late on Saturday to China's announcement of its latest east coast patrol, Taiwan's Coast Guard said two Chinese ships were involved and that it had sent its own forces to monitor.

Chinese state media showed video of one of its ships broadcasting a radio message that it was carrying out law enforcement and offering help should any be needed. It did not say who the message was broadcast at, but showed footage of an unidentified fishing trawler.

Taiwan's Coast Guard said China's attempts to harass ships at sea through radio communications were a clear expansion of power that violate international law, undermine regional peace and stability, and deviate from reality.

Taiwan holds sovereign rights and jurisdiction over the exclusive economic zone east of Taiwan, and China has absolutely no jurisdiction over any of Taiwan's waters, its Coast Guard added.

China on Saturday also sent another Coast Guard ship close to the Taiwan-controlled Pratas Islands, which are strategically located at the top of the South China Sea, Taiwan's Coast Guard said separately.

Taiwan has so far this year expelled Chinese Coast Guard vessels from Pratas waters 13 times, it added. The Pratas have no permanent civilian population and lie roughly between southern Taiwan and Hong Kong. Security experts see them as vulnerable to Chinese attack due to their distance — more than 400 km (250 miles) — from Taiwan.
