Keisha Lance Bottoms Wins Georgia Governor Race, First Black Woman Elected Governor of a US State

politics · 4 Nov 2026 · 5 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Bottoms, 56, is the former mayor of Atlanta and previously served as a senior adviser to former President Joe Biden.

politics · 4 Nov 2026 · 2 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Democrat Keisha Lance Bottoms won Georgia's race for governor on Tuesday, becoming the first Black woman elected governor of a US state and delivering Democrats a victory in one of the nation's most closely divided political battlegrounds.

The former Atlanta mayor, who will also be the first female governor of the state, defeated Republican businessman Rick Jackson, according to US media projections. The Associated Press called the race for Bottoms, and with 75 percent of the vote counted she had 55.4 percent to Jackson's 44.6 percent. By Wednesday morning she had secured 54.31 percent of the vote, more than 360,000 votes ahead of Jackson.

She succeeds Republican Governor Brian Kemp, who is leaving office after serving two consecutive terms. Bottoms, 56, previously served as a senior adviser to former President Joe Biden and director of the White House Office of Public Engagement. She will be Georgia's 84th governor, and she takes office in January.

The victory marked a new milestone for Black women in American politics, who have long been a core base within the Democratic Party but remain underrepresented in the nation's highest elected offices. Bottoms is the first Black woman ever elected governor in the United States, and the first Black governor of Georgia. Her win was also the first time Georgia has elected a Democratic governor in more than 20 years, breaking the Republican hold on the governor's office that had lasted since the early 2000s. Republicans had controlled the governor's office for more than two decades.

Jackson, the billionaire founder of healthcare staffing company Jackson Healthcare, cast himself as a political outsider and campaigned on tax cuts, public safety and reducing government regulation. He was endorsed by President Trump, and he spent more than $200 million of his personal fortune on the campaign, in an attempt to succeed the popular outgoing governor. On election night, Bottoms told supporters that no amount of money could buy the power of the people, a line aimed directly at the scale of Jackson's spending. The two candidates had come into election day in a statistical tie in polling, which made the scale of Bottoms' eventual victory all the more striking.

Bottoms' campaign focused on affordability, Medicaid expansion, health care access and housing, the issues that polls showed mattered most to Georgia voters. Georgia is one of the states that has not expanded Medicaid under the Affordable Care Act, and Bottoms made expansion a central promise, arguing it would bring health coverage to hundreds of thousands of Georgians and support rural hospitals that have been closing. On housing, she promised action on rising costs, and on affordability more broadly she argued that working families were being squeezed by prices that had risen faster than wages.

Her path to the governorship ran through Atlanta politics and the White House. She entered politics on the Atlanta City Council in 2009, and in 2017 she was elected mayor of Atlanta, leading the city through the COVID-19 pandemic and the protests that followed the killing of George Floyd. She chose not to run for a second term as mayor in 2021, and in 2022 she joined President Biden's administration as a senior adviser and director of the Office of Public Engagement, a role that put her at the centre of the administration's outreach to communities across the country.

Bottoms' win comes eight years after fellow Democrat Stacey Abrams became the first Black woman nominated for governor by a major US political party. Abrams lost governor's races in Georgia in 2018 and 2022, but her campaigns helped to build a multigenerational, multiracial coalition that boosted Democratic turnout, helped elect two Democratic US senators and delivered Georgia's electoral votes to a Democratic presidential candidate for the first time in decades. Bottoms' victory was the third consecutive time Georgia Democrats had nominated a Black woman for governor, and it was propelled in part by the state's significant Black voting bloc, which turned out in large numbers during early voting.

The same evening, Stacey Plaskett was elected governor of the US Virgin Islands, a US territory, becoming the first Black woman elected governor of a US jurisdiction. And in Florida, Republican Representative Byron Donalds won the governor's race, becoming that state's first Black governor. Together with Maryland Governor Wes Moore, who won re-election on the same day, Lance Bottoms and Donalds will join a rare group of only five Black elected governors in American history, and a record five Black governors will serve at the same time beginning in January, nearly doubling the number of Black Americans ever elected governor in the United States.

The victory may not be the end of the fight. Before Bottoms can be sworn in, there may be a battle in the state legislature over redistricting, with Republicans in the state house considering new maps before she takes office. For now, though, the result stands as a historic moment for Georgia and for the country: a Black woman, a former mayor of Atlanta, elected to lead a Southern state that was, for more than two decades, a Republican stronghold.
