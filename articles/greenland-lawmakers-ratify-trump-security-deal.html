Greenland Lawmakers Set to Ratify Trump Security Deal

world · 3 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

The deal expands the US military role in Greenland, paving the way for two new bases, while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to self-determination.

Greenland's parliament is set to ratify on Tuesday the Arctic island's security agreement struck with US President Donald Trump, after a majority of lawmakers signalled support for the deal in a first debate last month.

The United States, Denmark and Greenland signed the agreement in September, aimed at ending tensions after Trump's repeated threats to take control of the strategically located and mineral-rich island, a self-governing Danish territory.

The deal expands the US military's role in Greenland, paving the way for two new military bases and Trump's “Golden Dome” missile defence system, while reaffirming Danish sovereignty and Greenlanders' right to self-determination.

Greenlandic Foreign Minister Mute Egede said the ratification was another important step for the island. “The road to this point has been a difficult one for us as a people. But we have stood firm on our fundamental principles, chosen dialogue and taken responsibility for our country's interests,” he wrote in a post on Facebook.

“This is about all of us. The Greenland we love. About our security. And about our right to shape our own future,” he added.

The Danish parliament is due to give the bill a first reading on November 11, followed by a second reading and a vote on November 19. The executive agreement will enter into force once Denmark and Greenland have ratified it and does not require approval from the US Senate.
