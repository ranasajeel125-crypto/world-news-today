Russia Says Europe Can Join Ukraine Peace Talks Only If It Shows ‘Readiness to Contribute’

world · 13 Nov 2026 · 3 min read · By World News Today (World Desk)

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov said Moscow had not yet committed to a European seat at the next round of talks, a day after US envoys said Mr Putin had agreed to it.

The Kremlin said on Friday that Europe must show it is ready to contribute to the Ukraine peace process before any decision is made on letting Europeans join the negotiations, casting fresh doubt on whether Moscow has actually agreed to a European seat at the table.

The comment by Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov suggests Russia has not yet committed to including Europeans in the next round of talks, despite US negotiators saying on Thursday that President Vladimir Putin had agreed to their participation. Asked whether peace talks would soon resume with Europeans added, Mr Peskov said: “Once we understand their genuine intention and readiness to contribute to the peace process, then it will be possible to make a decision on holding dialogue on a Ukrainian settlement in an expanded format.”

The talks have so far involved Russian, Ukrainian and US officials, who met in Abu Dhabi this week for the first time in nine months. Two US envoys, Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner, then flew to Moscow to meet Mr Putin and later met Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy in Dublin. Russian and Ukrainian officials said some progress was made at the Abu Dhabi talks, but there was no sign of a major breakthrough on key issues such as territory.

The question of a European role burst into the open on Thursday, when Mr Witkoff said Mr Putin had agreed to let European representatives join the next round of talks. Mr Kushner added that Washington was working toward what he called the war’s “largest prisoner exchange”, writing on X that it could “potentially reunite thousands of people with their families”, without giving details of the deal. Mr Zelenskiy welcomed the prospect of a European presence, saying he hoped the agenda would include efforts towards a ceasefire and defusing the conflict.

Mr Putin, however, struck a sharper note on Thursday, saying the US must work with its European allies to understand whether they wanted peace or war in Ukraine. Mr Peskov said Moscow would be open to some form of contacts with Europe before any European participation in negotiations, but that no concrete discussions on this had taken place.

Who would speak for Europe is itself unsettled. Mr Peskov declined to comment on the possibility of European Union foreign policy chief Kaja Kallas representing the EU at the negotiations. Ms Kallas, who has frequently questioned Russia’s good faith in the peace process and taken a lead in imposing successive waves of sanctions on Moscow, said on Friday it was not up to Russia to say who should represent the EU. “They don’t want anybody strong on the other side because they want to push through their maximum demands,” she said.

The background: European involvement would mark a new phase in efforts to end a conflict that has defied attempts by US President Donald Trump to secure a peace agreement since he returned to the White House. Russia’s relations with European powers are dire. European states have armed Ukraine and imposed sanctions on Moscow, and most have shunned direct contact with Mr Putin since he sent troops into Ukraine in February 2022. France’s Europe minister, Benjamin Haddad, said this week that the EU is preparing new sanctions covering about 150 entities and 50 banks, due on Monday, according to Reuters.

Moscow has also made clear what kind of outcome it wants. Russia says it wants a full peace settlement, not a ceasefire. Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov said this week that Moscow wanted to negotiate a broad European security framework before tackling the specifics of the war — a position that would put the continent’s wider security architecture on the table alongside the fate of Ukrainian territory.

That territorial question remains the hardest obstacle. Russia holds a fifth of Ukraine, and Kyiv has refused Russia’s demands to give up territory in the eastern region of Donbas that it has not lost in fighting. Mr Putin has shown no signs of being ready to moderate his territorial demands, which include a buffer zone in parts of Ukraine that Russia has not formally claimed as its own.

What happens next: there is still no date for the next round of talks, and no agreement yet on who sits at the table. Washington is pressing ahead with the prisoner exchange effort, which Mr Kushner described as potentially the largest of the war, while the question of Europe’s role now hangs on Moscow’s test of “readiness to contribute” — a condition European capitals have yet to answer in public. For Kyiv, which has long argued that no settlement about Europe’s security can be made without Europe, the coming days will show whether Thursday’s announcement of a European seat was a breakthrough or only a promise that Moscow is already walking back.
